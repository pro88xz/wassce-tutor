### 1. `bio-po-01`

- **A.** combines with haemoglobin and stops the blood carrying oxygen  ✅
- **B.** destroys the ozone layer
- **C.** causes acid rain
- **D.** has a strong smell that irritates the lungs

Carbon monoxide binds to haemoglobin far more firmly than oxygen does, so less oxygen reaches the brain and other organs. Generators and charcoal stoves belong outside.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- destroys the ozone layer: The ozone layer is damaged by CFCs, not by carbon monoxide.

- has a strong smell that irritates the lungs: Carbon monoxide has no colour and no smell, which is why victims do not notice it.

- **B.** the algae produce too much oxygen
- **C.** the dead algae stop the fish reaching the surface to breathe air
- **D.** bacteria decomposing the dead algae use up the oxygen dissolved in the water  ✅

This is eutrophication: nutrients → algal bloom → algae die → decomposers multiply and respire → dissolved oxygen falls → fish suffocate. Sewage has the same effect.

### 8. `bio-mo-01`

- **A.** They have a cell wall and cytoplasm
- **B.** They can reproduce only inside a living host cell  ✅
- **C.** They reproduce by binary fission
- **D.** They are killed by antibiotics

A virus takes over a host cell and makes it build new viruses, often destroying the cell. HIV, Ebola, measles and cassava mosaic disease are caused by viruses.

**Why the other options are wrong**

## Public Health and Disease Prevention

_Syllabus: Personal and community hygiene; safe water; disposal of refuse and sewage; the VIP latrine; vector control; immunity and vaccination_

### 22. `bio-ph-01`

A baby receives antibodies from its mother's breast milk. This gives the baby

- **A.** natural active immunity
- **B.** artificial active immunity
- **C.** artificial passive immunity
- **D.** natural passive immunity  ✅

Passive immunity uses antibodies made by another body; it acts at once but does not last. Active immunity is slower to start but lasts, because memory cells are made.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- natural active immunity: Active immunity means the body makes its own antibodies, for example after recovering from measles.
- artificial active immunity: Artificial active immunity comes from vaccination.
- artificial passive immunity: Artificial passive immunity comes from an injection of ready-made antibodies, such as anti-tetanus serum.

### 23. `bio-ph-02`

A vaccine protects a person against a disease because it

- **A.** makes the body produce its own antibodies and memory cells, without causing the disease  ✅
- **B.** contains ready-made antibodies that last for life
- **C.** kills all the bacteria in the body
- **D.** is an antibiotic that kills the pathogen

A vaccine contains a dead or weakened pathogen, or part of one. If the real pathogen enters later, memory cells make antibodies quickly, before the disease can develop.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- contains ready-made antibodies that last for life: Ready-made antibodies give passive immunity, which lasts only a short time.
- kills all the bacteria in the body: A vaccine does not kill microbes; it trains the immune system to recognise one pathogen.
- is an antibiotic that kills the pathogen: Antibiotics are medicines that kill bacteria; vaccines prevent disease.

- bacteria were selectively bred over many generations: Selective breeding cannot give bacteria a human gene.

### 39. `bio-ab-04`

In a biogas digester, animal dung and plant waste are broken down by

- **A.** anaerobic bacteria, producing methane that can be burned for cooking  ✅
- **B.** aerobic bacteria, producing oxygen
- **C.** yeast, producing alcohol for fuel
- **D.** fungi, producing carbon monoxide for lighting

The sealed digester has no air, so anaerobic bacteria break the waste down to methane. The sludge left behind makes a good fertiliser.

**Why the other options are wrong**

### 40. `bio-ab-05`

When making gari, grated cassava is left to ferment for two or three days before it is pressed and roasted. The fermentation

- **B.** helps to remove poisonous cyanide compounds and gives gari its sour taste  ✅
- **C.** makes the cassava sweeter by producing sugar
- **D.** kills all the microorganisms in the cassava

Cassava roots contain compounds that release cyanide. Fermentation by lactic acid bacteria and other microbes, followed by pressing and roasting, removes most of it.

### 43. `bio-pb-01`

A student views a slide with a ×10 eyepiece and a ×40 objective. What is the total magnification?

- **A.** ×4
- **B.** ×50
- **C.** ×400  ✅
- **D.** ×4000

Total magnification = eyepiece magnification × objective magnification = 10 × 40 = ×400.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- ×4: This divides 40 by 10; the two magnifications must be multiplied.
- ×50: This adds 10 and 40; the two magnifications must be multiplied.
- ×4000: This multiplies by 10 once too often: 10 × 40 = 400.

### 44. `bio-pb-02`

A student's drawing of a leaf is 120 mm long. The leaf itself is 40 mm long. What is the magnification of the drawing?

- **A.** ×0.33
- **B.** ×3  ✅
- **C.** ×80
- **D.** ×4800

Magnification = length of drawing ÷ actual length = 120 ÷ 40 = ×3. Both lengths must be in the same units.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- ×0.33: This divides the actual length by the drawing length, the wrong way round.
- ×80: This subtracts the lengths (120 − 40); magnification is a ratio, found by dividing.
- ×4800: This multiplies the lengths (120 × 40); they must be divided.

### 45. `bio-pb-03`

After changing to the high-power objective of a microscope, a student should focus the specimen using

- **A.** only the fine focus knob  ✅
- **B.** only the coarse focus knob
- **C.** the mirror
- **D.** the stage clips

Focus first at low power with the coarse knob, looking from the side while lowering the objective; then at high power use only the fine knob.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- only the coarse focus knob: The coarse knob moves the lens a long way, so at high power it can crash the objective into the slide.
- the mirror: The mirror controls the light reaching the specimen, not the focus.
- the stage clips: The clips hold the slide in place; they do not focus.

### 46. `bio-pb-04`

A food sample turns violet when sodium hydroxide solution and a few drops of copper(II) sulfate solution are added. The food contains

- **A.** starch
- **B.** protein  ✅
- **C.** reducing sugar
- **D.** fat

This is the Biuret test. The violet (purple) colour shows protein; a blue colour that does not change shows no protein.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- starch: Starch is shown by iodine solution turning blue-black.
- reducing sugar: Reducing sugar gives a brick-red precipitate when heated with Benedict's solution.
- fat: Fat leaves a translucent spot on paper, or gives a cloudy white emulsion with ethanol and water.

### 47. `bio-pb-05`

Sucrose gives a brick-red precipitate with Benedict's solution only after it has first been

- **A.** mixed with iodine solution
- **B.** shaken with ethanol
- **C.** boiled with dilute hydrochloric acid and then neutralised  ✅
- **D.** cooled in ice

Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar. Boiling with acid breaks it into glucose and fructose, which are reducing sugars, so the Benedict's test then works.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- mixed with iodine solution: Iodine solution tests for starch; it does not change sucrose.
- shaken with ethanol: Shaking with ethanol is the emulsion test for fat.
- cooled in ice: Cooling does not change sucrose; Benedict's test needs heat, not cold.

### 49. `bio-pb-07`

Which apparatus is used to collect small animals, such as mites and springtails, from soil and leaf litter?

- **A.** Tullgren funnel  ✅
- **B.** Pitfall trap
- **C.** Sweep net
- **D.** Baermann funnel

In a Tullgren funnel, a lamp above the soil heats and dries it, so the animals move down, away from the heat, and fall through a sieve into a collecting tube.

- Baermann funnel: A Baermann funnel collects animals that live in soil water, such as nematodes.

### 50. `bio-pb-08`

In an experiment on the effect of temperature on the enzyme amylase, the control is a tube of starch with

- **A.** amylase kept at body temperature
- **B.** boiled (denatured) amylase  ✅
- **C.** amylase and iodine solution kept in the dark
- **D.** twice as much amylase

The control is identical except that the enzyme cannot work. If starch disappears only where active amylase is present, the enzyme must be the cause.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- amylase kept at body temperature: A tube at body temperature (about 37 °C) is one of the test tubes in the experiment, not the control.
- amylase and iodine solution kept in the dark: Light is not a factor in this experiment, so darkness is irrelevant.
- twice as much amylase: Changing the amount of enzyme adds a new variable, which makes the test unfair.
