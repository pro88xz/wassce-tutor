## Characteristics of Living Things

- **A.** Irritability (sensitivity)  ✅
- **B.** Growth
- **C.** Reproduction
- **D.** Excretion

Bending towards light is a response to a stimulus (light), so it shows irritability, also called sensitivity.

**Why the other options are wrong**

## Transport in Plants

_Syllabus: Xylem and phloem; absorption of water by root hairs; transpiration and the factors affecting it; translocation and the ringing experiment_

### 26. `bio-tp-01`

Water and mineral salts are carried up the stem of a plant in the

- **A.** phloem
- **B.** xylem  ✅
- **C.** cambium
- **D.** cortex

Xylem vessels are long, dead, hollow tubes that carry water and mineral salts from the roots to the leaves.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- phloem: Phloem carries food made in the leaves.
- cambium: Cambium is a layer of dividing cells that makes the stem thicker.
- cortex: The cortex is a packing and storage tissue.

---

### 27. `bio-tp-02`

The loss of water vapour from the leaves of a plant is called

- **A.** translocation
- **B.** guttation
- **C.** transpiration  ✅
- **D.** osmosis

Transpiration is the evaporation of water from the leaf cells and its loss as vapour through the stomata.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- translocation: Translocation is the movement of food in the phloem.
- guttation: Guttation is the loss of liquid water droplets, not vapour.
- osmosis: Osmosis is the movement of water from cell to cell across a membrane.

---

### 28. `bio-tp-03`

Which conditions would make a plant transpire fastest?

- **A.** Cold, humid and still
- **B.** Warm but very humid
- **C.** Dark, with the stomata closed
- **D.** Hot, dry and windy  ✅

Heat speeds evaporation, dry air keeps a steep gradient, and wind carries away the moist air around the leaf.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- Cold, humid and still: Cold, moist, still air slows evaporation.
- Warm but very humid: Humid air reduces the difference in water vapour between leaf and air.
- Dark, with the stomata closed: With the stomata closed, very little water vapour escapes.

**Why the other options are wrong**

## Mendelian Genetics

_Syllabus: Genes and alleles; dominant and recessive; genotype and phenotype; monohybrid crosses; sex determination; inheritance of blood groups_

### 36. `bio-mg-01`

Two tall pea plants, both heterozygous (Tt), are crossed. What ratio of tall to short plants is expected among the offspring?

- **A.** 1 tall : 1 short
- **B.** 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt
- **C.** All tall
- **D.** 3 tall : 1 short  ✅

Tt × Tt gives TT, Tt, Tt and tt. Three have the dominant T, so 3 tall : 1 short.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 1 tall : 1 short: A 1 : 1 ratio comes from crossing Tt with tt.
- 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt: That is the ratio of genotypes, not of tall to short plants.
- All tall: All tall offspring would come from crossing TT with any plant.

### 40. `bio-mg-05`

A man of blood group AB and a woman of blood group O have a child. Which blood groups could the child have?

- **A.** AB or O
- **B.** O only
- **C.** A, B, AB or O
- **D.** A or B only  ✅

The father gives A or B; the mother gives O. The child is AO (group A) or BO (group B).

**Why the other options are wrong**

- AB or O: AB needs an A and a B allele, but the mother can give only O; O needs O from both parents, but the father gives A or B.
- O only: The father always passes on A or B, so the child cannot be group O.
- A, B, AB or O: All four groups are possible only when the parents are, for example, AO and BO.
