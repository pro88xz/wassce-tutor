### 1. `bio-ib-01`

A farmer notices that maize growing next to a compost heap is taller than maize elsewhere on the farm, and suggests that compost makes maize grow taller. In the scientific method, the farmer's suggestion is

- **A.** a hypothesis  ✅
- **B.** an observation
- **C.** a conclusion
- **D.** a theory

The steps run: observation (taller maize near compost), hypothesis (a testable explanation: compost makes maize taller), experiment (grow maize with and without compost, all else the same), results, and conclusion.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- an observation: The observation is what the farmer noticed: taller maize near the compost. The suggestion that explains it comes next.
- a conclusion: A conclusion is drawn only after the hypothesis has been tested by an experiment and the results analysed.
- a theory: A theory is an explanation that has been supported by many tested experiments; this idea has not yet been tested at all.

### 2. `bio-ib-02`

Why is a control set up alongside a biological experiment?

- **A.** To make sure that the hypothesis is proved correct
- **B.** To give a spare set-up in case the first one is damaged
- **C.** To show that any change observed is caused by the factor being tested  ✅
- **D.** To make the experiment finish more quickly

A control is identical to the experiment except for the one factor being investigated. If only the experiment changes, the change can be put down to that factor and not to something else, such as temperature or time.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- To make sure that the hypothesis is proved correct: An experiment tests a hypothesis; the results may support it or show that it is wrong.
- To give a spare set-up in case the first one is damaged: A control is not a spare: it is set up differently on purpose, without the factor being tested.
- To make the experiment finish more quickly: A control does not change how long the experiment takes.

### 22. `bio-cd-01`

The body cells of a maize plant have 20 chromosomes. How many chromosomes are there in a male gamete in a pollen grain, and in a root-tip cell made by mitosis?

- **A.** 20 in the gamete and 10 in the root-tip cell
- **B.** 10 in the gamete and 10 in the root-tip cell
- **C.** 20 in the gamete and 20 in the root-tip cell
- **D.** 10 in the gamete and 20 in the root-tip cell  ✅

Gametes come from meiosis, so they are haploid: 20 ÷ 2 = 10. Root-tip cells come from mitosis, so they are diploid like the parent: 20. At fertilisation 10 + 10 restores 20.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 20 in the gamete and 10 in the root-tip cell: This is the wrong way round: meiosis halves the number in gametes, while mitosis keeps it the same.
- 10 in the gamete and 10 in the root-tip cell: Mitosis gives daughter cells with the same number of chromosomes as the parent cell: 20.
- 20 in the gamete and 20 in the root-tip cell: Gametes are made by meiosis, which halves the chromosome number to 10.

### 36. `bio-tl-01`

In an *Amoeba* living in fresh water, the contractile vacuole

- digests the food particles that the cell takes in: Food is digested in food vacuoles.
- takes in oxygen for respiration: Oxygen diffuses in across the whole cell membrane.
- stores reserve food for the cell: The contractile vacuole holds water, not food.

### 37. `bio-tl-02`

*Amoeba* needs no blood or blood vessels to supply its cell with oxygen because

- **A.** it does not respire
- **B.** its large surface area to volume ratio allows diffusion to reach every part  ✅
- **C.** it lives in water, so it needs no oxygen

### 38. `bio-tl-03`

What is the surface area to volume ratio of a cube with sides of 2 cm?

- **A.** 1 : 3
- **B.** 3 : 1  ✅
- **C.** 6 : 1
- **D.** 24 : 1

Surface area = 6 × 2 × 2 = 24 cm². Volume = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 cm³. Ratio = 24 : 8 = 3 : 1. The bigger the organism, the smaller this ratio, which is why large organisms need transport systems.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 1 : 3: This is volume to surface area, the ratio the wrong way round.
- 6 : 1: 6 : 1 is the ratio for a cube with 1 cm sides.
- 24 : 1: 24 cm² is the surface area alone; it must be compared with the volume, 8 cm³.

### 39. `bio-tl-04`

A strip of onion epidermis is left in a concentrated sugar solution for 30 minutes and then viewed under a microscope. The cells will appear

- **A.** turgid, with the cytoplasm pressed against the cell wall
- **B.** burst, because water has rushed into them
- **C.** plasmolysed, with the cytoplasm shrunk away from the cell wall  ✅
- **D.** unchanged, because the cell wall stops water leaving

The sugar solution has a lower water potential than the cell sap, so water leaves by osmosis. The vacuole and cytoplasm shrink and the membrane pulls away from the wall.

### 40. `bio-tl-05`

Red blood cells are placed in distilled water. What happens to them?

- **A.** They shrink, because water leaves by osmosis
- **B.** They become turgid but do not burst
- **C.** They do not change, because water cannot cross their membrane
- **D.** They swell and burst, because water enters by osmosis  ✅

Distilled water has a higher water potential than the cytoplasm, so water moves in. With no cell wall to resist, the cells swell and burst (haemolysis).

**Why the other options are wrong**

- They shrink, because water leaves by osmosis: Cells shrink in a solution more concentrated than their cytoplasm, not in pure water.
- They become turgid but do not burst: Only plant cells become turgid; their cell wall stops them bursting. Red cells have no wall.
- They do not change, because water cannot cross their membrane: The cell membrane lets water through easily.

### 41. `bio-tl-06`

A single-celled alga takes in potassium ions from pond water, although the concentration of potassium inside the cell is already higher than in the water. This uptake is by

- **A.** active transport, which needs energy from respiration  ✅
- **B.** diffusion, which needs energy from respiration
- **C.** diffusion, which needs no energy
- **D.** osmosis, which needs no energy

Moving ions from a low concentration to a high one, against the gradient, is active transport. It uses carrier proteins and energy (ATP) from respiration.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- diffusion, which needs energy from respiration: Diffusion is passive: it needs no energy from the cell.
- diffusion, which needs no energy: Diffusion moves particles down a gradient, so it would carry potassium out of the cell, not in.
- osmosis, which needs no energy: Osmosis is the movement of water only, not of ions.

### 42. `bio-tl-07`

Which change would make oxygen diffuse into a cell faster?

- **A.** A thicker cell membrane
- **B.** A steeper concentration gradient across the cell membrane  ✅
- **C.** A lower temperature
- **D.** A smaller surface area for exchange

Diffusion is faster with a steeper concentration gradient, a larger surface area, a shorter distance and a higher temperature.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A thicker cell membrane: A thicker membrane makes the diffusion distance longer, which slows diffusion.
- A lower temperature: At a lower temperature particles move more slowly, so diffusion slows.
- A smaller surface area for exchange: Less surface means fewer particles can cross at once.
