- forms an airtight layer over the scratch: The scratch exposes the iron; protection is sacrificial.
- reacts with rust to form iron: Zinc does not reverse rusting.

## Energy Changes in Reactions

_Syllabus: Exothermic and endothermic reactions; ΔH; heat of reaction from temperature change; bond energies_

### 41. `chem-en-01`

In an exothermic reaction

- **A.** heat is taken in and ΔH is negative
- **B.** heat is given out and ΔH is negative  ✅
- **C.** heat is given out and ΔH is positive
- **D.** heat is taken in and ΔH is positive

The products have less energy than the reactants; the difference is released to the surroundings, which get warmer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- heat is taken in and ΔH is negative: Heat taken in means ΔH is positive (endothermic).
- heat is given out and ΔH is positive: When heat is given out, the products have less energy, so ΔH is negative.
- heat is taken in and ΔH is positive: That describes an endothermic reaction.

- **B.** 210 J
- **C.** 300 J
- **D.** 1260 J  ✅

$Q = mc\Delta T = 50 \times 4.2 \times 6 = 1260$ J

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 300 J: You left out the specific heat capacity.

### 43. `chem-en-03`

50 cm³ of 1.0 mol/dm³ HCl is mixed with 50 cm³ of 1.0 mol/dm³ NaOH, and the temperature rises by 6.8 °C. What is the enthalpy of neutralisation? (Mass of solution = 100 g; c = 4.2 J/g/°C)

- **A.** −57.12 kJ/mol  ✅
- **B.** −28.56 kJ/mol
- **C.** −2.856 kJ/mol
- **D.** 57.12 kJ/mol

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 57.12 kJ/mol: Neutralisation gives out heat, so ΔH is negative.

### 44. `chem-en-04`

Use the bond energies H–H = 436, Cl–Cl = 242 and H–Cl = 431 kJ/mol to find ΔH for H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl.

- **A.** −1540 kJ/mol
- **B.** −184 kJ/mol  ✅
- **C.** 184 kJ/mol
- **D.** 247 kJ/mol

## Chemical Equilibrium

### 51. `chem-eqm-01`

A reversible reaction is in dynamic equilibrium when

- **A.** both reactions have stopped
- **B.** the concentrations of reactants and products are equal
- **C.** all the reactants have been used up
- **D.** the forward and backward reactions continue at equal rates  ✅

Because the rates are equal, the concentrations stop changing even though particles keep reacting both ways.

**Why the other options are wrong**

### 55. `chem-eqm-05`

For H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), the equilibrium concentrations are [H₂] = 0.1, [I₂] = 0.1 and [HI] = 0.8 mol/dm³. What is Kc?

- **B.** 8
- **C.** 64  ✅
- **D.** 80

$K_c = \dfrac{[\text{HI}]^2}{[\text{H}_2][\text{I}_2]} = \dfrac{0.8^2}{0.1 \times 0.1} = \dfrac{0.64}{0.01} = 64$

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 8: You divided by only one of the reactant concentrations and did not square [HI].
- 0.015625: You put the reactants over the products.
