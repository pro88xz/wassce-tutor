### 46. `chem-eq-01`

Which equation for the burning of aluminium is correctly balanced?

- **A.** 2Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃
- **B.** 4Al + 6O₂ → 2Al₂O₃
- **C.** 2Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃
- **D.** 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃  ✅

Left: 4 Al and 6 O. Right: 2 × Al₂O₃ = 4 Al and 6 O. Both elements balance.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 2Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃: Oxygen is not balanced: 2 atoms on the left, 3 on the right.
- 4Al + 6O₂ → 2Al₂O₃: Oxygen is not balanced: 12 atoms on the left, 6 on the right.
- 2Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃: Aluminium is not balanced: 2 atoms on the left, 4 on the right.

### 50. `chem-eq-05`

4 g of hydrogen is exploded with 16 g of oxygen: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. What mass of water forms?

- **A.** 9 g
- **B.** 18 g  ✅
- **C.** 20 g
- **D.** 36 g

H₂: $\dfrac{4}{2} = 2$ mol; O₂: $\dfrac{16}{32} = 0.5$ mol. O₂ needs only 1 mol H₂, so O₂ is limiting. Water $= 2 \times 0.5 = 1$ mol $= 18$ g

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 36 g: You assumed hydrogen is used up; oxygen (0.5 mol) is the limiting reagent.
- 20 g: You added the masses of the reactants; excess hydrogen is left over.
- 9 g: You used 0.5 mol of water; 0.5 mol O₂ gives 1 mol H₂O.

## Empirical and Molecular Formulas

_Syllabus: Empirical and molecular formulas; percentage composition; water of crystallisation_

### 51. `chem-ef-01`

A compound contains 40.0% carbon, 6.7% hydrogen and 53.3% oxygen by mass. What is its empirical formula?

- **A.** CH₂O  ✅
- **B.** C₆H₁₂O₆
- **C.** CHO
- **D.** C₂H₄O

C: 40.0 ÷ 12 = 3.33; H: 6.7 ÷ 1 = 6.7; O: 53.3 ÷ 16 = 3.33. Dividing by 3.33 gives 1 : 2 : 1, so CH₂O.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- C₆H₁₂O₆: That is a molecular formula (glucose); the empirical formula is the simplest ratio.
- CHO: The moles of hydrogen (6.7) are twice those of carbon (3.33).
- C₂H₄O: The moles of carbon and oxygen are equal (3.33 each), not 2 : 1.

### 52. `chem-ef-02`

A hydrocarbon has the empirical formula CH₂ and a relative molecular mass of 56. What is its molecular formula?

- **A.** C₂H₄
- **B.** C₄H₈  ✅
- **C.** C₄H₁₀
- **D.** C₅H₁₀

Mᵣ of CH₂ = 14; n = 56 ÷ 14 = 4, so the molecular formula is (CH₂)₄ = C₄H₈.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- C₂H₄: C₂H₄ has Mᵣ 28; you need (CH₂)ₙ with 14n = 56.
- C₄H₁₀: C₄H₁₀ has Mᵣ 58 and is not a multiple of CH₂.
- C₅H₁₀: C₅H₁₀ has Mᵣ 70.

### 54. `chem-ef-04`

2.86 g of hydrated sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃·xH₂O, gives 1.06 g of anhydrous Na₂CO₃ on heating. Find x. (Na₂CO₃ = 106, H₂O = 18)

- **A.** 0.1
- **B.** 1.8
- **C.** 5
- **D.** 10  ✅

- 5: You used 36 as the molar mass of water.
