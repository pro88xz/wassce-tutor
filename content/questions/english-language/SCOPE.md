# English Language: what the practice sets cover, and the rules for writing them

## The objective paper

Every source we found agrees that the WASSCE English Language objective paper
tests **lexis** (vocabulary: words nearest or opposite in meaning, idioms,
words in context) and **structure** (grammar: completing a sentence with the
correct form), with four options **A–D**. The sources do not agree on the
number of items or the time allowed, and WAEC's own e-learning toolkit for
English is no longer online, so those numbers are not stated here.

The essay, comprehension and summary questions (written answers) and the oral
English paper (sounds, stress, intonation) need different practice formats and
are not covered by these objective sets yet.

## Set 1: structure (grammar), 50 questions

Ten topics from *Grammar: word classes* and *Sentence structure*, five items
each, matching the lessons already on the site: Nouns, Pronouns, Verbs and Verb
Forms, Tenses, Active and Passive Voice, Non-finite Verb Forms, Adjectives,
Prepositions, Conjunctions, Subject-Verb Agreement.

## Set 2: structure and lexis, 50 questions

Structure: Parts of Speech, Adverbs, Articles, Question Tags, Modal Verbs,
Conditionals, Direct and Reported Speech. Lexis: Synonyms and Antonyms (words
nearest and opposite in meaning), Idioms and Idiomatic Expressions, Common
Confusable Words. WAEC underlines the word being tested; the app shows it in
**bold**. With sets 1 and 2, every English topic that has lessons has a set.

Next: Phrases and Clauses, Sentence Types, Punctuation, Homonyms and
Homophones, Figures of Speech, Word Formation, Vocabulary in Context; then
oral English (vowels, consonants, stress) once its lessons exist.

## House rules

1. **Original only.** Never copy or reword a WAEC question. Testing the same
   grammar point is fine; the sentence, names and context must be ours.
2. **Standard British English** is the reference, as on WAEC papers (*good at*,
   *married to*, *the taller of the two*).
3. **Exactly one acceptable option.** If a careful teacher could defend a
   second option in modern usage, rewrite the item. Avoid points where usage is
   genuinely divided (for example *It was he / It was him*, singular *their*,
   *between* for three or more).
4. **Every wrong option is a real, common error**, and its explanation names the
   rule it breaks.
5. **Sierra Leone context** where it fits naturally: names, towns, poda-podas,
   the bus park, Connaught Hospital, school life.
6. Answers cannot be recomputed by code as in Mathematics, so an editor checks
   each one, and the review sheet (`*.review.md`) says so. A teacher should read
   a set before it is used in class.

## Sources consulted for the format

Secondary summaries of the WAEC English Language syllabus (studentship.com.ng,
syllabus.ng, cegastacademy.com, waecdirect.blogspot.com). They agree on the
lexis/structure split and the A–D format, and disagree on item counts.
