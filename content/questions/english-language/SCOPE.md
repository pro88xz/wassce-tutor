# English Language: what the practice sets cover, and the rules for writing them

## The objective paper

Every source we found agrees that the WASSCE English Language objective paper
tests **lexis** (vocabulary: words nearest or opposite in meaning, idioms,
words in context) and **structure** (grammar: completing a sentence with the
correct form), with four options **A–D**. The sources do not agree on the
number of items or the time allowed, and WAEC's own e-learning toolkit for
English is no longer online, so those numbers are not stated here.

The essay, comprehension and summary questions are written answers on Paper 2,
and oral English is its own paper. Sets 5 and 6 practise them in objective form:
the test-of-orals formats for sounds, stress and intonation, and the conventions
the written tasks are marked on (format, register, organisation, technique).

## Set 1: structure (grammar), 50 questions

Ten topics from *Grammar: word classes* and *Sentence structure*, five items
each, matching the lessons already on the site: Nouns, Pronouns, Verbs and Verb
Forms, Tenses, Active and Passive Voice, Non-finite Verb Forms, Adjectives,
Prepositions, Conjunctions, Subject-Verb Agreement.

## Set 2: structure and lexis, 50 questions

Structure: Parts of Speech, Adverbs, Articles, Question Tags, Modal Verbs,
Conditionals, Direct and Reported Speech. Lexis: Synonyms and Antonyms (words
nearest and opposite in meaning), Idioms and Idiomatic Expressions, Common
Confusable Words. WAEC underlines the word being tested; the app shows it in
**bold**. With sets 1 and 2, every English topic that has lessons has a set.

## Set 3: sentences, punctuation and vocabulary, 35 questions

Phrases and Clauses, Sentence Types, Punctuation, Homonyms and Homophones,
Figures of Speech, Word Formation, Vocabulary in Context (five each). With
sets 1 to 3, every grammar and vocabulary topic in the syllabus list has a
practice set: 135 questions in 27 topics.

## Set 4: comprehension and summary, 18 questions on 3 passages

Three original passages (a village rebuilding its footbridge; a grandfather's
letter about reading; advice on revision), six questions each:

- *Answering Comprehension Questions*: facts, inferences, meaning in context
  and the grammatical name of an expression.
- *Tone and Attitude in Comprehension*: tone, attitude, purpose and mood.
- *Summary Writing*: main points, the main idea, and what to leave out.

On WASSCE Paper 2 these are written answers. The objective versions here
practise the same reading skills; every answer is backed by a sentence in its
passage. A set with a passage has `paper_type` *Comprehension*, and the
practice screen shows the passage above the questions.

## Set 5: oral English, 40 questions

Eight items each on Vowels and Diphthongs, Consonants and Consonant Clusters,
Word Stress, Sentence Stress and Intonation, and Listening Comprehension Skills,
in the test-of-orals formats (same sound, rhyme, odd one out, stress pattern,
emphatic stress, tunes). RP symbols as in the lessons; letters in **bold** mark
the sound tested and CAPITALS mark stress. Listening items carry a short
transcript in the question.

**Audio.** Vowel, consonant and word-stress items end their explanation with a
**Listen** row: every word in the question, spoken in standard British English
with its RP transcription. Listening items have a play button above the
transcript (a man's and a woman's voice in the dialogues). Sentence stress and
intonation items have none yet: a synthetic voice cannot be relied on to put
emphatic stress or a tune exactly where the question needs it. Clips are made
and checked by `scripts/audio/oral_audio.py` (see its README); a clip that fails
its checks is not linked, and `AUDIO.md` records the result for every clip.

## Set 6: reading approach and writing tasks, 84 questions

Seven items each on Approaching a Comprehension Passage, Essay Writing
Fundamentals, Formal and Informal Letter Writing, Speech Writing, Narrative,
Descriptive, Argumentative and Expository Essays, Report Writing, Article
Writing and Creative Writing. Wrong options are the mistakes examiners report.

## Top-up

Sets 1 to 4 have two more items per topic (one per passage in set 4), added at
the end of each file so earlier answer letters never move. Every English topic
now has at least seven questions: 334 in all.

Next: more passages (narrative, argumentative, a report), and audio in the
oral English lessons.

## House rules

1. **Original only.** Never copy or reword a WAEC question. Testing the same
   grammar point is fine; the sentence, names and context must be ours.
2. **Standard British English** is the reference, as on WAEC papers (*good at*,
   *married to*, *the taller of the two*).
3. **Exactly one acceptable option.** If a careful teacher could defend a
   second option in modern usage, rewrite the item. Avoid points where usage is
   genuinely divided (for example *It was he / It was him*, singular *their*,
   *between* for three or more).
4. **Every wrong option is a real, common error**, and its explanation names the
   rule it breaks.
5. **Sierra Leone context** where it fits naturally: names, towns, poda-podas,
   the bus park, Connaught Hospital, school life.
6. Answers cannot be recomputed by code as in Mathematics, so an editor checks
   each one, and the review sheet (`*.review.md`) says so. A teacher should read
   a set before it is used in class.

## Sources consulted for the format

Secondary summaries of the WAEC English Language syllabus (studentship.com.ng,
syllabus.ng, cegastacademy.com, waecdirect.blogspot.com). They agree on the
lexis/structure split and the A–D format, and disagree on item counts.
