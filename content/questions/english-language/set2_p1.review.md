## Parts of Speech

_Syllabus: Structure: identifying word classes by their job in the sentence_

### 1. `eng-pos-01`

What part of speech is the word in bold?

Abu ran **fast** to catch the last poda-poda.

- **A.** adverb  ✅
- **B.** adjective
- **C.** verb
- **D.** noun

A word's part of speech depends on its job in the sentence. *Fast* tells us **how** Abu ran, so it modifies the verb: an adverb.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- adjective: *Fast* is an adjective when it describes a noun (*a fast car*). Here it tells us how Abu ran.
- verb: *To fast* (go without food) is a verb, but here *ran* is the verb.
- noun: *A fast* (a period without food) is a noun, but here the word describes the action.

## Articles

### 11. `eng-art-01`

- **B.** (no article)
- **C.** an  ✅
- **D.** much

The choice between *a* and *an* depends on the first **sound**, not the letter: *an honest girl*, *an hour*, but *a university*.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- a: *Honest* begins with a vowel sound because the *h* is silent, so it takes *an*.

- much: *Much* is used with uncountable nouns, not before *honest girl*.

*Must* expresses a strong obligation or rule: *candidates must switch off their phones*.

## Conditionals

### 26. `eng-cond-01`

- **B.** will postpone  ✅

First conditional (a real possibility): *If* + present simple, *will* + base form: *If it rains, the school will postpone…*

- would have postponed: *Would have* belongs to the third conditional, about the past.

### 31. `eng-rep-01`

- Aminata said that she was going to the market now.

## Common Confusable Words

_Syllabus: Lexis: words often confused (affect/effect, than/then, lose/loose)_

### 46. `eng-conf-01`

Choose the option that best completes the sentence.

The late rains will badly ____ this year's rice harvest.

- **A.** effect
- **B.** affect  ✅
- **C.** infect
- **D.** afect

*Affect* is the verb (*the rain will affect the harvest*); *effect* is the noun (*the rain had a bad effect*).

**Why the other options are wrong**

- effect: *Effect* is usually a noun (*the effect of the rain*). The verb meaning *to influence* is *affect*.
- infect: *Infect* means to pass on a disease.
- afect: The verb is spelt with two f's: *affect*.

### 47. `eng-conf-02`

Choose the option that best completes the sentence.

Our school compound is much bigger ____ theirs.

- **A.** then
- **B.** that
- **C.** than  ✅
- **D.** as

Use *than* after a comparative (*bigger than*); use *then* for time or sequence.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- then: *Then* refers to time (*we ate, then we left*). Comparisons use *than*.
- that: *That* does not introduce a comparison after *bigger*.
- as: *As* is used in *as big as*, not after the comparative *bigger*.

### 48. `eng-conf-03`

Choose the option that best completes the sentence.

The ____ asked the pupils to sit quietly before the visitors arrived.

- **A.** principle
- **B.** principel
- **C.** principle's
- **D.** principal  ✅

*Principal* is the head of a school (and also means *main*); a *principle* is a rule or belief.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- principle: A *principle* is a rule or belief, not a person.
- principel: This is a misspelling; the word is *principal*.
- principle's: *Principle* is the wrong word, and the apostrophe makes it possessive.

### 49. `eng-conf-04`

Choose the option that best completes the sentence.

Do not ____ your admission card before the examination.

- **A.** lose  ✅
- **B.** loose
- **C.** loss
- **D.** lost

*Lose* (verb, one o) means to stop having something; *loose* (adjective, two o's) means not tight.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- loose: *Loose* is an adjective meaning *not tight*, as in *a loose button*.
- loss: *Loss* is a noun. After *do not* we need a verb.
- lost: After *do not* we need the base form of the verb, not the past.

Choose the option that best completes the sentence.

The bookshop near the school sells pens, rulers, exercise books and other ____.
