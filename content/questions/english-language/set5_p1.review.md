## Vowels and Diphthongs

_Syllabus: Oral English: the 12 pure vowels and 8 diphthongs; same-sound, rhyme and odd-one-out questions; vowel pairs often confused in West African English_

### 1. `eng-vow-01`

Choose the word that has the same vowel sound as the one represented by the letters in bold.

f**oo**d

- **A.** shoe  ✅
- **B.** good
- **C.** blood
- **D.** boat

*Food* has the long vowel /uː/, the same sound as in *shoe*, *blue* and *true*. The spelling *oo* can stand for /uː/, /ʊ/ or /ʌ/, so listen to the sound, not the letters.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- good: *Good* has the short vowel /ʊ/, as in *put*, although it is spelt with *oo* like *food*.
- blood: *Blood* has /ʌ/, as in *cut*: another *oo* word that does not sound like *food*.
- boat: *Boat* has the diphthong /əʊ/, as in *go*.

### 4. `eng-vow-04`

- **B.** her
- **C.** hat
- **D.** heart

*Hair* has the diphthong /eə/: the tongue glides from /e/ towards /ə/. In a pure vowel the tongue stays in one position, as in *her*, *hat* and *heart*.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- her: *Her* has the pure long vowel /ɜː/.
- hat: *Hat* has the pure short vowel /æ/.
- heart: *Heart* has the pure long vowel /ɑː/.

## Consonants and Consonant Clusters

_Syllabus: Oral English: the 24 consonants; voiced and voiceless sounds; /θ/, /ð/, /ʃ/, /tʃ/ and /ʒ/; spellings with more than one sound; the -ed and -s endings; consonant clusters; silent letters_

### 9. `eng-con-01`

Which of the following words begins with the sound /θ/?

- **A.** these
- **B.** thick  ✅
- **C.** Thomas
- **D.** though

/θ/ is the voiceless *th* of *thin*, *think* and *three*. Its voiced partner /ð/ begins *this*, *these* and *though*. Saying /θ/ as /t/ (*tick* for *thick*) is a common substitution.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- these: *These* begins with the voiced sound /ð/, as in *this*.
- Thomas: In *Thomas* the *th* is pronounced /t/.
- though: *Though* begins with the voiced sound /ð/.

---

### 10. `eng-con-02`

Which of the following words contains the sound /ʒ/?

- **A.** mission
- **B.** nation
- **C.** television  ✅
- **D.** pressure

/ʒ/ is the voiced partner of /ʃ/, heard in *measure*, *pleasure*, *vision* and *television* (/ˈtelɪvɪʒn/). It is rare in English and often replaced by /ʃ/ or /dʒ/.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- mission: *Mission* contains /ʃ/: /ˈmɪʃn/.
- nation: *Nation* contains /ʃ/: /ˈneɪʃn/.
- pressure: *Pressure* contains /ʃ/: /ˈpreʃə/.

---

### 11. `eng-con-03`

In which of the following words is the ending *-ed* pronounced /ɪd/?

- **A.** hoped
- **B.** begged
- **C.** fixed
- **D.** landed  ✅

*-ed* adds a syllable, /ɪd/, only after /t/ or /d/: *land* + *-ed* = /ˈlændɪd/. After other voiceless sounds it is /t/; after voiced sounds it is /d/.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- hoped: *Hope* ends in the voiceless /p/, so *-ed* is /t/: /həʊpt/.
- begged: *Beg* ends in the voiced /g/, so *-ed* is /d/: /beɡd/.
- fixed: *Fix* ends in /s/ (the *x* is /ks/), a voiceless sound, so *-ed* is /t/.

### 12. `eng-con-04`

In which of the following words is the final *-s* or *-es* pronounced /ɪz/?

- **A.** judges  ✅
- **B.** beds

- **D.** rooms

The ending adds a syllable, /ɪz/, after the hissing sounds /s/, /z/, /ʃ/, /ʒ/, /tʃ/ and /dʒ/. *Judge* ends in /dʒ/, so *judges* is /ˈdʒʌdʒɪz/.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- beds: *Bed* ends in the voiced /d/, so the ending is /z/.
- caps: *Cap* ends in the voiceless /p/, so the ending is /s/.
- rooms: *Room* ends in the voiced /m/, so the ending is /z/.

## Word Stress

_Syllabus: Oral English: stressed syllables; two-syllable nouns, adjectives and verbs; noun-verb pairs; suffixes that fix the stress; compound words; words often mis-stressed_

### 17. `eng-ws-01`

Which of the following words is stressed on the first syllable?

- **A.** arrive
- **B.** village  ✅
- **C.** complete
- **D.** believe

Two-syllable nouns and adjectives are usually stressed on the first syllable: VILlage, MARket, HAPpy. Many two-syllable verbs stress the second.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- arrive: *Arrive* is a verb stressed on the second syllable: arRIVE.
- complete: *Complete* is stressed on the second syllable: comPLETE.
- believe: *Believe* is stressed on the second syllable: beLIEVE.

### 18. `eng-ws-02`

In which sentence is the word *record* correctly stressed? (The stressed syllable is in capitals.)

- **A.** She will REcord the song tomorrow.
- **B.** He broke the world reCORD.
- **C.** She will reCORD the song tomorrow.  ✅
- **D.** The teacher keeps a reCORD of attendance.

Many noun–verb pairs are spelt alike but stressed differently: the noun on the first syllable (a REcord, a PREsent), the verb on the second (to reCORD, to preSENT).

**Why the other options are wrong**

- She will REcord the song tomorrow.  
  Here *record* is a verb, so the stress falls on the second syllable.
- He broke the world reCORD.  
  Here *record* is a noun, so the stress falls on the first syllable: REcord.
- The teacher keeps a reCORD of attendance.  
  Here *record* is a noun, so it should be REcord.

### 19. `eng-ws-03`

Which of the following shows the correct stress for *examination*?

- **A.** exAMination
- **B.** EXamination
- **C.** examinaTION
- **D.** examiNAtion  ✅

Words ending in *-tion* and *-sion* are stressed on the syllable just before the ending: examiNAtion, eduCAtion, deCIsion.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- exAMination: The stress is not on the second syllable; *-tion* moves it to the syllable just before.
- EXamination: The first syllable is not stressed.
- examinaTION: The ending *-tion* is never stressed; it is the weak syllable /ʃn/.

## Sentence Stress and Intonation

_Syllabus: Oral English: content and function words; emphatic (contrastive) stress; falling and rising tunes; question tags; lists and echo questions_

### 25. `eng-ss-01`

In the sentence below, the word in capitals carries the emphatic stress.

*My brother SOLD the car.*

Which question does the sentence answer?

- **A.** Did your sister sell the car?
- **B.** Did your brother buy the car?  ✅
- **C.** Did your brother sell the bicycle?
- **D.** Did your brother sell the car yesterday?

Emphatic stress falls on the word that corrects the question. SOLD corrects the action: not *buy*, but *sold*.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- Did your sister sell the car?  
  This question would be answered by stressing BROTHER, to correct *sister*.
- Did your brother sell the bicycle?  
  This question would be answered by stressing CAR, to correct *bicycle*.
- Did your brother sell the car yesterday?  
  Nothing in this question is corrected by the stress on SOLD.

---

### 26. `eng-ss-02`

A pupil is asked: *Did Musa travel to Bo on Monday?* The pupil answers: *No, Musa travelled to Bo on Tuesday.* Which word in the answer should carry the emphatic stress?

- **A.** Musa
- **B.** travelled
- **C.** Tuesday  ✅
- **D.** Bo

Find the detail that changes between question and answer: *Monday* becomes *Tuesday*, so TUESDAY is stressed.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- Musa: Musa is not being corrected: the question already names him.
- travelled: The action is not being corrected: he did travel.
- Bo: The place is not being corrected: he did go to Bo.

### 27. `eng-ss-03`

Which of the following would normally be spoken with a rising tune at the end?

- **A.** Where are you going?
- **B.** Close the door.
- **C.** What a lovely dress!
- **D.** Are you coming to the match?  ✅

Yes/no questions rise at the end. Statements, commands, wh- questions and exclamations fall.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- Where are you going?

- Close the door.  
  Commands have a falling tune.
- What a lovely dress!  
  Exclamations have a falling tune.

### 28. `eng-ss-04`

A speaker says: *It's very hot today, isn't it?* with a falling tune on the tag. The speaker

- **A.** is sure and expects the listener to agree  ✅
- **B.** is not sure and is really asking for information
- **C.** is angry with the listener
- **D.** has not finished the sentence

Falling tag: the speaker is sure and invites agreement. Rising tag: the speaker is unsure and is asking.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- is not sure and is really asking for information: A real question is shown by a rising tune on the tag.
- is angry with the listener: The tune of the tag shows certainty, not anger.
- has not finished the sentence: A falling tune sounds complete; an unfinished statement rises.

### 29. `eng-ss-05`

In normal speech, which words carry the stress in this sentence?

*The boys are playing football in the field.*

- **A.** The, are, in, the
- **B.** boys, playing, football, field  ✅
- **C.** boys, are, in, field
- **D.** Every word is stressed equally

Content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs) carry the stress; function words (articles, auxiliaries, prepositions, pronouns) are weak. This gives English its rhythm.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- The, are, in, the: These are function words, which are normally weak and unstressed.
- boys, are, in, field: *Are* and *in* are function words, normally unstressed.
- Every word is stressed equally: Giving every word equal weight is not the rhythm of standard English.

### 30. `eng-ss-06`

How is the list in this sentence normally spoken?

*I bought rice, oil, onions and pepper.*

- **A.** Falling on every item
- **B.** Rising on every item, including *pepper*
- **C.** Rising on *rice*, *oil* and *onions*, and falling on *pepper*  ✅
- **D.** Rising only on *pepper*

Each item but the last rises, showing more is coming; the last falls, showing the list is complete.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- Falling on every item: Falling on each item would make each one sound like the end of the sentence.
- Rising on every item, including *pepper*: A rise on the last item would make the list sound unfinished.
- Rising only on *pepper*: The last item falls, to show the list is complete.

### 31. `eng-ss-07`

The word in capitals carries the emphatic stress.

*I didn't say SHE took the money.*

What does the speaker mean?

- **A.** Someone else, not the speaker, said it
- **B.** The speaker suggested it but did not actually say it
- **C.** She borrowed the money; she did not take it
- **D.** Someone else, not she, took the money  ✅

Moving the stress changes the meaning, because the stressed word is the one being contrasted. Stress on SHE contrasts her with another person.

## Listening Comprehension Skills

_Syllabus: Listening: preparing before the passage; main idea, details, inference, attitude and purpose; signal words; combining details; avoiding traps in the options_

### 33. `eng-lis-01`

In the few seconds before a listening passage is read, the most useful thing a candidate can do is to

- **A.** try to write down every word that will be read
- **B.** read the questions and mark the key words to listen for  ✅
- **C.** close the eyes and wait for the passage to begin
- **D.** start answering the questions by guessing

Knowing the questions tells you what to listen for: a time, a name, a reason. Then you can predict and note details briefly.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- try to write down every word that will be read: No one can write down every word; it means missing the meaning.
- close the eyes and wait for the passage to begin: This wastes the only chance to prepare.
- start answering the questions by guessing: Answers should come from the passage, not from guessing in advance.

### 34. `eng-lis-02`

A speaker says:

> Many people think the market opens at eight o'clock. However, it actually opens at nine.

At what time does the market open?

- **A.** Eight o'clock
- **B.** Half past eight
- **C.** Nine o'clock  ✅
- **D.** The speaker does not say

Signal words such as *however*, *actually* and *in fact* warn that a correction is coming, and the correction is usually the answer. Test-setters often put the first-mentioned detail in a wrong option.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- Eight o'clock: Eight is what people *think*; *however* signals the correction.
- Half past eight: No such time is mentioned.
- The speaker does not say: The speaker clearly says the market opens at nine.

### 36. `eng-lis-04`

How much does the man pay altogether?

### 37. `eng-lis-05`

- **B.** Enthusiastic
- **C.** Angry

### 38. `eng-lis-06`

A speaker at a school meeting says:

> Last year, three pupils from this very school were injured crossing the highway. That is why we are asking the council to build a footbridge.

Why does the speaker mention the three injured pupils?

- **A.** To praise the three pupils
- **B.** To show why a footbridge is needed  ✅
- **C.** To announce the pupils' names
- **D.** To ask pupils to stop using the highway

*That is why* links the accident to the request: the injuries are evidence for the footbridge. Purpose questions ask why a detail is included.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- To praise the three pupils: Nothing in the talk praises them.
- To announce the pupils' names: No names are given.
- To ask pupils to stop using the highway: The speaker asks the council for a footbridge, not pupils to avoid the road.

### 39. `eng-lis-07`

The following announcement is read aloud.

> Good morning. From Monday, the library will open at eight o'clock instead of nine, and close at six. Pupils may now borrow three books at a time instead of two, and books may be kept for two weeks.

What is the announcement mainly about?

- **A.** The opening of a new library
- **B.** How to find a book in the library
- **C.** Changes to the library's opening times and borrowing rules  ✅
- **D.** The closing of the library

The main idea covers all the details: new times, more books, a longer loan. Each wrong option fits only part of the passage or none of it.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- The opening of a new library: The library already exists; only its rules are changing.
- How to find a book in the library: Finding books is not mentioned.
- The closing of the library: The library is not closing; it is opening earlier.

### 40. `eng-lis-08`

In a listening test, one option repeats several words exactly as the speaker said them. What should the candidate do?

- **A.** Choose it at once, because it uses the speaker's words
- **B.** Reject it at once, because the right answer never repeats the speaker's words
- **C.** Leave the question blank
- **D.** Check that the option matches the meaning, because repeated words are often a trap  ✅

Judge every option by the meaning of what was said, not by matching words. Eliminate what you know is wrong, then choose.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- Choose it at once, because it uses the speaker's words: Wrong options often reuse the speaker's words in a way that changes the meaning.
- Reject it at once, because the right answer never repeats the speaker's words: The right answer may repeat the speaker's words; the point is to check the meaning.
- Leave the question blank: A blank earns nothing; eliminate options and choose the best one.
