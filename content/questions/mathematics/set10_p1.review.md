## Similar and Congruent Figures

_Syllabus: D(c)(iii),(vi) Similar and congruent triangles; ratio of sides and areas; scale_

### 1. `maths-similar-06`

Two right-angled triangles have equal hypotenuses, and one other side of the first equals one side of the second. Which condition shows they are congruent?

- **A.** RHS (right angle, hypotenuse, side)  ✅
- **B.** AAA (three angles)
- **C.** SAS (two sides and the included angle)
- **D.** ASA (two angles and the included side)

For right-angled triangles, a right angle, the hypotenuse and one other side equal (RHS) is enough to prove congruence.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- AAA (three angles): Equal angles show triangles are similar, not that they are the same size.
- SAS (two sides and the included angle): The equal side and hypotenuse do not enclose a known equal angle here, so SAS does not apply directly.
- ASA (two angles and the included side): Only one angle (the right angle) is known to be equal.

### 2. `maths-similar-07`

On a map with a scale of 1 : 20 000, a rectangular field measures 3 cm by 2 cm. Find the real area of the field in hectares. (1 hectare = 10 000 m²)

- **A.** $2.4$ hectares
- **B.** $12$ hectares
- **C.** $24$ hectares  ✅
- **D.** $240$ hectares

Real lengths: $3 \times 20\,000 = 60\,000$ cm $= 600$ m and $2 \times 20\,000 = 40\,000$ cm $= 400$ m.

Area $= 600 \times 400 = 240\,000$ m² $= 24$ hectares.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- $2.4$ hectares: Check the conversion: 240 000 m² $\div$ 10 000 = 24 hectares.
- $12$ hectares: You multiplied the map area (6 cm²) by 20 000 once. Areas scale by the square of the scale factor.
- $240$ hectares: 1 hectare is 10 000 m², not 1 000 m².

### 3. `maths-similar-08`

At the same time of day, a vertical stick 1.5 m tall casts a shadow 2 m long, and a tree casts a shadow 12 m long. Find the height of the tree.

- **A.** $9$ m  ✅
- **B.** $10.5$ m
- **C.** $11.5$ m
- **D.** $16$ m

The stick and the tree make similar triangles with their shadows:

$\dfrac{h}{12} = \dfrac{1.5}{2} \Rightarrow h = 12 \times 0.75 = 9$ m

**Why the other options are wrong**

- $10.5$ m: You subtracted 1.5 from 12. The shapes are similar, so use a ratio.
- $11.5$ m: You subtracted the difference between the stick and its shadow. Use the ratio height : shadow.
- $16$ m: You used shadow ÷ height for the stick. Height ÷ shadow $= \dfrac{1.5}{2}$.

### 4. `maths-similar-09`

Two similar triangles have areas of 25 cm² and 64 cm². The perimeter of the smaller triangle is 20 cm. Find the perimeter of the larger triangle.

- **A.** $12.5$ cm
- **B.** $23$ cm
- **C.** $32$ cm  ✅
- **D.** $51.2$ cm

Ratio of areas $= 25 : 64$, so the ratio of lengths $= \sqrt{25} : \sqrt{64} = 5 : 8$.

Perimeter of the larger $= 20 \times \dfrac{8}{5} = 32$ cm

- $12.5$ cm: You used the ratio the wrong way round. The larger triangle has the larger perimeter.
- $23$ cm: You added the difference of the square roots. Lengths are in the ratio $5 : 8$.
- $51.2$ cm: You used the ratio of the areas, $\dfrac{64}{25}$. Lengths use its square root, $\dfrac{8}{5}$.

### 5. `maths-similar-10`

Two similar solids have volumes of 8 cm³ and 27 cm³. Find the ratio of their surface areas.

- **A.** $4 : 9$  ✅
- **B.** $2 : 3$
- **C.** $8 : 27$
- **D.** $16 : 81$

- $8 : 27$: $8 : 27$ is the ratio of the volumes.

## Loci

_Syllabus: D(f) Loci in two dimensions and their intersections_

### 6. `maths-loci-06`

The point inside a triangle that is equidistant from all three sides is where

- **A.** the perpendicular bisectors of the sides meet
- **B.** the bisectors of the angles meet  ✅
- **C.** the medians meet
- **D.** the altitudes meet

The locus of points equidistant from two lines is the bisector of the angle between them. The point equidistant from all three sides lies on all three angle bisectors. (It is the centre of the circle that touches all three sides.)

**Why the other options are wrong**

- the perpendicular bisectors of the sides meet: That point is equidistant from the three vertices (corners), not the sides.
- the medians meet: The medians meet at the centre of mass, which is not equidistant from the sides in general.
- the altitudes meet: The altitudes meet at a point that is not, in general, equidistant from the sides.

### 7. `maths-loci-07`

The point that is equidistant from the three vertices of a triangle is where

- **A.** the perpendicular bisectors of the sides meet  ✅
- **B.** the bisectors of the angles meet
- **C.** the medians meet
- **D.** the altitudes meet

Points equidistant from two vertices lie on the perpendicular bisector of the side joining them. The point on all three perpendicular bisectors is equidistant from all three vertices (the centre of the circle through them).

**Why the other options are wrong**

- the bisectors of the angles meet: That point is equidistant from the three sides, not the vertices.
- the medians meet: The medians meet at the centre of mass, which is not equidistant from the vertices in general.
- the altitudes meet: The altitudes do not, in general, meet at a point equidistant from the vertices.

### 9. `maths-loci-09`

- **A.** $y = 3$

- **C.** $x = 6$
- **D.** $x = 3$  ✅

### 12. `maths-construction-07`

How is an angle of 45° constructed with ruler and compasses?

- **A.** Construct 60° and bisect it
- **B.** Construct 90° and bisect it  ✅
- **C.** Bisect an angle of 120°
- **D.** Construct 60° and subtract 30° from 90°

Construct a perpendicular (90°), then bisect it: $90° \div 2 = 45°$.

### 13. `maths-construction-08`

When bisecting an angle with ruler and compasses, what is the first step after drawing the angle?

- **A.** Draw a line from the vertex at 45° to one arm
- **B.** With centre at the end of one arm, draw a circle through the vertex
- **C.** Join the ends of the two arms
- **D.** With centre at the vertex, draw an arc cutting both arms  ✅

1. With centre at the vertex, draw an arc cutting both arms.
2. From each of those points, draw arcs of equal radius that cross.
3. Join the vertex to the crossing point: this line bisects the angle.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- Draw a line from the vertex at 45° to one arm: Measuring 45° needs a protractor, and the bisector depends on the size of the angle.
- With centre at the end of one arm, draw a circle through the vertex: The arcs are drawn from the vertex first, so that they mark equal distances on both arms.
- Join the ends of the two arms: The arms can be of any length, so joining their ends does not help find the bisector.

### 14. `maths-construction-09`

The perpendicular bisector of a line segment $AB$

- **B.** passes through $A$ at right angles to $AB$
- **C.** passes through the mid-point of $AB$ at 60° to $AB$
- **D.** passes through $B$, parallel to $AB$

The perpendicular bisector cuts $AB$ into two equal halves and is at right angles to it. Every point on it is equidistant from $A$ and $B$.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- passes through the mid-point of $AB$ at 60° to $AB$: A perpendicular bisector meets $AB$ at 90°.
- passes through $B$, parallel to $AB$: A parallel line never meets $AB$, so it cannot bisect it.

### 15. `maths-construction-10`

To construct a line through a point $P$ parallel to a given line $AB$, one method is to

- **C.** draw a circle with centre $P$ passing through $A$

### 21. `maths-differentiation-06`

Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ if $y = (2x + 1)^{2}$.

- **A.** $4x + 2$
- **B.** $4x + 4$
- **C.** $8x + 4$  ✅
- **D.** $4x^{2} + 4x$

Expand: $y = 4x^{2} + 4x + 1$. Then $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 8x + 4$.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- $4x + 2$: You wrote $2(2x + 1)$ and forgot the factor 2 from inside the bracket. Expand first: $4x^{2} + 4x + 1$.
- $4x + 4$: Expanding gives $4x^{2} + 4x + 1$; the derivative of $4x^{2}$ is $8x$, not $4x$.
- $4x^{2} + 4x$: That is $y - 1$, not its derivative.

### 22. `maths-differentiation-07`

Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ if $y = \dfrac{4}{x^{2}}$.

- **A.** $\dfrac{8}{x^{3}}$
- **B.** $-\dfrac{8}{x}$
- **C.** $\dfrac{4}{2x}$
- **D.** $-\dfrac{8}{x^{3}}$  ✅

$y = 4x^{-2}$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 4 \times (-2)x^{-3} = -8x^{-3} = -\dfrac{8}{x^{3}}$

**Why the other options are wrong**

- $\dfrac{8}{x^{3}}$: $y = 4x^{-2}$; bringing down the power $-2$ makes the answer negative.
- $-\dfrac{8}{x}$: Subtract 1 from the power: $-2 - 1 = -3$, not $-1$.
- $\dfrac{4}{2x}$: You differentiated the bottom only. Write $y = 4x^{-2}$ first.

### 23. `maths-differentiation-08`

Find the maximum value of $y = 6x - x^{2}$.

- **A.** $-9$
- **B.** $3$
- **C.** $9$  ✅
- **D.** $18$

$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 6 - 2x = 0 \Rightarrow x = 3$.

$y = 6(3) - 3^{2} = 18 - 9 = 9$. (It is a maximum because the $x^{2}$ term is negative.)

**Why the other options are wrong**

- $-9$: At $x = 3$, $y = 18 - 9 = +9$.
- $3$: 3 is the value of $x$ at the maximum, not the maximum value of $y$.
- $18$: You worked out only $6x$ at $x = 3$; subtract $x^{2} = 9$.

### 26. `maths-integration-06`

Find $\displaystyle\int (4x^{3} - 2x)\, dx$.

- **A.** $x^{4} - x^{2} + c$  ✅
- **B.** $12x^{2} - 2 + c$
- **C.** $x^{4} - 2x^{2} + c$
- **D.** $4x^{4} - 2x^{2} + c$

Add 1 to each power and divide by the new power:

$\int 4x^{3}\, dx = \dfrac{4x^{4}}{4} = x^{4}$ and $\int 2x\, dx = \dfrac{2x^{2}}{2} = x^{2}$.

$\int (4x^{3} - 2x)\, dx = x^{4} - x^{2} + c$

**Why the other options are wrong**

- $12x^{2} - 2 + c$: You differentiated instead of integrating.
- $x^{4} - 2x^{2} + c$: $\int 2x\, dx = \dfrac{2x^{2}}{2} = x^{2}$: divide by the new power.
- $4x^{4} - 2x^{2} + c$: You raised the powers but did not divide by them.
