# Further Mathematics: what candidates are examined on

Research behind the Further Mathematics notes and question bank, from WAEC's own
e-learning pages (listed at the end). The aim is practice that matches the real
paper and targets the mistakes examiners actually report.

## The exam

- **Paper 2** (essay) has **15 questions**: the e-learning portal numbers each year's
  questions 1 to 15. Questions are multi-part.
- **Paper 1** is objective (multiple choice). Our questions use four options, A–D.
- The examiners group the subject into **Pure Mathematics**, **Statistics and
  Probability**, and **Vectors and Mechanics**.

Topics seen on recent Paper 2s (only some question pages are readable as text):

| Year | Questions with a readable topic |
|---|---|
| 2023 | forces on a particle (resultant); motion from acceleration to velocity, time to rest and distance; arithmetic progression (graph and sum); binomial and Poisson probability ("at most two"); mean and variance, probability from grouped data; projectile motion and equilibrium of a non-uniform beam |
| 2022 | binomial-type probability over six days; Spearman's rank correlation; equilibrium of a suspended body pulled by a horizontal force; probability without replacement |

## Where candidates lose marks

From the Chief Examiner's "weaknesses" pages. These shape the wrong options in our
questions and the "common mistake" boxes in the notes.

| Year | Weaknesses reported |
|---|---|
| 2019, 2020 | premature approximation; omitting units; mechanics; linear transformations; not following rubrics; misreading questions; drawing diagrams |
| 2022 | premature approximation; omitting units; mechanics; **the domain of a function**; **simplifying combined (composite) functions**; scatter diagrams and the line of best fit; differentiation and integration |
| 2023 | premature approximation; omitting units; **vectors**; **matrices and determinants**; **integration by the trapezium rule**; over-reliance on calculators, so working is left out |

Strengths reported: Pure Mathematics (except matrices), and Statistics and Probability
(2019, 2020, 2022).

## What this means for the notes and questions

1. **Keep full accuracy to the end.** Every worked example carries exact values or at
   least 4 significant figures until the last line, and the exam-focus boxes say so.
2. **Show working.** Examiners give method marks for substitutions such as
   $P(2) = 16 - 12 + 8 - 5$; the notes model this.
3. **Functions, matrices, vectors, mechanics and the trapezium rule** get extra worked
   examples and questions, because they are the weak areas.
4. **Units** are given in every mechanics and statistics answer.

## House rules for writing questions

1. **Original only.** Never copy or reword a WAEC question. Testing the same skill is
   fine; the numbers, context and wording must be ours.
2. **Four options, A–D.** Numbers go in ascending order (the build does this).
3. **Every wrong option is a real mistake**, recorded in its `why`, with a `derive`
   that reproduces it wherever the mistake is a calculation.
4. **Answers are computed** (`mq` in `fm_common.py`); statements and forms
   (`concept`) are checked against the notes by an editor.
