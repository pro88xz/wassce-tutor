_Syllabus: Meaning; price index; causes; effects; control; deflation_

### 49. `eco-if-01`

Inflation is best defined as

- **A.** a rise in the price of one good
- **B.** a persistent rise in the general price level  ✅
- **C.** a fall in the general price level
- **D.** an increase in the money supply only

Inflation means prices in general keep rising, so the value of money falls.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- a rise in the price of one good: One price rising is not inflation.
- a fall in the general price level: That is deflation.
- an increase in the money supply only: Money growth may cause inflation, but is not its definition.

---

### 50. `eco-if-02`

A consumer price index rises from 150 to 165 in a year. What is the rate of inflation?

- **A.** 9.09 %
- **B.** 10 %  ✅
- **C.** 15 %
- **D.** 110 %

Inflation $= \dfrac{165 - 150}{150} \times 100 = 10\%$.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 15 %: That is the rise in index points, not the percentage.
- 9.09 %: You divided by this year's index; divide by last year's.
- 110 %: That is the new index as a percentage of the old one.

### 51. `eco-if-03`

Rice (weight 5), fuel (weight 3) and transport (weight 2) have price relatives of 120, 120 and 140. What is the weighted price index?

- **A.** 124  ✅
- **B.** 126.67
- **C.** 380
- **D.** 1240

Index $= \dfrac{120 \times 5 + 120 \times 3 + 140 \times 2}{10} = \dfrac{1240}{10} = 124$.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 126.67: You took a simple average and ignored the weights.
- 1240: You did not divide by the sum of the weights.
- 380: You added the price relatives.

### 52. `eco-if-04`

Demand-pull inflation occurs when

- **A.** costs of production rise
- **B.** import prices rise
- **C.** total demand rises faster than total supply  ✅
- **D.** the general price level falls

Too much money chases too few goods.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- costs of production rise: That is cost-push inflation.
- import prices rise: That is imported inflation.
- the general price level falls: That is deflation.

### 53. `eco-if-05`

Which group generally gains from inflation?

- **A.** creditors
- **B.** people on fixed incomes
- **C.** savers
- **D.** debtors  ✅

Debtors repay loans with money that buys less than when they borrowed it.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- creditors: Creditors are repaid in money worth less.
- people on fixed incomes: Their real income falls.
- savers: Savings lose purchasing power.

### 54. `eco-if-06`

A bank pays 10% interest on savings while inflation is 15%. Approximately what is the real rate of interest?

- **A.** -5 %  ✅
- **B.** 1.5 %
- **C.** 5 %
- **D.** 25 %

Real interest ≈ nominal interest − inflation = 10% − 15% = −5%.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 25 %: You added the rates; subtract inflation from the interest rate.
- 5 %: The real rate is negative because inflation is higher than interest.
- 1.5 %: You divided the rates.

### 55. `eco-if-07`

A monetary measure to reduce inflation is to

- **A.** raise the policy interest rate  ✅
- **B.** cut income taxes
- **C.** increase government spending
- **D.** lower the cash reserve ratio

Higher interest rates make borrowing dearer and reduce spending.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- cut income taxes: Tax cuts are fiscal and raise spending.
- increase government spending: This is fiscal and raises demand.
- lower the cash reserve ratio: This lets banks lend more.

### 56. `eco-if-08`

Deflation is

- **A.** a fall in the rate of inflation
- **B.** a persistent fall in the general price level  ✅
- **C.** rising prices together with high unemployment
- **D.** a fall in the value of money

When prices keep falling, each leone buys more.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- a fall in the rate of inflation: That is disinflation.
- rising prices together with high unemployment: That is stagflation.
- a fall in the value of money: Deflation raises the value of money.

## Public Finance and Taxation

_Syllabus: Revenue and expenditure; budgets; public debt; canons and types of tax; incidence_

### 57. `eco-pf-01`

Which of these is recurrent government expenditure?

- **A.** building a new bridge
- **B.** constructing a hospital
- **C.** salaries of government teachers  ✅
- **D.** building a hydro-electric dam

Recurrent spending is day-to-day spending that comes back every year.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- building a new bridge: Bridges are capital expenditure.
- constructing a hospital: New buildings are capital expenditure.
- building a hydro-electric dam: Dams are capital expenditure.

---

### 58. `eco-pf-02`

A government plans to spend NLe 18,000 million and expects revenue of NLe 15,000 million. What is its budget deficit?

- **A.** 1.2 NLe million
- **B.** 3000 NLe million  ✅
- **C.** 15000 NLe million
- **D.** 33000 NLe million

Deficit = expenditure − revenue = 18,000 − 15,000 = NLe 3,000 million.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 33000 NLe million: You added spending and revenue.
- 1.2 NLe million: You divided spending by revenue.
- 15000 NLe million: That is the revenue.

### 59. `eco-pf-03`

Deducting income tax from workers' salaries before they are paid (PAYE) best satisfies the canon of

- **A.** equity
- **B.** certainty
- **C.** economy
- **D.** convenience  ✅

PAYE is collected at a time and in a way convenient to the taxpayer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- equity: Equity concerns ability to pay.
- certainty: Certainty concerns knowing the amount and time of payment.
- economy: Economy concerns the cost of collection.

### 60. `eco-pf-04`

Which of these is a direct tax?

- **A.** personal income tax  ✅
- **B.** the Goods and Services Tax (GST)
- **C.** import duty
- **D.** excise duty

A direct tax is paid by the person on whom it is imposed and cannot easily be passed on.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- the Goods and Services Tax (GST): GST is charged on goods and services, so it is indirect.
- import duty: Import duty is indirect.
- excise duty: Excise duty is indirect.

### 61. `eco-pf-05`

Incomes of NLe 1,000 and NLe 4,000 pay taxes of NLe 50 and NLe 400. This tax is

- **A.** proportional
- **B.** progressive  ✅
- **C.** regressive
- **D.** an indirect tax

The rate rises from 5% to 10% as income rises.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- proportional: The rates differ: 5% and 10%.
- regressive: The rate rises with income, not falls.
- an indirect tax: A tax on income is direct.

### 62. `eco-pf-06`

A regressive tax is one that

- **A.** takes the same percentage of income from everyone
- **B.** takes a larger percentage of income from the rich
- **C.** takes a larger percentage of income from the poor than from the rich  ✅
- **D.** is paid only by firms

Taxes on essentials take a bigger share of a poor family's income.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- takes the same percentage of income from everyone: That is a proportional tax.
- takes a larger percentage of income from the rich: That is a progressive tax.
- is paid only by firms: Who pays does not decide whether a tax is regressive.

### 63. `eco-pf-07`

A surplus budget is most suitable when the government wants to

- **A.** reduce unemployment
- **B.** finance development by borrowing
- **C.** increase total demand
- **D.** reduce inflation  ✅

Taking in more than it spends reduces total spending in the economy.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- reduce unemployment: A deficit budget raises demand to cut unemployment.
- finance development by borrowing: Borrowing finances a deficit.
- increase total demand: A surplus reduces demand.

### 64. `eco-pf-08`

A shop's taxable sales for a month are NLe 20,000 and the Goods and Services Tax rate is 15%. How much GST is due?

- **A.** 300 NLe
- **B.** 1333.33 NLe
- **C.** 3000 NLe  ✅
- **D.** 23000 NLe

GST = 15% of 20,000 = 0.15 × 20,000 = NLe 3,000.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 1333.33 NLe: You divided by 15 instead of taking 15%.
- 23000 NLe: That is the sales value including tax.
- 300 NLe: You took 1.5% instead of 15%.
