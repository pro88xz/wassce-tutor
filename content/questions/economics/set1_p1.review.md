- **D.** 490 NLe

MC = TC₅ − TC₄ = 270 − 220 = NLe 50.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 54 NLe: That is the average total cost at 5 units.
- 270 NLe: That is the total cost, not the extra cost.
- 490 NLe: You added the totals.

### 53. `eco-cr-05`

A sandal maker sells 3 pairs at NLe 16 each, or 4 pairs at NLe 14 each. What is the marginal revenue of the 4th pair?

- **A.** 2 NLe
- **B.** 8 NLe  ✅
- **C.** 14 NLe
- **D.** 56 NLe

TR₃ = 3 × 16 = 48; TR₄ = 4 × 14 = 56; MR = 56 − 48 = NLe 8.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 14 NLe: That is the price of the 4th pair; MR is the change in total revenue.
- 56 NLe: That is total revenue from 4 pairs.
- 2 NLe: That is the fall in price, not the change in revenue.

### 54. `eco-cr-06`

A firm maximises its profit at the output where

- **A.** total revenue is at its maximum
- **B.** average cost is at its minimum
- **C.** marginal cost equals marginal revenue  ✅
- **D.** price equals average fixed cost

Profit rises while MR > MC and falls once MC > MR, so it is greatest where MC = MR.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- total revenue is at its maximum: Highest revenue is not highest profit.
- average cost is at its minimum: Lowest average cost is not necessarily the most profitable output.
- price equals average fixed cost: This has no special meaning for profit.

### 55. `eco-cr-07`

When a firm can sell any quantity at a constant price,

- **A.** marginal revenue is greater than average revenue
- **B.** marginal revenue is always zero
- **C.** average revenue is less than marginal revenue
- **D.** price, average revenue and marginal revenue are equal  ✅

Every unit sells for the same price, so P = AR = MR.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- marginal revenue is greater than average revenue: With a constant price, MR equals AR.
- marginal revenue is always zero: Each unit adds its price to revenue.
- average revenue is less than marginal revenue: They are equal at a constant price.
