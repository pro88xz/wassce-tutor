- Organic matter sinks to the bottom first: Organic matter is light and floats on the water.
- All the particles settle together in one mixed layer: Particles settle at different rates according to their size, so they form separate layers.

### 40. `agr-sp-03`

Compared with a sandy soil, a clay soil

- **A.** holds more water but is poorly aerated  ✅
- **B.** drains water away faster
- **C.** is lighter and easier to dig
- **D.** holds fewer plant nutrients

Clay has very small pores, so it holds water but lets little air in and can become waterlogged. Sand has large pores and drains quickly.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- drains water away faster: Sandy soil drains fastest because of its large pore spaces.
- is lighter and easier to dig: Sandy soil is light; clay is heavy and sticky when wet.
- holds fewer plant nutrients: Clay particles hold nutrients well; sandy soils are usually poorer.

- **C.** Clay soil, because it holds the most water
- **D.** Gravelly soil, because it is easiest to dig

Loam is a mixture of sand, silt and clay with humus. It drains well yet holds enough water and nutrients, and is easy to work.

- Clay soil, because it holds the most water: Clay holds water but is heavy, poorly aerated and easily waterlogged.
- Gravelly soil, because it is easiest to dig: Gravelly soil holds little water or nutrients.

Many leached upland soils in Sierra Leone are acidic. Their acidity is corrected by adding

Most crops prefer a pH of about 5.5 to 7. Heavy rainfall leaches bases from the soil and leaves it acidic; liming raises the pH.

## Soil Fertility and Fertilizers

_Syllabus: Soil fertility and productivity; macronutrients and micronutrients and their deficiency symptoms; causes of loss of fertility; maintaining fertility; organic and inorganic, straight and compound fertilisers; methods of application_

### 45. `agr-fe-01`

A soil contains plenty of plant nutrients, but crops on it give a poor yield because it is waterlogged. This soil is

- **A.** productive but not fertile
- **B.** fertile but not productive  ✅
- **C.** neither fertile nor productive
- **D.** both fertile and productive

Fertility is the ability to supply nutrients; productivity is the ability to give a high yield, which also needs good drainage, structure, water and management. A productive soil must be fertile, but a fertile soil is not always productive.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- productive but not fertile: It cannot be productive, because its yields are poor.
- neither fertile nor productive: It has plenty of nutrients, so it is fertile.
- both fertile and productive: Its poor yields show that it is not productive.

### 46. `agr-fe-02`

The older leaves of a maize crop turn yellow and the plants are stunted. The nutrient most likely to be lacking is

- **A.** phosphorus
- **B.** potassium
- **C.** nitrogen  ✅
- **D.** calcium

Nitrogen is needed for chlorophyll and proteins. When it is short, the plant moves it from old leaves to young ones, so the older leaves yellow first (chlorosis).

**Why the other options are wrong**

- phosphorus: Phosphorus deficiency gives purplish leaves, poor roots and delayed maturity.
- potassium: Potassium deficiency gives scorched leaf edges and weak stems that fall over.
- calcium: Calcium deficiency kills the growing points.

### 47. `agr-fe-03`

Which nutrient is most important for root development, flowering and seed formation?

- **B.** Potassium
- **C.** Magnesium
- **D.** Phosphorus  ✅

Phosphorus also hastens maturity. Single superphosphate is a straight phosphorus fertiliser.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- Nitrogen: Nitrogen mainly promotes leaf and stem growth.
- Potassium: Potassium mainly strengthens stems, improves disease resistance and helps form starch and sugar.
- Magnesium: Magnesium is part of the chlorophyll molecule.

Which of the following is a micronutrient (trace element)?

NPK 15-15-15 is an example of

### 50. `agr-fe-06`

Urea contains 46% nitrogen. How much nitrogen is there in a 50 kg bag of urea?

### 52. `agr-fe-08`

Which farm practice reduces soil fertility?

- **A.** Adding compost
- **B.** Growing legumes such as cowpea
- **C.** Mulching
- **D.** Bush burning  ✅

Burning destroys organic matter, loses nitrogen and sulfur as gases and leaves the soil bare to erosion. Leaching, erosion, crop removal and continuous cropping also reduce fertility.

## Soil Conservation and Erosion

_Syllabus: Agents and types of erosion; causes and effects of erosion; cultural and mechanical methods of soil conservation_

### 53. `agr-sc-01`

Run-off collects into many small channels a few centimetres deep, which can be removed by ploughing. This type of erosion is

- **A.** rill erosion  ✅
- **B.** sheet erosion
- **C.** gully erosion
- **D.** splash erosion

Erosion often goes splash → sheet → rill → gully as run-off gathers into larger and larger channels.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- sheet erosion: Sheet erosion removes a thin, even layer of topsoil from a wide area, without channels.
- gully erosion: Gullies are deep, wide channels that cannot be crossed by machines or ploughed out.
- splash erosion: Splash erosion is caused by raindrops knocking soil particles out of place.

### 54. `agr-sc-02`

The main agent of soil erosion in Sierra Leone is

- **A.** wind
- **B.** water from heavy rainfall and run-off  ✅
- **C.** ice
- **D.** grazing animals

Many parts of Sierra Leone receive over 2500 mm of rain a year, often in intense storms, so bare soil is quickly washed away.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- wind: Wind erosion matters mainly in dry areas and on bare, dry soil in the Harmattan.
- ice: There are no glaciers in Sierra Leone.
- grazing animals: Overgrazing and trampling expose the soil, but the water that follows does most of the removing.

### 55. `agr-sc-03`

Growing alternate strips of a close-growing crop, such as grass or legumes, and a row crop, such as maize, across a slope is called

- **A.** terracing
- **B.** contour bunding
- **C.** strip cropping  ✅
- **D.** mulching

The close-growing strips slow down run-off from the row-crop strips and trap the soil it carries.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- terracing: Terracing is cutting level steps into a steep slope.
- contour bunding: Contour bunds are low earth or stone walls built across the slope.
- mulching: Mulching is covering the soil surface with dry grass, leaves or crop residues.

On a very steep hillside that is to be farmed, the best mechanical method of controlling erosion is

- **A.** ploughing up and down the slope

- **C.** leaving the soil bare after harvest

Terraces cut the slope into level steps, so water cannot gather speed and soaks into the soil instead.

- burning the vegetation before planting: Burning leaves the soil bare and increases erosion.
- leaving the soil bare after harvest: Bare soil is quickly washed away.

### 57. `agr-sc-05`

A gully that is cutting across a farm is best controlled by

- **C.** burning the grass around the gully
- **D.** digging the gully deeper so it drains faster

Check dams slow the water so it drops its soil, gradually filling the gully; grass then holds the trapped soil in place.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- ploughing along the bottom of the gully: Ploughing loosens the soil and makes the gully worse.
- burning the grass around the gully: Burning removes the plant cover that holds the soil.
- digging the gully deeper so it drains faster: Faster water carries away even more soil.

A living cover breaks the force of raindrops and holds the soil with its roots. Leguminous covers also add nitrogen.

### 59. `agr-sc-07`

Which of the following is an effect of soil erosion on land and water downstream?

- **B.** Faster growth of the crops on the eroded slope
- **C.** Silting of rivers, swamps and reservoirs, which leads to more flooding  ✅
- **D.** Less flooding along rivers

Erosion removes the most fertile soil from one place and dumps it where it causes harm, blocking channels and filling dams and swamps.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A rise in the fertility of the eroded farm: The eroded farm loses its fertile topsoil, so its fertility falls.
- Faster growth of the crops on the eroded slope: Crops on the eroded slope grow worse on the poor subsoil left behind.
- Less flooding along rivers: Silt raises riverbeds and fills reservoirs, so flooding becomes more likely.
