### 1. `agr-ae-01`

A farmer can use a plot either for groundnut, expected to earn NLe 4000, or for pepper, expected to earn NLe 7000. She chooses pepper. What is the opportunity cost of her choice?

- **A.** The NLe 7000 she will earn from pepper
- **B.** NLe 3000, the difference between the two
- **C.** The NLe 4000 she could have earned from groundnut  ✅
- **D.** NLe 11 000, the two earnings added together

Opportunity cost is the next best alternative given up when a choice is made. Choosing pepper means giving up the groundnut income.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- The NLe 7000 she will earn from pepper: This is what she gains from her choice, not what she gives up.
- NLe 3000, the difference between the two: Opportunity cost is the whole of the next best alternative given up, not the difference.
- NLe 11 000, the two earnings added together: She cannot have both; only the alternative she gives up counts.

### 3. `agr-ae-03`

The law of diminishing returns states that, as more and more units of a variable input are added to a fixed input,

- **A.** the extra output from each additional unit eventually falls  ✅
- **B.** total output falls from the very first unit
- **C.** the extra output from each unit keeps rising for ever
- **D.** average output stays exactly the same

For example, adding more workers to one hectare: the first few raise output a lot, later ones add less and less, and too many can even reduce total output.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- total output falls from the very first unit: Total output rises at first; it is the extra (marginal) output that eventually falls.
- the extra output from each unit keeps rising for ever: Extra output rises at first but must eventually fall, because the fixed input limits production.
- average output stays exactly the same: Average output changes as more input is added.

### 41. `agr-pk-06`

In a practical examination, specimen X is a piece of woody stem about 25 cm long, with several nodes and buds. It is most likely

- **A.** a cassava cutting  ✅
- **B.** a yam sett
- **C.** a plantain sucker
- **D.** a maize grain

Describe what you see: 'a woody stem piece about 25 cm long with six nodes and buds' earns more marks than just 'cassava'.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- a yam sett: A yam sett is a piece of tuber, not a woody stem with nodes.
- a plantain sucker: A plantain sucker is a young shoot growing from the corm at the base of the plant.
- a maize grain: A maize grain is a single seed, not a piece of stem.

### 42. `agr-pk-07`

Pigs are best guided from one pen to another using

- **A.** a halter tied to the horns
- **B.** a pig board  ✅
- **C.** a sweep net
- **D.** a rope tied around the tail

A pig board is a flat board held beside the pig's head to block its view and steer it. A snout rope is used to restrain a pig.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- a halter tied to the horns: Pigs have no horns; halters are used for cattle.
- a sweep net: A sweep net is for catching insects.
- a rope tied around the tail: Pulling by the tail injures the pig.
