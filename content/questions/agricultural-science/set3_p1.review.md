## Weed Control

_Syllabus: Harmful effects and uses of weeds; classification of weeds by life cycle, leaf type, habitat and mode of life; preventive, cultural, mechanical, biological and chemical control; types of herbicide_

### 1. `agr-wc-01`

Witchweed (*Striga*) is especially harmful to cereals such as maize and sorghum because it

- **A.** attaches to their roots and takes their food and water  ✅
- **B.** climbs over them and shades out their leaves
- **C.** releases poison gas into the soil
- **D.** is eaten by the pests that attack cereals

Parasitic weeds can cause heavy losses even when few are visible, because much of the damage happens underground before the weed emerges.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- climbs over them and shades out their leaves: *Striga* is a root parasite; it does not climb over the crop.
- releases poison gas into the soil: *Striga* harms the crop by feeding on it, not with gas.
- is eaten by the pests that attack cereals: *Striga* is not a food plant for cereal pests; its harm is as a parasite.

### 2. `agr-wc-02`

Spear grass (*Imperata cylindrica*) is difficult to control mainly because it

- **A.** is an annual that produces seeds only once
- **B.** is a parasite on the roots of crops
- **C.** is a perennial that regrows from underground rhizomes  ✅
- **D.** grows only in water

Slashing or hoeing removes only the top; the rhizomes sprout again. A systemic herbicide such as glyphosate, which moves down to the rhizomes, is effective.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- is an annual that produces seeds only once: An annual completes its life in one season; spear grass lives for many years.
- is a parasite on the roots of crops: Spear grass is free-living; *Striga* is the parasitic weed.
- grows only in water: Spear grass grows on dry land; water hyacinth and water lettuce are aquatic weeds.

### 4. `agr-wc-04`

- **A.** selective  ✅
- **B.** non-selective
- **C.** contact
- **D.** pre-emergence

Rice and maize are grasses, so 2,4-D, which acts on broad-leaved plants, kills the broad-leaved weeds and spares the crop.

### 25. `agr-ps-01`

A farmer harvests 600 kg of maize grain with a moisture content of 25% and dries it to 10%. What will the grain weigh after drying?

- **A.** 450 kg
- **B.** 500 kg  ✅
- **C.** 510 kg
- **D.** 540 kg

Dry matter = 75% of 600 kg = 450 kg. After drying, the dry matter is 90% of the new mass, so new mass = 450 ÷ 0.90 = 500 kg.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 450 kg: 450 kg is the dry matter alone; the dried grain still contains 10% water.
- 510 kg: This takes 15% (25% − 10%) off the fresh mass, but the percentages are of different totals.
- 540 kg: This takes only 10% off the fresh mass.
