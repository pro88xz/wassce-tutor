- **D.** taking the reading quickly

Viewing perpendicularly to the scale removes the apparent shift caused by viewing at an angle.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- reading the scale from one side: That introduces parallax.
- using a longer scale: Scale length does not remove parallax.
- taking the reading quickly: Speed does not affect parallax.

### 135. `phy-prc-07`

In the glass-block experiment, the refractive index is found from the gradient of a graph of

- **A.** i against r
- **B.** sin r against i
- **C.** 1/i against 1/r
- **D.** sin i against sin r  ✅

$\sin i = n\sin r$: a graph of sin i against sin r is a straight line of gradient n.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- i against r: Snell's law relates the sines, so i against r is not a straight line through the origin.
- sin r against i: Use the sines of both angles.
- 1/i against 1/r: That has no simple relation to n.

### 136. `phy-prc-08`

A graph of T² (s²) against l (m) for a pendulum has a gradient of 4.0 s² m⁻¹. What is g? (take π² = 9.87)

- **A.** 0.101 m s⁻²
- **B.** 2.47 m s⁻²
- **C.** 4.0 m s⁻²
- **D.** 9.87 m s⁻²  ✅

$T^2 = \dfrac{4\pi^2}{g}l$, so $g = \dfrac{4\pi^2}{\text{gradient}} = \dfrac{4 \times 9.87}{4.0} = 9.87\ \text{m s}^{-2}$.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- 4.0 m s⁻²: That is the gradient; g = 4π²/gradient.
- 0.101 m s⁻²: You inverted the relation: g = 4π² ÷ gradient.
- 2.47 m s⁻²: You used π² instead of 4π².
