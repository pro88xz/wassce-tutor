A soldier with a gun has power. A paramount chief with no weapon at all can still settle a land dispute and have both sides accept the ruling. The difference is **authority**: power that people accept as rightful. This lesson covers the key concepts used throughout Government: power, authority, legitimacy, influence, political culture, political socialisation and political participation.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define power and state its sources and types
- define authority and describe Max Weber's three types of authority
- explain legitimacy, how it is gained and how it is lost
- distinguish power, authority and influence
- explain political culture, political socialisation and its agents
- describe forms of political participation and the causes of political apathy

## Power

:::callout{type=definition}
**Power** is the ability of a person or group to make others act in a way they would not otherwise act, if necessary by using or threatening force (sanctions).
:::

**Sources of power:** control of the armed forces and police (coercion), wealth, knowledge and expertise, office or position, numbers (a large group or electorate), and personal qualities.

**Types of power:**

- **Political power:** the power to make binding decisions for society, held by the government
- **Economic power:** from control of wealth, land or resources
- **Military power:** from control of weapons and soldiers
- **Traditional power:** held by chiefs and elders by custom

## Authority and legitimacy

![Power plus legitimacy equals authority, with Weber's three sources of authority: traditional, charismatic and legal-rational](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/power-authority.svg)

*Figure 1. Authority is power that people accept as rightful. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=definition}
**Authority** is the **right** to give orders and be obeyed: power that is accepted as lawful and rightful. **Legitimacy** is the acceptance by the people that a government or ruler has the right to rule.
:::

**Max Weber's three types of authority:**

| Type | Based on | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Traditional** | Custom, history and inherited status | A paramount chief from a ruling house |
| **Charismatic** | The exceptional personal qualities of a leader, who inspires loyalty | A nationalist leader such as Kwame Nkrumah |
| **Legal-rational** | Laws and rules; obedience is to the office, not the person | The President of Sierra Leone exercising powers given by the 1991 Constitution |

Other forms are **delegated authority** (given by a higher authority, as when a minister acts for the President) and **de facto** versus **de jure** authority (in control in fact versus lawfully entitled).

**How a government gains legitimacy:**

- free and fair **elections**
- ruling according to the **constitution** and the **rule of law**
- **tradition** and custom
- **good performance**: providing security, services and development
- **recognition** by other states and international bodies

**How legitimacy is lost:** rigged elections, coups d'état, corruption, abuse of human rights, failure to provide basic services, and disregard for the constitution.

## Power, authority and influence compared

| Power | Authority | Influence |
|---|---|---|
| Ability to compel obedience | Right to be obeyed | Ability to persuade others to act |
| May rest on force | Rests on acceptance (legitimacy) | Rests on respect, argument or example |
| Can be used without consent | Requires consent | Cannot compel; others may refuse |
| Example: an army junta | Example: an elected President | Example: religious leaders urging peace |

## Political culture

:::callout{type=definition}
**Political culture** is the set of attitudes, beliefs, values and feelings that the people of a state hold about politics and government.
:::

Political scientists Almond and Verba described three types:

- **Parochial:** people know little about national government and focus on local affairs
- **Subject:** people know about government but see themselves as passive subjects of it
- **Participant:** people are informed and actively take part in politics

## Political socialisation

:::callout{type=definition}
**Political socialisation** is the process by which people acquire their political knowledge, attitudes and values, and so learn the political culture of their society.
:::

**Agents of political socialisation:**

1. **The family:** the first source of political attitudes, including party loyalty
2. **The school:** civic education, school prefect elections, the national anthem and pledge
3. **Peer groups:** friends and age-mates
4. **Religious bodies:** churches and mosques teaching values such as honesty and tolerance
5. **The mass media:** radio, television, newspapers and social media
6. **Political parties and pressure groups:** rallies, campaigns and party education

## Political participation and apathy

**Forms of political participation:** voting in elections, joining or campaigning for a party, contesting for office, attending rallies and public meetings, writing petitions, joining pressure groups, peaceful demonstration, and debating public issues in the media.

**Political apathy** is a lack of interest in politics. Its causes include poverty, illiteracy, disappointment with politicians, fear of violence or intimidation, rigged elections and the belief that one vote makes no difference.

:::callout{type=local-example}
In Sierra Leone the **paramount chief** has **traditional authority**: chiefs come from recognised ruling houses and are respected by custom. Under the Chieftaincy Act and the 1991 Constitution, chiefs also hold **legal-rational** authority, because the law sets out how they are elected and what they may do. One office can rest on more than one type of authority.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Power and authority are not the same. A military junta that seizes power by force has **power** but lacks **authority** until (if ever) the people accept it as legitimate.
:::

## Key points

- **Power** is the ability to compel; **authority** is the accepted right to rule; **legitimacy** is the people's acceptance of that right.
- Weber's types of authority: **traditional, charismatic and legal-rational**.
- Legitimacy comes from **elections, the constitution, tradition, performance and recognition**; it is lost through **rigging, coups, corruption and abuse of rights**.
- **Influence** persuades but cannot compel.
- **Political socialisation** (family, school, peers, religion, media, parties) shapes **political culture** (parochial, subject, participant).
- **Participation** includes voting, campaigning and petitions; **apathy** is caused by poverty, illiteracy, fear and disillusionment.

## Quick check

1. What is the difference between power and authority?
2. Name Max Weber's three types of authority.
3. State three agents of political socialisation.
4. Give two ways a government may lose legitimacy.

**Answers:**

1. Power is the ability to make others obey, possibly by force; authority is the accepted, rightful right to give orders and be obeyed.
2. Traditional, charismatic and legal-rational authority.
3. Any three: the family, the school, peer groups, religious bodies, the mass media, political parties and pressure groups.
4. Any two: rigging elections, coming to power by a coup, corruption, abusing human rights, failing to provide services, disregarding the constitution.
