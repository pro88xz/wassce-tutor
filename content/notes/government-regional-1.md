On 25 May 1963, leaders of 32 independent African states met in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, and founded the **Organisation of African Unity (OAU)**. Its great cause was ending colonial rule and apartheid. Nearly forty years later, Africa's leaders replaced it with the **African Union (AU)**, which aims at deeper unity, development and peace. This lesson explains the OAU and the AU, how they differ, and what they have achieved.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the origin, aims and principles of the OAU
- describe the organs of the OAU and its achievements and failures
- explain why the OAU was replaced by the African Union
- describe the aims and organs of the AU
- compare the OAU and the AU

## The Organisation of African Unity (OAU)

- **Founded:** **25 May 1963**, in **Addis Ababa**, by **32** independent states. The date is celebrated as **Africa Day**.
- **Leading figures:** Emperor Haile Selassie (Ethiopia), Kwame Nkrumah (Ghana), Julius Nyerere (Tanzania), Sékou Touré (Guinea) and others.
- **Sierra Leone** was a founding member.

**Aims:**

1. To promote the **unity and solidarity** of African states.
2. To **coordinate cooperation** to improve the lives of Africans.
3. To **defend the sovereignty, territorial integrity and independence** of members.
4. To **eradicate all forms of colonialism** from Africa.
5. To promote **international cooperation**, respecting the UN Charter.

**Principles:** sovereign equality of members; **non-interference** in internal affairs; respect for territorial integrity; **peaceful settlement** of disputes; condemnation of political assassination and subversion; dedication to the **liberation** of dependent territories; **non-alignment**.

**Organs:** the **Assembly of Heads of State and Government**; the **Council of Ministers**; the **General Secretariat**; the **Commission of Mediation, Conciliation and Arbitration**; and the **Liberation Committee**, which supported freedom movements.

| Achievements of the OAU | Failures of the OAU |
|---|---|
| Helped end **colonialism** in Africa and supported liberation movements | **Non-interference** stopped it acting against dictators and civil wars |
| Helped end **apartheid** in South Africa | Could not prevent **coups** and **genocide** (Rwanda, 1994) |
| Gave Africa a **common voice** at the UN | **Poor funding**: many members did not pay their dues |
| Settled some **border disputes** | **No army** to enforce decisions |
| Respected colonial borders, avoiding endless border wars | Divisions among members |

## The African Union (AU)

**Why the OAU was replaced:** the end of colonialism and apartheid had achieved its main aims; Africa needed a body that could deal with **civil wars, coups, human rights abuses and economic development**.

- The **Sirte Declaration** (Libya, **9 September 1999**) called for a new union.
- The **Constitutive Act** was adopted at **Lomé** in **2000**.
- The AU was launched in **Durban**, South Africa, on **9 July 2002**.
- **Headquarters:** Addis Ababa. **Members:** all **55** African states.

**Aims:** greater **unity**; defending sovereignty; accelerating **political and economic integration**; promoting **peace, security and stability**; promoting **democracy, human rights and good governance**; promoting **sustainable development**.

**Organs:**

| Organ | Role |
|---|---|
| **Assembly** of Heads of State and Government | Supreme organ |
| **Executive Council** of ministers | Coordinates policy |
| **AU Commission** | Secretariat, headed by a Chairperson |
| **Pan-African Parliament** | Consultative parliament, in Midrand, South Africa |
| **Peace and Security Council** | Responds to conflicts and crises |
| **African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights** | Rules on human rights cases |
| **Economic, Social and Cultural Council** | Voice of civil society |

## OAU and AU compared

![A comparison of the OAU and the AU: founded 1963 and 2002, both in Addis Ababa; the OAU focused on ending colonialism with non-interference, the AU on integration, peace and development, with power to intervene and to suspend governments that seize power](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/oau-to-au.svg)

*Figure 1. The OAU and the AU compared. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

The AU's **Constitutive Act, Article 4(h)**, gives it the **right to intervene** in a member state in grave circumstances: **war crimes, genocide and crimes against humanity**. It also **suspends** governments that come to power unconstitutionally, as it has done after coups in several West African states.

**Major AU programmes:** **Agenda 2063** (a long-term plan for Africa's development); the **African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA)**; peace-support operations; and the **African Peer Review Mechanism**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone hosted the **OAU summit in Freetown in 1980**, and President **Siaka Stevens** served as OAU chairman. Under the AU, Sierra Leone **coordinates the Committee of Ten (C-10)**, the group of heads of state that leads Africa's campaign for **reform of the UN Security Council**, including permanent seats for Africa.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The **OAU** was founded in **1963** and the **AU** in **2002**. Do not mix up the dates, and remember that the AU, unlike the OAU, can **intervene** in grave cases and **suspends** coup governments.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Organisation of African Unity (OAU)**: the body founded on 25 May 1963 in Addis Ababa by 32 independent states, Sierra Leone among them.
- **Africa Day**: 25 May, the anniversary of the founding of the OAU.
- **AU Commission**: the secretariat of the African Union, headed by a Chairperson.
- **Pan-African Parliament**: the consultative parliament of the African Union, in Midrand, South Africa.
- **Peace and Security Council**: the African Union organ that responds to conflicts and crises.
:::

## Key points

- **OAU**: founded **25 May 1963**, Addis Ababa, **32** states; aims: **unity, sovereignty, end colonialism**; principle of **non-interference**.
- OAU successes: **decolonisation, end of apartheid**; failures: **coups, civil wars, poor funding, no army**.
- **AU**: **Sirte Declaration (1999)**, **Constitutive Act (2000)**, launched **9 July 2002** in **Durban**; **55** members.
- AU organs: **Assembly, Executive Council, Commission, Pan-African Parliament, Peace and Security Council, African Court**.
- AU can **intervene** (Article 4(h)) and **suspends** unconstitutional governments; programmes: **Agenda 2063, AfCFTA**.

## Quick check

1. When and where was the OAU founded?
2. State two achievements of the OAU.
3. When was the African Union launched?
4. Give one important difference between the OAU and the AU.

**Answers:**

1. On 25 May 1963, in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia.
2. Any two: helped end colonialism; helped end apartheid; gave Africa a common voice; settled some border disputes; preserved existing borders.
3. On 9 July 2002, in Durban, South Africa.
4. The OAU followed strict non-interference, while the AU can intervene in cases of war crimes, genocide and crimes against humanity and suspends governments that seize power unconstitutionally.
