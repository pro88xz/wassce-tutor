Every few years, a payroll check in some West African country finds thousands of "ghost workers": names on the government payroll who do not exist or no longer work. Salaries are paid to them anyway, and someone pockets the money. Problems like this show how a civil service can fail the public it is meant to serve. This lesson explains the problems of the civil service, how it is controlled and how it can be reformed.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the problems of the civil service in West Africa
- explain how the civil service is controlled
- explain the relationship between ministers and civil servants
- suggest ways of improving the civil service

## Problems of the civil service

| Problem | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Corruption** | Bribery, embezzlement and demanding payment for services |
| **Ghost workers** | Fake or departed names kept on the payroll |
| **Politicisation** | Appointments and promotions based on party loyalty, undermining neutrality |
| **Nepotism and tribalism** | Jobs given to relatives or people of the same ethnic group |
| **Low pay and poor conditions** | Lead to low morale, absenteeism and corruption |
| **Red tape (bureaucracy)** | Rigid rules and long procedures that delay decisions |
| **Overstaffing** | Too many workers for the work available, wasting money |
| **Lack of training** and modern equipment | Poor efficiency |
| **Brain drain** | Skilled officers leave for the private sector or abroad |
| **Political instability** and coups | Disrupt administration |

## Control of the civil service

![The civil service at the centre, controlled by ministers, Parliament, the courts, the Public Service Commission, the Auditor-General, the Anti-Corruption Commission, the Ombudsman, and the press and public opinion](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/civil-service-control.svg)

*Figure 1. Controls on the civil service. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

1. **Ministerial control:** ministers direct and supervise their ministries.
2. **Parliamentary control:** questions to ministers, debates, and the **Public Accounts Committee**, which examines how money was spent.
3. **Judicial control:** the courts can reverse unlawful decisions and award damages to citizens.
4. **The Public Service Commission:** disciplines officers who misbehave.
5. **The Auditor-General:** audits all government accounts and reports to Parliament.
6. **The Anti-Corruption Commission:** investigates and prosecutes corruption.
7. **The Ombudsman:** investigates complaints of injustice or maladministration by public officials.
8. **Financial regulations and internal audit** within ministries.
9. **The press and public opinion:** expose wrongdoing.

## Ministers and civil servants

| Minister | Civil servant |
|---|---|
| Political head, usually a party member | Career official, politically neutral |
| Temporary: leaves with reshuffles or elections | Permanent |
| Often a non-specialist | Specialist or experienced administrator |
| Makes policy decisions | Advises on and implements policy |
| Publicly responsible to the President and Parliament | Anonymous |

Good government needs both: the minister provides **political direction**, the civil servant provides **expertise and continuity**. Problems arise when ministers ignore expert advice, or when senior officials obstruct policy.

## Improving the civil service

- **Better pay and conditions** to raise morale and reduce corruption
- **Recruitment and promotion strictly on merit** through the Public Service Commission
- **Regular training** and modern equipment, including computerised records
- **Payroll verification** and biometric registers to remove ghost workers
- **Strong anti-corruption agencies** and prosecution of offenders
- **Reduced red tape**, with clear service standards
- **Protection from political interference**
- **Performance management**: targets, appraisal and rewards

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone has repeatedly used **payroll verification**, matching every name on the government payroll to a real, present worker, to remove ghost workers and save public money. The **Anti-Corruption Commission** (first established in 2000 and strengthened by the Anti-Corruption Act 2008) investigates and prosecutes officials, and the **Audit Service Sierra Leone** publishes an annual report on government accounts that Parliament's Public Accounts Committee examines in public hearings.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**"Suggest five ways of reducing corruption in the civil service."** Pair each measure with how it works:

- **Better pay:** reduces the temptation to take bribes.
- **Payroll verification:** removes ghost workers.
- **Prosecution by the Anti-Corruption Commission:** deters others.
- **Computerised services and payments:** fewer chances for officials to demand bribes face to face.
- **Annual audits published and debated in Parliament:** misuse is exposed.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
"Red tape" is not always bad. Rules and procedures protect **fairness and accountability**, for example by requiring tenders for contracts. The problem is **excessive** red tape that causes needless delay.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Ghost workers**: fake or departed names kept on the payroll.
- **Red tape** (bureaucracy): rigid rules and long procedures that delay decisions.
- **Politicisation**: appointments and promotions based on party loyalty, which undermine the neutrality of the civil service.
- **Nepotism**: giving jobs to relatives or people of the same ethnic group.
- **Public Accounts Committee**: the committee of Parliament that examines how public money has been spent.
:::

## Key points

- Problems: **corruption, ghost workers, politicisation, nepotism, low pay, red tape, overstaffing, brain drain**.
- Controls: **ministers, Parliament (Public Accounts Committee), courts, Public Service Commission, Auditor-General, Anti-Corruption Commission, Ombudsman, press**.
- Ministers are **temporary political heads**; civil servants are **permanent, neutral experts**.
- Reforms: **better pay, merit, training, payroll verification, anti-corruption action, less red tape, performance management**.

## Quick check

1. What are ghost workers?
2. Name two bodies in Sierra Leone that help control the civil service.
3. State two differences between a minister and a civil servant.
4. Give two ways to improve the civil service.

**Answers:**

1. Fake or departed names kept on the government payroll, whose salaries are collected by others.
2. Any two: the Public Service Commission; the Auditor-General (Audit Service); the Anti-Corruption Commission; the Ombudsman; Parliament's Public Accounts Committee; the courts.
3. Any two: a minister is a political appointee, a civil servant is a neutral career official; a minister is temporary, a civil servant is permanent; a minister makes policy, a civil servant advises and implements; a minister is publicly responsible, a civil servant is anonymous.
4. Any two: better pay; merit-based recruitment and promotion; training and equipment; payroll verification; anti-corruption action; reduced red tape; protection from political interference; performance management.
