**Freetown** was founded in **March 1792** by about **1,190 Nova Scotians**, formerly enslaved African Americans who had fought for or escaped to the British side in the **American War of Independence** and had been resettled in **Nova Scotia** in Canada. Led by **Lieutenant John Clarkson** of the **Sierra Leone Company**, they sailed from **Halifax** in fifteen ships. The young colony faced **disease, a French attack (1794)**, disputes over **land and quitrents**, and a **settlers' rising in 1800**, which was put down with the help of newly arrived **Jamaican Maroons**. Financial failure led the Company to hand Sierra Leone to the **British Crown** on **1 January 1808**. This lesson explains **the Nova Scotian settlers, the Maroons and the Sierra Leone Company**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain who the Nova Scotians were and why they came to Sierra Leone
- describe the founding of Freetown in 1792
- describe the government of the colony under the Sierra Leone Company
- explain the causes and results of the Nova Scotian rising of 1800
- explain who the Jamaican Maroons were and their role
- explain why the Company handed the colony to the Crown in 1808

## The Nova Scotians

**Who they were:** During the American War of Independence, the British offered **freedom** to enslaved people who left rebel masters to serve the British side. These **Black Loyalists** were taken to **Nova Scotia** (Canada) in **1783**.

**Why they left Nova Scotia:**

1. They were given **poor land, late or not at all**, unlike white Loyalists.
2. They faced **racial discrimination** and violence.
3. The **cold climate** made farming hard.
4. Some were **forced into labour** like servants.

**Thomas Peters**, a Black Loyalist leader, travelled to **London in 1790** to complain. The Sierra Leone Company offered free passage and land in Sierra Leone, and **John Clarkson**, a naval officer and brother of the abolitionist Thomas Clarkson, organised the migration.

## The founding of Freetown, 1792

1. **Fifteen ships** left **Halifax** on **15 January 1792** carrying about **1,190** people.
2. They reached Sierra Leone between **late February and March 1792**; some had died on the voyage.
3. The settlement was named **Free Town**, later **Freetown**. By tradition, the settlers held a **thanksgiving service** under a great **cotton tree**, which became the city's symbol.
4. **Clarkson** became the first **Governor**. Leading settlers included **Thomas Peters**, who died soon after arrival, and preachers such as **David George** (Baptist), **Boston King** and **Moses Wilkinson** (Methodists).

## Government under the Sierra Leone Company

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **Governor and Council** | appointed by the Company directors in London; the Governor ran the colony |
| **Hundredors and tithingmen** | settlers elected representatives (one tithingman for every ten families, one hundredor for every hundred), based on Sharp's ideas; they wanted a real say in government |
| **Governors** | **John Clarkson (1792)**, **William Dawes**, **Zachary Macaulay** (the most important, 1790s), **Thomas Ludlam** |
| **Economy** | farming on small plots; Company store; trade with the hinterland |

## Problems of the colony

1. **Disease** and deaths in the rainy season.
2. **Land:** settlers received **less land** than promised, and later.
3. **Quitrents:** the Company demanded a yearly **quitrent** (land rent), which settlers saw as unfair and as a sign that they did not own their land.
4. **The French attack:** in **September 1794** a **French squadron** sacked and burned Freetown during the war between Britain and France.
5. **Temne hostility:** disputes over land led to **Temne attacks** on Freetown in **1801 and 1802**.
6. **Company losses:** the Company spent far more than it earned.

## The Nova Scotian rising of 1800

**Causes:**

1. Anger over **quitrents** and **land**.
2. The settlers' demand for **self-government** through their hundredors and tithingmen.
3. A **Royal Charter (1799)** gave the Company and Governor **more power**, ignoring the settlers' representatives.

**Course and result:** In **September 1800**, a group of settlers declared their own laws and tried to take control. The Governor's forces were helped by the **Jamaican Maroons**, who arrived at exactly that moment. The rising was **crushed**; some leaders were **executed** or **banished**.

## The Jamaican Maroons

The **Maroons of Trelawny Town** in Jamaica were descendants of Africans who had escaped slavery and lived free in the mountains. After the **Second Maroon War (1795–96)**, the British **deported** them to **Nova Scotia** in **1796**. In **1800**, about **550** were brought to **Freetown**. They helped to defeat the Nova Scotian rising and were given land, founding **Maroon Town** in the west of Freetown.

## The Crown takes over, 1808

The Sierra Leone Company handed the colony to the **British government** on **1 January 1808** because:

1. it had **lost large sums of money**;
2. it could not defend the colony against **the French** and **the Temne**;
3. it faced **settler unrest**;
4. after the **abolition of the slave trade (1807)**, Britain needed Freetown as a **naval base** and a place to land freed captives.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Freetown's old buildings and places preserve this history: **St John's Maroon Church** (built by the Maroons), the **Cotton Tree** in the city centre (until it fell in a storm in **May 2023**), and the original street plan laid out for the Nova Scotian settlers in the 1790s.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The Nova Scotians were **not** Canadians by origin. They were **African Americans**, many born in Africa or the American colonies, who had escaped slavery, sided with Britain, and were moved to Nova Scotia only after the war.
:::

## Key points

- **Nova Scotians:** Black Loyalists moved to Nova Scotia (1783); left because of **poor land, discrimination and cold**.
- **Thomas Peters** petitioned London (1790); **John Clarkson** led about **1,190** settlers from **Halifax** (Jan 1792); **Freetown founded, March 1792**.
- Problems: **disease, land, quitrents, French attack (1794), Temne attacks (1801–02), Company losses**.
- **1800 rising** over quitrents and self-government, crushed with the help of about **550 Jamaican Maroons**.
- **1 January 1808:** Sierra Leone became a **Crown Colony**.

1. Who were the Nova Scotians?
2. Give two reasons why they left Nova Scotia.
3. Who led the Nova Scotians to Sierra Leone, and when did they arrive?
4. What were quitrents, and why were they resented?
5. Who were the Maroons, and what part did they play in 1800?
6. Give three reasons why the Company handed the colony to the Crown.

**Answers:**

1. Black Loyalists, formerly enslaved Africans in America who sided with Britain in the American War of Independence and were moved to Nova Scotia.
2. Any two: poor land given late or not at all; racial discrimination; cold climate; forced labour.
3. John Clarkson; they arrived in March 1792.
4. Yearly land rents demanded by the Company; settlers felt they had been promised the land and that rents denied their ownership.
5. Jamaican Maroons, descendants of escaped slaves, deported to Nova Scotia and brought to Freetown in 1800; they helped to put down the Nova Scotian rising.
6. Any three: financial losses; inability to defend the colony; settler unrest; Britain needed a naval base and a place to land freed captives after 1807.
