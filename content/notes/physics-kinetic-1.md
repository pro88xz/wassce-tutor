Why does the smell of fried fish reach the next room? Why does a wet cloth dry in the sun? Why does ice melt but a stone does not? All of these are explained by one idea: everything is made of tiny particles that never stop moving.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the main points of the kinetic theory of matter
- describe the arrangement and motion of particles in solids, liquids and gases
- explain Brownian motion and diffusion as evidence for moving particles
- use the kinetic theory to explain the properties of the three states

## The kinetic theory of matter

:::callout{type=definition}
The **kinetic theory of matter** states that:

1. all matter is made up of **tiny particles** (atoms, molecules or ions)
2. these particles are in **constant, random motion**
3. there are **forces of attraction** between the particles, which are strongest when they are close together
4. the **higher the temperature**, the **faster** the particles move (the greater their average kinetic energy)
:::

## The three states of matter

![Three boxes of particles: in a solid they are tightly packed in a regular pattern; in a liquid they are close but irregular; in a gas they are far apart moving quickly in random directions](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/states-of-matter.svg)

*Figure 1. Particles in a solid, a liquid and a gas. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Property | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrangement | closely packed, regular pattern | close together, irregular | far apart, random |
| Motion | vibrate about fixed positions | move past one another | move rapidly in all directions |
| Forces between particles | very strong | strong, but weaker than in solids | very weak (almost none) |
| Shape | fixed | takes the shape of the container | fills the whole container |
| Volume | fixed | fixed | not fixed |
| Compressibility | almost incompressible | almost incompressible | easily compressed |
| Density | high | high | low |

### Explaining the properties

- **Solids keep their shape** because strong forces hold each particle in place; the particles can only vibrate.
- **Liquids flow** because the particles, though close, can slide past one another.
- **Gases fill their containers and are easily squashed** because the particles are far apart with large empty spaces between them.

## Evidence for moving particles

### Brownian motion

:::callout{type=definition}
**Brownian motion** is the continuous, random, zig-zag movement of small particles (such as smoke or pollen grains) suspended in a fluid.
:::

**Smoke-cell experiment.** A little smoke is trapped in a small glass cell, lit from the side and viewed through a microscope. The smoke particles appear as bright specks that move jerkily and randomly.

**Explanation:** the smoke particles are being **bombarded unevenly** by the invisible air molecules, which are moving rapidly and randomly. At any moment more molecules hit one side than the other, so the particle is pushed first one way, then another. Raising the temperature makes the motion more vigorous.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
In the smoke cell you **do not see air molecules** — they are far too small. You see the **smoke particles**, which are moved about by the air molecules hitting them.
:::

### Diffusion

:::callout{type=definition}
**Diffusion** is the spreading of particles from a region of **higher concentration** to a region of **lower concentration** until they are evenly mixed, as a result of their random motion.
:::

- The smell of perfume or of food cooking spreads through a room.
- A crystal of potassium permanganate placed in water slowly colours all the water purple, without stirring.
- Bromine vapour spreads through a gas jar of air.

Diffusion is **fastest in gases** (particles far apart and fast), **slower in liquids**, and extremely slow in solids. It is **faster at higher temperatures** and for **lighter** molecules.

:::callout{type=local-example}
When a neighbour fries fish with pepper in the compound, you can smell it inside your house within seconds. The smell molecules diffuse through the air. On a hot afternoon the smell travels faster than on a cool morning.
:::

## Changes of state, explained

| Change | What happens to the particles |
|---|---|
| **Melting** (solid → liquid) | heat makes particles vibrate so strongly that they break free of fixed positions |
| **Boiling / evaporation** (liquid → gas) | the most energetic particles escape the attractive forces completely |
| **Condensation** (gas → liquid) | cooled particles slow down and are pulled together by attractive forces |
| **Freezing** (liquid → solid) | particles lose energy and settle into fixed positions |

During melting and boiling the energy supplied breaks the bonds between particles instead of raising the temperature. This is the **latent heat**, studied later.

## Gas pressure and temperature

Gas particles constantly collide with the walls of their container. Each collision exerts a tiny force; together they produce the **pressure** of the gas. Heating the gas makes the particles move faster, so they hit the walls more often and harder: the pressure rises. This is why a car tyre's pressure is higher after a long drive and why aerosol cans warn "do not heat".

## Key points

- Matter is made of tiny particles in constant random motion with attractive forces between them.
- Solid: fixed positions, vibrating. Liquid: close but mobile. Gas: far apart, fast, random.
- **Brownian motion** (smoke cell) and **diffusion** are evidence that particles move.
- Higher temperature → faster particles → faster diffusion, more gas pressure.

## Quick check

1. In Brownian motion, what causes the random movement of smoke particles?
2. Why is a gas easily compressed but a liquid is not?
3. Give one everyday example of diffusion.

**Answers:** 1. Uneven bombardment by fast-moving, invisible air molecules. 2. Gas particles are far apart with large spaces between them; liquid particles are already close together. 3. For example: the smell of cooking spreading through a house.
