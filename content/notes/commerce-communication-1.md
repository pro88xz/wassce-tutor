A wholesaler's price change is useless if retailers never hear of it, and an order is worthless if the supplier misreads it. **Communication** is the passing of information, ideas and instructions between people. In commerce it removes the **hindrance of knowledge (information)**: buyers and sellers must know about each other before they can trade.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define communication and explain its importance in commerce
- describe the communication process
- classify the types and methods of communication
- compare oral, written, visual and electronic communication
- explain the barriers to effective communication and how to overcome them

## Meaning and importance of communication

:::callout{type=definition}
**Communication** is the transfer of messages (information, ideas, instructions or feelings) from a **sender** to a **receiver** through a **channel**, with the receiver **understanding** and responding.
:::

**Importance of communication in commerce:**

1. **Links buyers and sellers**: enquiries, quotations, orders and complaints.
2. **Speeds up business**: decisions and transactions are made faster.
3. **Advertising** depends on it to inform and persuade consumers.
4. **Coordinates work** within a firm: instructions, reports and meetings.
5. **Market information**: traders learn about prices, demand and competitors.
6. **Helps other aids to trade**: banking, insurance and transport rely on messages.
7. **Makes foreign trade possible** by linking traders across countries.
8. **Reduces costs** of travel and wasted journeys.
9. **Good public relations** with customers and the public.

## The communication process

![The communication process: a sender encodes a message, which passes through a channel to a receiver who decodes it and gives feedback; noise can distort it](https://wasscetutor.com/img/commerce/communication-process.svg)

*Figure 1. Communication is complete only when feedback returns to the sender. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sender** | The person or firm with the message |
| **Encoding** | Putting the idea into words, figures, pictures or signals |
| **Message** | The information itself |
| **Channel (medium)** | The means used: letter, telephone, email, meeting, poster |
| **Receiver** | The person who gets the message |
| **Decoding** | The receiver interpreting the message |
| **Feedback** | The receiver's response, showing whether the message was understood |
| **Noise** | Anything that distorts the message (poor network, jargon, distraction) |

## Types of communication

### By direction

- **Internal**: within a firm (manager to staff, between departments). It may be **downward** (instructions), **upward** (reports, suggestions) or **horizontal** (between people at the same level).
- **External**: between the firm and outsiders (customers, suppliers, banks, government).

### By formality

- **Formal**: through official channels and records (letters, memos, minutes).
- **Informal**: casual conversation, "grapevine" information.

## Methods of communication

| Method | Examples | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Oral (verbal)** | Face-to-face talk, telephone, meetings, interviews, radio | Quick; immediate feedback; personal; questions answered at once | No permanent record; can be forgotten or denied; may be misheard |
| **Written** | Letters, memos, reports, notices, minutes, circulars, invoices | Permanent **record** and evidence; can be checked and copied; detailed | Slower; costly in time; no instant feedback; can be misread |
| **Visual** | Charts, graphs, posters, signs, photographs, films | Attracts attention; easy to understand; crosses language barriers | Limited detail; may be misinterpreted |
| **Electronic** | Email, SMS, WhatsApp, video calls, websites, social media | Very fast; cheap; reaches many people; works over long distances; can keep records | Needs power, devices and network; risk of hacking, fraud and viruses |

:::callout{type=local-example}
A market trader in Freetown may phone a supplier in Kenema to check prices (oral), send the order on WhatsApp (electronic), receive a written invoice with the goods and pay by mobile money with an SMS receipt. One deal can use several methods, and the written and electronic ones leave a **record**.
:::

## Qualities of good communication

The message should be:

- **clear**: simple words
- **correct**: accurate facts and figures
- **complete**: all the necessary details
- **concise**: no unnecessary words
- **courteous**: polite
- **timely**: sent at the right time
- sent through the **right channel**

## Barriers to effective communication

| Barrier | How it is overcome |
|---|---|
| **Language differences** and **jargon** | Use simple, common language; interpreters |
| **Noise**: poor network, distractions | Better equipment; quiet place; repeat important points |
| **Poor listening** or lack of attention | Active listening; ask for feedback |
| **Wrong choice of channel** | Choose the channel that suits the message and receiver |
| **Distance and time differences** | Telephone, email, video calls |
| **Illiteracy** | Oral and visual messages; local languages |
| **Emotions and attitudes**: anger, bias, fear | Calm, respectful tone |
| **Too much information** (overload) | Keep messages short and focused |
| **Cultural differences** | Respect for customs; local agents |
| **Poor infrastructure**: power cuts, weak networks | Backup power; several networks |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Communication is not complete when the message is **sent**. It is complete when the receiver **understands** it and gives **feedback**. Answers that leave out feedback lose marks.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Encoding**: putting an idea into words, figures, pictures or signals.
- **Decoding**: the receiver interpreting a message.
- **Channel** (medium): the means used to send a message, such as a letter, telephone call, email or meeting.
- **Feedback**: the receiver's response, showing whether a message was understood.
- **Noise**: anything that distorts a message, such as a poor network, jargon or distraction.
:::

## Key points

- **Communication** removes the hindrance of **knowledge**; it links buyers and sellers.
- Process: **sender → encoding → message → channel → receiver → decoding → feedback**, with **noise** as interference.
- Methods: **oral, written, visual and electronic**; written methods give a **record**, oral gives **quick feedback**.
- Barriers: **language, noise, poor listening, wrong channel, illiteracy, emotions, overload, poor infrastructure**.

## Quick check

1. Which hindrance to trade does communication remove?
2. What is feedback, and why is it important?
3. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of written communication.
4. Give three barriers to effective communication.

**Answers:** 1. The hindrance of knowledge (information). 2. The receiver's response to a message; it shows the sender whether the message was understood. 3. Advantage: a permanent record (or evidence, detail). Disadvantage: slow (or no instant feedback, can be misread). 4. Any three: language and jargon, noise, poor listening, wrong channel, distance, illiteracy, emotions, overload, cultural differences, poor infrastructure.
