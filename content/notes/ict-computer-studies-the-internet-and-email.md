:::callout{type=definition}
The **Internet** is the worldwide network of computer networks that uses a common set of rules (TCP/IP) to exchange data. **Email** (electronic mail) is a service that sends messages and files from one person's computer or phone to another's over the Internet.
:::

Checking WASSCE results online, watching a lesson video, sending a CV to an employer by email and chatting on WhatsApp all use the **Internet**. This lesson explains **how the Internet works** (IP addresses, domain names and DNS), the **difference between the Internet and the World Wide Web**, the **parts of a URL**, browsers and search engines, Internet service providers, and **how to send an email**, including CC, BCC and attachments.

## The Internet and the World Wide Web

The Internet grew from **ARPANET**, a network set up in 1969 by the United States Department of Defense. In 1989 **Tim Berners-Lee**, working at CERN in Switzerland, invented the **World Wide Web**, which made the Internet easy to use with linked pages.

:::callout{type=definition}
**World Wide Web (WWW):** the collection of linked web pages and other resources stored on computers across the Internet and viewed with a web browser.

**Web page:** a single document on the World Wide Web, which can contain text, pictures, video and links.

**Website:** a group of related web pages under one domain name, such as a school's or a newspaper's site.

**Home page:** the first or main page of a website.

**Hyperlink:** a word, picture or button on a web page that, when clicked, opens another page or file.
:::

| The Internet | The World Wide Web |
|---|---|
| the **network**: cables, routers and computers worldwide | a **service** that runs on the Internet |
| carries email, video calls, file transfers and the Web | is the web pages and links |
| began as ARPANET in 1969 | invented by Tim Berners-Lee in 1989 |

*Figure 1. The five steps between typing a web address and seeing the page. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=definition}
**IP address (Internet Protocol address):** the unique number that identifies a device on a network or the Internet, written in the common IPv4 form as four numbers from 0 to 255, such as 203.0.113.10.

**Domain name:** the easy-to-remember name of a website, such as example.com, that stands for its IP address.

**Domain Name System (DNS):** the Internet's directory service, which translates domain names into IP addresses.

**Internet service provider (ISP):** a company that connects customers to the Internet, usually for a fee, such as a mobile network or a fibre provider.
:::

Because the old IPv4 system has only about 4.3 billion addresses, a newer system, **IPv6**, uses much longer addresses.

## The parts of a URL

:::callout{type=definition}
**URL (uniform resource locator):** the full address of a web page or file on the Internet, such as https://www.example.com/results/sss3.html.

**HTTPS (Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure):** the secure, encrypted form of HTTP, the protocol used to send web pages; a padlock in the address bar shows that a page uses it.
:::

![The parts of a web address and an email address: in https://www.example.com/results/sss3.html, https:// is the protocol, www. the host, example the domain name, .com the top-level domain and /results/sss3.html the path to the page; in aminata.kamara@example.com, aminata.kamara is the user name, @ means at, and example.com is the domain of the mail provider](https://wasscetutor.com/img/ict/url-and-email-address.svg)

*Figure 2. The parts of a URL and of an email address. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

The **top-level domain** shows the kind of site or its country:

| Top-level domain | Used by |
|---|---|
| **.com** | companies and general sites |
| **.org** | organisations, often non-profit |
| **.edu** | universities and colleges |
| **.gov** | governments (.gov.sl in Sierra Leone) |
| **.net** | network and Internet companies |
| **.sl**, **.gh**, **.ng**, **.uk** | countries: Sierra Leone, Ghana, Nigeria, the United Kingdom |

## Browsers and search engines

:::callout{type=definition}
**Web browser:** an application used to find, open and display web pages, such as Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Microsoft Edge, Safari or Opera Mini.

**Search engine:** a website that searches the Web for pages that match the words you type, such as Google, Bing or DuckDuckGo.
:::

A browser is the program; a search engine is a website you open **in** the browser. Browsers keep **bookmarks** (favourites) of pages you want to find again and a **history** of pages visited.

**Searching well:**

- use a few **key words**, not a whole sentence: *types of printers WAEC*
- put an exact phrase in **quotation marks**: *"mail merge"*
- put a **minus sign** before a word to leave it out: *python -snake*
- check that a result comes from a **trustworthy** source, and compare more than one

## Downloading, uploading and connecting

:::callout{type=definition}
**Download:** to copy a file from the Internet or another computer onto your own device.

**Upload:** to send a file from your own device to the Internet or another computer, such as posting a photo or submitting an assignment.
:::

People connect to the Internet through **mobile data** on a phone (3G, 4G or 5G), **fibre** or **cable** broadband, a **Wi-Fi** network at school or home, or **satellite** in remote places. Speed depends on the connection's bandwidth.

An **intranet** is a private network that works like the Internet but is open only to the members of one organisation, such as staff notices on a bank's internal site. An **extranet** is an intranet that also lets chosen outsiders, such as suppliers, log in.

## Email

:::callout{type=definition}
**Email address:** the unique address to which email is delivered, made of a user name, the @ sign and a domain, such as aminata.kamara@example.com.

**Attachment:** a file, such as a document, photo or PDF, sent together with an email message.

**CC (carbon copy):** the field for people who should receive a copy of an email; every recipient can see their addresses.

**BCC (blind carbon copy):** the field for people who receive a copy of an email without the other recipients seeing their addresses.

**Spam:** unwanted email sent in bulk, usually advertising or scams; most email services move it to a Spam or Junk folder.
:::

### Parts of an email message

| Part | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **To** | the main recipient's address |
| **CC / BCC** | addresses of people who receive copies |
| **Subject** | a short title saying what the email is about |
| **Body** | the message itself |
| **Attachment** (paper-clip icon) | files sent with the message |
| **Signature** | your name and contact details, added at the end |

**Email folders:** **Inbox** (received mail), **Sent**, **Drafts** (unfinished messages), **Spam or Junk**, and **Trash or Bin**.

- **Reply** answers only the sender; **Reply all** answers the sender and everyone else who received the email.
- **Forward** sends a message you received on to someone new.

**Sending an email:** open your email service (such as Gmail or Outlook) → click **Compose** or **New** → type the address in **To** → type a clear **Subject** → write the message → click the **paper clip** to attach a file → check it → click **Send**.

| Advantages of email | Disadvantages of email |
|---|---|
| arrives within seconds, anywhere in the world | needs an Internet connection and a device |
| cheap: no stamps or paper | spam and scam emails |
| one message can go to many people | attachments can carry viruses |
| files can be attached | messages can be sent to the wrong person by mistake |
| messages are stored and can be searched | not suitable for very urgent matters if the reader is offline |

## Internet services

Besides the Web and email, the Internet carries **instant messaging** (WhatsApp, Telegram), **voice and video calls** (VoIP, such as WhatsApp calls and Zoom), **social media** (Facebook, TikTok, X), **online banking**, **online learning**, **file transfer** (FTP), **cloud storage** and **streaming** of music and video.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Checking your WASSCE result online.** You open a browser, type the result checker's address (it begins https://, so the page is encrypted), enter your index number and the card's PIN, and the server looks up your record and sends the result back to your phone in seconds: the browser, URL, HTTPS, the server and the ISP all working together.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Saying "the Internet" and "the Web" mean the same.** The **Internet** is the network itself; the **Web** is one service on it. Email and WhatsApp use the Internet but are not the Web.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. State two differences between the Internet and the World Wide Web."}
The **Internet** is the worldwide **network** of computers; the **Web** is a **service** of linked pages that runs on it. The Internet also carries email and calls, which are not part of the Web. The Internet began in 1969 (ARPANET); the Web was invented in 1989 by Tim Berners-Lee.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Name the parts of the URL https://www.example.gov.sl/news.html"}
**https://** protocol; **www** host; **example** domain name; **.gov.sl** top-level domains (government, Sierra Leone); **/news.html** the path to the page.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. What is the function of DNS?"}
The **Domain Name System** translates **domain names** (such as example.com) into the **IP addresses** that computers use to find each other.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Distinguish between CC and BCC in an email."}
People in **CC** receive a copy, and **all recipients can see** their addresses. People in **BCC** receive a copy, but **other recipients cannot see** their addresses.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Distinguish between a web browser and a search engine, with an example of each."}
A **web browser** is a program that opens and displays web pages, such as **Chrome** or **Firefox**. A **search engine** is a website that finds pages matching your key words, such as **Google** or **Bing**, used inside a browser.
:::

## Key points

- The **Internet** is the global network of networks; the **WWW** is the linked pages on it.
- Devices are found by **IP address**; **DNS** turns **domain names** into IP addresses; an **ISP** connects you.
- **URL** parts: protocol (https), host (www), domain name, top-level domain (.com, .org, .gov, .sl), path.
- A **browser** shows pages; a **search engine** finds them. **Download** to your device, **upload** from it.
- Email: **To, CC, BCC, Subject, body, attachment, signature**; **Reply, Reply all, Forward**; folders **Inbox, Sent, Drafts, Spam, Trash**.
