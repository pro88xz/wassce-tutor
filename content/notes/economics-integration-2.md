Sierra Leone belongs to several regional organisations at once: the Economic Community of West African States, the Mano River Union and the African Union's continental free trade area. Each aims to tie West African economies more closely together. This lesson covers their aims, institutions, achievements and problems.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the aims, institutions and achievements of ECOWAS
- explain the problems facing ECOWAS
- describe the Mano River Union
- outline other regional and continental arrangements: WAMZ, WAEMU and the AfCFTA

## The Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS)

**ECOWAS** was set up on **28 May 1975** by the **Treaty of Lagos**, signed by **15** West African countries (Cape Verde joined in 1977). Its headquarters is in **Abuja, Nigeria**. A **revised treaty** was signed in 1993. Mauritania left in 2000. In **January 2025**, **Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger** left ECOWAS, leaving **12 member states**, including Sierra Leone.

### Aims of ECOWAS

1. To promote **cooperation and integration** in economic, social and cultural activities.
2. To **remove tariffs and other barriers** to trade among members.
3. To establish a **common external tariff** and a common market.
4. To allow **free movement of people, goods, services and capital**.
5. To **harmonise** agricultural, industrial, monetary and fiscal policies.
6. To develop **joint infrastructure**: roads, telecommunications, energy.
7. To promote **peace, security and good governance** in the region.
8. To raise **living standards** and promote economic development.

### Main institutions

| Institution | Role |
|---|---|
| **Authority of Heads of State and Government** | The highest decision-making body |
| **Council of Ministers** | Implements the Authority's decisions |
| **ECOWAS Commission** | The executive secretariat, running day-to-day work |
| **Community Parliament** | A forum for representatives of member states |
| **Community Court of Justice** | Settles disputes and interprets ECOWAS laws |
| **ECOWAS Bank for Investment and Development (EBID)** | Finances development projects in member states |

### Achievements of ECOWAS

1. **Free movement protocol** (1979): ECOWAS citizens can travel and stay in other member states without a visa for up to 90 days, using an ECOWAS passport or identity card.
2. **ECOWAS Trade Liberalisation Scheme (ETLS)**: goods originating in member states can be traded free of import duty.
3. **Common External Tariff** adopted in 2015.
4. **Peacekeeping**: ECOMOG forces intervened in the civil wars in **Liberia and Sierra Leone** in the 1990s, helping to restore peace.
5. **Regional infrastructure**: projects such as the Trans-West African Coastal Highway and regional power pooling.
6. **EBID financing** for projects in member countries.
7. **Promotion of democracy** and responses to unconstitutional changes of government.

### Problems facing ECOWAS

1. **Political instability and coups**, which have led to suspensions and the 2025 withdrawals.
2. **Similar primary products**, so little intra-regional trade.
3. **Many currencies** and inconvertible currencies.
4. **Poor transport and communications** links.
5. **Non-tariff barriers**: roadblocks, delays and harassment at borders.
6. **Loss of customs revenue** and fears of domination by larger economies such as Nigeria.
7. **Overlapping memberships** (for example WAEMU), with different rules.
8. **Late payment of contributions** by member states.
9. **Language and colonial ties** to different European countries.

## The Mano River Union (MRU)

The **Mano River Union** was set up in **1973** by **Liberia and Sierra Leone**, named after the river on their border. **Guinea** joined in **1980** and **Côte d'Ivoire** in **2008**.

**Aims:** a customs union among members; cooperation in trade, transport, energy and industry; joint development projects; and, since the civil wars of the region, **peace and security** along shared borders.

**Problems:** the civil wars in Liberia and Sierra Leone in the 1990s and instability in Guinea halted its work for years; members have weak finances; and members produce similar goods.

## Other arrangements

- **The West African Monetary Zone (WAMZ)**: formed by mainly anglophone ECOWAS states, including **Sierra Leone**, Ghana, Nigeria, Guinea, The Gambia and Liberia, to work towards a **common currency** (planned as the "eco" for ECOWAS).
- **The West African Economic and Monetary Union (WAEMU / UEMOA)**: eight mainly francophone states sharing the **CFA franc** and a common central bank (BCEAO).
- **The African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA)**: an agreement of the **African Union** to create a single continental market, with trading under it beginning in **2021**. Its secretariat is in **Accra, Ghana**.
- **The African Union (AU)**, founded in 2002 to replace the Organisation of African Unity, promotes unity, development and peace across Africa.

:::callout{type=local-example}
The free movement protocol matters to Sierra Leone's traders: market women travel to Guinea and Liberia to buy and sell goods, and many Sierra Leoneans work in neighbouring countries. ECOMOG's role in ending the civil war is remembered as one of ECOWAS's most important achievements.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
ECOWAS is **not** a single-currency union. Most members still have their own currencies (the leone, the naira, the cedi), and the planned common currency has not yet been introduced. WAEMU members share the CFA franc, but they are only part of ECOWAS.
:::

## Key points

- **ECOWAS** (1975, Treaty of Lagos, Abuja headquarters) aims at a common market, free movement and regional peace; it has **12 members** since 2025.
- Achievements: **free movement protocol, trade liberalisation scheme, common external tariff, ECOMOG peacekeeping, EBID**.
- Problems: **instability, similar products, many currencies, poor transport, non-tariff barriers**.
- The **Mano River Union** (1973) links Sierra Leone, Liberia, Guinea and Côte d'Ivoire.
- **WAMZ** works towards a common currency; the **AfCFTA** aims at a continental free trade area.

## Quick check

1. When and by which treaty was ECOWAS formed?
2. Give two achievements of ECOWAS.
3. Which countries were the founding members of the Mano River Union?
4. What does the AfCFTA aim to create?

**Answers:** 1. In 1975, by the Treaty of Lagos. 2. Any two: free movement protocol, trade liberalisation scheme, common external tariff, ECOMOG peacekeeping, EBID financing, regional infrastructure. 3. Liberia and Sierra Leone (in 1973). 4. A single continental market for goods and services across Africa.
