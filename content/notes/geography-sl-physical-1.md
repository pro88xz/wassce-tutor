**Sierra Leone** is a country on the Atlantic coast of West Africa, lying between about **latitudes 7°N and 10°N** and **longitudes 10°W and 13°W**, with an area of about **71,740 km²**. It is bordered by **Guinea** to the north and east, **Liberia** to the south-east and the **Atlantic Ocean** to the west and south-west. Its **relief** rises from a low coastal plain of swamps and estuaries, through interior lowland plains and plateaux, to the **Loma Mountains**, where **Bintumani (1,945 m)** is the highest point. Its main rivers flow from north-east to south-west into the Atlantic. This lesson covers the location, relief regions and drainage of Sierra Leone.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the location, size and boundaries of Sierra Leone
- describe the main relief regions of Sierra Leone
- name the main mountains and highlands and their heights
- describe the drainage of Sierra Leone and name its main rivers
- explain the importance of the relief and rivers to people
- draw a sketch map of Sierra Leone with major features

## Relief regions

![A west to east cross-section of Sierra Leone: the Freetown Peninsula mountains, the coastal plain with swamps, the interior lowland plains with isolated hills, the plateau of the north and east and the Loma Mountains with Bintumani](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/sierra-leone-relief-section.svg)

*Figure 2. The relief regions of Sierra Leone, from west to east (simplified). WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Region | Description |
|---|---|
| **1. Coastal plain (coastal swamps)** | a low, flat belt along the coast; **mangrove swamps** along estuaries and creeks; sandy beaches and spits; **Sherbro Island** off the south coast |
| **2. Freetown Peninsula** | a block of mountains rising steeply from the sea, up to **Picket Hill (888 m)**; Freetown is built on its northern slopes |
| **3. Interior lowland plains** | gently rolling plains inland of the coastal plain, broken by isolated hills (**inselbergs**) such as the Kangari Hills; seasonally flooded grasslands called **bolilands** in the north |
| **4. Plateaux** | higher land, about **300–600 m**, in the **north and east**, deeply cut by rivers |
| **5. Mountains** | the **Loma Mountains**, with **Bintumani (1,945 m)**, the highest point in Sierra Leone, and the **Tingi Hills** in Kono |

:::callout{type=local-example}
The steep, forested slopes of the **Freetown Peninsula** catch heavy rain and feed the streams and the **Guma Valley dam** that supply Freetown's water. Building on these slopes has increased the danger of **landslides**, as the **Regent mudslide of August 2017** showed.
:::

## Drainage

Sierra Leone's main rivers rise in the highlands of Guinea or in the north-east of the country and flow roughly **from north-east to south-west**, almost **parallel** to one another, into the Atlantic Ocean.

| River | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Great Scarcies (Kolenten)** | forms part of the north-western border with Guinea |
| **Little Scarcies (Kaba)** | in the north-west |
| **Rokel (Seli)** | flows past Bumbuna and Magburaka to the **Sierra Leone River estuary** at Freetown; **Bumbuna dam** is on the Seli |
| **Jong (Taia)** | flows through the south-centre to the Sherbro area |
| **Sewa** | drains the centre and south; alluvial diamonds are mined along it and its tributaries |
| **Moa** | flows through the east past **Tiwai Island** |
| **Mano** | forms part of the border with **Liberia** |

Features of the drainage:

1. Rivers have **rapids and waterfalls** where they drop from the plateau to the plains. These block navigation but give sites for **hydroelectric power**.
2. Rivers are **full in the rainy season** and **low in the dry season**, following the rainfall.
3. Near the coast they form wide **estuaries** fringed with mangroves, such as the **Sierra Leone River** estuary, which gives Freetown one of the largest natural harbours in Africa.

## Importance of relief and drainage

1. Rivers provide **water**, **fish** and **transport** on their lower courses.
2. Waterfalls and rapids are sites for **hydroelectric power** (Bumbuna, Dodo).
3. Swamps and floodplains are used for **rice farming**.
4. River gravels contain **alluvial diamonds and gold**.
5. The **natural harbour** of Freetown supports the port and the city.
6. Mountains and forests attract **tourists** and protect **wildlife and water supply**.
7. Steep slopes and swamps make **road building** costly.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not say Sierra Leone's rivers flow north. Almost all flow **south-westwards** to the Atlantic, because the land rises towards the north-east.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Coastal plain**: the low, flat belt along Sierra Leone's coast, with mangrove swamps along estuaries and creeks.
- **Freetown Peninsula**: a block of mountains rising steeply from the sea, up to Picket Hill (888 m), with Freetown on its northern slopes.
- **Inselberg**: an isolated hill rising from a plain, such as the Kangari Hills.
- **Loma Mountains**: the mountains of the north-east of Sierra Leone, where Bintumani (1,945 m) is the highest peak.
:::

## Key points

- Sierra Leone lies between about **7°N–10°N** and **10°W–13°W**; area about **71,740 km²**; bordered by **Guinea** and **Liberia**.
- Relief: **coastal plain**, **Freetown Peninsula** (Picket Hill 888 m), **interior plains**, **plateaux**, **mountains** (Loma, Bintumani 1,945 m; Tingi Hills).
- Rivers flow **NE → SW**: **Great and Little Scarcies, Rokel (Seli), Jong, Sewa, Moa, Mano**.
- Rapids give **HEP** sites; estuaries give **harbours**; swamps grow **rice**.

## Quick check

1. Name the two countries that border Sierra Leone.
2. What is the highest point in Sierra Leone, and how high is it?
3. In which general direction do Sierra Leone's rivers flow?
4. On which river is the Bumbuna dam?
5. Give two ways in which rivers are important in Sierra Leone.

**Answers:**

1. Guinea and Liberia.
2. Bintumani, in the Loma Mountains, at 1,945 m.
3. From north-east to south-west.
4. The Seli (Rokel) River.
5. Any two: water supply; fishing; transport; hydroelectric power; rice farming on floodplains; alluvial diamonds; natural harbours.
