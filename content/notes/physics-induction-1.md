Move a magnet into a coil and a current appears from nowhere — no battery needed. Faraday's discovery of electromagnetic induction is how almost all the world's electricity is generated, from Bumbuna's turbines to a bicycle dynamo.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe experiments showing electromagnetic induction
- state Faraday's and Lenz's laws of electromagnetic induction
- use Fleming's right-hand rule to find the direction of an induced current
- state the factors affecting the size of the induced e.m.f.

## Electromagnetic induction

![A bar magnet being pushed into a coil connected to a galvanometer; the galvanometer deflects while the magnet moves](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/electromagnetic-induction.svg)

*Figure 1. Inducing a current by moving a magnet into a coil. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=definition}
**Electromagnetic induction** is the production of an **e.m.f.** (and a current, if the circuit is complete) in a conductor when the **magnetic flux** linking it **changes**.
:::

**Observations with a magnet and a coil connected to a galvanometer:**

| Action | Galvanometer |
|---|---|
| magnet pushed **into** the coil | deflects one way |
| magnet **held still** inside the coil | **no** deflection |
| magnet pulled **out** | deflects the **opposite** way |
| magnet moved **faster** | **larger** deflection |
| **stronger** magnet, or coil with **more turns** | larger deflection |

The same happens if the coil is moved instead of the magnet: what matters is **relative motion** — a **changing** magnetic field through the coil.

A straight wire **moved across** a magnetic field (cutting the field lines) also has an e.m.f. induced in it.

## Faraday's law

:::callout{type=definition}
**Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction:** the **magnitude** of the induced e.m.f. is **directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux** linking the circuit:

$$E = -N\dfrac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t}$$
:::

Here Φ is the magnetic flux (in **webers, Wb**; $\Phi = BA$ for a flux density $B$ through an area $A$ at right angles), and $N$ the number of turns.

### Factors affecting the induced e.m.f.

1. the **speed** of relative motion (rate of change of flux)
2. the **strength** of the magnetic field
3. the **number of turns** on the coil
4. the **area** of the coil

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**The flux through a coil of 200 turns changes from 0.004 Wb to 0.001 Wb in 0.1 s. Find the average induced e.m.f.**

$$E = N\dfrac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t} = 200 \times \dfrac{0.004 - 0.001}{0.1} = 200 \times 0.03 = 6\ \text{V}$$
:::

For a straight conductor of length $L$ moving at speed $v$ at right angles to a field $B$:

$$E = BLv$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A rod 0.5 m long moves at 4 m s⁻¹ at right angles to a magnetic field of 0.2 T. Find the e.m.f. induced across its ends.**

$$E = BLv = 0.2 \times 0.5 \times 4 = 0.4\ \text{V}$$
:::

## Lenz's law

:::callout{type=definition}
**Lenz's law:** the **direction** of the induced current is always such as to **oppose the change** producing it.
:::

When the N pole of a magnet is pushed **into** a coil, the induced current makes that end of the coil a **N** pole, which **repels** the approaching magnet. When the magnet is pulled **out**, that end becomes a **S** pole, attracting it back. Either way, you must do **work** against the opposing force — and that work becomes the electrical energy. Lenz's law is the **conservation of energy** in electromagnetism (the minus sign in Faraday's law).

## Fleming's right-hand rule

:::callout{type=definition}
**Fleming's right-hand rule** (the dynamo rule): hold the **thumb**, **first finger** and **second finger** of the **right hand** mutually at right angles. If the **thuMb** points in the direction of **Motion** of the conductor and the **First finger** along the **Field** (N → S), the **seCond finger** gives the direction of the induced **Current**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Left hand for motors** (force on a current), **right hand for generators** (induced current). A memory aid: "**G**enerators are **R**ight" (dynamo — right; motor — left).
:::

## Key points

- A changing magnetic flux through a circuit induces an e.m.f.; no change, no e.m.f.
- Faraday: e.m.f. ∝ rate of change of flux; $E = N\dfrac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t}$, $E = BLv$.
- Lenz: the induced current opposes the change causing it (conservation of energy).
- Fleming's right-hand rule gives the direction of the induced current.

## Quick check

1. What happens to the galvanometer when a magnet is held still inside a coil?
2. State Lenz's law.
3. Find the e.m.f. induced in a 0.4 m wire moving at 5 m s⁻¹ across a 0.1 T field.

**Answers:** 1. Nothing: there is no deflection because the flux is not changing. 2. The induced current flows in a direction that opposes the change producing it. 3. $E = 0.1 \times 0.4 \times 5 = 0.2$ V.
