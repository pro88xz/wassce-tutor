Ray diagrams show where an image is; the lens formula tells you exactly. With it you can find image distances, magnifications and the power of spectacle lenses in a few lines of working.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- use the lens formula $\dfrac{1}{u} + \dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f}$ with the "real is positive" convention
- calculate linear magnification
- define the power of a lens in dioptres
- determine the focal length of a converging lens experimentally

## The lens formula

$$\dfrac{1}{u} + \dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f}$$

where $u$ = object distance, $v$ = image distance, $f$ = focal length (all measured from the optical centre).

**Sign convention ("real is positive"):**

- real objects and real images: **positive** distances
- virtual images: **negative** distances
- converging lens: $f$ **positive**; diverging lens: $f$ **negative**

## Magnification

$$m = \dfrac{\text{image height}}{\text{object height}} = \dfrac{v}{u}$$

(using the sizes of $u$ and $v$; $m > 1$ means magnified).

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**An object 4 cm tall stands 30 cm from a converging lens of focal length 20 cm. Find the position, nature and size of the image.**

$$\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{20} - \dfrac{1}{30} = \dfrac{3 - 2}{60} = \dfrac{1}{60} \Rightarrow v = 60\ \text{cm}$$

$v$ positive: **real** (and inverted), 60 cm on the other side. $m = \dfrac{60}{30} = 2$, so the image is **8 cm** tall. (Object between F and 2F → magnified real image: a projector.)
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**An object is 8 cm from a converging lens of focal length 12 cm. Find the image.**

$$\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{12} - \dfrac{1}{8} = \dfrac{2 - 3}{24} = -\dfrac{1}{24} \Rightarrow v = -24\ \text{cm}$$

$v$ negative: **virtual** (and upright), 24 cm from the lens on the **same side** as the object. $m = \dfrac{24}{8} = 3$. This is a magnifying glass.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A diverging lens of focal length 15 cm forms an image of an object placed 30 cm away. Find the image position and magnification.**

$f = -15$ cm:

$$\dfrac{1}{v} = -\dfrac{1}{15} - \dfrac{1}{30} = -\dfrac{3}{30} \Rightarrow v = -10\ \text{cm}$$

Virtual, upright, 10 cm from the lens on the object side; $m = \dfrac{10}{30} = \dfrac{1}{3}$.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not forget the sign of $f$ for a **diverging** lens. Using $f = +15$ for a concave lens gives a real image, which a diverging lens can never produce.
:::

## Power of a lens

:::callout{type=definition}
The **power** of a lens is the reciprocal of its focal length **in metres**:

$$P = \dfrac{1}{f}\ (\text{f in m})$$

The unit is the **dioptre (D)**. Converging lenses have **positive** power; diverging lenses **negative** power.
:::

- A converging lens of focal length 25 cm has power $\dfrac{1}{0.25} = +4$ D.
- A diverging lens of focal length 50 cm has power $-\dfrac{1}{0.5} = -2$ D.

Opticians prescribe spectacles in dioptres: "−2.0 D" means a diverging lens for short sight.

## Determining the focal length of a converging lens

### Method 1: plotting $1/v$ against $1/u$

1. Place an illuminated object (cross-wire), the lens and a screen on a metre rule or optical bench.
2. For a chosen object distance $u$ (greater than $f$), move the screen until a **sharp image** forms; measure $v$.
3. Repeat for at least five values of $u$.
4. Plot $\dfrac{1}{v}$ against $\dfrac{1}{u}$: a straight line with gradient −1 whose intercepts on both axes equal $\dfrac{1}{f}$.

### Method 2: plotting $uv$ against $u + v$

From the lens formula, $uv = f(u + v)$. A graph of $uv$ against $(u + v)$ is a straight line through the origin with **gradient $f$**.

### Method 3: displacement (when $u = v$)

When the image is the same size as the object, $u = v = 2f$.

**Precautions:** the object, lens and screen should be centred on the same axis; find the sharp image by moving the screen back and forth; measure from the centre of the lens; work in a darkened room.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A student finds $u = 30$ cm and $v = 15$ cm for a lens. What is its focal length and power?**

$$\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{30} + \dfrac{1}{15} = \dfrac{3}{30} \Rightarrow f = 10\ \text{cm} = 0.10\ \text{m}, \quad P = +10\ \text{D}$$
:::

## Key points

- $\dfrac{1}{u} + \dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f}$; real distances positive, virtual negative; diverging $f$ negative.
- $m = v/u$.
- Power $P = 1/f$ (f in metres), in dioptres; converging positive, diverging negative.
- Focal length by experiment: $1/v$ against $1/u$, or $uv$ against $u + v$.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
**Lens-formula calculations** (position, nature and magnification of the image) appear every year — state whether the image is real or virtual from the sign of $v$. The **practical** often asks you to determine $f$ with an optical bench and plot $1/v$ against $1/u$ or $uv$ against $u + v$. Define the **dioptre**.
:::

## Quick check

1. A converging lens has focal length 20 cm. What is its power?
2. An object is placed 40 cm from a converging lens of focal length 20 cm. Where is the image?
3. A lens has power −4 D. What kind of lens is it and what is its focal length?

**Answers:** 1. $+5$ D. 2. $\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{20} - \dfrac{1}{40} = \dfrac{1}{40}$, so $v = 40$ cm (real, same size, at 2F). 3. A diverging lens of focal length 25 cm.
