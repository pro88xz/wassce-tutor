Most electrical accidents are avoidable. This lesson covers the dangers of electricity, first aid for electric shock, two-way switching, and energy-saving choices that cut bills.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the dangers of electricity and the precautions that prevent them
- describe first aid for a person receiving an electric shock
- explain two-way switching for stair lighting
- suggest ways of saving electrical energy at home

## Dangers of electricity

| Hazard | Danger |
|---|---|
| **damaged insulation** or bare wires | contact with the live wire gives a shock |
| **overloaded sockets** (many adaptors in one socket) | too much current overheats the wiring: fire |
| **wet hands, wet floors, water near sockets** | water lowers the body's resistance, so a larger, more dangerous current flows |
| **wrong fuse rating** or a nail in place of a fuse | no protection; cables overheat |
| **illegal connections** and loose joints | sparking, fires and electrocution |
| **long cables under carpets** | damage goes unseen; overheating |
| **flying kites near overhead lines** / climbing poles | contact with high-voltage lines can kill |

A current of only about **50 mA through the heart** can be fatal.

## Precautions

1. Use correctly rated **fuses** and good-quality, certified cables and plugs.
2. **Do not overload** sockets; avoid chains of adaptors.
3. Keep electrical appliances away from **water**; never touch switches with wet hands.
4. **Switch off at the mains** before repairing any wiring or changing a lamp holder.
5. Replace **damaged flexes** and cracked plugs at once.
6. Ensure appliances with metal cases are **earthed**.
7. Use **RCDs** (residual current devices) where possible.
8. Never touch fallen electrical lines; report them.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Fires in crowded markets and homes in Freetown have been blamed on overloaded extension boards and illegal connections. A licensed electrician, correct fuses and one plug per socket are cheap protection compared with the cost of a fire.
:::

## First aid for electric shock

1. **Do not touch** the person while they are still in contact with the supply — you would get a shock too.
2. **Switch off** the supply at the mains or pull out the plug.
3. If you cannot switch off, **push the person clear** with a **dry insulator** such as a wooden broom handle, a dry plank or a rubber mat — while standing on something dry and insulating.
4. **Call for medical help.**
5. If the person is not breathing, give **mouth-to-mouth resuscitation** and, if there is no pulse, **chest compressions (CPR)** if trained.
6. Treat burns by cooling with clean water once the person is clear of the supply.

## Two-way switching

Stairs and long corridors need a light that can be switched **on or off from either end**. This uses two **two-way switches**, each with three terminals, connected by two wires (strappers).

- Each switch can connect its common terminal to either of its two other terminals.
- The lamp lights only when **both switches connect to the same strapper wire**; changing **either** switch breaks or completes the circuit.

So a person at the bottom of the stairs can switch the light on, and switch it off again at the top.

## Saving electrical energy

| Action | Why it saves energy |
|---|---|
| replace filament bulbs with **LED bulbs** | LEDs use about one-sixth of the power for the same light |
| **switch off** lights, fans and TVs when not needed | standby and unused appliances still use energy |
| choose appliances with a good **energy rating** | they do the same job with less power |
| keep fridge doors closed and seals clean | less work for the compressor |
| iron clothes in **batches** | the iron heats up only once |
| use **natural light** and ventilation | less need for lamps and fans |
| install **solar panels** where possible | free, renewable electricity |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A home replaces six 60 W filament bulbs with six 10 W LED bulbs, each used 5 hours a day. If a unit costs NLe 2.50, how much is saved in 30 days?**

Power saved $= 6 \times (60 - 10) = 300$ W $= 0.3$ kW.

Energy saved $= 0.3 \times 5 \times 30 = 45$ kWh. Money saved $= 45 \times 2.50 = $ **NLe 112.50**.
:::

## Key points

- Water, damaged insulation, overloading and wrong fuses are the main dangers.
- In a shock: switch off first, or push the person clear with a dry insulator; get help.
- Two-way switches let one lamp be controlled from two places.
- LED bulbs, switching off and efficient appliances reduce energy use and bills.

1. Why should you never touch a switch with wet hands?
2. What is the first thing to do if someone is receiving an electric shock?
3. What is the purpose of two-way switching on a staircase?

**Answers:** 1. Water lowers the resistance of the skin, so a dangerous current could flow through you. 2. Switch off the supply (or separate the person from it with a dry insulator) without touching them. 3. To allow the light to be switched on or off from either the top or the bottom of the stairs.
