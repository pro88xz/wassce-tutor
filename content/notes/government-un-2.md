In 2000, the blue helmets of UN peacekeepers were a common sight across Sierra Leone. At its peak the UN mission there had more than 17,000 troops, one of the largest in the world. Sierra Leone also belongs to a very different international body: the **Commonwealth**, a family of countries mostly linked by a history of British rule. This lesson explains the UN's role in Sierra Leone and the organisation, aims and value of the Commonwealth.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the role of the UN in Sierra Leone during and after the civil war
- explain how UN agencies support development in Sierra Leone
- explain the origin, membership and aims of the Commonwealth
- describe the organs and activities of the Commonwealth
- explain the benefits of Commonwealth membership to Sierra Leone

## The UN in Sierra Leone

![A timeline: 1961 Sierra Leone joins the UN; 1998 UNOMSIL; 1999 UNAMSIL peacekeepers; 2002 Special Court; 2006 UNIOSIL; 2008 UNIPSIL; 2014 Ebola response; 2024 Security Council seat](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/un-in-sl.svg)

*Figure 1. The United Nations in Sierra Leone. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Mission or body | Period | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **UNOMSIL** (UN Observer Mission) | 1998–1999 | Monitored security and the disarmament of fighters |
| **UNAMSIL** (UN Mission in Sierra Leone) | 1999–2005 | Peacekeeping; supported **disarmament** of more than 70,000 fighters, the 2002 elections and the extension of government authority |
| **Special Court for Sierra Leone** | 2002–2013 | Set up by agreement between the UN and the government to try those most responsible for war crimes |
| **UNIOSIL** (Integrated Office) | 2006–2008 | Helped build institutions after peacekeepers left |
| **UNIPSIL** (Integrated Peacebuilding Office) | 2008–2014 | Supported elections, reconciliation and reform |
| **UN Peacebuilding Commission** | from 2006 | Sierra Leone was one of the first countries on its agenda |

**UN agencies in Sierra Leone:**

- **WHO**: health systems, immunisation and the **Ebola** response (2014–2016).
- **UNICEF**: child health, nutrition, education and child protection.
- **WFP**: school feeding and food aid.
- **UNDP**: governance, elections support and development.
- **UNHCR**: refugees, especially during the war.
- **FAO**: agriculture and food security.

## The Commonwealth

:::callout{type=definition}
The **Commonwealth of Nations** is a **voluntary association of independent sovereign states**, most of them former British colonies, that cooperate on the basis of shared values such as democracy, human rights and the rule of law.
:::

**Origin and membership:**

- It grew out of the **British Empire**. The **London Declaration (1949)** created the modern Commonwealth, allowing republics such as India to remain members.
- It has **56** members in Africa, Asia, the Americas, Europe and the Pacific. Some, such as **Mozambique** and **Rwanda**, were never British colonies.
- **Sierra Leone** joined at independence in **1961**.
- The **Head of the Commonwealth** is the British monarch, **King Charles III** (since 2022), a symbolic role.

**Organs and activities:**

| Organ or activity | Role |
|---|---|
| **Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting (CHOGM)** | Leaders meet about every two years to set policy |
| **Commonwealth Secretariat** (London, 1965) | Runs programmes; headed by the **Secretary-General** |
| **Commonwealth Ministerial Action Group (CMAG)** | Deals with serious violations of Commonwealth values; can recommend suspension |
| **Commonwealth Fund for Technical Co-operation** | Provides experts and technical assistance |
| **Commonwealth Games** | Sporting competition every four years |
| **Commonwealth Scholarships** | Higher education opportunities in member countries |

**Values:** set out in the **Harare Declaration (1991)** and the **Commonwealth Charter (2013)**: democracy, human rights, the rule of law, good governance, freedom of expression and sustainable development.

## Benefits to Sierra Leone

1. **Technical assistance** and experts for government and the public service.
2. **Scholarships** and training for Sierra Leoneans.
3. **Election observation** and support for democracy.
4. **Trade and investment** links, and a common language and legal tradition.
5. **Diplomatic voice** for a small state in a large group.
6. **Sport and cultural exchange** through the Commonwealth Games.

**Criticisms:** the Commonwealth has **no binding power**, gives **limited aid**, and is sometimes seen as a **relic of empire**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
After the **AFRC coup** of **May 1997**, the Commonwealth **suspended Sierra Leone's military regime** from its councils and supported efforts to restore the elected government of President Kabbah, which returned in **1998**. This showed the Commonwealth using membership to defend democratic values.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The Commonwealth is **not** a government over its members, and the British monarch has **no political power** over member states. Members are fully sovereign and can leave at any time; The Gambia, for example, left in 2013 and rejoined in 2018.
:::

## Key points

- UN in Sierra Leone: **UNOMSIL (1998–99), UNAMSIL (1999–2005), UNIOSIL (2006–08), UNIPSIL (2008–14)**, the **Special Court**, and agencies such as **WHO, UNICEF, WFP, UNDP**.
- The **Commonwealth** is a voluntary association of **56** sovereign states; modern form from the **London Declaration (1949)**; **Secretariat in London (1965)**; Sierra Leone joined in **1961**.
- Values: **Harare Declaration (1991)** and **Commonwealth Charter (2013)**.
- Benefits: **technical help, scholarships, election support, trade links, diplomatic voice**.

## Quick check

1. What was UNAMSIL?
2. When did Sierra Leone join the Commonwealth?
3. Where is the Commonwealth Secretariat?
4. State two benefits of Commonwealth membership to Sierra Leone.

**Answers:**

1. The United Nations Mission in Sierra Leone (1999–2005), a peacekeeping mission that supported disarmament, elections and the restoration of government authority.
2. In 1961, at independence.
3. In London.
4. Any two: technical assistance; scholarships and training; election observation; trade and investment links; a diplomatic voice; sporting and cultural exchange.
