The **books of original entry** (books of prime entry, or subsidiary books) are where transactions are **first recorded**, in date order, from source documents, before they are posted to the ledger. They are the **sales day book**, **purchases day book**, **returns inwards book**, **returns outwards book**, **cash book**, **petty cash book** and **general journal**. Grouping similar transactions in one book saves time: instead of posting every invoice to the sales account, only the **monthly total** is posted. This lesson explains each book, how to write it up, and how its entries are posted to the ledger.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the meaning and advantages of books of original entry
- name the book of original entry for each type of transaction
- write up the sales, purchases and returns day books and post them to the ledger
- write journal entries with narrations for opening entries, assets bought on credit and corrections
- explain why the journal is needed

**Advantages of books of original entry:**

1. Transactions are recorded in **date order** in one place.
2. The ledger is kept **less crowded**: only **totals** are posted to the sales, purchases and returns accounts.
3. **Work can be divided** among several clerks.
4. Errors are easier to **trace**.
5. They provide a **summary** of each type of transaction for the period.

## The day books

The day books record **credit** transactions in **goods for resale** only. Cash sales and cash purchases go to the **cash book**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Write up the sales day book of Amie's Store for May 2026 and post it to the ledger.**

- 4 May: sold goods on credit to Kamara, invoice 101, NLe 6,000.
- 12 May: sold goods on credit to Bah, invoice 102, NLe 4,500.
- 25 May: sold goods on credit to Kamara, invoice 103, NLe 2,500.

**Sales day book**

| May | Customer (invoice) | NLe |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Kamara (101) | 6,000 |
| 12 | Bah (102) | 4,500 |
| 25 | Kamara (103) | 2,500 |
| 31 | **Total to sales account** | **13,000** |

**Posting:**

- Each customer's account is **debited** individually: Kamara 6,000 and 2,500; Bah 4,500.
- The **sales account** is **credited** once with the total, NLe 13,000.

Debits $= 6\,000 + 4\,500 + 2\,500 = 13\,000$ = credit 13,000 ✓.
:::

| Book | Each individual item posted to | Total posted to |
|---|---|---|
| **Sales day book** | **debit** each customer (sales ledger) | **credit** sales account |
| **Purchases day book** | **credit** each supplier (purchases ledger) | **debit** purchases account |
| **Returns inwards book** | **credit** each customer | **debit** returns inwards account |
| **Returns outwards book** | **debit** each supplier | **credit** returns outwards account |

## The general journal

The **journal** records transactions that do not belong in any other book of original entry:

1. **Opening entries** when a business starts or new books are opened.
2. **Purchase or sale of non-current assets on credit.**
3. **Correction of errors.**
4. **Transfers** between accounts, and year-end adjustments.
5. **Bad debts written off.**
6. **Goods taken by the owner** for private use.

Each journal entry shows the account debited (first), the account credited (indented), the amounts and a short **narration** explaining the entry.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Show the journal entries for: (a) 1 July, bought a delivery van on credit from Freetown Motors for NLe 60,000; (b) 20 July, wrote off Bah's debt of NLe 1,500 as bad.**

| Date | Particulars | Dr NLe | Cr NLe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jul 1 | Motor van | 60,000 | |
| | Freetown Motors | | 60,000 |
| | *Van bought on credit* | | |
| Jul 20 | Bad debts | 1,500 | |
| | Bah | | 1,500 |
| | *Bah's debt written off as bad* | | |
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Opening journal entry. On 1 January a business has: premises NLe 100,000, furniture NLe 15,000, inventory NLe 20,000, cash at bank NLe 9,000, and owes Sesay NLe 4,000. Find the capital and show the opening entry.**

Capital $= (100\,000 + 15\,000 + 20\,000 + 9\,000) - 4\,000 = 140\,000$

| Particulars | Dr NLe | Cr NLe |
|---|---|---|
| Premises | 100,000 | |
| Furniture | 15,000 | |
| Inventory | 20,000 | |
| Bank | 9,000 | |
| Sesay | | 4,000 |
| Capital | | 140,000 |
| | **144,000** | **144,000** |
| *Assets, liability and capital at the start* | | |
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A **van bought on credit** is **not** entered in the purchases day book: the day books are for **goods for resale** only. Non-current assets bought on credit are recorded in the **general journal**.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Sales day book**: the book of original entry for credit sales; each customer is debited in the sales ledger.
- **Purchases day book**: the book of original entry for credit purchases; each supplier is credited in the purchases ledger.
- **Returns inwards book**: the book of original entry for goods returned by customers.
- **Returns outwards book**: the book of original entry for goods returned to suppliers.
- **General journal**: the book of original entry for opening entries, credit purchases and sales of non-current assets, correction of errors, transfers and other items not in the day books.
:::

## Key points

- Books of original entry: **sales, purchases, returns inwards, returns outwards day books; cash book; petty cash book; general journal**.
- Day books record **credit** dealings in **goods for resale**; only their **totals** go to the sales, purchases and returns accounts.
- Individual items go to the **personal accounts** in the sales and purchases ledgers.
- The **journal** records opening entries, assets bought on credit, corrections, transfers and bad debts, each with a **narration**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **write up day books from a list of transactions and post them to the ledger**, to **prepare journal entries with narrations**, and to **state the uses of the journal**. Remember: credit transactions in goods go to day books; cash transactions go to the cash book.
:::

## Quick check

1. Name four books of original entry.
2. Which account receives the total of the sales day book, and on which side?
3. Where is a computer bought on credit first recorded?
4. Give three uses of the general journal.
5. Credit sales in a month were NLe 3,000, 5,500 and 2,500. What is credited to the sales account?

**Answers:**

1. Any four: sales day book; purchases day book; returns inwards book; returns outwards book; cash book; petty cash book; general journal.
2. The sales account, on the credit side.
3. In the general journal.
4. Any three: opening entries; non-current assets bought or sold on credit; correction of errors; transfers; bad debts written off; goods taken for private use.
5. $3\,000 + 5\,500 + 2\,500 = 11\,000$: NLe 11,000.
