Without commerce, every family would have to grow its own food, sew its own clothes and make its own tools. Commerce lets people specialise and still get everything they need. This lesson explains the functions and importance of commerce, how it developed, and the problems it faces in West Africa today.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the functions of commerce
- explain the importance of commerce to a country such as Sierra Leone
- describe how commerce developed from barter to modern e-commerce
- explain the factors that promote commercial activity and the problems facing commerce in West Africa

## Functions of commerce

1. **Links producers and consumers**: goods made in factories and farms reach the people who need them.
2. **Removes the hindrances to trade**: place (transport), time (warehousing), risk (insurance), finance (banking), ignorance (advertising) and distance between traders (communication).
3. **Makes specialisation possible**: people can concentrate on one job because commerce lets them exchange their output for everything else.
4. **Creates utility**: commerce adds **place utility** (goods where they are wanted), **time utility** (goods when they are wanted) and **possession utility** (goods in the hands of those who want them).
5. **Provides information**: advertising and market information tell buyers what is available and tell producers what buyers want.
6. **Finances production and trade**: banks and the capital market supply funds.

## Why commerce is important

| Importance | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Raises living standards** | People can obtain a wide variety of goods, including those not produced locally |
| **Creates employment** | Traders, drivers, bankers, insurers, warehouse staff, advertisers, mobile money agents |
| **Encourages production** | A wide market makes it worthwhile for farmers and firms to produce more |
| **Earns foreign exchange** | Export trade brings in foreign currency to pay for imports |
| **Provides government revenue** | Taxes on trade: import and export duties, GST, licence fees |
| **Promotes international relations** | Trade links countries and encourages cooperation, for example within ECOWAS |
| **Stabilises prices** | Warehousing and trade move goods from areas of surplus to areas of shortage |
| **Spreads ideas and technology** | New products and methods arrive through trade |

## How commerce developed

| Stage | Features |
|---|---|
| **Self-sufficiency** | Each family produced what it consumed; little exchange |
| **Barter** | Direct exchange of goods; limited by the double coincidence of wants |
| **Money and markets** | Money made exchange easy; regular markets developed |
| **Specialist traders** | Wholesalers, retailers and transporters emerged |
| **Aids to trade** | Banks, insurance companies, ports and postal services grew with trade |
| **Modern commerce** | Telecommunications, containers, supermarkets and the stock exchange |
| **E-commerce** | Buying and selling by phone and internet, paid with mobile money or cards |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's commerce shows every stage side by side: barter still happens in some villages, weekly *lumo* markets link farmers with traders, wholesalers and supermarkets serve Freetown, and mobile money services let a trader in Kailahun pay a supplier in Freetown in seconds.
:::

## Factors that promote commerce

1. **Good transport and communication** networks.
2. A **stable currency** and a sound banking system.
3. **Peace and political stability**, which encourage traders to invest.
4. **Large markets**, due to population and incomes.
5. **Government support**: good laws, low barriers, infrastructure, credit schemes.
6. **Availability of capital** and **entrepreneurial skill**.
7. **Education**, so traders can keep records and use modern methods.

## Problems facing commerce in West Africa

1. **Poor roads and transport**, especially feeder roads in rural areas.
2. **Unreliable electricity**, which raises the costs of storage, cold rooms and communication.
3. **Lack of capital and credit** for small traders.
4. **Inadequate storage**, causing waste of perishable goods.
5. **Inflation and an unstable exchange rate**, which make pricing and importing difficult.
6. **Illiteracy and poor record keeping** among small traders.
7. **Smuggling and unfair competition** from dumped imports.
8. **Bureaucracy and corruption** at ports and borders.
9. **Insecurity** and **multiple taxes** and levies.

**Solutions:** better roads and power, credit schemes and microfinance, training in record keeping, simpler customs procedures, stable economic policies and the spread of mobile money and digital services.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
"Commerce" questions often ask about **importance** and **functions** separately. Functions are what commerce **does** (links producers and consumers, removes hindrances); importance is what it **achieves** for the country (jobs, revenue, higher living standards). Keep them apart.
:::

## Key points

- Commerce **links producers and consumers**, removes the **hindrances to trade** and creates **place, time and possession utility**.
- It raises **living standards**, creates **jobs**, earns **foreign exchange** and **government revenue**.
- Commerce developed from **self-sufficiency and barter** to **money, specialist traders, aids to trade and e-commerce**.
- In West Africa it is held back by **poor infrastructure, lack of credit, poor storage, inflation and red tape**.
