A thermometer with no scale marked on it is useless — or is it? With two fixed points and a little proportion, you can calibrate any thermometer, whatever its thermometric property. That calculation is one of WASSCE's favourites.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- calibrate a thermometer from its fixed points and calculate unknown temperatures
- describe the thermocouple, resistance thermometer and constant-volume gas thermometer
- explain how a maximum and minimum (Six's) thermometer works
- choose a suitable thermometer for a given job

## Calibrating a thermometer

If a thermometric property $X$ (a length, resistance, pressure…) has value $X_0$ at the ice point and $X_{100}$ at the steam point, then the temperature θ corresponding to a value $X_\theta$ is

$$\theta = \dfrac{X_\theta - X_0}{X_{100} - X_0} \times 100\ °\text{C}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**The mercury thread of an unmarked thermometer is 2.0 cm long in melting ice and 22.0 cm long in steam. What is the temperature when the thread is 9.0 cm long?**

$$\theta = \dfrac{9.0 - 2.0}{22.0 - 2.0} \times 100 = \dfrac{7.0}{20.0} \times 100 = 35\ °\text{C}$$
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A platinum resistance thermometer has resistance 4.00 Ω at 0 °C and 5.50 Ω at 100 °C. Find the temperature when its resistance is 4.90 Ω.**

$$\theta = \dfrac{4.90 - 4.00}{5.50 - 4.00} \times 100 = \dfrac{0.90}{1.50} \times 100 = 60\ °\text{C}$$
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A faulty thermometer reads 2 °C in melting ice and 98 °C in steam. What is the true temperature when it reads 50 °C?**

Its scale has 96 divisions between the fixed points instead of 100.

$$\theta = \dfrac{50 - 2}{98 - 2} \times 100 = \dfrac{48}{96} \times 100 = 50\ °\text{C}$$

(At other readings the error shows: a reading of 26 °C means a true temperature of $\dfrac{24}{96} \times 100 = 25$ °C.)
:::

## Other thermometers

### Thermocouple

Two wires of **different metals** (for example copper and iron) are joined at two junctions. When the junctions are at **different temperatures**, an **e.m.f.** is produced, measured by a millivoltmeter. One junction is kept in melting ice; the other is placed where the temperature is wanted.

**Advantages:** very **wide range** (up to about 1500 °C); **small** junction, so it measures the temperature at a point and **responds quickly**; its output is electrical, so it can be recorded remotely. Used in furnaces and engines.

### Platinum resistance thermometer

The **resistance** of a platinum wire increases with temperature. It is very **accurate** over a wide range (about −200 °C to 1200 °C) but **slow** to respond and bulky. Used as a standard in laboratories.

### Constant-volume gas thermometer

The **pressure** of a fixed mass of gas at constant volume rises with temperature. It is very **accurate** and is used to **calibrate** other thermometers, but it is bulky and slow.

## The maximum and minimum (Six's) thermometer

It records the highest and lowest temperatures reached since it was last reset — useful in weather stations and greenhouses.

- A U-shaped tube contains **alcohol** (the thermometric liquid) in the bulbs and a thread of **mercury** in the bend.
- When the temperature rises, the alcohol in the main bulb expands and pushes the mercury, which pushes a small **steel index** up one side: the index stays at the **maximum**.
- When the temperature falls, the alcohol contracts, and the mercury moves the other way, pushing a second index up the other side: it records the **minimum**.
- The indexes are reset with a **magnet**.

## Choosing a thermometer

| Job | Suitable thermometer | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Body temperature | clinical (or digital) thermometer | short range, accurate to 0.1 °C, keeps its reading |
| Very cold places | alcohol-in-glass | alcohol freezes only at −115 °C |
| Furnace or engine | thermocouple | wide range, small, quick |
| Highest and lowest temperature of the day | Six's thermometer | records both automatically |
| Calibrating other thermometers | constant-volume gas thermometer | very accurate |
| Rapidly changing temperatures | thermocouple | very small heat capacity, fast response |

## Key points

- $\theta = \dfrac{X_\theta - X_0}{X_{100} - X_0} \times 100\ °\text{C}$ for any thermometric property $X$.
- Thermocouple: e.m.f., wide range, fast. Resistance thermometer: accurate, slow. Gas thermometer: the standard.
- Six's thermometer records maximum and minimum temperatures with steel indexes.

## Quick check

1. A thread is 4 cm at 0 °C and 24 cm at 100 °C. What temperature does 14 cm represent?
2. Give one advantage of a thermocouple.
3. Which liquid shows the temperature in Six's thermometer?

**Answers:** 1. $\dfrac{10}{20} \times 100 = 50$ °C. 2. For example: a very wide range, or a quick response, or it measures the temperature at a point. 3. Alcohol (the mercury only moves the indexes).
