The **founding of Freetown** began in **May 1787**, when about **400 settlers**, mostly the **"Black Poor" of London**, landed on the Sierra Leone peninsula to found the **Province of Freedom**, a free settlement planned by the abolitionist **Granville Sharp**. Land was obtained from the Koya Temne ruler **King Tom**, and the grant was confirmed by **King Naimbana** in **1788**. The settlement, called **Granville Town**, suffered from **disease, hunger, desertion and conflict with the Temne**, and was **burned in 1789**. It was revived in **1791** by the new **Sierra Leone Company**. This lesson explains **why and how the Province of Freedom was founded**, its problems, and why it failed.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain who the Black Poor were and why they were sent to Sierra Leone
- describe the role of Granville Sharp and other abolitionists
- describe the founding of the Province of Freedom in 1787

- explain the problems that led to the failure of Granville Town
- describe the formation and aims of the Sierra Leone Company

## The Black Poor of London

After the **American War of Independence (1775–1783)**, many Black people who had served or escaped to the British side ended up in **London**. With others already there, such as discharged sailors and formerly enslaved servants, they formed a group known as the **Black Poor**. Many were **unemployed, homeless and destitute**.

In **1786** the **Committee for the Relief of the Black Poor**, led by philanthropists such as **Jonas Hanway**, gave them food and help. The naturalist **Henry Smeathman**, who had lived on the Banana Islands, proposed settling them in **Sierra Leone**, and the British government agreed to pay for the voyage.

## Granville Sharp's Province of Freedom

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figure"}
**Granville Sharp** was a leading **abolitionist**, famous for the **Somerset case (1772)**.
:::

He planned a **free, self-governing settlement** in Sierra Leone, the **Province of Freedom**, where:

1. settlers would govern themselves through elected leaders (a system he called **frankpledge**);
2. there would be **no slavery**;
3. settlers would farm and trade, showing that free Africans could prosper;
4. the settlement would spread **Christianity** and **legitimate trade**.

## The landing of 1787

1. The settlers sailed from England in early **1787**, escorted by the naval ship **HMS Nautilus** under **Captain Thomas Boulden Thompson**.
2. About **400 settlers** reached the Sierra Leone River in **May 1787**. They included Black men and women and a smaller number of **white women**, many of them wives of Black settlers.
3. Thompson obtained land along the coast from **King Tom**, a Koya Temne sub-chief, in exchange for goods.
4. The settlers built **Granville Town**, named after Sharp, on the hillside near the present site of Freetown.

:::callout{type=definition}
The **Province of Freedom** (1787–1789) was the first settlement of free Black people on the Sierra Leone peninsula, planned by **Granville Sharp** and founded by the **Black Poor** from London. Its town was **Granville Town**.
:::

## The land question

King Tom was only a **sub-chief**; the overlord of the Koya Temne was **King Naimbana**. In **1788** Naimbana signed a new agreement **confirming the grant** of land to the settlers.

But the two sides understood the agreements differently:

| Settlers' view | Temne view |
|---|---|
| the land had been **bought** and now **belonged** to the settlers | the settlers were **strangers** given the **right to use** the land, under the authority and protection of the Temne rulers, who expected continued gifts and respect |

This difference caused **land disputes** for many years.

## Why Granville Town failed

1. **Arrival in the rainy season:** settlers arrived as the **rains** began and could not build or plant properly.
2. **Disease:** **fever and dysentery** killed many; about a third of the settlers died within months.
3. **Lack of farming skills and supplies:** many were town people; food ran short.
4. **Desertion:** some left to work for **slave traders**, including at Bunce Island, and some were even sold into slavery.
5. **Poor leadership and quarrels** among the settlers.
6. **Conflict with the Temne:** in **1789**, after a British naval captain burned a Temne town in a quarrel, **King Jimmy**, King Tom's successor, retaliated by **burning Granville Town** (December 1789).

## The Sierra Leone Company

To save the settlement, Sharp and his allies formed the **St George's Bay Company** (1790), which became the **Sierra Leone Company** by Act of Parliament in **1791**.

| Leaders | Aims |
|---|---|
| **Henry Thornton** (chairman), **William Wilberforce**, **Granville Sharp**, **Thomas Clarkson** and other abolitionists | to trade in **legitimate goods** instead of slaves; to spread **Christianity and Western education**; to show that free Africans could build a successful colony; to help **end the slave trade**; and to make a **profit** for shareholders |

In **1791** the Company sent **Alexander Falconbridge** to gather the surviving settlers, who rebuilt a settlement at **Fourah Bay**. The Company's real new beginning came with the arrival of the **Nova Scotians in 1792**, studied in the next lesson.

:::callout{type=local-example}
The names of places in Freetown still recall its early history: **King Tom**, **King Jimmy** (the market and wharf), **Naimbana Street** and **Kroo Town** carry memories of the Temne rulers who granted land and of the peoples who came to live in the town.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not confuse the **Province of Freedom (1787)** with the founding of **Freetown (1792)**. The 1787 settlement of the Black Poor, Granville Town, failed in 1789; **Freetown** was founded by the **Nova Scotians** under the **Sierra Leone Company** in **1792**.
:::

## Key points

- The **Black Poor** of London were helped by the **Committee for the Relief of the Black Poor (1786)**.
- **Granville Sharp** planned the **Province of Freedom**, founded in **May 1787** by about **400 settlers**.
- Land from **King Tom (1787)**, confirmed by **King Naimbana (1788)**; Temne saw settlers as **strangers**, not owners.
- **Granville Town** failed: **rains, disease, lack of skills, desertion, quarrels**, and was **burned in 1789**.
- The **Sierra Leone Company (1791)**: **Thornton, Wilberforce, Sharp, Clarkson**; aims of legitimate trade, Christianity and profit.
