Honey pours slowly; water pours quickly. A steel ball dropped into a tall tube of engine oil soon falls at a steady speed. Both are the result of viscosity, the friction inside a fluid.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define viscosity and compare viscous and non-viscous fluids
- explain terminal velocity for a body falling through a fluid
- describe an experiment to compare the viscosities of liquids
- state the effect of temperature on viscosity and give applications

## Viscosity

:::callout{type=definition}
**Viscosity** is the **internal friction** of a fluid: the property that **opposes the relative motion between its layers**. A fluid with high viscosity flows slowly; one with low viscosity flows easily.
:::

| High viscosity | Low viscosity |
|---|---|
| honey, engine oil, glycerine, tar, palm oil (cool) | water, petrol, kerosene, alcohol, air |

When a liquid flows through a pipe, the layer touching the wall is almost still, and each layer further in moves a little faster. Viscosity is the "drag" between these layers.

## Forces on a body falling through a fluid

A steel ball dropped into a tall jar of glycerine is acted on by three forces:

1. its **weight** $W$, downwards
2. the **upthrust** $U$, upwards (Archimedes' principle)
3. the **viscous drag** $F$, upwards, which **increases with speed**

At first the ball accelerates because $W > U + F$. As its speed grows, $F$ grows, until

$$W = U + F$$

The resultant force is then **zero** and the ball falls at a constant speed called its **terminal velocity**.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Terminal velocity** is the constant velocity reached by a body falling through a fluid when the **viscous drag plus upthrust equals its weight**, so that the resultant force is zero.
:::

The velocity–time graph rises steeply at first and then levels off to a horizontal line at the terminal velocity.

## Experiment: comparing viscosities

1. Fill two tall measuring cylinders, one with glycerine and one with engine oil, to the same height.
2. Mark two levels, A and B, well below the surface (so the ball has already reached terminal velocity at A).
3. Drop identical small steel ball bearings into each liquid.
4. Time each ball between A and B.
5. The ball takes **longer** in the **more viscous** liquid.

**Precautions:** use identical balls; drop them centrally so they do not touch the walls; mark A far enough down for terminal velocity to be reached; keep the liquids at the same temperature.

## Effect of temperature

- The viscosity of **liquids decreases** as temperature rises: warm honey or palm oil pours easily.
- The viscosity of **gases increases** slightly as temperature rises.

:::callout{type=local-example}
On a cold harmattan morning, a motorbike can be slow to start because the engine oil is thicker. Once the engine warms, the oil thins and flows easily round the moving parts. Motor oils are graded (for example 20W-50) to stay suitably viscous over a range of temperatures.
:::

## Applications of viscosity

- **Lubricating oils** must be viscous enough to keep a film between moving parts.
- **Shock absorbers** use oil flowing through small holes to damp vibrations.
- **Streamlining** of cars, aircraft and boats reduces viscous drag from air and water.
- **Raindrops and dust** fall at small terminal velocities because of air's viscosity.
- **Parachutes** increase drag, lowering the terminal velocity to a safe landing speed.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A ball of weight 0.20 N falls through oil and reaches terminal velocity. The upthrust on it is 0.03 N. What is the viscous drag at terminal velocity?**

At terminal velocity, $W = U + F$, so

$$F = W - U = 0.20 - 0.03 = 0.17\ \text{N}$$
:::

## Key points

- Viscosity is the internal friction of a fluid, opposing motion between its layers.
- A body falling through a fluid reaches **terminal velocity** when weight = upthrust + viscous drag.
- Liquid viscosity falls as temperature rises; gas viscosity rises slightly.
- Compare viscosities by timing identical balls between two marks in each liquid.

## Quick check

1. Name the three forces acting on a ball falling through a liquid.
2. What is the resultant force on a body moving at terminal velocity?
3. How does heating affect the viscosity of engine oil?

**Answers:** 1. Weight (down), upthrust (up), viscous drag (up). 2. Zero. 3. It reduces it: the oil flows more easily.
