:::callout{type=definition}
**Stock (inventory) valuation** is the process of putting a money value on the goods a business holds at the end of a period.
:::

Because identical goods are often bought at **different prices**, the business needs a rule for which costs belong to the goods **sold** and which to the goods **still in stock**. The main methods are **FIFO** (first in, first out), **LIFO** (last in, first out) and **AVCO** (weighted average cost). The method chosen changes the **closing inventory**, the **cost of sales** and therefore the **gross profit**. This lesson works through all three methods with the same data.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain why inventory valuation matters
- value closing inventory using FIFO, LIFO and AVCO
- calculate cost of sales and gross profit under each method
- compare the effect of the methods when prices are rising
- state the advantages and disadvantages of each method

## Why valuation matters

**Closing inventory** is deducted in calculating the **cost of sales**. So:

- a **higher** closing inventory means **lower** cost of sales and **higher** gross profit;
- a **lower** closing inventory means **higher** cost of sales and **lower** gross profit.

It also appears as a **current asset**, so it affects the statement of financial position.

## The methods

| Method | Assumption | Closing inventory valued at |
|---|---|---|
| **FIFO** | the **oldest** goods are sold first | the **latest** prices |
| **LIFO** | the **newest** goods are sold first | the **oldest** prices |
| **AVCO** | each issue is at the **average cost** of the goods in stock | a weighted average, recalculated after each purchase |

## Worked example

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Kamara Hardware deals in one type of cement bag. January 2026 (NLe):**

- 1 Jan: opening inventory 100 bags at 50 = 5,000
- 5 Jan: bought 100 bags at 62 = 6,200
- 12 Jan: sold 150 bags at 80 each
- 20 Jan: bought 150 bags at 60 = 9,000
- 28 Jan: sold 120 bags at 85 each

Bags left: $100 + 100 - 150 + 150 - 120 = 80$. Total cost of goods available $= 5\,000 + 6\,200 + 9\,000 = 20\,200$. Sales $= 150 \times 80 + 120 \times 85 = 12\,000 + 10\,200 = 22\,200$.
:::

**FIFO**

- 12 Jan: issue 100 at 50 and 50 at 62; left: 50 at 62.
- 20 Jan: add 150 at 60.
- 28 Jan: issue 50 at 62 and 70 at 60; left: **80 at 60 = 4,800**.

**LIFO** (newest issued first)

- 12 Jan: issue 100 at 62 and 50 at 50; left: 50 at 50.
- 20 Jan: add 150 at 60.
- 28 Jan: issue 120 at 60; left: 50 at 50 and 30 at 60 $= 2\,500 + 1\,800 =$ **4,300**.

**AVCO**

- After 5 Jan: 200 bags cost $11\,200$; average $= 56$.
- 12 Jan: issue 150 at 56 $= 8\,400$; left: 50 at 56 $= 2\,800$.
- After 20 Jan: 200 bags cost $2\,800 + 9\,000 = 11\,800$; average $= 59$.
- 28 Jan: issue 120 at 59 $= 7\,080$; left: **80 at 59 = 4,720**.

## Comparing the results

| | FIFO | AVCO | LIFO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Closing inventory | 4,800 | 4,720 | 4,300 |
| Cost of sales (20,200 − closing) | 15,400 | 15,480 | 15,900 |
| Gross profit (22,200 − cost of sales) | **6,800** | **6,720** | **6,300** |

When **prices are rising**, **FIFO** gives the **highest** closing inventory and profit, and **LIFO** the **lowest**; AVCO is in between. When prices fall, the order reverses.

## Advantages and disadvantages

| Method | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **FIFO** | realistic: shops usually sell old stock first; closing inventory at recent prices; accepted by IAS 2 | in rising prices, cost of sales uses old, low prices, so profit looks high |
| **LIFO** | cost of sales uses recent prices | closing inventory at out-of-date prices; **not permitted by IAS 2** |
| **AVCO** | smooths out price changes; accepted by IAS 2 | more calculation; averages not actual prices |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Under **FIFO**, the closing inventory is valued at the **latest** prices, because the oldest goods have already been sold. Students often value it at the oldest prices by mistake.
:::

## Key points

- Closing inventory affects **cost of sales, gross profit** and **current assets**.
- **FIFO:** oldest out first; closing stock at **latest** prices. **LIFO:** newest out first; closing stock at **oldest** prices. **AVCO:** weighted average.
- In **rising** prices: FIFO gives the **highest** profit, LIFO the **lowest**.
- **IAS 2** allows FIFO and AVCO, **not LIFO**.

## Quick check

1. What does FIFO assume?
2. In rising prices, which method gives the highest gross profit?
3. A business has 50 units at NLe 20 and buys 50 at NLe 30. What is the average cost per unit?
4. Why is LIFO not used for published financial statements?
5. If closing inventory is overstated, what happens to gross profit?

**Answers:**

1. That the goods bought first are sold first, so closing inventory is valued at the latest prices.
2. FIFO.
3. $\frac{1\,000 + 1\,500}{100} = 25$: NLe 25.
4. Because IAS 2 does not permit it: it values closing inventory at out-of-date prices.
5. Gross profit is overstated by the same amount.
