The iron ore dug at Tonkolili, the cement made at a factory near Freetown and the new houses built with it in Lumley are all the work of **industry**. Industry is the part of business that produces goods: it takes resources from nature, turns them into finished products and builds structures. This lesson explains what industry is and how its branches are classified.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define industry
- describe the types of industry: extractive, genetic, manufacturing and constructive
- classify industries as primary, secondary and tertiary production
- give examples of each type in Sierra Leone

## What is industry?

:::callout{type=definition}
**Industry** is the branch of production concerned with extracting natural resources, rearing living things, changing raw materials into finished goods, and building.
:::

Industry produces **goods**; commerce **distributes** them; direct services serve people directly. Together they make up all production.

## Types of industry

### 1. Extractive industry

:::callout{type=definition}
**Extractive industry** is the branch of industry that takes natural resources directly from the land, water or air without growing or breeding them.
:::

Examples: mining (iron ore, diamonds, rutile, bauxite, gold), quarrying stone and sand, fishing in the sea, felling natural forest for timber.

### 2. Genetic industry

:::callout{type=definition}
**Genetic industry** is the rearing and reproduction of plants and animals for sale.
:::

Examples: poultry farms, cattle rearing, fish ponds, plant nurseries, oil palm and cocoa plantations, seed multiplication.

### 3. Manufacturing industry

:::callout{type=definition}
**Manufacturing industry** is the branch of industry that changes raw materials or partly finished goods into finished goods that are more useful.
:::

Examples: rice milling, bread baking, soap making, brewing, cement and block making, garment making, palm oil processing.

### 4. Constructive industry

:::callout{type=definition}
**Constructive industry** is the branch of industry that builds and assembles structures such as houses, roads, bridges, dams and railways.
:::

Examples: building contractors, road construction companies, bridge builders.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Fishing in the open sea is **extractive** (the fish were not reared), but fish **farming** in ponds is **genetic** (the fish are bred). Ask: were the plants or animals raised by people?
:::

## Primary, secondary and tertiary production

Economists also group all production into three stages:

| Stage | What it covers | Types of industry included | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary production** | Taking resources from nature | Extractive and genetic | Mining, fishing, farming |
| **Secondary production** | Changing raw materials into finished goods, and building | Manufacturing and constructive | Rice milling, block making, road building |
| **Tertiary production** | Services | Commerce and direct services | Trade, banking, transport, teaching, health care |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Following one product through the stages.** Cocoa grown in Kailahun:

1. Farmers grow and harvest cocoa beans: **primary** (genetic industry).
2. A factory processes the beans into cocoa butter and powder: **secondary** (manufacturing).
3. Transporters, exporters, insurers and banks move, sell and finance it: **tertiary** (commerce).
:::

## Importance of industry

- It provides **goods** to satisfy wants.
- It creates **employment** and incomes.
- It earns **foreign exchange** when goods are exported, and saves it when goods are made locally instead of imported.
- It adds **value** to raw materials: processed palm oil or milled rice is worth more than the raw produce.
- It supports other businesses that supply it or buy from it.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's economy depends heavily on primary production: farming, fishing and mining. Much of the iron ore, rutile and diamonds is exported in raw form. Developing more **manufacturing**, such as processing cocoa, cashew and palm oil locally, would add value, create more jobs and earn more from each tonne exported.
:::

## Key points

- **Industry** extracts resources, rears living things, manufactures goods and builds.
- Types: **extractive** (mining, fishing), **genetic** (farming, rearing), **manufacturing** (processing), **constructive** (building).
- **Primary** production: extractive and genetic; **secondary**: manufacturing and constructive; **tertiary**: commerce and services.
- Industry provides goods, jobs and foreign exchange, and adds value to raw materials.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **define industry**, to **name and explain the types of industry with examples**, and to **classify occupations** into primary, secondary and tertiary production. Use Sierra Leonean examples, and be careful with fishing (extractive) and fish farming (genetic).
:::

## Quick check

1. Define industry.
2. Which type of industry is a poultry farm?
3. Which stage of production does a cement factory belong to?
4. Why is processing palm oil locally better than exporting the raw fruit?

**Answers:** 1. The branch of production that extracts natural resources, rears living things, changes raw materials into finished goods, and builds. 2. Genetic. 3. Secondary production (manufacturing). 4. It adds value, creates jobs and earns more foreign exchange per tonne.
