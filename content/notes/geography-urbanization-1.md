**Urbanization** is the increase in the proportion of a country's population living in towns and cities. It happens mainly through **rural–urban migration**, the movement of people from villages to towns, and through **natural increase** in the towns themselves. More than half of the world's people now live in urban areas, and Africa's cities are growing faster than any others. This lesson explains the causes of urbanization, its pattern in West Africa and Sierra Leone, and the functions cities perform.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define urbanization and distinguish it from urban growth
- explain the causes of urbanization in West Africa
- explain the push and pull factors of rural–urban migration
- describe the pattern of urbanization in Sierra Leone
- state the benefits of urbanization

## Urbanization and urban growth

:::callout{type=definition}
**Urbanization** is the **increase in the percentage** of the population living in urban areas. **Urban growth** is the increase in the **number** of people in towns. A country urbanizes when its towns grow faster than its countryside.
:::

Since about **2007**, more than half of the world's people have lived in towns. In Africa, cities such as Lagos, Kinshasa, Abidjan, Accra and Freetown have grown very rapidly, often faster than housing, jobs and services can be provided.

## Causes of urbanization

![Push factors in a rural area and pull factors in a town, with a migration arrow from the rural area to the town and obstacles such as cost, distance and family ties](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/rural-urban-migration.svg)

*Figure 1. Push and pull factors of rural–urban migration. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

### Push factors (from rural areas)

1. **Poverty** and low farm incomes.
2. **Unemployment** and underemployment, especially in the dry season.
3. **Lack of amenities:** schools, hospitals, electricity, pipe-borne water.
4. **Land shortage** and poor harvests due to soil exhaustion or drought.
5. **Insecurity**, conflict and harsh traditional obligations.
6. **Natural disasters** such as floods.

### Pull factors (towards towns)

1. **Jobs** in industry, trade, government and services, and higher wages.
2. **Better social amenities:** education, health care, water and electricity.
3. **Security** and protection, especially in times of war.
4. **Entertainment and city lifestyle** (the "bright lights").
5. **Relatives and friends** already in the town who offer a place to stay.
6. **Further education and training.**

### Other causes

- **Natural increase** in the towns (births exceeding deaths).
- **Expansion of town boundaries** that brings villages into the city.
- **Government policies** that concentrate industries and offices in the capital.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Freetown's population grew rapidly during and after the **civil war (1991–2002)**, as people fled the fighting in the provinces for the relative safety of the capital, and many never returned. Since then, young people continue to move to Freetown in search of jobs, education and services. The city and its surroundings are now home to well over a million people, far more than its narrow site between the sea and the mountains was planned for.
:::

## Functions and benefits of cities

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| **Administration** | government ministries, Parliament, courts |
| **Commerce** | markets, banks, offices, shops |
| **Industry** | factories and workshops |
| **Transport hub** | ports, airports, road and rail terminals |
| **Education and health** | universities, teaching hospitals |
| **Culture and entertainment** | stadiums, museums, media |

**Benefits of urbanization:**

1. Concentration of **jobs** and economic opportunities.
2. Easier provision of **services** to many people at once.
3. **Markets** for farm produce from rural areas.
4. **Innovation**, education and the exchange of ideas.
5. **Remittances** sent from town workers to rural families.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Urbanization is a **percentage** change, not just more people in towns. If both towns and villages grow at the same rate, towns grow but the country does **not** urbanize.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Rural-urban migration**: the movement of people from the countryside to towns and cities.
- **Push factors**: conditions that drive people away from rural areas, such as poverty, unemployment and lack of amenities.
- **Pull factors**: attractions that draw people to towns, such as jobs, higher wages and better amenities.
:::

## Key points

- **Urbanization:** rising **percentage** of people in towns; **urban growth:** rising **numbers**.
- Causes: **rural–urban migration** (push and pull factors), **natural increase**, boundary changes, government policy.
- Push: **poverty, unemployment, poor amenities, land shortage, insecurity**. Pull: **jobs, amenities, security, lifestyle, relatives, education**.
- Freetown grew rapidly during and after the **civil war**.
- Cities provide **administration, commerce, industry, transport, education, culture**.

## Quick check

1. Define urbanization.
2. Give three pull factors that attract people to towns.
3. Give two push factors from rural areas.
4. Why did Freetown grow quickly during the 1990s?
5. State two benefits of urbanization.

**Answers:**

1. The increase in the proportion (percentage) of a population living in urban areas.
2. Any three: jobs and higher wages; better amenities; security; city lifestyle; relatives; education.
3. Any two: poverty; unemployment; lack of amenities; land shortage; poor harvests; insecurity; disasters.
4. People fled the civil war in the provinces to the relative safety of the capital.
5. Any two: jobs; easier provision of services; markets for farm produce; innovation and education; remittances.
