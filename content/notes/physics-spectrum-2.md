Visible light is a tiny slice of a much larger family. Radio waves that carry the news, the microwaves in a phone call, the infrared from a hot iron, the ultraviolet that tans skin, X-rays in a hospital and gamma rays from radioactive sources are all the same kind of wave: electromagnetic.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- list the members of the electromagnetic spectrum in order of wavelength or frequency
- state the properties common to all electromagnetic waves
- give sources, detection methods and uses of each region
- describe dangers and precautions for harmful radiations

## The electromagnetic spectrum

![The electromagnetic spectrum from radio waves through microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet and X-rays to gamma rays, with frequency increasing to the right and wavelength increasing to the left](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/em-spectrum.svg)

*Figure 1. The electromagnetic spectrum. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

In order of **increasing frequency** (decreasing wavelength):

**radio waves → microwaves → infrared → visible light → ultraviolet → X-rays → gamma rays**

## Properties common to all electromagnetic waves

1. They are **transverse** waves (oscillating electric and magnetic fields).
2. They travel through a **vacuum** at the **same speed**, $c = 3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m s⁻¹.
3. They obey $c = f\lambda$.
4. They can be reflected, refracted, diffracted, can interfere and can be polarised.
5. They carry **energy**; they are **not** deflected by electric or magnetic fields (they carry no charge).

The **energy** carried by each photon increases with **frequency**: gamma rays are the most energetic and penetrating.

## The regions: sources, detectors and uses

| Region | Approx. wavelength | Sources | Detectors | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Radio waves** | 1 mm to km | oscillating electric circuits, aerials | radio and TV receivers (aerials) | radio and TV broadcasting, two-way radio |
| **Microwaves** | 1 mm to 30 cm | magnetrons, phone masts | receiving aerials | mobile phones, satellite TV, radar, microwave ovens, Wi-Fi |
| **Infrared** | 700 nm to 1 mm | all warm bodies, the Sun, heaters | skin, thermometers with blackened bulbs, thermopiles | remote controls, night-vision cameras, cooking, heating, physiotherapy |
| **Visible light** | 400 to 700 nm | the Sun, lamps, flames | the eye, photographic film, light sensors | seeing, photography, optical fibres |
| **Ultraviolet** | 10 to 400 nm | the Sun, mercury-vapour lamps, welding arcs | fluorescent materials, photographic film | sterilising water and instruments, detecting forged banknotes, vitamin D production |
| **X-rays** | 0.01 to 10 nm | X-ray tubes | photographic film, fluorescent screens | medical images of bones, airport security, crystal structure |
| **Gamma rays** | below 0.01 nm | radioactive nuclei | Geiger–Müller tube | killing cancer cells (radiotherapy), sterilising medical equipment and food |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Every day in Sierra Leone you rely on almost the whole spectrum: FM radio and TV (radio waves), your phone (microwaves), the TV remote (infrared), sunlight, UV lamps used by banks to check leone notes, and X-ray machines at Connaught and other hospitals.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A mobile phone network uses microwaves of frequency 900 MHz. Find their wavelength.**

$$\lambda = \dfrac{c}{f} = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{900 \times 10^{6}} = 0.33\ \text{m}$$
:::

## Dangers and precautions

| Radiation | Danger | Precaution |
|---|---|---|
| Microwaves | heating of body tissue in high doses | ovens are shielded; avoid standing very close to strong transmitters |
| Infrared | burns | protective clothing near furnaces |
| Ultraviolet | sunburn, skin cancer, eye damage (cataracts) | sunscreen, hats, UV-blocking sunglasses; welders wear dark visors |
| X-rays and gamma rays | damage to cells and DNA; cancer; radiation sickness | lead shielding, lead aprons, keeping exposure short, film badges for workers |

The **higher the frequency**, the more **ionising** and dangerous the radiation: ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays can ionise atoms and damage living cells.

## Key points

- Order (increasing frequency): radio, microwaves, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma.
- All are transverse, travel at $3 \times 10^{8}$ m s⁻¹ in a vacuum and obey $c = f\lambda$.
- Each region has typical sources, detectors and uses.
- Higher-frequency radiations (UV, X, gamma) are ionising and need protection.

## Quick check

1. Which electromagnetic wave has the longest wavelength?
2. State two properties shared by all electromagnetic waves.
3. Give one use of ultraviolet radiation.

**Answers:** 1. Radio waves. 2. For example: they travel at $3 \times 10^{8}$ m s⁻¹ in a vacuum; they are transverse; they need no medium. 3. For example: sterilising water or instruments, or detecting forged banknotes.
