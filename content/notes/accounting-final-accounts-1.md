The **income statement** (the **trading, profit and loss account**) of a sole trader shows the **profit or loss** made over a period. Its **trading section** compares **net sales** with the **cost of sales** to find the **gross profit**; its **profit and loss section** adds other income and deducts **expenses** to find the **net profit**, which belongs to the owner and is added to capital. This lesson explains every line of the income statement and prepares one in full from a trial balance.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the purpose of the income statement
- calculate net sales, cost of sales, gross profit and net profit
- distinguish carriage inwards from carriage outwards and say where each goes
- prepare an income statement of a sole trader from a trial balance
- calculate gross profit margin and mark-up

## Structure of the income statement

![The income statement built up: net sales minus cost of sales gives gross profit; gross profit plus other income minus expenses gives net profit](https://wasscetutor.com/img/accounting/income-statement-flow.svg)

*Figure 1. How the income statement is built up. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Term | Calculation |
|---|---|
| **Net sales (revenue)** | sales − returns inwards |
| **Net purchases** | purchases − returns outwards |
| **Cost of sales** | opening inventory + net purchases + carriage inwards − closing inventory |
| **Gross profit** | net sales − cost of sales |
| **Net profit** | gross profit + other income − expenses |

**Carriage inwards** (transport on goods **bought**) is part of the **cost of sales**. **Carriage outwards** (delivery of goods **sold** to customers) is a **selling expense** in the profit and loss section.

**Workings:**

- Net sales $= 160\,000 - 2\,000 = 158\,000$
- Cost of sales $= 18\,000 + 93\,000 + 1\,500 - 20\,000 = 92\,500$

- Net profit $= 65\,500 + 3\,500 - 41\,500 = 27\,500$

## Closing entries in the ledger

At the year end, income and expense accounts are **closed** by transferring their balances to the income statement:

- **Sales, discount received, other income:** debit the account, credit the income statement.
- **Purchases, wages, rent and other expenses:** credit the account, debit the income statement.
- **Closing inventory:** debit inventory account (asset carried forward), credit the income statement (cost of sales).

The **net profit** is then transferred: **debit income statement, credit capital**.

## Gross profit margin and mark-up

$$\text{Gross profit margin} = \frac{\text{Gross profit}}{\text{Net sales}} \times 100\%$$

$$\text{Mark-up} = \frac{\text{Gross profit}}{\text{Cost of sales}} \times 100\%$$

For Kargbo Stores:

- Margin $= \frac{65\,500}{158\,000} \times 100\% \approx 41.5\%$
- Mark-up $= \frac{65\,500}{92\,500} \times 100\% \approx 70.8\%$

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not put **carriage outwards** in the cost of sales, and do not leave **returns inwards** and **returns outwards** as separate expenses or income. Returns are deducted from sales and purchases; carriage inwards is part of cost of sales; carriage outwards is a selling expense.
:::

## Key points

- **Net sales − cost of sales = gross profit**; **gross profit + other income − expenses = net profit**.
- **Cost of sales = opening inventory + net purchases + carriage inwards − closing inventory.**
- **Carriage inwards:** cost of sales. **Carriage outwards:** expense.
- Net profit is transferred to **capital**.
- **Margin** is based on sales; **mark-up** on cost of sales.

## WASSCE examination focus

Use clear headings, show cost of sales in full, and label gross and net profit. Examiners often report poor presentation and missing headings as reasons for lost marks.

## Quick check

1. Define gross profit.
2. Opening inventory NLe 10,000, net purchases NLe 60,000, carriage inwards NLe 1,000, closing inventory NLe 12,000. Find the cost of sales.
3. Where does carriage outwards appear?
4. Net sales are NLe 100,000 and gross profit NLe 25,000. Find the gross profit margin.
5. How is net profit transferred at the year end?

**Answers:**

1. Net sales less the cost of sales.
2. $10\,000 + 60\,000 + 1\,000 - 12\,000 = 59\,000$: NLe 59,000.
3. In the profit and loss section, as a selling expense.
4. $\frac{25\,000}{100\,000} \times 100\% = 25\%$.
5. Debit the income statement, credit the capital account.
