Cattle, goats, sheep and pigs are the main livestock of Sierra Leone. N'Dama cattle are herded across the grasslands of the north; goats and sheep are kept by most rural households; and pigs are raised in some communities and on commercial farms. Each species needs its own housing, feeding, breeding and health care. This lesson describes the management of cattle, goats and sheep, and pigs, from housing and feeding through breeding and care of the young to marketing, with worked figures that help a farmer plan.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the management of beef and dairy cattle
- describe the management of goats and sheep
- describe the management of pigs, including care of the sow and piglets
- state the routine management practices for each species
- calculate simple production figures such as feed conversion and stocking

## Cattle

### Breeds and systems

Sierra Leone's cattle are mostly **N'Dama**, small, hardy and tolerant of trypanosomiasis, kept mainly in the north (Koinadugu, Bombali, Kambia and nearby areas), often by **Fula herders** using **extensive** grazing and **transhumance** in the dry season. Exotic dairy breeds such as Friesians need intensive care and are rare.

### Management

- **Housing**: a simple **kraal** (night enclosure) protects against theft and predators; dairy cattle need a shed with a concrete floor, feeding trough and water.
- **Feeding**: grazing on natural or improved pasture; supplementing with crop residues, hay or silage and **salt licks** in the dry season; dairy cows need concentrates for milk.
- **Breeding**: select good bulls; one bull to about 25–30 cows; castrate other males; first calving at about 3 years in N'Dama.
- **Health**: vaccination (e.g. against contagious bovine pleuropneumonia, anthrax); tick control by **spraying or dipping**; deworming; trypanosomiasis control.
- **Calf care**: colostrum, navel dipping, weaning at about 6–8 months.

### Dairy management

Milk the cow at the **same times** every day, in a clean place, with clean hands and udder; use the **strip cup** to check for mastitis; cool the milk quickly; dry the cow off about **2 months** before calving.

## Goats and sheep

**Breeds:** **West African Dwarf** goats and sheep (Djallonké) are hardy, **prolific** (often twins) and tolerant of trypanosomiasis.

| Practice | Details |
|---|---|
| **housing** | a raised house with a **slatted floor** (droppings fall through, keeping animals dry and reducing worms and foot rot); good roof and ventilation |
| **feeding** | grazing (sheep) and **browsing** (goats prefer shrubs and tree leaves); cut-and-carry forage; cassava peels, groundnut haulms; salt lick; clean water |
| **breeding** | one buck or ram to about 25–30 females; first kidding at about 12–15 months; avoid inbreeding by changing males |
| **health** | **PPR vaccination**; deworming every few months; foot trimming; tick control; quarantine of new animals |
| **young** | colostrum; keep kids warm and dry; wean at about 3–4 months; castrate males for fattening |

:::callout{type=tip}
**Goats are browsers; sheep are grazers.** Goats eat leaves, shrubs and tree shoots well above the ground, which is why they damage young trees and crops if not controlled. Sheep graze short grass close to the ground. Mixed grazing uses pasture more fully.
:::

## Pigs

Pigs grow fast, have **large litters** (8–12 piglets), and convert feed and kitchen waste efficiently into meat, but they need concentrated feed and good hygiene.

**Breeds:** Large White, Landrace, Duroc (exotic, fast-growing); local pigs (hardy but slow).

### Management

- **Housing**: concrete-floored **pens** that slope for drainage; a roof for shade (pigs suffer badly in heat because they sweat very little); separate pens for boars, sows, fatteners; a **farrowing pen** with a **creep** area or guard rails so the sow does not crush piglets.
- **Feeding**: balanced rations of maize, rice bran, palm kernel cake, fish meal or groundnut cake, minerals; **never feed uncooked kitchen swill**, which can spread African swine fever.
- **Breeding**: gilts first mated at about 8 months; heat every 21 days; **gestation 114 days**; up to two litters a year.
- **Piglet care**: clip needle teeth; **iron injection** at 3 days (sow's milk lacks iron and piglets kept on concrete get none from soil); castrate males not kept for breeding; provide **creep feed** from about 2 weeks; **wean** at 6–8 weeks.
- **Health**: strict **biosecurity** (African swine fever has no vaccine), deworming, mange control, clean water, good drainage.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A sow weans 9 piglets per litter and has 2 litters a year. Each pig is sold at 6 months for NLe 1500. Feed and other costs are NLe 900 per pig sold, plus NLe 4000 a year for the sow. What is the yearly profit from the sow?**

Pigs sold $= 9 \times 2 = 18$. Income $= 18 \times 1500 = $ NLe 27 000.

Costs $= 18 \times 900 + 4000 = 16\,200 + 4000 = $ NLe 20 200.

**Profit $= 27\,000 - 20\,200 = $ NLe 6 800** a year.
:::

## Routine practices across species

**Identification**, **record keeping** (births, weights, treatments, sales), **castration**, **dehorning or disbudding**, **hoof trimming**, **deworming**, **dipping or spraying** against ticks, **culling** of unproductive animals, **vaccination**, and **marketing** at the right weight and season (prices for goats and sheep rise before festivals).

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Keeping every animal "for luck".** Old, barren, sickly or slow-growing animals eat feed and spread disease without producing. **Culling** them each year and keeping the best for breeding steadily improves the herd and the profit.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Selling goats before the festivals.** The price of goats and sheep rises sharply before **Eid al-Adha** and Christmas. Farmers who plan their breeding so that well-grown animals are ready for these festivals, and fatten them with extra feed for a few weeks beforehand, earn much more per animal than those who sell whenever cash is needed.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Why are N'Dama cattle suited to Sierra Leone?"}
They are **hardy** in hot, humid conditions and **tolerant of trypanosomiasis**, which is spread by tsetse flies.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Give two advantages of raised, slatted floors for goat houses."}
Droppings and urine fall through, keeping animals **dry and clean**; fewer worms and less foot rot; better ventilation.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Why are piglets given an iron injection?"}
Sow's milk is low in iron, and piglets kept on concrete cannot get iron from soil, so without it they become **anaemic**.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Why should uncooked kitchen swill not be fed to pigs?"}
It can carry the **African swine fever** virus (and other diseases), for which there is no vaccine.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. A sow weans 10 piglets per litter and farrows twice a year. How many piglets does she wean in 3 years?"}
$10 \times 2 \times 3 = 60$ piglets.
:::

## Key points

- **Cattle**: mostly N'Dama, extensive and transhumant; kraal, grazing plus dry-season supplements, tick and disease control; hygienic milking for dairy.
- **Goats and sheep**: West African Dwarf; **raised slatted houses**, browsing and grazing, PPR vaccination, regular deworming.
- **Pigs**: large litters, 114-day gestation; concrete pens with shade; farrowing pens; **iron injection**, teeth clipping, creep feed, weaning at 6–8 weeks; no uncooked swill.
- Routine: **identification, records, castration, deworming, dipping, culling, vaccination, timely marketing**.
