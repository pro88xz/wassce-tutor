How do we know whether Sierra Leone sells more to the world than it buys? Governments keep careful accounts of foreign trade, and they also control it with taxes and limits. This lesson explains the balance of trade, the balance of payments, the terms of trade and the restrictions governments place on foreign trade.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define and calculate the balance of trade and the balance of payments
- distinguish visible and invisible trade
- calculate the terms of trade
- explain the methods and reasons for restricting foreign trade
- describe the role of customs and export promotion

## Visible and invisible trade

| Visible trade | Invisible trade |
|---|---|
| Trade in **goods** that can be seen and recorded at customs | Trade in **services** |
| Exports of iron ore, cocoa; imports of rice, fuel | Shipping, insurance, banking, tourism, air travel, consultancy |

## The balance of trade and the balance of payments

:::callout{type=definition}
- The **balance of trade** is the difference between a country's **visible exports and visible imports** in a period.
- The **balance of payments** is a record of **all** a country's transactions with the rest of the world: goods, services, income, transfers and capital flows.
:::

- If visible exports > visible imports, there is a **favourable (surplus)** balance of trade.
- If visible imports > visible exports, there is an **unfavourable (deficit)** balance of trade.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A country's trade in one year (NLe million):**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Visible exports | 9,000 |
| Visible imports | 13,000 |
| Invisible exports (services sold abroad) | 1,500 |
| Invisible imports (services bought abroad) | 3,500 |

- **Balance of trade** $= 9000 - 13000 = -4000$: an **unfavourable** balance of NLe 4,000 million.
- **Balance of invisible trade** $= 1500 - 3500 = -2000$.
- **Balance on goods and services** $= -4000 + (-2000) = -6000$ (NLe million).
:::

**Ways to correct an unfavourable balance:** increase and diversify exports; reduce imports (tariffs, quotas, import substitution); encourage tourism and other invisible exports; attract foreign investment; borrow in the short term; devalue the currency (making exports cheaper and imports dearer).

## The terms of trade

$$\text{terms of trade} = \dfrac{\text{index of export prices}}{\text{index of import prices}} \times 100$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Export prices index 110, import prices index 125 (base year 100).**

$$\text{terms of trade} = \dfrac{110}{125} \times 100 = 88$$

The terms of trade are **unfavourable**: each unit of exports now buys 12% fewer imports than in the base year.
:::

## Restricting foreign trade

### Methods

| Method | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Tariffs (customs duties)** | Taxes on imports (import duties) or exports (export duties) |
| **Quotas** | Limits on the **quantity** of a good that may be imported or exported |
| **Embargo** | A complete **ban** on trade in a good or with a country |
| **Import and export licences** | Permission from the government is needed to trade certain goods |
| **Exchange control** | Rationing of foreign currency for imports |
| **Subsidies** | Help to local producers so they can compete with imports |
| **Administrative barriers** | Strict standards, health and safety rules, slow procedures |

### Reasons for restricting trade

1. **Protect infant industries** until they can compete.
2. **Protect jobs** in local industries.
3. **Correct a balance of payments deficit**.
4. **Raise government revenue** through duties.
5. **Prevent dumping** of goods sold below cost.
6. **Protect health and safety**: ban dangerous drugs, unsafe food or toxic waste.
7. **Strategic reasons**: food security and defence.
8. **Conserve scarce resources** by limiting their export.

**Disadvantages of restrictions:** higher prices for consumers; less choice; inefficient protected firms; retaliation by other countries; smuggling.

## Customs and export promotion

The **Customs Services** of the **National Revenue Authority (NRA)** control goods entering and leaving Sierra Leone. They:

- collect **import and export duties** and taxes on imports
- check **documents** and inspect goods
- prevent **smuggling** and the import of banned goods
- keep **trade statistics**
- supervise **bonded warehouses**

**Export promotion** helps exporters through information on markets, trade fairs, quality standards, credit and reduced duties on export inputs. In Sierra Leone this is the work of **SLIEPA** and the Ministry of Trade and Industry, supported by regional schemes such as the **ECOWAS Trade Liberalisation Scheme**, which allows goods made in member states to move duty-free.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not call the balance of trade "the balance of payments". The balance of trade covers **goods only**; the balance of payments covers **all** transactions with the rest of the world.
:::

## Key points

- **Visible trade** is in goods; **invisible trade** is in services.
- **Balance of trade** = visible exports − visible imports; **balance of payments** records all transactions.
- **Terms of trade** = export price index ÷ import price index × 100.
- Restrictions: **tariffs, quotas, embargoes, licences, exchange control, subsidies**; reasons include **infant industries, jobs, revenue and anti-dumping**.
- **Customs** collects duties and controls trade; **export promotion** helps exporters.
