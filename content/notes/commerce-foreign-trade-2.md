# Balance of trade, exchange rates and trade restrictions

## Learning objectives
Calculate a country's visible trade balance and explain how exchange rates and trade policies affect imports and exports.

## Balance of trade
The **balance of trade in goods** is the value of merchandise exports minus the value of merchandise imports over a period.

**Balance of trade = Value of exports − Value of imports**

If a country exports goods worth US$800 million and imports goods worth US$1,050 million, its trade balance is −US$250 million, a deficit in goods trade.

A surplus occurs when exports exceed imports. A deficit occurs when imports exceed exports. The balance of trade in goods is not the same as the balance of payments, which records a much wider set of transactions with the rest of the world.

## Exchange rates
An exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another. If the leone depreciates against the US dollar, more leones may be required to buy a given amount of dollars. Imported goods priced in dollars can therefore become more expensive in leones, although the final effect also depends on supplier prices, taxes, freight, margins and timing.

A depreciation may make exports cheaper to foreign buyers, but exporters may rely on imported fuel, machinery or packaging, raising their costs. The effect on trade volumes depends on demand responsiveness and how quickly contracts and production can adjust.

## Trade restrictions
- **Tariff:** a tax on imported or exported goods, commonly used on imports.
- **Quota:** a limit on the quantity or value of a good that may be imported.
- **Import licence:** official permission required for specified imports.
- **Embargo:** a prohibition on trade with a country or in a product.
- **Subsidy:** government support that reduces producers' costs or supports output.

Restrictions may protect infant industries, raise revenue or address health and security concerns. They can also raise consumer prices, reduce choice, encourage inefficiency or trigger retaliation.

## WASSCE examination focus
State whether a question concerns trade in goods or the wider balance of payments. Show the subtraction clearly and label a negative result as a deficit.

## Quick check
1. Define balance of trade.
2. Exports are US$420m and imports US$500m. Calculate the balance.
3. Distinguish a tariff from a quota.
4. Give one possible benefit and one cost of import protection.

**Answers:** 1. Exports of goods minus imports of goods. 2. 420 − 500 = −US$80m, a deficit. 3. A tariff is a tax; a quota limits quantity or value. 4. Benefit: protects a developing industry; cost: higher prices or reduced competition.
