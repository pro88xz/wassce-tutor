Freetown's streams carry rubbish to the sea after every storm, and mining pits in diamond and gold areas fill with stagnant water. In 2012 Sierra Leone suffered a large **cholera** outbreak spread by water contaminated with sewage. Water and land pollution affect health directly. This lesson covers the main water and land pollutants, their effects, and how waste can be managed safely.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name the main water pollutants and their sources
- explain eutrophication and the effect of organic waste on dissolved oxygen
- describe the dangers of heavy metals, oil spills and mining waste
- describe the causes and effects of land pollution
- distinguish biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste
- describe methods of waste management and the "3 Rs"

## Water pollution

| Pollutant | Sources | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| **Sewage** | Toilets and drains emptying into rivers and the sea | Spreads **cholera, typhoid** and dysentery; decaying matter uses up dissolved oxygen |
| **Fertilisers** (nitrates, phosphates) | Run-off from farms | **Eutrophication**; nitrates in drinking water harm babies |
| **Detergents** | Homes, laundries | Foam; phosphates cause eutrophication |
| **Heavy metals** (Pb, Hg, Cd) | Factories, mining, batteries, e-waste | **Poisonous**; build up in food chains (fish) |
| **Oil** | Spills from ships and pipelines; waste engine oil | Coats birds and fish; stops oxygen entering water; kills mangroves |
| **Pesticides** | Farms, insect control | Kill fish and useful insects; build up in food chains |
| **Silt and mining waste** | Mining, deforestation | Muddy water blocks light; covers fish breeding grounds |
| **Hot water** | Cooling water from power stations and factories | Less oxygen dissolves in warm water (**thermal pollution**) |
| **Plastics** | Litter, dumping | Swallowed by fish, birds and turtles; block drains |

### Eutrophication and oxygen demand

![Nutrients enter the water; algae grow rapidly; light is blocked and plants die; bacteria decompose dead matter, using up dissolved oxygen; fish suffocate](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/eutrophication.svg)

*Figure 1. How excess nutrients starve water of oxygen. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=definition}
**Eutrophication** is the over-enrichment of water with nutrients (nitrates and phosphates), leading to rapid growth of algae, followed by **oxygen depletion** when the algae die and decay.
:::

Organic waste such as sewage has the same final effect: **aerobic bacteria** use dissolved oxygen to break it down. The amount of oxygen needed is the **biochemical oxygen demand (BOD)**: the higher the BOD, the more polluted the water. Clean river water has a low BOD and plenty of dissolved oxygen.

### Heavy metals

**Mercury, lead and cadmium** are **cumulative poisons**: the body cannot get rid of them, so they build up. They also become more concentrated at each step of a food chain (**bioaccumulation**), so fish-eating people and animals receive the largest doses. Mercury damages the nervous system and kidneys; lead harms the brain and blood; cadmium damages bones and kidneys.

## Land pollution

- **Solid waste:** household rubbish, plastics, glass, metal cans, **e-waste** (old phones, computers, batteries containing lead, mercury and cadmium)
- **Mining:** open pits, spoil heaps, chemicals (mercury, cyanide) and loss of topsoil
- **Agriculture:** overuse of pesticides and fertilisers; salt build-up in soil
- **Oil spills** and waste oil poured onto the ground
- **Deforestation:** leads to soil erosion and loss of fertility

**Effects:** ugly surroundings, breeding grounds for **mosquitoes, flies and rats** (malaria, cholera), blocked drains and **flooding**, contaminated groundwater, loss of farmland, and fires and smoke from burning dumps.

## Biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste

| Biodegradable | Non-biodegradable |
|---|---|
| Broken down by **micro-organisms** (bacteria, fungi) into simple substances | **Not** broken down by micro-organisms, or only over hundreds of years |
| Food waste, paper, leaves, wood, cotton, animal dung | **Plastics**, glass, metal cans, synthetic fibres, e-waste |
| Can be **composted** | Must be **reused, recycled** or disposed of safely |

## Waste management

| Method | How it works | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Reduce** | Use less: fewer disposable items, less packaging | The best option: no waste is made |
| **Reuse** | Use items again: bottles, containers, bags | Saves resources and money |
| **Recycle** | Collect and process waste into new materials (metals, paper, plastics, glass) | Saves raw materials and energy |
| **Composting** | Bacteria and fungi break down plant and food waste into fertiliser | For biodegradable waste |
| **Sanitary landfill** | Waste buried in lined pits, compacted and covered with soil daily | Better than open dumping; methane can be collected as fuel |
| **Incineration** | Burning at high temperature in a closed furnace | Reduces volume; energy can be used; gases must be cleaned |
| **Sewage treatment** | Screening, settling, then bacteria break down organic matter; sludge treated | Protects rivers from disease and eutrophication |

**Open dumping and open burning** should be avoided: they spread disease, pollute water and release toxic smoke.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Interpreting data.** Dissolved oxygen was measured at points along a river: 8.5 mg/dm³ upstream of a town, 2.0 mg/dm³ just below a sewage outlet, and 7.0 mg/dm³ 10 km further downstream. Explain the pattern.

- **Upstream:** clean water with high dissolved oxygen.
- **Below the outlet:** bacteria use oxygen to decompose the sewage (high BOD), so oxygen falls sharply; fish cannot survive there.
- **Downstream:** most organic matter has been broken down, and oxygen dissolves back in from the air and from water plants, so the river **recovers**.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Freetown's main dumpsites at **Kingtom** and **Granville Brook** receive much of the city's waste, which is often burned in the open. In mining areas such as **Kono**, abandoned diamond pits fill with water that breeds mosquitoes, and small-scale gold miners sometimes use **mercury**, which pollutes rivers and builds up in fish. Separating food waste for compost, recycling plastics and metals, and refilling mining pits all reduce these problems.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Fertilisers do not kill fish directly. They feed **algae**; it is the **decay** of dead algae by bacteria that uses up the **oxygen**, and lack of oxygen kills the fish.
:::

## Key points

- Water pollutants: **sewage** (disease, high BOD), **fertilisers and phosphates** (**eutrophication**), **heavy metals** (cumulative poisons, bioaccumulate), **oil**, pesticides, silt, hot water, plastics.
- **Eutrophication:** nutrients → algal bloom → plants die → bacteria use up O₂ → fish die.
- Land pollution: rubbish, plastics, **e-waste**, mining waste, pesticides; it breeds disease vectors and causes flooding.
- **Biodegradable** waste is broken down by micro-organisms; **non-biodegradable** waste is not.
- Manage waste by **reduce, reuse, recycle**, composting, sanitary landfill, incineration and sewage treatment.

## Quick check

1. Name two diseases spread by water polluted with sewage.
2. What is meant by biochemical oxygen demand?
3. Why are mercury and lead called cumulative poisons?
4. Give two advantages of recycling.

**Answers:**

1. Any two: cholera, typhoid, dysentery.
2. The amount of dissolved oxygen needed by micro-organisms to break down the organic matter in a sample of water.
3. The body cannot get rid of them, so they build up in tissues over time (and become concentrated along food chains).
4. Any two: saves raw materials; saves energy; reduces the amount of waste sent to dumps; reduces pollution.
