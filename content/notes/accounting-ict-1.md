:::callout{type=definition}
**Computerised accounting** is the use of **computers and accounting software** to record, process and report financial transactions.
:::

Instead of writing entries by hand in day books and ledgers, the user enters each transaction once from its source document, and the software **posts it automatically by double entry** and produces the trial balance and final accounts in seconds. Popular packages include **QuickBooks**, **Sage** and **Tally**, while **spreadsheets** such as Microsoft Excel are widely used by small businesses. This lesson compares manual and computerised accounting and explains the main features, advantages and disadvantages of accounting software.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the meaning of computerised accounting
- describe the input, processing and output stages of a computerised system
- name the main modules of an accounting package
- compare manual and computerised accounting
- state the advantages and disadvantages of computerised accounting

## How a computerised system works

*Figure 1. A computerised accounting system. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Input** | data from source documents (invoices, receipts, payroll records) is keyed in, scanned or imported from bank feeds |
| **Processing** | the software posts entries by double entry, updates ledgers and inventory, and calculates balances, tax and payroll |
| **Output** | reports: trial balance, income statement, statement of financial position, aged receivables, cash flow, tax returns |
| **Storage** | data is saved on the computer, a server or the cloud, and **backed up** |

## Modules of an accounting package

| Module | Function |
|---|---|
| **Sales ledger (receivables)** | invoices, customer accounts, statements, aged receivables |
| **Purchases ledger (payables)** | supplier invoices and payments |
| **General (nominal) ledger** | all other accounts; produces the trial balance and final accounts |
| **Cash book / banking** | receipts, payments and bank reconciliation |
| **Inventory (stock control)** | quantities, costs, reorder levels |
| **Payroll** | wages, deductions (income tax, NASSIT social security), payslips |
| **Fixed assets** | asset register and depreciation |

## Manual and computerised accounting compared

| Manual accounting | Computerised accounting |
|---|---|
| entries written by hand in books | entries keyed in once |
| double entry done by the clerk | double entry done **automatically** |
| slow, especially for many transactions | **fast**, even for thousands of transactions |
| arithmetical errors are common | calculations are **accurate** |
| reports take time to prepare | reports are produced **instantly** |
| cheap to start | costs of computers, software and training |
| works without electricity | needs **electricity**, hardware and skills |

## Advantages

1. **Speed** of recording and reporting.
2. **Accuracy**: automatic posting and calculation.
3. **Up-to-date information** for decisions at any time.
4. **Less paper** and easier storage and retrieval.
5. **Many reports** from the same data (aged receivables, budgets, ratios).
6. **Integration**: sales, inventory and payroll all update the ledgers.
7. **Fewer staff** needed for routine bookkeeping.

## Disadvantages

1. **Cost** of computers, software, licences and maintenance.
2. **Training** needed for staff.
3. Dependence on **reliable electricity** and internet.
4. Risk of **data loss** from hardware failure, theft or viruses.
5. Risk of **fraud and unauthorised access** without good controls.
6. **Input errors** are still possible: "garbage in, garbage out".

:::callout{type=local-example}
Banks, mobile network operators and large firms in Sierra Leone use full accounting systems, and the Accountant-General's Department uses an **Integrated Financial Management Information System (IFMIS)** for government accounts. Many small businesses use a **spreadsheet** and a **mobile money** record of payments, which already gives a cheap computerised cash book.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A computer does not remove the need to **understand double entry**. If a transaction is entered to the wrong account, the software will post it there and produce wrong reports. Accounting knowledge is still needed to set up accounts and check results.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Accounting software**: a computer program that posts entries by double entry, updates ledgers and produces reports such as the trial balance and final accounts.
- **Bank feed**: a link that imports bank transactions directly into accounting software.
- **Payroll**: the record and calculation of wages and deductions, such as income tax and NASSIT contributions.
:::

## Key points

- **Computerised accounting** uses software to post entries automatically and produce reports.
- Stages: **input → processing → output**, with secure **storage**.
- Modules: **sales, purchases, general ledger, cash book, inventory, payroll, fixed assets**.
- Advantages: **speed, accuracy, up-to-date reports, integration**. Disadvantages: **cost, training, power, data loss, fraud risk, input errors**.
