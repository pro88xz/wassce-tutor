To open a small bakery in Waterloo you need a building and a plot to put it on, people to mix and bake, money to buy an oven and flour, and someone with the idea and the courage to bring it all together. These are a business's **resources**, often called the **factors of production**. Every business, from a roadside stall to a mining company, needs all of them.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define business resources and the factors of production
- explain land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship, with their rewards
- explain the place of information and technology as modern resources
- explain why managers must use resources efficiently

## What are business resources?

:::callout{type=definition}
**Business resources** are all the inputs a business uses to produce goods and services: land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship, together with information and technology.
:::

Economists call the first four the **factors of production**. Each earns a **reward** when it is used.

## The factors of production

### 1. Land

:::callout{type=definition}
**Land** is every natural resource used in production: the ground itself, minerals, water, forests, fish and the climate.
:::

Land is fixed in supply and cannot be moved. Its reward is **rent**.

Examples: a plot for a shop, farmland, the iron ore at Tonkolili, the fish in the sea off Tombo.

### 2. Labour

:::callout{type=definition}
**Labour** is the human effort, physical and mental, used in production.
:::

Labour includes a carpenter's skill, a clerk's work and a manager's thinking. Its reward is **wages** or **salaries**. The quality of labour depends on education, training, health and motivation.

### 3. Capital

:::callout{type=definition}
**Capital** is man-made wealth used to produce more goods and services, such as machines, tools, buildings, vehicles and money invested in a business.
:::

Its reward is **interest**. Capital may be **fixed** (machines, buildings, vehicles, used again and again) or **working** (stock, raw materials and cash, used up or turned over in the course of trade).

### 4. Entrepreneurship

:::callout{type=definition}
An **entrepreneur** is the person who brings land, labour and capital together, takes the decisions and bears the risk of a business.
:::

Entrepreneurship is the factor that organises the other three. Its reward is **profit**, the reward for bearing risk.

| Factor | What it is | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| Land | Natural resources | Rent |
| Labour | Human effort | Wages and salaries |
| Capital | Man-made aids to production | Interest |
| Entrepreneurship | Organising and risk-bearing | Profit |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Money kept under the bed is **not** capital in the business sense. Money becomes capital when it is used to buy machines, stock or buildings for production. And remember that **profit** is the reward of the entrepreneur, not of capital (whose reward is interest).
:::

## Modern resources: information and technology

Today businesses also depend on:

- **Information**: knowledge about customers, prices, competitors and sales, used to make better decisions.
- **Technology**: computers, mobile phones, the internet and modern machines that raise output and cut costs.

A trader who checks prices by phone before travelling to market, or a shop that keeps its stock records on a computer, is using these resources.

## Using resources efficiently

Resources are **scarce**: a business never has as much land, labour, capital or money as it would like. Managers must therefore:

- choose the **right mix** of resources for the job
- avoid **waste** of materials, time and money
- keep machines in good repair and workers well trained
- measure output against what was used, to see where improvements can be made

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Identifying resources and rewards.** A fish-smoking business at Tombo uses:

1. The beach plot where the ovens stand. **Land: rent**
2. Six women who clean and smoke the fish. **Labour: wages**
3. Ovens, a canoe and a loan from the bank. **Capital: interest on the loan**
4. The owner who decided to start the business, borrowed the money and takes the risk. **Entrepreneur: profit**
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone is rich in **land** resources (fertile soil, minerals, fish and forests) and has a young **labour** force, but businesses are often held back by shortages of **capital** and skilled labour. Access to credit, training and technology is therefore a key to business growth.
:::

## Key points

- **Business resources** are land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship, with information and technology.
- Rewards: land → **rent**; labour → **wages**; capital → **interest**; entrepreneurship → **profit**.
- Capital is **fixed** or **working**; money is capital only when invested in production.
- Resources are scarce, so managers must use them **efficiently**.

3. Give one example of fixed capital and one of working capital.
