Friction on a slope decides whether a parked car rolls away and how steep a road can be. Friction in fluids, called viscosity, decides how fast a raindrop or a ball bearing falls. This lesson completes the topic with both, plus the practical way to measure μ.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- relate the coefficient of friction to the angle of friction on an inclined plane
- describe an experiment to measure the coefficient of static friction
- calculate forces needed to move bodies up and down rough slopes
- list the advantages and disadvantages of friction and ways of reducing it

## The angle of friction

Place a block on a board and slowly raise one end. At a certain angle θ the block **just begins to slide**. At that moment friction is limiting:

$$F = mg\sin\theta \quad\text{and}\quad R = mg\cos\theta$$

$$\mu_s = \dfrac{F}{R} = \dfrac{mg\sin\theta}{mg\cos\theta} = \tan\theta$$

:::callout{type=definition}
The **angle of friction** (angle of repose) is the angle of a slope at which a body resting on it is **just on the point of sliding**. Then

$$\mu_s = \tan\theta$$
:::

### Measuring μ with an inclined plane (practical)

1. Place the block on the plank and raise one end slowly, tapping gently.
2. Stop when the block just begins to slide; measure the height $h$ and the horizontal length $l$ of the slope.
3. $\tan\theta = \dfrac{h}{l}$, so $\mu_s = \dfrac{h}{l}$.
4. Repeat several times and average.

**Alternative (horizontal method):** pull the block with a spring balance or with a string over a pulley carrying increasing masses. Record the force that just starts it moving for different loads placed on the block. A graph of limiting friction (y) against normal reaction (x) is a straight line through the origin with gradient $\mu_s$.

## Moving a body on a rough slope

![A block on a slope with weight split into mg sin theta down the slope and mg cos theta into the slope, normal reaction R and friction F](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/inclined-plane-forces.svg)

*Figure 1. The forces on a block on a slope. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

Friction always opposes the motion, so its direction depends on which way the body moves.

| Motion | Force $P$ parallel to the slope needed (steady speed) |
|---|---|
| Pushed **up** the slope | $P = mg\sin\theta + \mu mg\cos\theta$ |
| Held from sliding **down** (just about to slide) | $P = mg\sin\theta - \mu mg\cos\theta$ |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A 50 kg load is pulled up a ramp inclined at 30° at constant speed by a rope parallel to the ramp. $\mu = 0.2$. Find the tension and the work done in moving it 6 m along the ramp. ($g = 10$ m s⁻²)**

$mg = 500$ N.

$$P = 500\sin 30° + 0.2 \times 500\cos 30° = 250 + 86.6 = 336.6\ \text{N}$$

Work $= 336.6 \times 6 = 2020$ J. Of this, $500 \times (6\sin 30°) = 1500$ J becomes potential energy; the remaining 520 J is lost as heat through friction.
:::

## Friction in fluids: viscosity

Fluids (liquids and gases) also resist motion through them. This fluid friction is called **viscosity** (studied further with surface tension). Its effects:

- a falling body reaches a **terminal velocity** when viscous drag equals its weight (minus upthrust)
- thick liquids such as honey and engine oil have high viscosity; water has low viscosity
- viscosity of liquids **decreases** as temperature rises: engine oil flows more easily when warm

## Advantages and disadvantages of friction

| Advantages (useful friction) | Disadvantages (unwanted friction) |
|---|---|
| Walking and running without slipping | Wear and tear of machine parts, tyres and shoes |
| Brakes stop vehicles | Heat produced in engines and bearings |
| Tyres grip the road | Energy wasted, lowering efficiency |
| Nails and screws hold; knots stay tied | More fuel needed to overcome it |
| Writing with chalk or pencil; striking a match | Noise from moving parts |

## Ways of reducing friction

1. **Lubrication** with oil or grease, which separates the surfaces.
2. **Ball bearings and roller bearings**, which replace sliding with rolling (rolling friction is much smaller).
3. **Smoothing and polishing** surfaces.
4. **Streamlining** vehicles and aircraft to reduce air resistance.
5. **Air cushions** (as in a hovercraft) to keep surfaces apart.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Motorbike riders in Bo and Kenema oil their chains regularly: a dry chain wears quickly, wastes fuel and can snap. On a wet laterite road after rain, tyres have far less grip, so stopping distances become much longer.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Lubrication**: reducing friction with oil or grease, which separates the surfaces.
- **Ball bearings**: small steel balls that replace sliding with rolling, which has much less friction.
- **Streamlining**: shaping vehicles and aircraft to reduce air resistance.
- **Viscosity**: the friction in a fluid, which resists its flow.
:::

## Key points

- At the angle of friction, $\mu_s = \tan\theta$; measure it with a tilted plank.
- On a rough slope, friction is added to $mg\sin\theta$ when pulling **up** and subtracted when holding a body from sliding **down**.
- Viscosity is fluid friction; it gives falling bodies a terminal velocity.
- Friction is reduced by lubrication, bearings, polishing and streamlining.

## Quick check

1. A block just begins to slide when a plank is tilted to 35°. Find $\mu_s$.
2. Give two ways of reducing friction in a machine.
3. Why does a car need a longer distance to stop on a wet road?

**Answers:** 1. $\tan 35° = 0.70$. 2. For example: lubricating with oil; using ball bearings. 3. The coefficient of friction between tyres and a wet road is smaller, so the braking force and deceleration are smaller.
