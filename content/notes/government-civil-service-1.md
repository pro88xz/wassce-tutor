Ministers come and go with elections and reshuffles, but the officials who keep the schools paid, the hospitals supplied and the records kept stay at their desks. They are the **civil service**, the permanent machinery of government. This lesson explains what the civil service is, its main features, how it is organised and what it does, with examples from Sierra Leone.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define public administration and the civil service
- explain the features (principles) of the civil service
- describe the structure of a ministry and the grades of the civil service
- explain the functions of the civil service
- describe the role of the Public Service Commission

## Public administration and the civil service

:::callout{type=definition}
**Public administration** is the organisation and management of government business: carrying out public policy through ministries, departments and agencies. The **civil service** is the body of **permanent, paid, professional officials** who work in government ministries and departments to advise ministers and implement government policy.
:::

The **public service** is wider than the civil service. It includes the civil service plus the police, the armed forces, teachers in government schools, staff of public corporations and local councils.

## Features of the civil service

1. **Permanence**: civil servants stay in office when governments change, giving **continuity**.
2. **Political neutrality**: they serve any government loyally and do not take part in partisan politics.
3. **Anonymity**: they work behind the scenes; the minister takes public responsibility for the ministry's actions.
4. **Impartiality**: they treat all citizens equally, without favour.
5. **Hierarchy**: a clear chain of command from the top to the bottom.
6. **Expertise and professionalism**: officials are trained specialists.
7. **Recruitment and promotion on merit**, through the Public Service Commission.
8. **Security of tenure**: they cannot be dismissed at will.
9. **Rules and procedures** (sometimes called "red tape") that ensure consistency.

## Structure of a ministry

![A ministry headed by the Minister and Deputy Minister(s) at the political level, with the Permanent Secretary and a professional head leading the permanent civil service, directors and divisions, and district offices](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/ministry-structure.svg)

*Figure 1. Who runs a ministry. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Officer | Role |
|---|---|
| **Minister** | Political head: sets policy and answers to the President and Parliament |
| **Deputy Minister** | Assists the minister |
| **Permanent Secretary** | Administrative head and **accounting officer** of the ministry; chief adviser to the minister |
| **Professional head** | Leads technical staff, for example the Chief Medical Officer in the Ministry of Health |
| **Directors** | Head divisions or departments within the ministry |
| **District officers** | Deliver services in the districts |

At the top of the whole service in Sierra Leone is the **Secretary to the Cabinet and Head of the Civil Service**.

### Grades (classes) of the civil service

| Class | Work |
|---|---|
| **Administrative** | Senior officials who advise on and coordinate policy (permanent secretaries, directors) |
| **Professional** | Specialists such as doctors, engineers, lawyers and economists |
| **Executive** | Middle-level officers who carry out policy and supervise |
| **Technical** | Technicians, surveyors, laboratory staff |
| **Clerical** | Clerks, typists and records staff |
| **Subordinate (support)** | Drivers, messengers, cleaners and security staff |

## Functions of the civil service

1. **Implementing government policies** and laws.
2. **Advising ministers** on policy, based on expertise and records.
3. **Drafting** policy papers, bills and regulations.
4. **Preparing the budget** estimates of ministries.
5. **Collecting revenue** (taxes, fees and duties).
6. **Providing continuity** when governments change.
7. **Keeping records** and statistics.
8. **Making delegated legislation** under powers given by Acts.
9. **Representing the government** at meetings and in negotiations.
10. **Delivering public services** such as education and health.

## The Public Service Commission

The **Public Service Commission (PSC)**, established by the 1991 Constitution, is responsible for **appointing, promoting, transferring and disciplining** public officers, so that these decisions are made on **merit** and not by politicians. The **Human Resource Management Office (HRMO)** manages the civil service workforce and payroll records.

:::callout{type=local-example}
When the **SLPP** took over from the **APC** in **2018**, ministers changed but most permanent secretaries, directors and other civil servants remained in post. This **continuity** meant that salaries were still paid, schools still opened and records were not lost during the change of government: the civil service at work as the **permanent executive**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The **minister**, not the civil servant, answers publicly for a ministry's decisions. That is the principle of **anonymity** and **ministerial responsibility**. Civil servants advise privately, then carry out the minister's decision even if they disagreed.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Permanent Secretary**: the administrative head and accounting officer of a ministry, and chief adviser to the minister.
- **Political neutrality**: the rule that civil servants serve whichever government is in power and stay out of party politics.
- **Anonymity**: the rule that civil servants work out of public view, while the minister answers publicly for the ministry.
- **Public Service Commission**: the body that appoints, promotes and disciplines public officers on merit.
:::

## Key points

- The **civil service** is the permanent, professional body of officials who **advise ministers and implement policy**.
- Features: **permanence, neutrality, anonymity, impartiality, hierarchy, expertise, merit, security of tenure**.
- A ministry is led politically by the **Minister** and administratively by the **Permanent Secretary** (accounting officer).
- Functions: **implementing policy, advising ministers, drafting laws, budgeting, revenue collection, continuity, records, delegated legislation**.
- The **Public Service Commission** appoints, promotes and disciplines public officers on merit.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **define the civil service**, to **explain its features** (especially neutrality, anonymity and permanence), to **state its functions**, and to **describe the role of the Public Service Commission**. Questions may ask you to distinguish the civil service from the public service.
:::

## Quick check

1. Who is the administrative head of a ministry?
2. Explain the principle of anonymity in the civil service.
3. State three functions of the civil service.
4. What is the main function of the Public Service Commission?

**Answers:**

1. The Permanent Secretary.
2. Civil servants work behind the scenes and are not publicly named or blamed for policies; the minister takes public responsibility for the ministry.
3. Any three: implementing policy; advising ministers; drafting bills and regulations; preparing budget estimates; collecting revenue; providing continuity; keeping records; delegated legislation; representing government; delivering services.
4. To appoint, promote, transfer and discipline public officers on merit, free from political interference.
