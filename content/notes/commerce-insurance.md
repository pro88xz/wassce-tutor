# Insurance: principles, types and protection against risk

## Learning objectives
Define insurance, distinguish risk, peril and hazard, explain major insurance principles and identify common classes of cover.

## Meaning of insurance
Insurance is a contract under which an insurer agrees, in return for a premium, to provide specified financial protection against defined risks, subject to policy terms, conditions, limits and exclusions. The person or organisation covered is the insured; the company providing cover is the insurer; and the payment for cover is the premium.

A **risk** is the possibility of loss. A **peril** is the event that causes loss, such as fire or theft. A **hazard** is a condition that increases the likelihood or severity of loss, such as faulty wiring or poor security. Insurance transfers specified financial consequences; it does not prevent the event itself.

## Principles of insurance
**Utmost good faith:** parties must disclose material information honestly when required. **Insurable interest:** the insured must have a recognised financial or legal interest in the subject matter at the relevant time. **Indemnity:** for many general-insurance contracts, compensation aims to restore the insured to the financial position before the loss, not create a profit; life insurance is generally not a contract of indemnity. **Contribution:** where the same risk is insured by more than one insurer under relevant indemnity policies, insurers may share a claim. **Subrogation:** after paying a valid indemnity claim, an insurer may acquire the insured's rights to recover from a responsible third party, subject to law and policy. **Proximate cause:** the effective or dominant cause of a loss is considered when deciding whether it is covered.

## Common types
Life insurance pays a stated benefit on death or another insured life event. Fire insurance covers specified fire-related losses to property. Marine insurance covers defined risks involving ships, cargo or marine operations. Motor insurance covers specified liabilities or damage arising from vehicle use; compulsory minimum cover depends on law. Accident and property policies cover defined events or assets according to their terms.

The actual cover varies. A policyholder must read the schedule, exclusions, excess, limits and claim requirements.

## Importance to commerce
Insurance encourages trade and investment by reducing uncertainty about specified losses. A trader can insure a warehouse or goods in transit, while a transport business may insure vehicles and liabilities. Premiums are a cost of managing risk, not a guarantee against every loss.

## WASSCE examination focus
Distinguish risk from peril and hazard. Do not claim all insurance is indemnity-based: life insurance generally pays a predetermined benefit rather than reimbursing a measurable property loss.
