In much of Sierra Leone, radio is still the main source of news. A farmer in Kailahun, a trader in Kambia and a student in Freetown may all hear the same programme at the same time. Newspapers, television, websites and social media add to the flow. Together these are the **mass media**, and they shape what citizens know and think about government. This lesson explains the forms and roles of the mass media, the limits on press freedom and how the media are regulated in Sierra Leone.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define the mass media and identify their forms
- explain the roles of the mass media in a democracy
- explain press freedom and the factors that limit it
- describe how the media are regulated in Sierra Leone
- distinguish government-owned and privately owned media

## What are the mass media?

:::callout{type=definition}
The **mass media** are the means of communication that reach **large numbers of people at the same time**: newspapers and magazines (print), radio and television (broadcast), and websites and social media (online).
:::

## Roles of the mass media

1. **Informing** the public about government decisions, events and issues.
2. **Educating** citizens about their rights, duties and public policy.
3. **Watchdog role**: exposing corruption, abuse of power and wrongdoing.
4. **Agenda-setting**: deciding which issues receive attention.
5. **Platform for debate**: phone-ins, talk shows and letters let citizens express views.
6. **Shaping public opinion.**
7. **Mobilising citizens** for elections, public health campaigns and development.
8. **Linking government and people**, carrying messages in both directions.
9. **Promoting national unity** and culture.
10. **Entertainment.**

## Press freedom

:::callout{type=definition}
**Press freedom** is the right of the media to gather, publish and broadcast information and opinions **without censorship or interference** by the government, subject only to reasonable laws such as those against defamation and incitement to violence.
:::

**Factors that limit press freedom:**

- **Censorship** and restrictive laws, such as criminal libel and sedition laws
- **Ownership** of media houses by government or politicians, who influence content
- **Harassment, arrest or attacks** on journalists
- **Poor pay**, which exposes journalists to bribery ("brown envelope" journalism)
- **Lack of funds** and equipment, especially for small radio stations
- **Self-censorship** out of fear
- **Low literacy**, which limits the reach of newspapers
- **Fake news and misinformation**, which undermine trust

## Government-owned and private media

| Government-owned (public) | Privately owned |
|---|---|
| Funded mainly by the state | Funded by owners, advertising and sales |
| May favour the ruling party | May favour owners' interests or party |
| Wide national reach | Often more critical of government |
| Example: **Sierra Leone Broadcasting Corporation (SLBC)** | Examples: private radio stations, television channels and newspapers |

## The media in Sierra Leone

- The **Independent Media Commission (IMC)** registers and regulates media houses and handles complaints against them; it operates under the **Independent Media Commission Act 2020**.
- The **Sierra Leone Association of Journalists (SLAJ)** represents journalists and campaigns for press freedom.
- The **Sierra Leone Broadcasting Corporation (SLBC)** was created in **2010** as a public broadcaster, combining the old state broadcaster with the UN's radio station.
- **Community radio stations** across the districts broadcast in local languages, carrying news and phone-in programmes.

:::callout{type=local-example}
For decades, journalists in Sierra Leone could be prosecuted and jailed under the **criminal libel** provisions of **Part V of the Public Order Act 1965**. After a long campaign by SLAJ and others, Parliament **repealed** those provisions in **2020**. Disputes about published content are now handled through the IMC and civil courts rather than criminal prosecution, a major step for press freedom.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**"Explain five ways the mass media promote democracy."** Link each role to democracy:

- **Information:** voters can make informed choices at elections.
- **Watchdog:** exposure of corruption holds leaders accountable.
- **Platform for debate:** citizens take part in public discussion between elections.
- **Political education:** citizens learn their rights and duties.
- **Monitoring elections:** live reporting of results deters rigging.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Press freedom is not unlimited. Journalists can still be held responsible for **defamation** (in civil courts), **incitement to violence** and breaches of **professional standards**. A free press is one that is not **censored or controlled** by government, not one that is free from all law.
:::

## Key points

- The **mass media** are **print, broadcast and online** channels that reach many people at once.
- Roles: **inform, educate, watchdog, set the agenda, platform for debate, mobilise, link government and people, entertain**.
- **Press freedom** is limited by **censorship, political ownership, harassment, poor pay and fake news**.
- Sierra Leone: **IMC** regulates; **SLAJ** represents journalists; **SLBC (2010)** is the public broadcaster; **criminal libel repealed in 2020**.

## Quick check

1. Name the three main forms of the mass media.
2. What is meant by the "watchdog" role of the press?
3. Which body regulates the media in Sierra Leone?
4. What change did Sierra Leone make to its criminal libel law in 2020?

**Answers:**

1. Print (newspapers, magazines), broadcast (radio, television) and online (websites, social media).
2. Monitoring those in power and exposing corruption, abuse of power and wrongdoing.
3. The Independent Media Commission (IMC).
4. Parliament repealed the criminal libel provisions of Part V of the Public Order Act 1965, so journalists can no longer be jailed for libel under those provisions.
