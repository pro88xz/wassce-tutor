$\sqrt{4} = 2$. Easy — it's a rational number. But $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41421\ldots$ — the decimal continues forever without repeating. It can't be written as a fraction. Numbers like this — irrational square roots of non-perfect-square integers — are called **surds**.

Surds appear all over WAEC: in geometry (lengths of diagonals), in algebra (solutions to quadratics with the quadratic formula), in trigonometry (exact values of sine and cosine). WAEC tests surd manipulation directly in nearly every paper.

This lesson defines surds, distinguishes them from rationals, and introduces the key notation. The next lesson covers simplification, operations, and rationalising denominators.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Surd:** An irrational number that can be expressed as the $n$-th root of a positive integer that is not a perfect $n$-th power.

For square roots: $\sqrt{a}$ is a surd if $a$ is a positive integer that is not a perfect square.

**Examples of surds:** $\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}, \sqrt{5}, \sqrt{6}, \sqrt{7}, \sqrt{10}, \sqrt{11}, \sqrt{12}, \ldots$

**NOT surds (rational results):** $\sqrt{1} = 1, \sqrt{4} = 2, \sqrt{9} = 3, \sqrt{16} = 4, \sqrt{25} = 5, \ldots$ (perfect squares).

**Cube roots:** $\sqrt[3]{2}, \sqrt[3]{4}, \sqrt[3]{5}$ are surds. $\sqrt[3]{8} = 2, \sqrt[3]{27} = 3$ are not.
:::


## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a surd and distinguish surds from rational numbers
- recognise square roots, cube roots and higher roots that are surds
- use the product and quotient properties of surds
- connect surds with fractional indices

## Recognising surds

### Square roots

The square roots of perfect squares are NOT surds (they're integers): $\sqrt{1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, \ldots}$.

The square roots of all other positive integers ARE surds: $\sqrt{2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, \ldots}$.

### Higher roots

For cube roots: $\sqrt[3]{a}$ is a surd if $a$ is not a perfect cube.

Perfect cubes: $1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729, 1000$.

So $\sqrt[3]{8} = 2$ (not a surd), $\sqrt[3]{27} = 3$ (not), but $\sqrt[3]{2}, \sqrt[3]{4}, \sqrt[3]{5}, \sqrt[3]{6}, \sqrt[3]{7}$ are all surds.

## Why surds matter

Surds can't be represented exactly by a terminating or repeating decimal. They must be expressed either:
- As the radical itself (e.g., $\sqrt{2}$).
- As a numerical approximation (e.g., $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$).

WAEC typically asks for the **exact** answer — in surd form — rather than a decimal approximation.

## Notation

- $\sqrt{a}$ or $a^{1/2}$ — the **square root** of $a$.
- $\sqrt[n]{a}$ or $a^{1/n}$ — the **$n$-th root** of $a$.
- $\sqrt[3]{a}$ — cube root.
- The expression inside the radical sign is called the **radicand**.
- A surd of the form $\sqrt[n]{a}$ where $a$ is positive and not a perfect $n$-th power is a **pure surd**.
- A surd combined with rational numbers, like $3\sqrt{2}$ or $2 + \sqrt{5}$, is a **mixed expression**.

## Connection to indices

From the Indices topic:
$$\sqrt[n]{a} = a^{1/n}$$

So $\sqrt{2} = 2^{1/2}$, $\sqrt[3]{5} = 5^{1/3}$, $\sqrt[4]{7} = 7^{1/4}$.

This lets us use the laws of indices to manipulate surds.

## Key properties of surds

### Product property

$$\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab}$$

This works for any $a, b \geq 0$.

Examples:
- $\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{3} = \sqrt{6}$.
- $\sqrt{5} \times \sqrt{7} = \sqrt{35}$.
- $\sqrt{6} \times \sqrt{2} = \sqrt{12}$.
- $\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{a} = \sqrt{a^2} = a$. (Squaring undoes square root.)

### Quotient property

$$\dfrac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{a}{b}} \quad (b > 0)$$

Examples:
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{8}}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{8}{2}} = \sqrt{4} = 2$.
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{12}}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{4} = 2$.
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{15}}{\sqrt{5}} = \sqrt{3}$.

### Power property

$$(\sqrt{a})^n = a^{n/2}$$

For $n = 2$: $(\sqrt{a})^2 = a$. This is the inverse of taking the square root.

### Negation of square root

The square root function returns the **non-negative** root by convention. So $\sqrt{4} = 2$, not $-2$. The negative root is $-\sqrt{4} = -2$.

*Note:* you can never take the square root of a negative number in the real number system. $\sqrt{-4}$ is not a real number. (It's a complex number, $2i$, beyond WAEC scope.)

## Examples — distinguishing surds and rationals

### Example 1

**Which of the following are surds, and which are rationals?**

(a) $\sqrt{16}$ — $\sqrt{16} = 4$, rational (not a surd).
(b) $\sqrt{20}$ — $20$ is not a perfect square. Surd.
(c) $\sqrt{49}$ — $\sqrt{49} = 7$, rational.
(d) $\sqrt[3]{27}$ — $\sqrt[3]{27} = 3$, rational.
(e) $\sqrt[3]{10}$ — $10$ is not a perfect cube. Surd.
(f) $\sqrt{0.04}$ — $\sqrt{0.04} = 0.2$, rational.
(g) $\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{2}}$ — contains a surd, the expression is irrational. (We would need to rationalise it: see the next lesson.)
(h) $\sqrt{2} + 3$ — contains a surd, irrational.

## Approximating surds

For problems where exact form isn't required, approximate by knowing nearby perfect squares.

### Example 2

**Estimate $\sqrt{50}$.**

Perfect squares near $50$: $49$ ($7^2$) and $64$ ($8^2$). So $\sqrt{49} = 7, \sqrt{64} = 8$. Therefore $\sqrt{50}$ is between $7$ and $8$, closer to $7$.

More precisely: $7^2 = 49$, $7.1^2 = 50.41$. So $\sqrt{50} \approx 7.07$.

### Example 3

**Estimate $\sqrt{75}$.**

$8^2 = 64, 9^2 = 81$. So between $8$ and $9$, closer to $9$. 
$8.5^2 = 72.25$, $8.7^2 = 75.69$. So $\sqrt{75} \approx 8.66$.

## When the radical is in algebraic form

Surds also appear with variables:

- $\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$ (absolute value, since the square root is non-negative).
- $\sqrt{x^2 y^2} = |xy|$ if $x, y$ have known signs; or $|x| \cdot |y|$ in general.
- $\sqrt{4x} = 2\sqrt{x}$ (if $x \geq 0$).

WAEC usually works with positive variables, so we often drop the absolute value: $\sqrt{x^2} = x$ (assuming $x \geq 0$).

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**For each, decide if it is a surd, rational, or undefined in the reals.**

**(a)** $\sqrt{25}$. Rational ($= 5$).

**(b)** $\sqrt{30}$. Surd.

**(c)** $\sqrt[3]{125}$. Rational ($= 5$).

**(d)** $\sqrt[3]{20}$. Surd.

**(e)** $\sqrt{-9}$. Undefined in reals.

**(f)** $\sqrt{0}$. Rational ($= 0$).

**(g)** $\sqrt{1.44}$. Rational ($= 1.2$).

**(h)** $\sqrt{2.5}$. Surd.

**(i)** $\sqrt{0.04}$. Rational ($= 0.2$).

**(j)** $\sqrt[4]{16}$. Rational ($= 2$).
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
The Sierra Leonean architect designing a building needs precise diagonal measurements. For a rectangular floor that is $3$ m by $4$ m, the diagonal is computed by Pythagoras: $d = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$ m. Rational — no surd needed.

But for a square floor $1$ m by $1$ m, the diagonal is $d = \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$ m. The diagonal is a surd.

Surds arise wherever irrational lengths appear — in nature (the golden ratio), in physics (relativity), in geometry (the unit circle). Working with surds in exact form is a core mathematical skill, exactly as WAEC tests.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Common errors with surds:**

**1. Thinking $\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$.**
- $\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b} \neq \sqrt{a + b}$ in general. (E.g., $\sqrt{4 + 9} = \sqrt{13} \neq \sqrt{4} + \sqrt{9} = 5$.)
- The correct product rule: $\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab}$. (Multiplication, not addition.)

**2. Treating $\sqrt{x^2}$ as just $x$ when $x$ might be negative.**
- $\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$. For positive $x$, this is $x$.

**3. Taking square root of a negative.**
- $\sqrt{-4}$ is not a real number. Don't write it as $-2$.

**4. Confusing $\sqrt[3]{a}$ with $\sqrt{a}^3$.**
- $\sqrt[3]{a}$ is the cube root: $a^{1/3}$.
- $(\sqrt{a})^3 = a^{3/2}$ — different.

**5. Treating $\sqrt{2}$ as exactly $1.414$.**
- $1.414^2 = 1.999396$, not $2$. The decimal approximation is just that — an approximation.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Irrational number**: a number that cannot be written as a fraction of two integers, such as $\sqrt{2}$ or $\pi$.
- **Rational number**: a number that can be written as a fraction of two integers, such as $\sqrt{0.25} = 0.5$.
:::

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Foundational surd questions in WAEC:

- **Identify surds.** *Which of the following is a surd? $\sqrt{4}, \sqrt{6}, \sqrt{9}, \sqrt{12}$.* (Answers: $\sqrt{6}$ and $\sqrt{12}$.)
- **Product of surds.** *Compute $\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{12}$.* (Answer: $6$.)
- **Quotient.** *Compute $\dfrac{\sqrt{20}}{\sqrt{5}}$.* (Answer: $2$.)
- **Recognize rational vs surd.** *Is $\sqrt{0.25}$ a surd?* (No — it's $0.5$.)
- **Compute $(\sqrt{a})^2$.** *Find $(\sqrt{7})^2$.* (Answer: $7$.)

Master the definitions and the product/quotient properties; simplification comes next.
:::
