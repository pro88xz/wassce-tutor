A **ratio** compares two or more quantities. *In a Sierra Leonean classroom, the ratio of boys to girls is $3 : 5$.* That means for every $3$ boys, there are $5$ girls. If there are $24$ boys, there are $40$ girls. If there are $9$ boys, there are $15$ girls. The ratio captures the **relative size** of the two groups without fixing the actual numbers.

Ratios are everywhere: recipes (rice to water $2:1$), maps (scale $1:50000$), business (profit-sharing $3:2:1$), sports (win-loss $4:1$). WAEC tests ratio problems in every paper.

This lesson introduces ratios, the standard notation, how to simplify them, how to convert between ratios and fractions/decimals, and how to compare ratios.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Ratio:** A way to compare two or more quantities of the same kind.

**Notation:** $a : b$ (read "$a$ to $b$"), where $a$ and $b$ are the two quantities being compared.

**Three-way ratio:** $a : b : c$ compares three quantities.

**Equivalent ratios:** Different ratios with the same proportional relationship. $1 : 2 = 2 : 4 = 3 : 6 = 5 : 10$.

**Simplest form:** A ratio is in simplest form when the parts share no common factor larger than $1$.
:::


## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain what a ratio compares
- write ratios in different forms and simplify them
- express ratios with units in the same units
- compare ratios and write them in the form $1 : n$

## Reading and writing ratios

Ratios can be written in three ways. Each is equivalent:

1. **Colon notation:** $3 : 5$.
2. **Word form:** "three to five."
3. **Fraction form:** $\dfrac{3}{5}$.

The order matters! $3 : 5$ is NOT the same as $5 : 3$. The first quantity is mentioned first.

### Examples

- *Boys to girls in a class:* $3 : 5$. 
 - For every $3$ boys, $5$ girls.
 - The boy-fraction of the class is $\dfrac{3}{3 + 5} = \dfrac{3}{8}$.
 - The girl-fraction is $\dfrac{5}{8}$.

- *Profit-sharing among three partners:* $4 : 3 : 2$. 
 - Total parts $= 4 + 3 + 2 = 9$.
 - First partner gets $\dfrac{4}{9}$, second $\dfrac{3}{9} = \dfrac{1}{3}$, third $\dfrac{2}{9}$.

- *Mixing rice and water in a recipe:* $2 : 1$. 
 - $2$ cups of rice for every $1$ cup of water.
 - For $4$ cups of water, you'd need $8$ cups of rice.

## Simplifying ratios

Divide both parts by their greatest common divisor (GCD).

### Example 1

**Simplify $12 : 18$.**

GCD of $12$ and $18$ is $6$. Divide both by $6$: $12/6 : 18/6 = 2 : 3$.

### Example 2

**Simplify $25 : 15$.**

GCD is $5$. $25/5 : 15/5 = 5 : 3$.

### Example 3

**Simplify $36 : 48 : 60$.**

GCD of all three is $12$. $36/12 : 48/12 : 60/12 = 3 : 4 : 5$.

### Example 4

**Simplify $0.6 : 1.2$.**

Multiply both by $10$ to clear decimals: $6 : 12$. GCD is $6$. Simplify: $1 : 2$.

### Example 5

**Simplify $\dfrac{1}{2} : \dfrac{1}{3}$.**

Multiply both by the LCD ($6$): $\dfrac{6}{2} : \dfrac{6}{3} = 3 : 2$.

### Example 6

**Simplify $0.4 : 0.7$.**

Multiply by $10$: $4 : 7$. Already in simplest form.

### Example 7

**Simplify $\dfrac{2}{5} : \dfrac{3}{10} : \dfrac{1}{2}$.**

LCD = $10$. Multiply all by $10$: $4 : 3 : 5$. Already in simplest form.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Simplify each ratio.**

**(a)** $20 : 30$. GCD $= 10$. $2 : 3$.

**(b)** $45 : 60$. GCD $= 15$. $3 : 4$.

**(c)** $24 : 16 : 8$. GCD $= 8$. $3 : 2 : 1$.

**(d)** $0.5 : 0.25$. Multiply by $100$: $50 : 25$. Simplify: $2 : 1$.

**(e)** $\dfrac{1}{4} : \dfrac{1}{6}$. LCD $= 12$. $3 : 2$.

**(f)** $1\dfrac{1}{2} : 2\dfrac{1}{4}$. Convert: $\dfrac{3}{2} : \dfrac{9}{4}$. LCD $= 4$. $6 : 9$. Simplify: $2 : 3$.

**(g)** $50\% : 30\%$. As decimals: $0.5 : 0.3$. Multiply by $10$: $5 : 3$.

**(h)** $200$ g $: 0.5$ kg. Convert to same units: $200 : 500$ g. Simplify: $2 : 5$.
:::

## Ratios with units

**Important:** the quantities being compared must be in the **same units**.

### Example 8

**Express $30$ minutes to $1$ hour as a ratio.**

Convert to same units. $30$ minutes $: 60$ minutes $= 30 : 60 = 1 : 2$.

### Example 9

**Express $500$ g to $2$ kg as a ratio.**

Convert to same units. $500$ g $: 2000$ g $= 500 : 2000 = 1 : 4$.

### Example 10

**Express $25$ cm to $1$ m as a ratio.**

$25$ cm $: 100$ cm $= 25 : 100 = 1 : 4$.

### Example 11

**Express $150$ Nigerian naira to $30$ NLe (assuming $1$ NLe = $5$ NGN) as a ratio.**

First convert to one currency. $150$ NGN = $30$ NLe (at the given rate).

So $30$ NLe $: 30$ NLe $= 1 : 1$.

Or if the rate is different (say $1$ NLe = $4$ NGN), $150$ NGN = $37.5$ NLe.
Ratio: $37.5 : 30 = 5 : 4$.

*Note:* the answer depends on the conversion rate used.

## Converting ratios to fractions and percentages

If a ratio is $a : b$, then $a$'s share of the total is $\dfrac{a}{a + b}$.

### Example 12

**A class has boys to girls in ratio $3 : 5$. What fraction of the class is boys? girls?**

Total parts $= 8$. 
Boy-fraction $= \dfrac{3}{8} = 37.5\%$. 
Girl-fraction $= \dfrac{5}{8} = 62.5\%$.

### Example 13

**A profit of $9000$ NLe is shared among three partners in the ratio $4 : 3 : 2$. What percentage does each receive?**

Total parts $= 9$. 
First: $\dfrac{4}{9} \approx 44.4\%$. 
Second: $\dfrac{3}{9} \approx 33.3\%$. 
Third: $\dfrac{2}{9} \approx 22.2\%$.

## Equivalent ratios

Multiplying or dividing both parts by the same nonzero number gives an equivalent ratio.

### Example 14

**Find three ratios equivalent to $2 : 3$.**

- Multiply by $2$: $4 : 6$.
- Multiply by $5$: $10 : 15$.
- Multiply by $10$: $20 : 30$.
- Divide by $2$: $1 : 1.5$ (or in fractions, $\dfrac{1}{1} : \dfrac{3}{2}$, the same thing).

### Example 15

**Are $4 : 6$ and $6 : 9$ equivalent?**

Simplify both: $4 : 6 = 2 : 3$ (divide by $2$). $6 : 9 = 2 : 3$ (divide by $3$). Both simplify to $2 : 3$. Equivalent. ✓

### Example 16

**Are $5 : 8$ and $10 : 15$ equivalent?**

Simplify: $5 : 8$ is already in simplest form. $10 : 15 = 2 : 3$.

NOT equivalent.

## The cross-multiplication test

Two ratios $a : b$ and $c : d$ are equivalent iff $ad = bc$.

### Example 17

**Test if $2 : 5$ and $6 : 15$ are equivalent.**

Cross-multiply: $2 \times 15 = 30$, $5 \times 6 = 30$. Equal. Equivalent ✓.

### Example 18

**Test if $3 : 4$ and $9 : 11$ are equivalent.**

$3 \times 11 = 33$, $4 \times 9 = 36$. Not equal. Not equivalent.

## Finding the missing part of a ratio

If $a : b = c : x$, then $ax = bc$, so $x = \dfrac{bc}{a}$.

### Example 19

**If $3 : 5 = 12 : x$, find $x$.**

$3x = 5 \times 12 = 60$. $x = 20$.

### Example 20

**If $2 : 7 = x : 21$, find $x$.**

$2 \times 21 = 7x = 42$. $x = 6$.

### Example 21

**If $4 : 6 = x : 15$, find $x$.**

$4 \times 15 = 6x = 60$. $x = 10$.

## Three-term ratios

For a ratio $a : b : c$, you can also find missing parts using cross-multiplication on pairs.

### Example 22

**If $2 : 3 : 5 = 8 : x : y$, find $x$ and $y$.**

From $2 : 3 = 8 : x$: $2x = 24$, $x = 12$.
From $2 : 5 = 8 : y$: $2y = 40$, $y = 20$.

So the equivalent ratio is $8 : 12 : 20$.

## Comparing ratios

To determine which of two ratios is larger, convert to common form (decimals, fractions with same denominator, or percentages).

### Example 23

**Which is larger: $3 : 5$ or $4 : 7$?**

As fractions: $\dfrac{3}{5} = 0.6$ and $\dfrac{4}{7} \approx 0.571$. So $3 : 5 > 4 : 7$.

Alternatively: cross-multiply for direct comparison. $3 \times 7 = 21$ and $5 \times 4 = 20$. Since $21 > 20$, the ratio $3 : 5$ is larger.

### Example 24

**Arrange in increasing order: $2 : 3, 3 : 5, 4 : 7$.**

Decimal: $\dfrac{2}{3} \approx 0.667, \dfrac{3}{5} = 0.6, \dfrac{4}{7} \approx 0.571$.

Increasing: $4 : 7, 3 : 5, 2 : 3$.

## Ratios and proportions in word problems

Many WAEC problems describe a ratio and ask you to find specific quantities.

### Example 25

**A school has $200$ students. The ratio of boys to girls is $3 : 5$. How many boys?**

Total parts $= 8$. Each part = $200/8 = 25$ students. 
Boys = $3 \times 25 = 75$. 
Girls = $5 \times 25 = 125$. 
Check: $75 + 125 = 200$. ✓

### Example 26

**A recipe uses rice and water in the ratio $2 : 3$. If you use $6$ cups of rice, how much water?**

Ratio $2 : 3 = 6 : x$. $2x = 18$. $x = 9$ cups of water.

### Example 27

**Two people share a bonus in ratio $5 : 3$. The first receives $1000$ NLe more than the second. How much does each receive?**

Let first $= 5k$ and second $= 3k$ for some constant $k$. Difference: $5k - 3k = 2k = 1000$, so $k = 500$.

First $= 5 \times 500 = 2500$ NLe. Second $= 3 \times 500 = 1500$ NLe.

Verify: difference $= 1000$. ✓

### Example 28

**In a Sierra Leonean village, the ratio of farms growing rice, cassava, and yams is $4 : 3 : 2$. If there are $36$ farms in total, how many of each type?**

Total parts $= 9$. Each part $= 36/9 = 4$ farms. 
Rice farms $= 4 \times 4 = 16$. 
Cassava farms $= 4 \times 3 = 12$. 
Yam farms $= 4 \times 2 = 8$. 
Check: $16 + 12 + 8 = 36$. ✓

:::callout{type=local-example}
In a Sierra Leonean palm wine cooperative, three vendors split daily proceeds. They contribute palm wine in the ratio $4 : 5 : 6$. If the total day's sales are $7500$ NLe, how much does each get?

Total parts $= 15$. Each part $= 7500/15 = 500$ NLe.

Vendor 1: $4 \times 500 = 2000$ NLe. 
Vendor 2: $5 \times 500 = 2500$ NLe. 
Vendor 3: $6 \times 500 = 3000$ NLe.

Verify: $2000 + 2500 + 3000 = 7500$. ✓

This kind of proportional sharing is everywhere in West African business: market vendors splitting profits, family inheritance, kontri organizations. The ratio captures the agreed proportions exactly.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Common errors with ratios:**

**1. Reversing the order.**
- $3 : 5$ ≠ $5 : 3$. The order matters.

**2. Mixing units.**
- Always convert to the same units before forming a ratio. $30$ cm to $1$ m is $30 : 100$, not $30 : 1$.

**3. Forgetting to simplify.**
- $20 : 30$ should be $2 : 3$. WAEC expects simplest form.

**4. Confusing ratio with fraction.**
- The ratio $3 : 5$ means $3$ to $5$ (so the first item is $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of the total, not $\dfrac{3}{5}$).

**5. Wrong total parts.**
- For $3 : 5$, total parts $= 8$ (i.e., $3 + 5$), not $5 - 3 = 2$.

**6. Wrong direction in word problems.**
- *"Boys to girls $3 : 5$, $40$ girls"* — the unknown is boys, but make sure you set up the cross-multiplication correctly. $3 : 5 = x : 40$ gives $x = 24$ boys.
:::

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Foundational ratio questions in WAEC:

- **Simplify a ratio.** *Simplify $24 : 36$.* (Answer: $2 : 3$.)
- **Ratio with units.** *Express $250$ g to $1$ kg as a ratio.* (Answer: $1 : 4$.)
- **Find the unknown.** *If $3 : 5 = 9 : x$, find $x$.* (Answer: $15$.)
- **Convert to fraction/percentage.** *In ratio $2 : 3$, what fraction is the first part?* (Answer: $\dfrac{2}{5}$.)
- **Word problem with sharing.** *Share $1200$ NLe in ratio $3 : 5$.* (Answer: $450$ and $750$.)

Master the basics — simplification, equivalence, conversion — before tackling word problems.
:::

## Practice problems

**1.** Simplify each ratio.
(a) $36 : 24$
(b) $45 : 90$
(c) $48 : 60 : 84$
(d) $\dfrac{1}{2} : \dfrac{1}{4}$
(e) $0.8 : 1.6$
(f) $1\dfrac{1}{2} : 3$

**2.** Express each as a ratio in simplest form.
(a) $30$ minutes to $2$ hours.
(b) $400$ g to $1$ kg.
(c) $50$ cm to $1.5$ m.
(d) $20\%$ to $40\%$.

**3.** Find the missing value.
(a) $4 : 5 = x : 25$.
(b) $7 : 3 = 28 : x$.
(c) $1 : 2 = x : 10 = 7 : y$.

**4.** Are these ratios equivalent? Show your working.
(a) $6 : 8$ and $15 : 20$.
(b) $3 : 4$ and $9 : 13$.

**5.** A class has $24$ boys and $36$ girls. What is the ratio of boys to girls in simplest form?

**6.** A school spends $30000$ NLe in the ratio $5 : 7$ between equipment and salaries. How much for each?

**7.** Aminata, Mohamed, and Foday split a $48000$ NLe inheritance in the ratio $3 : 4 : 5$. How much does each receive?

**8.** A recipe uses sugar, flour, and butter in ratio $1 : 3 : 2$. If you use $300$ g of flour, how much sugar and butter?

**9.** A bonus of $12000$ NLe is shared between two people. The first receives $2000$ NLe more than the second. In what ratio is the bonus shared?

**10.** Two roads have lengths in ratio $2 : 5$. If the shorter is $40$ km, find the longer.

**11.** Convert each ratio to a fraction and a percentage.
(a) $3 : 7$.
(b) $1 : 4$.
(c) $4 : 6$.

**12.** Which is larger: $2 : 5$ or $3 : 8$?

**13.** Arrange in decreasing order: $1 : 4, 2 : 9, 1 : 5$.

**14.** A mixture of palm wine and water is in ratio $4 : 1$. If the mixture is $25$ litres, how much palm wine and how much water?

### Worked solutions

**1.** (a) GCD $= 12$. $3 : 2$. (b) GCD $= 45$. $1 : 2$. (c) GCD $= 12$. $4 : 5 : 7$. (d) LCD $= 4$. $2 : 1$. (e) ×10: $8 : 16 = 1 : 2$. (f) $\dfrac{3}{2} : 3 = 3 : 6 = 1 : 2$.

**2.** (a) $30 : 120 = 1 : 4$. (b) $400 : 1000 = 2 : 5$. (c) $50 : 150 = 1 : 3$. (d) Cancel %: $20 : 40 = 1 : 2$.

**3.** (a) $4x = 100$, $x = 20$. (b) $3 \times 28 = 84$, $7x = 84$, $x = 12$. (c) From $1 : 2 = x : 10$: $x = 5$. From $1 : 2 = 7 : y$: $y = 14$.

**4.** (a) Simplify: $6 : 8 = 3 : 4$. $15 : 20 = 3 : 4$. Equivalent ✓. (b) $3 : 4$ already simplest. $9 : 13$ — cross-multiply: $3 \times 13 = 39$, $4 \times 9 = 36$. Not equivalent.

**5.** $24 : 36$. GCD $= 12$. $2 : 3$.

**6.** Total $= 12$ parts. Each part $= 30000/12 = 2500$. Equipment: $5 \times 2500 = 12500$ NLe. Salaries: $7 \times 2500 = 17500$ NLe.

**7.** Total parts $= 12$. Each part $= 48000/12 = 4000$. Aminata: $12000$, Mohamed: $16000$, Foday: $20000$ NLe.

**8.** Flour is $3$ parts $= 300$ g. So $1$ part $= 100$ g. Sugar = $100$ g. Butter = $2 \times 100 = 200$ g.

**9.** Let parts be $5k$ and $3k$ (first vs second). Total $5k + 3k = 8k = 12000$, $k = 1500$. First $= 7500$, second $= 4500$. Difference $= 3000$. Doesn't match. Let me redo. 
Let first $= a$, second $= b$, $a + b = 12000$, $a - b = 2000$. Solve: $a = 7000$, $b = 5000$. Ratio: $7000 : 5000 = 7 : 5$.

**10.** $2 : 5 = 40 : x$. $2x = 200$, $x = 100$ km.

**11.** (a) $\dfrac{3}{10}$ of $30\%$. (b) $\dfrac{1}{5} = 20\%$. (c) $\dfrac{4}{10} = 40\%$.

**12.** Cross-multiply: $2 \times 8 = 16$, $5 \times 3 = 15$. $16 > 15$, so $2 : 5 > 3 : 8$.

**13.** Decimal: $\dfrac{1}{4} = 0.25$, $\dfrac{2}{9} \approx 0.222$, $\dfrac{1}{5} = 0.2$. Decreasing: $1 : 4, 2 : 9, 1 : 5$.

**14.** Total parts $= 5$. Each part $= 25/5 = 5$ litres. Palm wine: $4 \times 5 = 20$ litres. Water: $5$ litres.
