## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define capital and describe its types
- explain capital formation and why it matters
- describe the functions of the entrepreneur

## Capital

:::callout{type=definition}
**Capital** is any **man-made** good used to produce other goods and services. Examples are machines, tools, buildings, vehicles and raw materials held by a firm.
:::

Capital goods are not wanted for their own sake but for what they help to produce. A sewing machine in a tailor's shop is capital; the same machine bought as a gift for someone who never uses it to earn a living is a consumer good.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
In everyday speech, "capital" often means money. In economics, **money is not capital** by itself: it becomes capital only when it is used to buy machines, tools or materials for production. A trader who keeps cash under the mattress has savings, not capital.
:::

### Types of capital

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed capital** | Lasts a long time and is used again and again | A rice mill, a fishing boat, a delivery van |
| **Working (circulating) capital** | Used up in one round of production | Paddy rice for milling, fuel, cloth for a tailor |
| **Social capital** | Owned by society and used by everyone | Roads, bridges, schools, hospitals |

### Features of capital

1. It is **man-made**.
2. It **depreciates**: machines wear out and lose value over time.
3. Its supply can be **increased** through saving and investment.
4. It is fairly **mobile**: many capital goods can be moved or used for other purposes.
5. Its reward is **interest**.

### Capital formation

:::callout{type=definition}
**Capital formation** (or capital accumulation) is the process of increasing the stock of capital goods in an economy. It requires **saving** (giving up some consumption today) and **investment** (using those savings to buy capital goods).
:::

Capital formation matters because more and better capital raises the productivity of labour, increases output and helps the economy grow. In many developing countries it is held back by low incomes (so little can be saved), poor banking services and political instability.

## The entrepreneur

:::callout{type=definition}
The **entrepreneur** is the person who **organises** the other factors of production, **makes the key decisions** and **bears the risks** of a business. The entrepreneur's reward is **profit**, and the risk is loss.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
A woman in Bo who rents a shop (land), employs two assistants (labour) and buys a freezer and stock (capital) to sell cold drinks and fish is an entrepreneur. If her sales cover her costs with something left over, she earns a profit; if not, she bears the loss.
:::

### Functions of the entrepreneur

1. **Organising production**: bringing land, labour and capital together in the right amounts.
2. **Decision-making**: what to produce, how much, where and by which method.
3. **Risk-bearing**: putting money into a business without knowing whether it will succeed.
4. **Innovation**: introducing new products, new methods or new markets.
5. **Controlling and supervising**: making sure work is done well and resources are not wasted.

### Uninsurable risks

Some risks can be insured against (fire, theft, accidents), but others cannot, such as a fall in demand, a change in fashion or a new competitor. Bearing these **uninsurable risks** is the special job of the entrepreneur, and profit is the reward for it.

## The four factors together

| Factor | Supply can be increased? | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| Land | No (fixed), though it can be improved | Rent |
| Labour | Yes, over time (population, migration, training) | Wages |
| Capital | Yes (saving and investment) | Interest |
| Entrepreneur | Yes (training, a better business climate) | Profit |

## Key points

- **Capital** is man-made goods used in production; its reward is **interest**.
- Types: **fixed**, **working (circulating)** and **social** capital.
- **Capital formation** needs saving and investment.
- The **entrepreneur** organises production, makes decisions and bears **uninsurable risks**; the reward is **profit**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Common questions: **distinguish fixed from working capital** with examples, **explain capital formation**, and **state the functions of the entrepreneur**. Always mention that profit rewards *risk-bearing*, and that the entrepreneur is the factor that combines the other three.
:::

## Quick check

1. Is the fuel used by a fishing boat fixed or working capital?
2. Why is money on its own not counted as capital?
3. What is the special risk that only the entrepreneur bears?

**Answers:** 1. Working (circulating) capital: it is used up in one round of production. 2. Because capital means man-made goods used in production; money becomes capital only when it is spent on such goods. 3. Uninsurable risks, such as a fall in demand or a change in consumers' tastes.
