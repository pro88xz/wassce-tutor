Electrolysis uses electricity to force a reaction. An **electrochemical cell** does the reverse: it uses a spontaneous redox reaction to **produce** electricity. Every torch battery, phone battery and car battery is one. This lesson explains how a simple cell works, the electrochemical series that predicts which metal reacts, and how to write cell notation.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- distinguish an electrochemical (galvanic) cell from an electrolytic cell
- describe the Daniell cell and explain how it produces a current
- state the function of the salt bridge
- use the electrochemical series to predict displacement and the direction of electron flow
- write cell notation (cell diagrams)

## Electrochemical and electrolytic cells

| Electrochemical (galvanic) cell | Electrolytic cell |
|---|---|
| Converts **chemical energy into electrical energy** | Converts **electrical energy into chemical energy** |
| Reaction is **spontaneous** | Reaction is **forced** by an external supply |
| Anode is **negative**, cathode is **positive** | Anode is **positive**, cathode is **negative** |
| Examples: Daniell cell, dry cell, car battery | Examples: electroplating, refining copper |

In **both** types, **oxidation occurs at the anode** and **reduction at the cathode**.

## The Daniell cell

![The Daniell cell: zinc in zinc sulphate and copper in copper(II) sulphate, joined by a salt bridge and a voltmeter; electrons flow from zinc to copper and the voltage is about 1.10 V](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/daniell-cell.svg)

*Figure 1. The Daniell cell. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

- **Zinc half-cell:** a zinc rod in zinc sulphate solution. Zinc is more reactive, so it loses electrons and dissolves:

$$\text{Zn(s)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \qquad \text{(oxidation: anode, negative)}$$

- **Copper half-cell:** a copper rod in copper(II) sulphate solution. Cu²⁺ ions gain the electrons and deposit copper:

$$\text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu(s)} \qquad \text{(reduction: cathode, positive)}$$

- **Overall reaction:**

$$\text{Zn(s)} + \text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + \text{Cu(s)}$$

- **Electrons flow through the external wire from zinc to copper.** The voltmeter reads about **1.10 V**.

**Observations over time:** the zinc rod gets thinner, the copper rod gets thicker, and the blue colour of the copper(II) sulphate fades.

### The salt bridge

The salt bridge is a tube or strip of filter paper soaked in a solution of an unreactive salt, such as potassium nitrate. It:

1. **completes the circuit** by letting ions move between the half-cells
2. **keeps each solution electrically neutral**: anions move towards the zinc half-cell (where Zn²⁺ is building up) and cations towards the copper half-cell (where Cu²⁺ is being used up)
3. prevents the two solutions from **mixing directly**

Without the salt bridge, the current stops at once.

## The electrochemical series

Metals (and hydrogen) can be arranged in order of their **tendency to lose electrons** and form ions:

| Most reactive (lose electrons most readily) | → | Least reactive |
|---|---|---|
| K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al | Zn, Fe, Sn, Pb, **H** | Cu, Hg, Ag, Au |

**Uses of the series:**

1. **Displacement:** a metal displaces any metal **below** it from a solution of its salt. Iron displaces copper from CuSO₄, but copper cannot displace iron.
2. **Reaction with acids:** metals **above hydrogen** displace hydrogen from dilute acids; those below (Cu, Ag, Au) do not.
3. **Cells:** in a cell made from two metals, the **higher** metal is the **negative electrode** (anode), and electrons flow from it to the lower metal.
4. The **further apart** the two metals are in the series, the **larger the voltage** of the cell.
5. **Electrolysis:** ions of metals **lower** in the series are discharged more easily at the cathode.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Predicting a cell.** A cell is made from magnesium in MgSO₄(aq) and copper in CuSO₄(aq).

- Magnesium is higher in the series, so it is the **negative electrode (anode)**: Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻.
- Copper is the **positive electrode (cathode)**: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu.
- Electrons flow from **magnesium to copper**.
- Mg and Cu are further apart than Zn and Cu, so the voltage is **greater than 1.10 V** (about 2.7 V).
:::

## Cell notation

A cell diagram is a short way of writing a cell:

$$\text{Zn(s)} \,|\, \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} \,\|\, \text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} \,|\, \text{Cu(s)}$$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Single line \| | A phase boundary (solid electrode in contact with solution) |
| Double line ‖ | The **salt bridge** |
| Left-hand side | The **anode** (oxidation) |
| Right-hand side | The **cathode** (reduction) |

Read the notation from left to right as the direction of **electron flow** in the external circuit: from zinc to copper.

:::callout{type=local-example}
A simple "fruit battery" can be made with a lime or lemon, a strip of zinc (from a galvanised nail) and a strip of copper (from electrical wire). The fruit juice acts as the electrolyte. The zinc is the negative electrode, and the voltage, about 0.9 V, is enough to light an LED when several are joined in series.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
In an electrochemical cell the **anode is negative**, the opposite of electrolysis. The rule that never changes is: **oxidation at the anode, reduction at the cathode**.
:::

## Key points

- An **electrochemical cell** turns a **spontaneous redox reaction** into electricity; an **electrolytic cell** uses electricity to force a reaction.
- In the **Daniell cell**, zinc is oxidised (anode, −) and Cu²⁺ is reduced (cathode, +); electrons flow **Zn → Cu**; voltage ≈ **1.10 V**.
- The **salt bridge** completes the circuit and keeps the solutions neutral.
- In the **electrochemical series**, higher metals displace lower ones, react with acids if above H, and form the negative electrode.
- Cell notation: **Zn | Zn²⁺ ‖ Cu²⁺ | Cu** (anode on the left).

## Quick check

1. State one difference between an electrochemical cell and an electrolytic cell.
2. In the Daniell cell, which electrode is negative?
3. State two functions of a salt bridge.
4. Will copper displace silver from silver nitrate solution? Explain.

**Answers:**

1. Any one: it produces rather than uses electricity; the reaction is spontaneous; the anode is negative.
2. The zinc electrode.
3. Completes the circuit; keeps the solutions electrically neutral; stops the solutions mixing.
4. Yes: copper is above silver in the electrochemical series, so it loses electrons more readily: Cu + 2Ag⁺ → Cu²⁺ + 2Ag.
