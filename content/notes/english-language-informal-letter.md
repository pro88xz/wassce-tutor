An informal letter is a letter to someone you know well: a friend, a brother or sister, a cousin, a classmate who has moved away. It is the most relaxed of the WASSCE letters, and that is exactly the danger. Students relax so much that they forget the format, fill the letter with greetings, and run out of real content halfway. This lesson shows you how to write an informal letter that is warm and natural **and** organised enough to score highly, with a complete model answer.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- set out an informal letter correctly
- write in a friendly, natural register that is still correct English
- open and close a letter warmly without wasting words
- give the body real, developed content that answers the question
- tell the difference between informal and semi-formal letters

## The layout

:::callout{type=definition}
An informal letter has **five** parts:

1. the **writer's address**, top right
2. the **date**, below it
3. the **salutation**: *Dear* + first name or family name, with a comma
4. the **body**
5. the **close** and your **first name**

There is **no recipient's address** and **no heading**.
:::

| Part | Example | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Address and date | *14 Hill Station Road, Freetown. 6th April, 2026.* | top right, as in a formal letter |
| Salutation | *Dear Kadie,* / *Dear Brother Sallu,* / *Dear Cousin Abu,* | a capital *D*, a comma; never *Dear Friend* alone |
| Close | *Yours sincerely,* / *Your loving friend,* / *Your brother,* | a comma after it |
| Name | *Isatu* | **first name only** |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Adding the recipient's address or a heading.** An informal letter to your cousin with *"The Cousin, 5 Main Street, Kenema"* and *"MY HOLIDAY"* in capitals has borrowed the formal layout. It costs organisation marks.
:::

## Informal register: friendly, not careless

You may write naturally: contractions (*I'm*, *you'll*), questions to your friend, a joke, an exclamation. But the English must still be **correct**.

| Too careless | Right for an informal letter |
|---|---|
| *hw r u? hope u ar fyn* | *How are you? I hope you're keeping well.* |
| *Me and Amie went to the beach.* | *Amie and I went to the beach.* |
| *it was sweet die!* | *It was the best day of the whole holiday!* |

:::callout{type=local-example}
You can mention local food, places and expressions to make the letter real (*cassava leaf*, *Lumley Beach*, *the Christmas masquerades*, *a family gathering in the village*), but write them in standard English sentences. A Krio phrase in quotation marks, used once for colour, can work; a letter written in Krio cannot.
:::

## The body: an opening, the content, a closing

### The opening paragraph (short)

Greet your friend, mention their last letter or some news, and lead into your reason for writing. **Two or three sentences are enough.**

> *It was lovely to receive your letter last week and to hear that you have settled in at your new school in Kenema. I have so much to tell you since you left that I hardly know where to begin, so I will start with the biggest news of all.*

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**A whole paragraph of greetings.** *"How are you? How is your mother? How is your father? How is your little sister? How are your teachers? I hope you are all fine. I am fine too. My mother is fine…"* uses fifty words and earns nothing. Greet briefly, then get to the point.
:::

### The content paragraphs (most of the letter)

This is where the marks are. Answer **every part** of the question, one main idea per paragraph, with real detail: what happened, what you saw, what you felt, what you think.

### The closing paragraph (short)

Round off, send greetings, and look forward:

> *I must stop here because Mama is calling me to help with the cooking. Please greet your parents for me, and write soon to tell me how your examinations went.*

## Informal or semi-formal?

| | Informal | Semi-formal |
|---|---|---|
| Reader | friend, brother, sister, cousin, classmate | an older person you know: uncle, aunt, teacher, family friend, pastor |
| Salutation | *Dear Kadie,* | *Dear Uncle Sorie,* / *Dear Mrs Conteh,* |
| Tone | relaxed, chatty | warm but respectful; fewer contractions, no slang |
| Close | *Your loving friend,* / *Yours sincerely,* | *Yours sincerely,* |
| Name | first name | full name, or first name if the relationship is close |

## A complete informal letter

**Question:** *Your friend has moved to another town. Write a letter telling them about the changes in your school since they left.*

::::model{title="To a friend about the changes at school" label="Model informal letter"}
:::right
Government Secondary School,\
P.M.B. 25,\
Bo.

6th April, 2026.
:::

Dear Kadie,

It was lovely to get your letter last week and to hear that you are settling in at your new school in Kenema. You asked whether anything has changed here since you left. Kadie, you would hardly recognise the place!

The biggest change is the library. You remember that dusty storeroom behind the science block, full of broken chairs and old exam papers? It has been painted bright yellow, fitted with shelves and filled with books donated by a charity in Freetown. Every student can now borrow two books a week, and there is even a corner with cushions where we read at break. I have already finished three novels this term, which I could never have afforded to buy.

We also have a new principal, Mrs Kamara, and she is very different from Mr Bangura. She arrives before seven every morning and stands at the gate to greet us by name, which means nobody dares to be late any more. She has started a homework club after school for Form Five, and I go three times a week. My Mathematics has improved so much that Mr Koroma read my test score aloud to the class. You can imagine how my face looked!

Not every change has been popular. Phones are now banned completely during school hours, and anyone caught with one has it locked in the office until the end of term. Even Musa, who used to film everything, had his taken in the very first week, and he walked around for days looking lost. At first we all complained, but I must admit the classrooms are quieter, and the long arguments about football videos at break have stopped.

There was also a wonderful inter-house sports day last month. Your old house, Blue, won the relay for the first time in five years, and the whole school ran onto the field when Abu crossed the line. Even Mrs Kamara joined in the singing afterwards, which nobody had ever seen a principal do before. I wished you had been there to see it.

The sad news is that our old football field has been fenced off for the new classroom block, so we now play on the small pitch near the road. Your old team still talks about the goal you scored against Christ the King College. They say nobody has scored one like it since.

I must stop here because Mama is calling me to help with the cooking. Please greet your parents and your little brother for me, and write soon to tell me about your new friends and your teachers. I miss our walks home from school more than I can say.

:::right
Your loving friend,

**Isatu**
:::
::::

### Why it works

- **Format**: address and date only, *Dear Kadie,*, *Your loving friend,* and a first name. No recipient's address, no heading.
- **Opening**: three sentences, then straight into the question (*"you would hardly recognise the place"*).
- **Content**: four changes (library, principal, phone ban, football field), each **developed** with detail, feeling and a result.
- **Tone**: warm and personal (*"You can imagine how my face looked!"*), but every sentence is correct standard English.
- **Length**: about 450 words in the body, comfortably at examination length.

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. What is wrong with this ending? 'Yours faithfully, Isatu Kamara (Senior Prefect)'"}
It is a **formal** close. An informal letter ends with *Yours sincerely,* or *Your loving friend,* and the **first name only**: *Isatu*.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Shorten this opening to two good sentences: 'How are you? How is your family? I hope you are all fine. I am fine. My family is fine. I am writing to tell you about my holiday.'"}
*I hope you and your family are keeping well. I have just come back from the most exciting holiday of my life, and I can't wait to tell you all about it.*
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Write one developed paragraph for a letter to your cousin about a festival or celebration in your town."}
*The highlight of the holiday was the Christmas masquerade parade in Freetown. On Boxing Day the whole of our street came out to watch the masked dancers, with drummers behind them so loud that the windows shook. My little brother hid behind Mama the first time a masquerade came near, but by the evening he was dancing with them. I have never seen our neighbourhood so happy and so full of colour.*
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Informal letter**: a personal letter to a friend or relative, warm in tone, with only the writer's address and date, "Dear" with a first name, and a friendly close.
- **Semi-formal letter**: a letter to someone such as an uncle or a teacher, warmer than a formal letter but more respectful than an informal one.
- **Tone**: the attitude a piece of writing shows towards its reader, such as warm, respectful or formal.
- **Friendly close**: the ending of an informal letter, such as "Your loving friend," followed by the writer's first name.
- **Developed point**: an idea explained with detail, feeling and a result, not just stated.
- **Standard English**: correct, generally accepted English, still needed in an informal letter despite its warm tone.
:::

## Key points

- Five parts: **address and date** (top right), **Dear + name**, **body**, **close**, **first name**. No recipient's address, no heading.
- Write in a **friendly but correct** register: contractions are fine; slang and text spellings are not.
- Keep the **opening and closing short**; put your effort into **developed content** that answers every part of the question.
- **Semi-formal** letters (to an uncle, a teacher) are warmer than formal ones but more respectful than informal ones.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Informal letters are set almost every year. Examiners check the **format** (no recipient's address, the right close), reward **developed content** that answers the question, and reward **expression** that sounds natural and personal while staying correct. Long strings of greetings and letters that wander off the topic are the commonest causes of low marks.
:::
