The empirical formula CH₂O fits methanal, ethanoic acid and glucose alike. To tell them apart you need the **molecular formula**, the actual number of atoms in one molecule. This lesson covers molecular formulas, percentage composition (and how farmers can use it to compare fertilisers), water of crystallisation, and finding formulas from combustion data.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- find a molecular formula from an empirical formula and a relative molecular mass
- use vapour density to find relative molecular mass
- calculate the percentage composition of a compound
- find the number of molecules of water of crystallisation in a hydrated salt
- find an empirical formula from combustion data

## Molecular formula

:::callout{type=definition}
The **molecular formula** shows the **actual number of atoms** of each element in one molecule. It is a **whole-number multiple** of the empirical formula:

$$\text{molecular formula} = (\text{empirical formula})_n, \qquad n = \dfrac{M_r}{\text{empirical formula mass}}$$
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**1. A compound has empirical formula CH₂O and relative molecular mass 180. Find its molecular formula.**

Empirical formula mass $= 12 + 2 + 16 = 30$; $n = 180 \div 30 = 6$.

Molecular formula $= (\text{CH}_2\text{O})_6 =$ **C₆H₁₂O₆** (glucose).

**2. A hydrocarbon has empirical formula CH₂ and Mᵣ = 56.** EF mass = 14; $n = 56 \div 14 = 4$, so the formula is **C₄H₈** (butene).
:::

### Vapour density

For gases and volatile liquids, the relative molecular mass can be found from the **vapour density** (the density of the gas compared with hydrogen):

$$M_r = 2 \times \text{vapour density}$$

A gas with vapour density 28 has $M_r = 56$.

## Percentage composition

$$\%\text{ of element} = \dfrac{\text{mass of element}}{M_r} \times 100$$

Here the mass of the element is its total mass in **one formula unit** (for example, 28 for the two nitrogen atoms in NH₄NO₃).

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Comparing nitrogen fertilisers.** (N = 14, H = 1, O = 16, C = 12, S = 32)

| Fertiliser | Formula | Mᵣ | Mass of N | % N |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ammonium nitrate | NH₄NO₃ | 80 | 28 | $\dfrac{28}{80} \times 100 = 35\%$ |
| Urea | CO(NH₂)₂ | 60 | 28 | $\dfrac{28}{60} \times 100 = 46.7\%$ |
| Ammonium sulphate | (NH₄)₂SO₄ | 132 | 28 | $\dfrac{28}{132} \times 100 = 21.2\%$ |

**Urea** supplies the most nitrogen per bag, so it is often the best value if prices per bag are similar.
:::

## Water of crystallisation

Many salts form crystals containing a fixed number of water molecules, called **water of crystallisation**, written after a dot: CuSO₄·5H₂O, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O.

**Heating** drives the water off:

$$\text{CuSO}_4\cdot5\text{H}_2\text{O(s)} \rightarrow \text{CuSO}_4\text{(s)} + 5\text{H}_2\text{O(g)}$$

Blue hydrated copper(II) sulphate turns into **white anhydrous** copper(II) sulphate. Adding water turns it blue again, which is a **test for water**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**2.50 g of hydrated copper(II) sulphate, CuSO₄·xH₂O, is heated to constant mass. 1.60 g of anhydrous CuSO₄ remains. Find x.** (CuSO₄ = 160, H₂O = 18)

- Mass of water lost $= 2.50 - 1.60 = 0.90$ g → $0.90 \div 18 = 0.050$ mol
- Moles of CuSO₄ $= 1.60 \div 160 = 0.010$ mol
- Ratio CuSO₄ : H₂O $= 0.010 : 0.050 = 1 : 5$

So **x = 5**, and the formula is CuSO₄·5H₂O.
:::

**Percentage of water** in CuSO₄·5H₂O $= \dfrac{5 \times 18}{250} \times 100 = 36\%$.

## Formulas from combustion data

When an organic compound burns completely, all its carbon becomes CO₂ and all its hydrogen becomes H₂O. Measuring the masses of these products gives the masses of C and H.

- Mass of C $= \text{mass of CO}_2 \times \dfrac{12}{44}$
- Mass of H $= \text{mass of H}_2\text{O} \times \dfrac{2}{18}$
- Any remaining mass of the sample is **oxygen**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**0.30 g of a hydrocarbon burns to give 0.88 g of CO₂ and 0.54 g of H₂O. Its Mᵣ is 30. Find its molecular formula.**

- C: $0.88 \times \tfrac{12}{44} = 0.24$ g → $0.24 \div 12 = 0.020$ mol
- H: $0.54 \times \tfrac{2}{18} = 0.06$ g → $0.06 \div 1 = 0.060$ mol
- Check: $0.24 + 0.06 = 0.30$ g, so there is no oxygen.
- Ratio C : H = 0.020 : 0.060 = 1 : 3 → empirical formula **CH₃** (mass 15)
- $n = 30 \div 15 = 2$ → molecular formula **C₂H₆** (ethane).
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Farmers in Sierra Leone often buy NPK or urea for rice and vegetables. The percentage composition calculation shows why urea, at 46% nitrogen, is a concentrated nitrogen source. But it must be applied correctly: much of its nitrogen can be lost to the air as ammonia if it is left on the surface of hot, dry soil.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Two compounds can share an empirical formula but be completely different substances: ethyne (C₂H₂) and benzene (C₆H₆) are both CH. Always use Mᵣ to find the **molecular** formula when it is asked for.
:::

## Key points

- **Molecular formula** = (empirical formula)ₙ with $n = M_r \div$ empirical formula mass.
- $M_r = 2 \times$ **vapour density**.
- **% of element** = mass of element in formula ÷ Mᵣ × 100.
- **Water of crystallisation:** find the moles of water and of anhydrous salt, then the ratio.
- **Combustion:** C from CO₂ (× 12/44), H from H₂O (× 2/18), oxygen by difference.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **find molecular formulas** from empirical formula and Mᵣ (or vapour density), to **calculate percentage composition** (often of fertilisers or hydrated salts), and to **find x in a hydrated salt** from heating data. Combustion-analysis questions appear in organic chemistry too.
:::

## Quick check

1. A compound has empirical formula CH and Mᵣ 78. Find its molecular formula.
2. Calculate the percentage of water in washing soda, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (Na₂CO₃ = 106).
3. 5.0 g of MgSO₄·xH₂O loses 2.56 g of water on heating. Find x. (MgSO₄ = 120)
4. What is Mᵣ of a gas with vapour density 22?

**Answers:**

1. EF mass 13; $78 \div 13 = 6$: C₆H₆.
2. Mᵣ $= 106 + 180 = 286$; % water $= \dfrac{180}{286} \times 100 \approx 62.9\%$.
3. MgSO₄ $= 2.44 \div 120 \approx 0.0203$ mol; water $= 2.56 \div 18 \approx 0.142$ mol; ratio ≈ 1 : 7, so x = 7.
4. $2 \times 22 = 44$.
