# Nouns: types, functions and correct usage

## Learning objectives
You should be able to define a noun; classify common types of nouns; identify noun functions in sentences; form plurals and possessives; and correct common agreement errors.

## Meaning and types
A noun is a word that names a person, place, thing, substance, quality or idea. Common nouns name general classes, such as teacher, town and river. Proper nouns identify particular people, places or institutions and begin with capital letters, such as Freetown and Sierra Leone. Concrete nouns refer to things that can be perceived through the senses; abstract nouns name ideas or qualities such as honesty, courage and freedom.

Collective nouns refer to groups considered as units, for example team, committee and audience. Countable nouns can be counted and usually have singular and plural forms: one book, three books. Uncountable nouns refer to substances, concepts or quantities not normally counted individually: water, information, furniture and advice. Some nouns can be countable or uncountable depending on meaning: “three coffees” can mean three cups or servings.

## Functions in a sentence
A noun phrase can function as the subject, object, complement or object of a preposition. In “The student answered the question,” “the student” is the subject and “the question” is the direct object. In “Aminata became a doctor,” “a doctor” is a subject complement after the linking verb. In “The book is on the table,” “the table” is the object of the preposition “on”.

A noun can be modified by determiners and adjectives: “those two difficult questions”. The whole phrase, not just the head noun, performs the grammatical function.

## Number and possession
Most regular nouns form the plural by adding -s or -es: class/classes, box/boxes. Some have spelling changes, such as city/cities, while irregular forms include child/children, man/men and mouse/mice. Some nouns have the same singular and plural form, such as sheep. Learn irregular forms rather than applying one rule to every word.

Possession is often shown with an apostrophe. A singular noun usually takes 's: the teacher's book. A regular plural ending in -s usually takes an apostrophe after the s: the teachers' room. An irregular plural not ending in -s takes 's: children's books. Do not use an apostrophe simply to make an ordinary plural.

## Agreement and common errors
A singular countable noun normally needs a determiner: “She bought a pen,” not “She bought pen.” Uncountable nouns generally take singular verb agreement: “The information is useful,” not “The informations are useful.” Subject–verb agreement depends on the head of the subject phrase: “The list of items is on the desk.” The head is “list”, not “items”.

## WASSCE examination focus
In grammar questions, identify the noun by its role in context rather than by memorising a word list. Check countability, number, capitalisation, apostrophe placement and subject–verb agreement. In sentence correction, explain the grammatical reason for the correction.

## Check your understanding
1. Define a noun.
2. Classify Freetown, honesty and committee.
3. Identify the subject and object in “The farmer harvested the rice.”
4. Correct: “The informations are useful.”
5. Write the possessive form for a room belonging to several teachers.

## Answers for self-assessment
1. A word naming a person, place, thing, substance, quality or idea.
2. Proper noun, abstract noun and collective noun.
3. Subject: the farmer; object: the rice.
4. “The information is useful.”
5. The teachers' room.
