Construction means drawing accurate figures using only a **ruler** (for straight lines), a pair of **compasses** (for arcs) and a pencil, with **no protractor** for the angles you construct. WASSCE Paper 2 often has a construction question: construct a triangle or quadrilateral from given measurements, bisect lines and angles, then measure a length or angle from your drawing. The marks are for **accuracy** and for leaving your **construction lines visible**. This lesson teaches each construction step by step.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- bisect a straight line (perpendicular bisector)
- bisect an angle
- construct angles of $90°$, $60°$, $45°$, $30°$, $120°$ and $75°$
- drop a perpendicular from a point to a line
- construct triangles and quadrilaterals from given data

## Equipment and good practice

- A **sharp pencil**: thick lines lose accuracy.
- Compasses that **hold their setting** firmly.
- **Never rub out** construction arcs: examiners look for them, and they earn marks.
- Measure your final answer carefully, usually to the nearest millimetre or degree.

## 1. The perpendicular bisector of a line

This cuts a line in half at right angles.

1. Open the compasses to **more than half** the length of AB.
2. With the point on A, draw arcs above and below the line.
3. **Without changing the radius**, put the point on B and draw arcs that cross the first two.
4. Join the two crossing points with a straight line.

Every point on this line is **equally distant from A and B**.

## 2. The bisector of an angle

1. With the point on the vertex, draw an arc that cuts **both arms** of the angle.
2. From each of those two points, draw arcs of the **same radius** that cross inside the angle.
3. Join the vertex to the crossing point.

Every point on this line is **equally distant from the two arms**.

## 3. Constructing angles

| Angle | How |
|---|---|
| **$60°$** | Draw a line; with centre at the end point, draw a long arc; with the **same radius**, centre where the arc meets the line, cut the arc. Join to that point. (The arcs make an equilateral triangle.) |
| **$120°$** | Construct $60°$ twice, round from the line. |
| **$30°$** | Bisect $60°$. |
| **$90°$** | Construct the perpendicular at a point on the line (bisect the straight angle $180°$), or bisect a line. |
| **$45°$** | Bisect $90°$. |
| **$75°$** | Construct $90°$ and $60°$ at the same point, then bisect the $30°$ between them: $60 + 15 = 75$. |
| **$105°$** | $90° + 15°$, or $60° + 45°$. |

:::callout{type=tip}
**Every constructible angle in WASSCE is built from 60 and 90 by bisecting and adding.** Before you start, write the angle as a combination: $135° = 90° + 45°$; $150° = 180° - 30°$; $22\tfrac12° = 45° \div 2$.
:::

## 4. A perpendicular from a point to a line

To drop a perpendicular from P (not on the line) to a line $l$:

1. With centre P, draw an arc cutting $l$ at two points, A and B.
2. Construct the perpendicular bisector of AB; it passes through P.

The perpendicular gives the **shortest distance** from P to the line, which is how you measure the height of a triangle.

## 5. Constructing triangles

| Given | Method |
|---|---|
| **three sides** (SSS) | draw one side; from each end, an arc with the other two lengths; join to where they cross |
| **two sides and the included angle** (SAS) | draw one side; construct the angle at one end; mark the second side along it; join |
| **two angles and a side** (ASA) | draw the side; construct each angle at its ends; the arms meet at the third vertex |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Construct triangle PQR with $PQ = 8$ cm, $\angle QPR = 60°$ and $PR = 6$ cm. Measure $QR$.**

1. **Sketch first** with the measurements labelled.
2. Draw $PQ = 8$ cm.
3. Construct $60°$ at P.
4. Mark $PR = 6$ cm along the arm with compasses.
5. Join $QR$ and measure: **$QR \approx 7.2$ cm**.

**Check by calculation** (cosine rule): $QR^2 = 8^2 + 6^2 - 2(8)(6)\cos 60° = 64 + 36 - 48 = 52$, so $QR = \sqrt{52} \approx 7.21$ cm ✓
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Using a protractor for the angle.** If the question says *"using a ruler and a pair of compasses only"*, a protractor-drawn angle earns **no** construction marks, even if it is accurate. Construct it with arcs.
:::

## 6. Quadrilaterals

Constructing a quadrilateral is usually a sequence of the steps above. For example, **construct a parallelogram ABCD with $AB = 7$ cm, $AD = 5$ cm and $\angle DAB = 75°$**: construct $75°$ at A, mark $AD = 5$; from D draw an arc of $7$ cm and from B an arc of $5$ cm; they meet at C.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Carpenters and masons in Sierra Leone use these same ideas without compasses: a rope with knots at 3, 4 and 5 units makes a perfect right angle (a 3-4-5 triangle) for setting out the corner of a house foundation, and a string fixed at a peg draws an accurate circle for a round hut.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. How would you construct an angle of 135°?"}
Construct $90°$, then bisect the $90°$ on the other side to add $45°$: $90° + 45° = 135°$. (Or: $180° - 45°$, bisecting a right angle on the straight line.)
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Which construction gives the set of points equidistant from A and B?"}
The **perpendicular bisector** of AB.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Triangle ABC has AB = 7 cm, BC = 5 cm, AC = 6 cm. Describe how to construct it."}
Draw $AB = 7$ cm. With centre A and radius 6 cm, draw an arc; with centre B and radius 5 cm, draw an arc crossing it. The crossing point is C. Join AC and BC.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. How do you construct an angle of 15°?"}
Construct $60°$, bisect it to get $30°$, then bisect $30°$ to get $15°$.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Perpendicular bisector**: the line that cuts a line segment in half at right angles.
- **Angle bisector**: the line that cuts an angle into two equal angles.
- **Perpendicular**: a line at right angles to another line.
- **Compasses**: an instrument for drawing circles and arcs and for marking off equal lengths.
- **Construction lines**: the arcs and lines drawn while constructing, which must be left visible.
- **Included angle**: the angle between two given sides of a triangle.
:::

## Key points

- Use only a **ruler and compasses**; keep all **construction arcs visible**.
- **Perpendicular bisector**: equal arcs from both ends. **Angle bisector**: arcs from the vertex, then equal arcs from the arms.
- Build angles from **$60°$ and $90°$** by bisecting and adding.
- **Sketch first** with all measurements; **measure** carefully at the end.
- Check a measured length with the cosine rule or Pythagoras when you can.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Construction questions in Paper 2 ask you to construct a triangle or quadrilateral from given data using ruler and compasses only, often including a bisector or a perpendicular, and then to **measure** a length or angle. Marks are awarded for each correct construction (with arcs shown) and for a measurement within the stated tolerance.
:::
