:::callout{type=definition}
**Cybersecurity** is the protection of computers, phones, networks and data from attack, damage and unauthorised access. A **computer virus** is a malicious program that attaches itself to files or programs and spreads to other computers, usually damaging data or slowing the system.
:::

A virus on a flash drive can wipe a student's project; a fake message can empty a mobile money account; ransomware can stop a hospital's computers. This lesson explains the **types of malware** (viruses, worms, Trojans, ransomware and spyware), **how viruses spread** and the **signs of a virus infection**, common cyber attacks such as **hacking**, **phishing** and **SIM swap fraud**, and the **ways to protect a computer**: antivirus software, firewalls, strong passwords, two-factor authentication, encryption and updates.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define malware and describe viruses, worms, Trojan horses, ransomware, spyware and adware
- state how computer viruses spread and the signs that a computer is infected
- explain hacking, phishing, social engineering, identity theft and denial-of-service attacks
- describe the use of antivirus software, firewalls, encryption and two-factor authentication
- create a strong password and state rules for keeping accounts safe
- state what to do when a computer is infected

## Malware

:::callout{type=definition}
**Malware (malicious software):** any program written to harm a computer, steal data or take control of a device without its owner's permission.

**Worm:** malware that copies itself and spreads across networks by itself, without attaching to a file or needing a user to open anything.

**Trojan horse:** malware disguised as a useful or harmless program, such as a free game, that lets an attacker into the computer once it is installed.

**Ransomware:** malware that locks a computer or encrypts its files and demands payment, a ransom, to unlock them.

**Spyware:** malware that secretly watches what a user does and sends information, such as passwords and browsing history, to someone else.

**Keylogger:** spyware that records every key pressed, to capture passwords and PINs.

**Adware:** software that floods the screen with unwanted advertisements, often installed with free programs.
:::

In 2017 the **WannaCry** ransomware spread like a worm to over 200,000 computers in about 150 countries, locking files in hospitals, businesses and homes.

### How viruses spread

- opening infected **email attachments** or links
- plugging in infected **flash drives** and memory cards
- **downloading** programs, films or music from untrustworthy sites
- installing **pirated** software
- visiting infected **websites** and clicking pop-up adverts
- sharing files over a **network**

### Signs that a computer may be infected

- it becomes **very slow** or keeps **freezing** and restarting
- files **disappear**, change or will not open; strange new files or shortcuts appear
- **pop-up** adverts and warnings appear all the time
- the browser's home page changes by itself
- the antivirus program is switched off and cannot be turned on
- friends receive messages from you that you did not send

## Cyber attacks

:::callout{type=definition}
**Hacking:** gaining access to a computer system or data without permission; a person who does this is a hacker.

**Phishing:** sending fake emails, text messages or websites that look as if they come from a trusted organisation, to trick people into giving away passwords, PINs or bank details.

**Social engineering:** tricking people, by lies or pressure rather than by technical means, into revealing confidential information or doing something unsafe.

**Identity theft:** stealing someone's personal details to pretend to be them, for example to borrow money or open accounts in their name.

**Denial-of-service (DoS) attack:** flooding a website or server with so many requests that it slows down or stops working for genuine users.
:::

**SIM swap fraud** is a common form of social engineering: a fraudster persuades a mobile operator to move a victim's number onto a new SIM card, then receives their one-time codes and empties their mobile money or bank account.

**Warning signs of a phishing message:** it creates **urgency** ("your account will be closed today"); asks for your **PIN or password**; has **spelling mistakes** or a strange sender address; offers a **prize** you never entered for; or has a link to a web address that does not match the real organisation.

## Protecting computers and data

:::callout{type=definition}
**Antivirus software:** a utility program that scans files, memory and downloads for known malware, and removes or quarantines what it finds; it must be updated regularly to recognise new threats.

**Firewall:** hardware or software that checks the data entering and leaving a computer or network and blocks traffic that breaks its security rules.

**Encryption:** scrambling data into an unreadable code that only someone with the correct key can turn back into readable form, so stolen data is useless to a thief.

**Two-factor authentication (2FA):** a log-in method that needs two different proofs of identity, such as a password and a one-time code sent to the user's phone.

**Software update (patch):** a fix released by a software maker to repair security weaknesses and errors in its program.
:::

Examples of antivirus programs: **Microsoft Defender** (built into Windows), **Avast**, **Norton**, **Kaspersky**, **McAfee**.

### Strong passwords

A **strong password** is long (at least 12 characters), mixes **upper- and lower-case letters, digits and symbols**, and is **not** a name, birthday or common word. A good method is a passphrase of unrelated words with numbers and symbols, such as *Mango!River7Kite*.

- use a **different password** for each important account
- **never share** passwords or PINs, even with friends or "bank staff"
- change a password at once if you think someone knows it
- **lock** your phone and computer when you leave them

### Other safeguards

- keep the operating system, browser and apps **updated**
- install software only from **official** sources
- **scan** flash drives before opening their files
- **back up** important files regularly, so ransomware cannot destroy your only copy
- check for **https** and the padlock before typing passwords on a website
- use **physical security**: locked rooms, cable locks and CCTV for computer rooms

### If a computer is infected

1. **Disconnect** it from the Internet and the network, so the malware cannot spread or send data.
2. Run a **full scan** with an up-to-date antivirus program, and remove or quarantine what it finds.
3. **Change passwords** from a different, clean device.
4. **Restore** damaged files from a backup.
5. If the problem continues, ask a technician to **reinstall** the operating system.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**The "wrong transfer" scam.** Ibrahim receives an SMS saying he has been sent Le 500 by mistake, followed by a call begging him to send it back. His balance shows no such payment: the SMS was **fake**. Scammers also call pretending to be from the mobile money provider and ask for a PIN or code. The provider will **never** ask for your PIN; hang up and check your balance yourself.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Thinking antivirus software alone keeps you safe.** Antivirus software cannot stop you giving your password to a **phishing** site or a **social engineering** caller. Safe habits, strong passwords, updates and two-factor authentication are needed as well.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Define a computer virus and state three ways viruses spread."}
A **malicious program that attaches itself to files** and spreads to other computers, damaging data. Spread (any three): infected **email attachments**; infected **flash drives**; **downloads** from untrustworthy sites; **pirated software**; infected **websites**; shared **network** files.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. State three signs that a computer may have a virus."}
Any three: it runs **very slowly** or **freezes**; files **disappear** or are **corrupted**; constant **pop-ups**; the home page **changes**; the antivirus is **switched off**; friends receive messages you did not send.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Distinguish between a virus and a worm."}
A **virus** attaches itself to a file and spreads only when the file is **opened**. A **worm** spreads **by itself** across networks without needing a host file or user action.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. What is phishing? State two ways to recognise a phishing message."}
**Fake messages or websites that pretend to come from a trusted organisation** to steal passwords, PINs or bank details. Signs (any two): **urgent** threats; requests for a **PIN or password**; **spelling mistakes** or odd sender address; an unexpected **prize**; a link that does not match the real organisation's address.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. State the function of (a) a firewall, (b) encryption."}
(a) A firewall **checks traffic** entering and leaving a computer or network and **blocks** what breaks its security rules. (b) Encryption **scrambles data** so that only someone with the **key** can read it.
:::

## Key points

- **Malware**: **virus** (attaches to files), **worm** (spreads itself), **Trojan horse** (disguised), **ransomware** (locks files for payment), **spyware** and **keyloggers** (secretly record), **adware**.
- Viruses spread through **attachments, flash drives, downloads, pirated software and websites**; signs include slowness, lost files and pop-ups.
- Attacks: **hacking, phishing, social engineering, SIM swap fraud, identity theft, denial of service**.
- Protection: **antivirus, firewall, encryption, two-factor authentication, strong passwords, updates, backups**.
- If infected: **disconnect, scan, change passwords, restore, reinstall**.
