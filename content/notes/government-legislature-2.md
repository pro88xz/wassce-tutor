A bill is only a proposal. Before it can bind anyone in Sierra Leone, it must survive several readings in Parliament, line-by-line study in committee, and the President's signature. This careful process gives MPs, the press and the public time to spot problems. This lesson traces how a bill becomes law and describes the Parliament of Sierra Leone.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- distinguish types of bills
- describe the stages through which a bill becomes law
- explain presidential assent and what happens if it is refused
- describe the composition and committees of the Parliament of Sierra Leone
- state the qualifications of a Member of Parliament

## Types of bills

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Government (public) bill** | Introduced by a minister on behalf of the government; most bills are of this kind |
| **Private member's bill** | Introduced by an ordinary MP, not a minister |
| **Money bill** | Deals with taxes, public spending or borrowing |
| **Private bill** | Affects a particular person, company or locality |
| **Constitutional amendment bill** | Changes the constitution; needs a special majority |

## How a bill becomes law

![Eight steps: drafting and gazetting, first reading, second reading, committee stage, report stage, third reading, presidential assent, Act of Parliament](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/bill-to-law.svg)

*Figure 1. The stages of a bill. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

1. **Drafting and publication:** the bill is drafted (government bills by the Law Officers' Department) and published in the **Gazette** so that the public can see it.
2. **First reading:** the Clerk reads the **title** of the bill. There is no debate. The bill is printed and circulated.
3. **Second reading:** the minister or sponsor explains the **purpose and principles** of the bill, and MPs debate them. If the House votes against it here, the bill dies.
4. **Committee stage:** a committee (or the whole House sitting as a committee) examines the bill **clause by clause** and may amend it.
5. **Report stage:** the committee reports its amendments to the House, which considers them.
6. **Third reading:** a final debate on the bill as amended, followed by a **vote**. Only minor wording changes are allowed now.
7. **Presidential assent:** the bill goes to the President, who signs it, or refuses and returns it to Parliament **within 14 days with the reasons**.
8. **Act of Parliament:** once signed, the bill becomes an **Act** and is published in the Gazette.

In a **bicameral** legislature, the bill must pass through the same stages in **both** houses before assent.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Why is the second reading the most important stage?** Because it is where the House decides whether it accepts the **main principle** of the bill. If MPs reject the principle, there is no point in going through it clause by clause. The committee stage then deals with **details**, not principles.
:::

## The Parliament of Sierra Leone

- **Unicameral**, sitting in Freetown.
- In the **2023** general election, **135** ordinary members were elected by **district block proportional representation**, and there are **14 Paramount Chief Members of Parliament (PCMPs)**, one for each provincial district, making **149** members in all.
- **Term:** five years.
- **Presiding officer:** the **Speaker**, elected by Parliament.
- The **Clerk of Parliament** heads its administration.

**Qualifications of a Member of Parliament** (ordinary member):

- a citizen of Sierra Leone
- at least **21 years** old
- able to speak and read **English** well enough to take part in proceedings
- a registered voter, and not disqualified (for example, by certain criminal convictions, insanity or holding certain public offices)

### Committees of Parliament

| Committee | Work |
|---|---|
| **Committee of the Whole House** | All MPs sitting as a committee, often for money bills |
| **Public Accounts Committee** | Examines the Auditor-General's report on government accounts |
| **Appointments and Public Service Committee** | Vets the President's nominees before Parliament approves them |
| **Finance Committee** | Studies the budget and money bills |
| **Legislative Committee** | Studies bills in detail |
| **Sectoral committees** | Oversee particular ministries, such as health or education |
| **Select (ad hoc) committees** | Set up to investigate a particular matter, then dissolved |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Before ministers take office in Sierra Leone, the President's nominees appear before Parliament's **Appointments and Public Service Committee** for public vetting, where MPs question them on their qualifications, assets and plans. The House then votes to approve or reject them. Vetting sessions are broadcast, so citizens can watch their future ministers being questioned.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not reverse the order of the **committee stage** and the **second reading**. The House first approves the **principle** (second reading), **then** the committee examines the **details**. Also, the President of Sierra Leone does not sign a bill automatically: assent may be refused, in which case the bill is returned with reasons.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Government bill**: a bill introduced by a minister on behalf of the government.
- **Private member's bill**: a bill introduced by an ordinary MP, not a minister.
- **Money bill**: a bill that deals with taxes, public spending or borrowing.
- **Committee of the Whole House**: all MPs sitting together as a committee, often for money bills.
:::

## Key points

- Bills may be **government, private member's, money, private or constitutional**.
- Stages: **gazetting → first reading → second reading → committee → report → third reading → assent → Act**.
- If the President refuses assent, the bill goes back to Parliament **within 14 days** with reasons.
- Sierra Leone's Parliament is **unicameral**: **135 elected members + 14 PCMPs = 149** (2023), elected for **five years**.
- MPs must be citizens aged at least **21** who can read and speak **English**.

## Quick check

1. At which stage is a bill examined clause by clause?
2. What happens at the first reading?
3. How many Paramount Chief Members of Parliament sit in the Parliament elected in 2023?
4. What is a private member's bill?

**Answers:**

1. The committee stage.
2. The title of the bill is read; there is no debate.
3. Fourteen, one for each provincial district.
4. A bill introduced by an ordinary Member of Parliament who is not a minister.
