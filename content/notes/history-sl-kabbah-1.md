:::callout{type=definition}
The **National Provisional Ruling Council (NPRC)** was the military government that ruled Sierra Leone from **29 April 1992** to **29 March 1996**.
:::

It was led first by **Captain Valentine Strasser**, who at **25** was one of the world's youngest heads of state, and from **January 1996** by **Brigadier Julius Maada Bio**. The NPRC was popular at first for promising to **end the war, fight corruption** and clean up the country, but it failed to defeat the **RUF**, carried out **executions** and abuses, and was accused of corruption itself. Under pressure from civil society at the **Bintumani conferences**, it held **elections in February 1996** and handed power to President **Ahmad Tejan Kabbah**. This lesson explains **the NPRC military government and the return to civilian rule**.

## The 1992 coup

On **29 April 1992**, young officers from the war front came to Freetown to protest about **unpaid salaries, lack of equipment and poor conditions**. Finding little resistance, they took power and formed the **NPRC**, with **Captain Valentine Strasser** as Chairman and head of state. **President Momoh** fled to **Guinea**.

**Causes:** the **neglect of soldiers**, **economic collapse**, **corruption** in the APC government, and its **failure to end the war** (see the previous topic).

## Aims and early popularity

The NPRC promised to:

1. **end the war** quickly;
2. **fight corruption** and recover stolen public money;
3. **revive the economy** and pay salaries;
4. **return the country to democracy** after a period of military rule.

At first it was **very popular**, especially with young people. Youth groups painted **murals**, cleaned streets and joined the NPRC's "**clean-up**" campaigns, and many saw the young soldiers as representatives of a new generation.

## Achievements of the NPRC

1. **Anti-corruption commissions** investigated former ministers and officials, and some property was recovered.
2. **Economic reforms** with the **IMF** and World Bank reduced inflation and improved the supply of fuel and rice for a time.
3. **Clean-up campaigns** improved the appearance and sanitation of Freetown.
4. **Youth involvement**: young people felt included in national life.
5. A **National Electoral Commission** was set up under **Dr James Jonah**, and a **transition programme** led to **elections in 1996**.

## Failures and abuses

1. **The war worsened**: the RUF spread across the country and, by **1995**, attacks came close to **Freetown**.
2. **Recruitment of untrained youths** into the army led to indiscipline; some soldiers became "**sobels**" who looted and attacked civilians.
3. **Executions**: in **December 1992**, the NPRC **executed** a number of people accused of plotting a coup, after a hasty military trial, drawing international condemnation.
4. **Human rights abuses**: detention of journalists and critics; restrictions on the press.
5. **Corruption** within the NPRC itself, including in the diamond trade.
6. **Delays** in returning to civilian rule.

## Mercenaries and the war

In **1995**, with the RUF threatening Freetown, the NPRC hired the South African private military company **Executive Outcomes**. Working with local **Kamajor** hunters and government troops, it **pushed the RUF back** from Freetown and recaptured the **Kono diamond fields**. This showed both the weakness of the national army and the role of diamonds in the war.

## The Bintumani conferences and the 1996 elections

1. **Bintumani I (August 1995)**: a national consultative conference of political parties, civil society, women's groups, chiefs and others, held at the **Bintumani Hotel**, agreed a **timetable for elections**.
2. **16 January 1996**: **Brigadier Julius Maada Bio** removed **Strasser** in a **palace coup**, accusing him of planning to stay in power.
3. **Bintumani II (February 1996)**: the NPRC suggested **peace before elections**, but the conference, led by **women's groups** and civil society, insisted on "**elections before peace**".
4. **26–27 February 1996**: **presidential and parliamentary elections** were held despite RUF attacks; many voters were threatened, and some had their hands cut off by rebels to stop them voting.
5. After a **run-off in March**, **Ahmad Tejan Kabbah** of the **SLPP** was elected President and sworn in on **29 March 1996**, when the NPRC handed over power.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Why the NPRC handed over power**

1. **Civil society pressure**, especially from **women's groups**, students, lawyers and journalists at the Bintumani conferences.
2. **International pressure** from the UN, Commonwealth, ECOWAS and donors, who linked aid to democracy.
3. The NPRC's own **promise** of a return to civilian rule.
4. **Divisions** within the NPRC, shown by the palace coup against Strasser.
5. The **failure to end the war** weakened its claim to rule.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
The "**elections before peace**" decision at **Bintumani II** is remembered as a turning point in which **Sierra Leonean women** led the call for democracy. The NPRC's second leader, **Julius Maada Bio**, later returned to politics as a civilian and was **elected President in 2018** and re-elected in **2023**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The NPRC had **two** leaders: **Valentine Strasser** (1992–January 1996) and **Julius Maada Bio** (January–March 1996). It was Bio who **handed over power** to the elected President Kabbah on **29 March 1996**.
:::

## Key points

- **NPRC coup: 29 April 1992**, by young officers from the war front; **Captain Valentine Strasser** head of state.
- Aims: **end the war, fight corruption, revive the economy, restore democracy**; popular with **youth** at first.
- Achievements: **anti-corruption commissions, economic reforms, clean-up campaigns, electoral commission**.
- Failures: **war worsened, sobels, executions (December 1992), abuses, corruption, delays**; **Executive Outcomes** hired (1995).
- **Bio replaced Strasser (16 January 1996)**; **Bintumani II**: "**elections before peace**"; elections **February 1996**; **Kabbah sworn in, 29 March 1996**.
