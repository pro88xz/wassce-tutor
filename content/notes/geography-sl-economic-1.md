**Agriculture** is the largest economic activity in **Sierra Leone**, employing more than half of the working population. Most farmers are **smallholders** growing **rice**, the staple food, together with **cassava**, groundnuts, sweet potatoes and vegetables, while **cocoa**, **coffee**, **oil palm** and **cashew** are grown as cash crops. **Fishing** along the coast and rivers provides much of the country's protein, and **forestry** supplies timber, firewood and charcoal. This lesson describes each activity, where it takes place and the problems it faces.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the main food and cash crops of Sierra Leone and where they are grown
- describe rice farming in uplands and swamps
- describe livestock rearing in Sierra Leone
- describe the fishing industry and its problems
- describe forestry and its problems
- suggest ways of improving agriculture and fishing

### Rice farming

| Upland rice | Swamp rice |
|---|---|
| grown on slopes after clearing and burning bush | grown in inland valley swamps, bolilands, riverine grasslands and mangrove swamps |
| depends entirely on rainfall | water from rivers, floods or tides |
| low yields; soils exhausted quickly | **higher yields**; soils stay fertile |
| the most common method | can give two crops a year with water control |

### Livestock

**N'Dama cattle**, a breed that tolerates the **tsetse fly**, are kept mainly in the north, especially **Koinadugu** and **Bombali**, often by **Fula** herders. Goats, sheep, pigs and poultry are kept across the country. Livestock numbers are small because of tsetse, disease, poor pasture in the forest zone and theft.

## Fishing

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone has rich fishing grounds on its wide **continental shelf**. **Artisanal (small-scale) fishermen** use canoes and nets from coastal towns such as **Tombo**, **Goderich**, **Shenge** and **Bonthe**, catching bonga, herring and other fish, much of which women smoke and sell. **Industrial trawlers**, many of them foreign-owned, fish further offshore. Rivers, swamps and fish ponds provide freshwater fish inland.
:::

**Importance:** cheap protein, jobs (fishing, smoking, trading, boat building), export earnings, government revenue from licences.

**Problems:** **illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing** by foreign trawlers, **overfishing**, old canoes and engines, lack of cold storage, high fuel costs, poor roads to markets, and the cutting of mangroves (fish breeding grounds) for smoking fish.

## Forestry

Forests provide **timber**, **firewood and charcoal** (the main cooking fuels), poles, medicines, bush meat and fruits. Forest reserves and national parks include **Gola**, the **Western Area Peninsula forest**, **Kambui Hills** and **Loma**.

**Problems:** **deforestation** for farms, charcoal, logging and mining; **illegal logging**; bush fires; and a lack of replanting.

## Problems of agriculture in Sierra Leone

1. **Low yields** from simple tools and upland bush fallowing.
2. **Under-use of swamps**, which need drainage and water control.
3. **Shortage of labour** as young people move to towns and mines.
4. **Poor feeder roads**, so produce is hard to market.
5. **Lack of credit, fertiliser and improved seeds.**
6. **Pests and diseases**, including birds, rodents and stem borers in rice.
7. **Post-harvest losses** from poor storage and processing.
8. **Low and unstable prices** for cash crops.

## Improving agriculture and fishing

1. Develop **inland valley swamps** and bolilands for **rice**, with water control.
2. Supply **improved seeds, fertiliser** and **machines** (power tillers, rice mills).
3. Provide **credit** and support **farmers' cooperatives**.
4. Build **feeder roads**, stores and processing plants.
5. Strengthen **extension services** and agricultural research (Njala University, the Rice Research Station at **Rokupr**).
6. **Patrol fishing waters** against illegal trawlers and provide cold storage.
7. **Replant** forests and enforce forestry laws.

The government's **Feed Salone** programme, launched in **2023**, aims to raise food production, especially rice, and reduce food imports.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Although Sierra Leone grows rice almost everywhere, it is **not self-sufficient** in rice and imports large amounts. Explain this with low upland yields, under-used swamps, labour shortages, poor roads and storage losses.
:::

## Key points

- Agriculture employs **more than half** of the workforce; most farmers are **smallholders**.
- **Rice** (upland and swamp) and **cassava** are staples; cash crops: **cocoa and coffee** (east), **oil palm**, **cashew**, **ginger**.
- **N'Dama cattle** in the north (tsetse-tolerant).
- **Fishing:** artisanal (Tombo, Goderich, Shenge, Bonthe) and industrial; problem of **illegal trawling**.
- **Forestry:** timber and charcoal; **deforestation**.

## Quick check

1. Name two cash crops grown in eastern Sierra Leone.
2. Give two advantages of swamp rice over upland rice.
3. Why are N'Dama cattle kept in Sierra Leone?
4. Name two important fishing towns.
5. State two problems of the fishing industry.

**Answers:**

1. Cocoa and coffee.
2. Any two: higher yields; soils stay fertile; water from rivers or tides, so less dependence on rain; two crops a year possible.
3. They tolerate the tsetse fly, which kills many other breeds.
4. Any two: Tombo; Goderich; Shenge; Bonthe.
5. Any two: illegal foreign trawlers; overfishing; old equipment; lack of cold storage; high fuel costs; poor roads; mangrove cutting.
