If a new phone stops working after a week and the shop refuses to help, where can the buyer turn? Consumers are protected from several directions: by government laws and agencies, by consumer associations, by producers who police themselves, by the media, and by their own care. This lesson explains each, and the duties consumers have in return.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain how government protects consumers through laws and agencies
- describe the role of consumer associations
- explain producers' self-regulation and the role of the media
- state the responsibilities of consumers
- describe the steps to seek redress, and the problems of consumer protection in West Africa

## Protection by government

### Laws

Governments pass laws dealing with:

| Area of law | Protects against |
|---|---|
| **Weights and measures** | Short weights; requires accurate, inspected scales and measures |
| **Sale of goods** | Goods not as described or of poor quality (implied conditions) |
| **Food and drugs** | Unsafe, adulterated or unlabelled food and medicines |
| **Trade descriptions and labelling** | False claims; requires contents, weight, price and expiry dates |
| **Price control** | Excessive prices for essential goods |
| **Hire purchase and credit** | Unfair credit terms and wrongful repossession |
| **Consumer protection** | Unfair practices generally; sets up complaint procedures |
| **Competition** | Monopolies and price-fixing cartels |

### Agencies

| Agency (Sierra Leone) | Role |
|---|---|
| **Sierra Leone Standards Bureau** | Sets **standards** for goods, tests products and certifies quality |
| **Pharmacy Board of Sierra Leone** | Registers medicines, licenses pharmacies and seizes fake or expired drugs |
| **Ministry of Trade and Industry** | Weights and measures inspection, price monitoring, trade policy |
| **Ministry of Health** (environmental health officers) | Inspects food premises, markets and sanitation |
| **Local councils** | License and inspect traders and markets |
| **Regulators** of utilities and telecoms | Control tariffs and service quality in electricity, water and telecommunications |
| **Courts** | Hear consumers' claims for compensation and punish offenders |

**Other government measures:**

- **consumer education** campaigns
- **licensing** of traders
- **inspecting** imports at ports
- **encouraging competition**, so that no single seller dominates

## Consumer associations

**Consumer associations** are voluntary bodies formed by consumers to protect their interests. They:

1. **Educate** consumers on their rights and on safe products.
2. **Test products** and publish comparisons.
3. **Receive complaints** and help consumers seek redress.
4. **Expose** firms that cheat or sell dangerous goods.
5. **Lobby** the government for better consumer laws.
6. **Represent consumers** on boards and committees.
7. **Organise boycotts** of firms that exploit consumers.

## Self-regulation by producers

Producers and trade associations protect consumers (and their own reputations) by:

- **codes of conduct** for members, with sanctions for those who cheat
- **quality control** in production
- **guarantees and warranties**, with **after-sales service**
- **clear labelling** and instructions
- **customer service desks** and complaint lines
- **branding**, since a brand's reputation depends on keeping quality high

## The media

Newspapers, radio, television and social media:

- **expose** bad practices and dangerous products
- **publicise** consumer complaints and product recalls
- **educate** consumers in phone-in programmes
- **refuse misleading adverts**, under media codes

## The consumer's own responsibilities

Consumers protect themselves when they:

1. **Examine goods** before buying; check weights, seals and **expiry dates**.
2. **Read labels** and instructions.
3. **Demand and keep receipts**, guarantees and warranty cards.
4. **Buy from reputable, licensed sellers**, especially for medicines.
5. **Compare prices** and quality before buying.
6. **Complain** promptly about faulty goods and poor service.
7. **Report** fake goods and cheats to the authorities.
8. **Use products as instructed**, and pay bills honestly.
9. Avoid buying **smuggled or suspiciously cheap** goods.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Seeking redress for a faulty phone.** Aminata buys a phone with a 6-month guarantee. After a week it will not charge.

1. **Return to the seller** with the **receipt** and guarantee card; ask for repair, replacement or refund.
2. If refused, **write a complaint** to the shop's management or the manufacturer's agent, keeping a copy.
3. Report to a **consumer association** or the relevant **government agency**, which may contact the seller.
4. Use the **media**, such as a radio consumer programme, if the seller still refuses.
5. As a last resort, **sue** in court under the **sale of goods** law: the phone was not of merchantable quality.

Without the **receipt**, steps 1 to 5 all become harder. That is why consumers must keep receipts.
:::

## Problems of consumer protection in West Africa

1. **Ignorance** of consumer rights and laws.
2. **Weak enforcement**: too few inspectors, and corruption.
3. **Weak or few consumer associations**.
4. **Open borders and smuggling** of fake and substandard goods.
5. **Poverty**, which drives people to buy cheap, unsafe goods.
6. **Slow and costly court processes**.
7. **Large informal markets** that are hard to regulate.
8. **Outdated laws** and low penalties.

**Solutions:**

- more consumer education
- more and better-trained inspectors
- stronger penalties
- support for consumer associations
- tighter border control
- simpler, faster complaint procedures

:::callout{type=local-example}
The **Pharmacy Board of Sierra Leone** regularly seizes and destroys fake and expired drugs found in markets, and warns the public to buy medicines only from licensed pharmacies. The **Standards Bureau** tests products such as cement, iron rods and packaged foods against national standards.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not list only consumer **rights** when asked about consumer **responsibilities**. Responsibilities are what consumers must **do**: check goods, keep receipts, complain, read labels and buy from licensed sellers.
:::

## Key points

- **Government** protects consumers through **laws** (weights and measures, sale of goods, food and drugs, price control) and **agencies** (Standards Bureau, Pharmacy Board, Ministry of Trade, local councils, courts).
- **Consumer associations** educate, test, receive complaints, expose cheats and lobby.
- **Producers** self-regulate through codes of conduct, quality control and guarantees; the **media** expose bad practice.
- **Consumers** must check goods, read labels, keep receipts, buy from licensed sellers and complain.
