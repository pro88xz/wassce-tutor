The **Portuguese** were the first Europeans to reach West Africa by sea, sailing down the coast from the **1440s** under the sponsorship of **Prince Henry the Navigator**. They came for **gold**, to find a **sea route to India**, to outflank the **Muslim** traders who controlled the trans-Saharan routes, to spread **Christianity**, and out of curiosity about the unknown. New ships and instruments, such as the **caravel**, the **compass** and the **astrolabe**, made the voyages possible. They were followed by the **Dutch, English, French, Danes** and others, who competed for trade from **forts** along the coast. This lesson explains why and how Europeans came to West Africa.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the motives of the Portuguese voyages to West Africa
- describe the technology that made the voyages possible
- outline the main stages of Portuguese exploration of the West African coast
- explain why other Europeans followed the Portuguese
- explain the purpose of European forts on the coast

## Why the Portuguese came

| Motive | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Gold** | to reach the source of the West African gold that reached Europe through North Africa |
| **Sea route to India** | to reach the spices of Asia by sailing around Africa, avoiding Muslim middlemen |
| **Religion** | to spread **Christianity** and to find **Prester John**, a legendary Christian king thought to rule in Africa or Asia |
| **Rivalry with Muslims** | to outflank the Muslim states of North Africa and weaken their control of trade |
| **Curiosity and knowledge** | to explore and map the unknown coast |
| **Prestige** | national glory for Portugal and its rulers |

## The technology of exploration

1. The **caravel**, a light ship with lateen (triangular) sails that could sail against the wind.
2. The **magnetic compass**, for direction.
3. The **astrolabe** and **quadrant**, to measure latitude from the sun or stars.
4. Better **maps** and the knowledge gathered by Prince Henry's sailors.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone got its name from the Portuguese explorer **Pedro de Sintra**, who in **1462** called the mountains of the peninsula **Serra Leoa** ("lion mountains"), perhaps because of their shape or the roar of thunder over them. The name changed over time into **Sierra Leone**.
:::

## Other Europeans follow

From the late sixteenth century, the **Dutch, English, French, Danes, Swedes and Brandenburgers** challenged Portugal's monopoly:

1. **Trade**: gold, ivory, pepper and, increasingly, **slaves** for the plantations of the Americas.
2. **The decline of Portuguese power**, especially when Portugal was united with Spain (1580–1640).
3. **Chartered companies** such as the Dutch West India Company and the English Royal African Company.

The **Dutch captured Elmina in 1637**; the English made **Cape Coast Castle** their headquarters from **1664**; and English traders set up a fort on **Bunce Island** in the Sierra Leone River around **1670**.

## The forts

European **forts and castles** along the coast, especially the **Gold Coast**, served to:

1. **Store goods** and later hold enslaved people before shipment.
2. **Protect** traders from rival Europeans and from attack.
3. Act as **trading posts** and administrative centres.
4. Show **claims** to trade on that part of the coast.

Europeans usually **rented** the land from local rulers and depended on African **middlemen**; they did not control the interior.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The Portuguese did not come to West Africa mainly for slaves. Their early aims were **gold**, a **sea route to India**, **Christianity** and **rivalry with Muslims**. The slave trade grew later, with the demand for labour in the Americas.
:::

## Key points

- Motives: **gold, sea route to India, Christianity (Prester John), rivalry with Muslims, curiosity, prestige**.
- Technology: **caravel, compass, astrolabe, maps**; sponsored by **Prince Henry the Navigator**.
- **1462**: Pedro de Sintra names **Sierra Leone**; **1482**: **Elmina Castle**.
- The **Dutch, English, French** and others followed; forts were **trading posts**, stores and defences.

## Quick check

1. Who sponsored the early Portuguese voyages?
2. Give three motives for the Portuguese voyages.
3. Who named Sierra Leone, and when?
4. Why was Elmina Castle built?
5. Give two uses of European forts on the coast.

**Answers:**

1. Prince Henry the Navigator.
2. Any three: gold; a sea route to India; spreading Christianity; rivalry with Muslims; curiosity and knowledge; prestige.
3. Pedro de Sintra, in 1462.
4. To control and protect the Portuguese gold trade on the Gold Coast.
5. Any two: storing goods and enslaved people; protection; trading posts; showing claims to trade.
