Look around any classroom in Sierra Leone: no two students are exactly alike. They differ in height, skin tone, blood group, the shape of their ears, whether they can roll their tongues. Yet every child also resembles its parents. **Variation** is the differences between members of a species; **heredity** is the passing of characteristics from parents to offspring. Both are explained by **DNA**, the molecule that carries the instructions for building and running every living thing. This lesson explains the types and causes of variation, how DNA, genes and chromosomes are related, and how mutations arise.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- distinguish continuous and discontinuous variation, with examples
- explain the genetic and environmental causes of variation
- describe the structure of DNA and the base-pairing rule
- explain the relationship between DNA, genes, alleles and chromosomes
- define the key terms of heredity
- describe mutation and its causes, and state some applications of genetics

## Two kinds of variation

![Left: a smooth bell-shaped histogram of student heights, showing continuous variation. Right: separate bars for blood groups A, B, AB and O, showing discontinuous variation](https://wasscetutor.com/img/biology/variation-graphs.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
| | **Continuous variation** | **Discontinuous variation** |
|---|---|---|
| values | any value within a range | a few separate, distinct categories |
| intermediates | many | none |
| graph | smooth, bell-shaped curve (histogram) | separate bars (bar chart) |
| controlled by | many genes **and** the environment | one or a few genes; little effect of environment |
| examples | height, body mass, skin colour, length of leaves | ABO blood group, sex, tongue rolling, sickle cell genotype |
:::

:::callout{type=tip}
Ask: **can I measure it on a scale, or do I sort it into groups?** Measure on a scale (height, mass) → continuous. Sort into groups (blood group A, B, AB, O) → discontinuous.
:::

## Causes of variation

**Genetic causes** (inherited):

- **Meiosis**: chromosomes are shuffled into gametes (independent assortment) and swap pieces (crossing over), so every gamete is different.
- **Random fertilisation**: any sperm can fuse with any egg.
- **Mutation**: a change in a gene or chromosome creates a new allele.

**Environmental causes** (not inherited): food, climate, disease, exercise, sunlight. A child who is well fed grows taller than an identical twin who is poorly fed; a plant in shade grows taller and paler than one in full sun.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Saying environmental variation is passed on.** A man who builds big muscles by farming does not pass bigger muscles to his children. Only variation caused by **genes** is inherited.
:::

## DNA: the molecule of heredity

![DNA shown untwisted as a ladder: two backbones of deoxyribose sugar and phosphate, with rungs of paired bases; A always pairs with T and G with C](https://wasscetutor.com/img/biology/dna-structure.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
**DNA** (deoxyribonucleic acid) is a **double helix**: two strands wound round each other like a twisted ladder.

- Each strand is a chain of **nucleotides**. A nucleotide = a **deoxyribose sugar** + a **phosphate** group + a **base**.
- The sugar and phosphate form the **backbone** (the sides of the ladder).
- The **bases** form the rungs. There are four: **adenine (A), thymine (T), guanine (G), cytosine (C)**.
- **Base pairing:** A always pairs with T; G always pairs with C, held by weak **hydrogen bonds**.
:::

The structure was worked out in 1953 by James Watson and Francis Crick, using X-ray photographs made by Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**One strand of DNA has the base sequence A T G C C A. Write the sequence of the complementary strand.**

Pair each base: A→T, T→A, G→C, C→G, C→G, A→T.

**Complementary strand: T A C G G T**
:::

### Why base pairing matters

Because each base has only one partner, each strand is a **template** for the other. Before a cell divides, the two strands separate and a new partner strand is built on each: **replication**. The two new DNA molecules are identical copies, so every cell gets the same instructions.

### DNA and RNA compared

| | DNA | RNA |
|---|---|---|
| sugar | deoxyribose | ribose |
| bases | A, T, G, C | A, **U** (uracil), G, C |
| strands | two (double helix) | one |
| found | mainly in the nucleus | nucleus and cytoplasm |

## From DNA to characteristics

![A homologous pair of chromosomes, one from each parent, with genes at matching loci; at one locus the alleles differ (T and t, heterozygous), at another they are the same (B and B, homozygous)](https://wasscetutor.com/img/biology/chromosome-alleles.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **chromosome** | a long, coiled molecule of DNA with proteins, in the nucleus |
| **gene** | a section of DNA that controls one characteristic (by coding for a protein) |
| **locus** | the position of a gene on a chromosome |
| **allele** | one of the different forms of the same gene (e.g. T for tall, t for dwarf) |
| **homologous chromosomes** | a matching pair, one from each parent, carrying the same genes |
| **genotype** | the alleles an organism has (e.g. Tt) |
| **phenotype** | the observable characteristic (e.g. tall), produced by genotype and environment |
| **homozygous** | two identical alleles (TT or tt) |
| **heterozygous** | two different alleles (Tt) |
| **dominant** | the allele that shows in the heterozygote (capital letter) |
| **recessive** | the allele that shows only when homozygous (small letter) |
:::

Human body cells have **46 chromosomes in 23 pairs**: 22 pairs of **autosomes** and one pair of **sex chromosomes** (XX in females, XY in males). Gametes have **23** single chromosomes (haploid), so fertilisation restores 46.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Mixing up gene and allele.** "Height" is controlled by a **gene**; "tall" and "dwarf" are its **alleles**. Every person has the gene for ABO blood group; the alleles are $I^A$, $I^B$ and $I^O$.
:::

## Mutation

:::callout{type=definition}
A **mutation** is a sudden, permanent change in the DNA of a cell.

- **Gene mutation**: a change in the base sequence of one gene. **Example:** sickle cell anaemia, caused by a change of one base in the gene for haemoglobin.
- **Chromosome mutation**: a change in the number or structure of chromosomes. **Example:** Down syndrome, with an extra copy of chromosome 21 (47 chromosomes).

**Mutagens** increase the rate of mutation: ionising radiation (X-rays, gamma rays), ultraviolet light, and some chemicals (such as those in tobacco smoke).
:::

Most mutations are harmful or have no effect, but a few are **useful**, and they are the raw material for evolution.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Genotype testing before marriage.** In Sierra Leone and across West Africa, many couples are now advised to test their haemoglobin genotype (AA, AS or SS) before marriage. Two AS partners have a 1 in 4 chance, with each child, of having a child with sickle cell disease. This **genetic counselling** is one of the most practical uses of heredity in daily life, and the next lesson shows how the chances are worked out.
:::

## Applications of genetics

- **Medicine**: blood transfusion (matching blood groups), genetic counselling, diagnosis of inherited disorders.
- **Agriculture**: breeding high-yielding, disease-resistant crops such as improved rice and cassava varieties, and better livestock.
- **Law**: DNA fingerprinting to identify people in crime and paternity cases.
- **Genetic engineering**: inserting genes into bacteria to make human insulin.

## Key points

- **Continuous** variation: a range of values (height); **discontinuous**: distinct groups (blood group).
- Variation comes from **genes** (meiosis, random fertilisation, mutation) and the **environment**; only genetic variation is inherited.
- **DNA** is a double helix of nucleotides; **A–T** and **G–C**.
- **Gene** = section of DNA for one characteristic; **alleles** = its forms; **genotype** and **phenotype**.
- Humans: **46** chromosomes (23 pairs), gametes **23**.
- **Mutation** = a permanent change in DNA; caused by mutagens; sickle cell and Down syndrome are examples.
