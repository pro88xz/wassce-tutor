A farmer who can recognise a pest or disease early, know how it lives and spreads, and choose the right control at the right time saves most of the crop. One who sprays the wrong chemical at the wrong stage wastes money and still loses the harvest. This lesson looks at crop pests and diseases from the farmer's side: how to classify and recognise them, the life cycles of insect pests, the major pests and diseases of Sierra Leone's crops, the factors that cause disease, and how to choose and apply control measures safely, including pesticide formulations and spraying equipment.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- classify crop pests and recognise their damage
- describe complete and incomplete metamorphosis and relate them to control
- name major pests and diseases of local crops with their symptoms
- explain the factors that lead to disease outbreaks
- describe methods of control and integrated pest management
- describe pesticide formulations, application equipment and safety

## Classifying pests

| Group | Examples | Typical damage |
|---|---|---|
| **biting and chewing insects** | grasshoppers, caterpillars, **fall armyworm**, beetles, termites | holes in leaves, eaten shoots, cut stems |
| **piercing and sucking insects** | aphids, whiteflies, mealybugs, **cocoa mirids**, cotton stainers, rice bugs | wilting, curling, dieback, empty grains; **spread viruses** |
| **boring insects** | **stem borers** of rice and maize, banana weevil | dead hearts, broken stems, tunnels in corms |
| **mites** | cassava green mite | speckled, yellow, small leaves |
| **nematodes** | root-knot nematodes | swollen galls on roots, stunting, wilting |
| **birds** | weaver birds, village weavers | grain eaten from panicles |
| **rodents** | rats, cane rats (grasscutters) | cut stems, eaten roots and grain, damage in stores |
| **storage pests** | weevils, grain moths, bruchids | holes and powder in grain and beans |

## Insect life cycles

![Complete metamorphosis (egg, larva, pupa, adult) in moths, beetles and armyworm, with the larva doing the damage; incomplete metamorphosis (egg, nymph, adult) in grasshoppers, aphids and bugs, where nymphs and adults both feed](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/insect-life-cycles.svg)

:::callout{type=tip}
**Knowing the life cycle tells you when to act.** For stem borers and armyworm, the **young larvae** feed before they tunnel into stems or hide deep in the whorl, so control must be early. For grasshoppers, the **young nymphs** gather in bands and are easier to control than flying adults.
:::

| Crop | Pest | Signs and notes |
|---|---|---|
| rice | stem borers; **African rice gall midge**; rice bugs; weaver birds; rats | dead hearts and white heads; onion-like galls instead of panicles; empty grains; eaten panicles |
| maize | stem borers; **fall armyworm**; weevils in store | ragged holes, sawdust-like droppings in the whorl; tunnels in stems |
| cassava | variegated grasshopper; cassava mealybug; green mite; cane rats | defoliation; bunched, distorted shoot tips; dug-up roots |
| cowpea | aphids; pod borers; pod-sucking bugs; bruchids in store | curled leaves; holes in pods; holes in stored beans |
| cocoa | **mirids (capsids)** | black lesions, dieback of shoots |
| plantain | banana weevil; nematodes | tunnels in the corm, toppling plants |
| vegetables | aphids, whiteflies, caterpillars, nematodes | curled leaves, viruses, holes, root galls |

## Crop diseases

### Causes and the disease triangle

A disease develops only when three things come together: a **susceptible host** (crop), a **pathogen** (fungus, bacterium or virus), and a **favourable environment** (often warm and wet). Take away any one and disease is reduced: plant resistant varieties, use clean planting material, or change conditions (spacing, drainage, pruning for air flow).

### Common symptoms

**Leaf spots**, **blights** (rapid browning), **wilting**, **rots** (of roots, pods or fruit), **mosaic** (mottled yellow and green leaves), **stunting**, **galls**, **damping off** (seedlings collapse), **dieback** of shoots.

| Crop | Disease | Cause | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| rice | **blast** | fungus | diamond-shaped spots; neck rot; spread by wind |
| rice | **rice yellow mottle** | virus | yellowing, stunting |
| maize | **maize streak** | virus | spread by leafhoppers |
| cassava | **cassava mosaic** | virus | whiteflies and infected cuttings |
| cassava | bacterial blight | bacterium | leaf spots, wilting; infected cuttings |
| cocoa | **black pod** | fungus (*Phytophthora*) | in wet weather; remove infected pods |
| plantain | **black sigatoka** | fungus | dark streaks, dead leaves |
| tomato, pepper | bacterial wilt; blights | bacterium; fungi | rotate crops; resistant varieties |
| seedlings | **damping off** | fungi | overwatering, crowding |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Treating every problem with the same spray.** An insecticide does nothing against fungi, bacteria or viruses; a fungicide does nothing against insects. Identify the **cause** first. For viral diseases there is **no cure**: control the vector and use clean, resistant planting material.
:::

## Methods of control

- **Cultural:** resistant varieties; clean seed and cuttings; crop rotation; timely planting; correct spacing; field sanitation (destroy crop residues and infected plants); balanced fertiliser; good drainage.
- **Biological:** natural enemies, such as the parasitic wasp that controls cassava mealybug, and predators such as ladybirds, spiders, birds, owls and snakes.
- **Physical and mechanical:** hand-picking egg masses and caterpillars; traps; bird scaring; rat guards; drying produce well; airtight storage.
- **Chemical:** insecticides, fungicides, nematicides, rodenticides, used correctly and only when needed.
- **Legislative:** **quarantine** laws stop infected plants and seeds entering the country or moving between districts.
- **Integrated pest management (IPM):** combine methods; monitor fields regularly; use chemicals only when pest numbers pass the level that causes economic loss.

## Pesticides

### Formulations

| Formulation | Description | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **dust** | fine powder applied dry | seed dressing, stored grain (approved products only) |
| **granules** | coarse grains applied to soil or into the whorl | soil pests, stem borers, fall armyworm |
| **wettable powder (WP)** | powder mixed with water | spraying |
| **emulsifiable concentrate (EC)** | liquid mixed with water to form an emulsion | spraying |
| **bait** | poison mixed with food | rats, slugs |
| **fumigant** | gas that penetrates stored produce | stores; **only by trained people** |

### Application equipment

- **Knapsack sprayer**: carried on the back; small and medium farms.
- **Motorised mist blower**: tree crops such as cocoa.
- **Boom sprayer**: tractor-mounted, large fields.
- **Duster** and **granule applicator**.

### Safety

Read and follow the **label**; wear **protective clothing** (gloves, mask, goggles, boots, long sleeves); mix in the open air; do not eat, drink or smoke while spraying; spray **with the wind behind you**, on calm, dry days; keep children and animals away; observe the **waiting period** before harvest; wash equipment and yourself; store chemicals locked away; never reuse empty containers for food or water; seek medical help at once for poisoning.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Scouting for fall armyworm.** Extension workers teach maize farmers to **walk through the field** once or twice a week from emergence, check a sample of plants in a W pattern, and look for egg masses, young caterpillars and fresh feeding in the whorl. Hand-picking and crushing egg masses, encouraging natural enemies, and spraying approved products only when many plants are infested save money and protect pollinators and farm families.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Give two examples each of chewing and sucking insect pests."}
Chewing: grasshoppers, caterpillars, fall armyworm, beetles (any two). Sucking: aphids, whiteflies, mealybugs, mirids, rice bugs (any two).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. State the stages of complete metamorphosis, and which stage of a moth damages crops."}
Egg → larva → pupa → adult. The **larva** (caterpillar) does the damage.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. What are the three parts of the disease triangle?"}
A **susceptible host**, a **pathogen**, and a **favourable environment**.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Name the cause and one control of black pod of cocoa."}
Cause: the fungus *Phytophthora*. Control: frequent harvesting; removing and destroying infected pods; pruning for air flow; copper fungicides (any one).
:::

:::reveal{title="5. List four precautions when spraying pesticides."}
Any four: read the label; wear protective clothing; do not eat, drink or smoke; spray with the wind behind you on calm days; keep children and animals away; observe the waiting period; wash equipment and yourself; store chemicals safely.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Pest**: any organism that damages crops, stored produce or animals.
- **Biting and chewing insects**: insects such as grasshoppers, caterpillars, fall armyworm and beetles that eat plant tissue.
- **Piercing and sucking insects**: insects such as aphids, whiteflies, mealybugs and cocoa mirids that suck plant sap.
- **Boring insects**: insects such as the stem borers of rice and maize that tunnel inside plants.
- **Fumigant**: a pesticide gas that penetrates stored produce.
- **Cultural control**: controlling pests and diseases through farm practices such as resistant varieties, clean planting material, crop rotation, timely planting and field sanitation.
- **Integrated pest management** (IPM): combining cultural, biological and chemical methods so that pesticides are used only when needed.
:::

## Key points

- Pests: **chewing, sucking and boring insects, mites, nematodes, birds, rodents, storage pests**.
- Life cycles: **egg–larva–pupa–adult** (larva damages) and **egg–nymph–adult**; control the young stages.
- Disease needs **host + pathogen + favourable environment**; viruses cannot be cured.
- Know local examples: **stem borers, gall midge, fall armyworm, mirids; blast, cassava mosaic, black pod, maize streak**.
- Control: **cultural, biological, physical, chemical, legislative**, combined in **IPM**.
- Pesticides: know the **formulations**, **equipment** and **safety rules**.
