Waves bounce, bend and spread. The difference between reflection, refraction and diffraction in one line each: **reflection** is a wave bouncing back from a surface, **refraction** is a wave bending because its speed changes as it enters a new medium, and **diffraction** is a wave spreading out through a gap or round an obstacle. You hear your voice echo back from a hill, you see a straight stick look bent in water, and you hear a friend talking round a corner. All three happen to every kind of wave: water, sound and light.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the laws of reflection and describe the reflection of water waves
- explain refraction as a change of speed and use $n = \dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \dfrac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2}$
- describe diffraction and the effect of gap size
- give everyday examples of each phenomenon for water, sound and light
- state the differences between reflection, refraction and diffraction, and say where interference fits

## Reflection

:::callout{type=definition}
**Reflection** is the bouncing back of a wave when it strikes a barrier or a boundary between two media.

**Laws of reflection:**

1. The **angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection**.
2. The incident wave (ray), the reflected wave (ray) and the **normal** at the point of incidence all lie in the **same plane**.
:::

In a ripple tank, straight waves hitting a straight barrier at an angle are reflected at the same angle. Circular waves from a point reflected by a straight barrier appear to come from an image point behind it, just like light in a plane mirror. The **wavelength, frequency and speed are unchanged** by reflection.

Examples: echoes of sound, light in mirrors, radar, water waves at a harbour wall.

## Refraction

:::callout{type=definition}
**Refraction** is the **change in direction** of a wave as it passes from one medium into another, caused by a **change in its speed**.
:::

![Straight water waves passing from deep water into shallow water: the wavefronts are closer together in the shallow water and the direction of travel bends towards the normal](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/wave-refraction.svg)

*Figure 1. Water waves slow down in shallow water and bend towards the normal. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

When water waves move from **deep to shallow** water:

- their **speed decreases**
- their **wavelength decreases** (the wavefronts get closer together)
- their **frequency stays the same**
- their direction bends **towards the normal** (unless they meet the boundary head-on)

The **refractive index** from medium 1 to medium 2 is

$$n = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r} = \dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \dfrac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Water waves of wavelength 3.0 cm travel at 15 cm s⁻¹ in deep water. In shallow water their speed is 10 cm s⁻¹. Find (a) their frequency, (b) their wavelength in shallow water.**

(a) $f = \dfrac{v}{\lambda} = \dfrac{15}{3.0} = 5$ Hz (unchanged in both regions).

(b) $\lambda_2 = \dfrac{v_2}{f} = \dfrac{10}{5} = 2.0$ cm.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Waves rolling towards Lumley Beach slow down as the water gets shallower near the shore. Their fronts swing round until they arrive almost parallel to the beach, whatever direction they came from out at sea. That is refraction.
:::

## Diffraction

:::callout{type=definition}
**Diffraction** is the **spreading out** of waves as they pass through a gap (aperture) or round the edge of an obstacle.
:::

![Left: waves passing through a wide gap continue almost straight with little spreading. Right: waves passing through a narrow gap spread out in circular arcs](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/diffraction-gaps.svg)

*Figure 2. The narrower the gap compared with the wavelength, the more the waves spread. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

- Diffraction is **greatest when the gap is about the same size as the wavelength**.
- A gap much **wider** than the wavelength gives **little** diffraction.
- Diffraction does **not** change the wavelength, frequency or speed.

**Examples:**

- **Sound** (wavelengths about 0.3–3 m) diffracts round doorways and buildings, so you hear people you cannot see.
- **Light** has a tiny wavelength (about $5 \times 10^{-7}$ m), so it diffracts only through very narrow slits; that is why light seems to travel in straight lines and casts sharp shadows.
- **Long-wave radio** diffracts round hills, so it can be received in valleys; short-wave TV signals cannot, which is why some towns need relay masts.

## Difference between reflection, refraction and diffraction

The quickest way to remember them: **reflection bounces, refraction bends, diffraction spreads**. Interference, which you will meet in the next lesson, is what happens when two waves **overlap**.

| | Reflection | Refraction | Diffraction | Interference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What happens** | The wave bounces back from a barrier | The wave changes direction as it enters a new medium | The wave spreads out through a gap or round an obstacle | Two waves overlap and add up or cancel |
| **Cause** | Meeting a barrier or boundary | A change of **speed** | Passing a gap or edge | Superposition of two waves from coherent sources |
| **Direction** | Changes (angle i = angle r) | Changes, unless it meets the boundary head-on | Spreads out in arcs | Each wave keeps its direction |
| **Speed** | Unchanged | **Changes** | Unchanged | Unchanged |
| **Wavelength** | Unchanged | **Changes** (with the speed) | Unchanged | Unchanged |
| **Frequency** | Unchanged | Unchanged | Unchanged | Unchanged |
| **Water waves** | Bouncing off a harbour wall | Slowing and turning in shallow water near a beach | Spreading through a harbour entrance | Calm and rough lines from two dippers in a ripple tank |
| **Sound** | An echo from a hill or a wall | Sound heard farther at night, bent by layers of air at different temperatures | Hearing someone round a corner or through a doorway | Loud and quiet spots between two loudspeakers playing the same note |
| **Light** | An image in a mirror | A stick looks bent in water; a pool looks shallower than it is | Bright and dark fringes through a very narrow slit | Coloured bands on a soap bubble or an oil film |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Mixing up refraction and diffraction.** Both make a wave change direction, but for different reasons. **Refraction** happens at a **boundary between two media** because the wave's **speed changes**, so its wavelength changes too. **Diffraction** happens at a **gap or an edge** in the **same medium**: the wave spreads out, and its speed and wavelength stay the same.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**State two differences between refraction and diffraction of water waves.**

1. Refraction is caused by a change of speed when the waves pass from deep to shallow water; diffraction is caused by the waves passing through a gap or round an obstacle.
2. In refraction the wavelength changes (it is shorter in shallow water); in diffraction the wavelength stays the same.

Other acceptable answers: refraction needs two regions of different depth (two media), diffraction does not; refracted wavefronts stay straight but change direction, diffracted wavefronts curve and spread out.
:::

## Key points

- Reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection; λ, f and v unchanged.
- Refraction: change of direction due to change of speed; frequency unchanged; $n = \dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \dfrac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2} = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$.
- Diffraction: spreading through gaps and round obstacles; greatest when gap ≈ wavelength.
- The difference in a phrase: reflection **bounces**, refraction **bends**, diffraction **spreads**; interference is two waves **overlapping**.
- Only refraction changes the speed and wavelength; none of the four changes the frequency.

## Quick check

1. Which quantity does not change when a wave is refracted?
2. When is diffraction at a gap greatest?
3. Why can you hear someone around a corner but not see them?
4. Which of reflection, refraction and diffraction changes the speed of a wave?
5. A straight stick looks bent where it enters water. Is this reflection, refraction or diffraction?

**Answers:** 1. The frequency. 2. When the width of the gap is about equal to the wavelength. 3. Sound waves have wavelengths comparable to doorways, so they diffract a lot; light's wavelength is far smaller, so it hardly diffracts. 4. Only refraction. 5. Refraction: light changes speed and bends as it passes from water into air.
