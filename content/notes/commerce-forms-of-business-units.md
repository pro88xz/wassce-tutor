# Forms of business units: ownership, liability and control

## Learning objectives
Explain a business unit, compare common forms of ownership and distinguish limited from unlimited liability.

## Meaning
A business unit is an organisation established to carry out economic activities, usually to provide goods or services. Its legal form affects ownership, control, capital, continuity, profit distribution and responsibility for debts. The appropriate form depends on the size and risk of the activity, the capital required and the owners' objectives.

## Main forms of business
**Sole proprietorship:** owned and controlled by one person. It can be relatively simple to establish, decisions are quick and the owner receives the residual profit. Capital may be limited, and the owner normally has unlimited liability, meaning personal assets may be at risk for business debts subject to applicable law.

**Partnership:** two or more persons carry on business together under an agreement. Partners can combine capital, skills and contacts and share profits and responsibilities. In an ordinary partnership, partners may have unlimited liability; the precise position depends on the registered form and governing law. Disagreements may affect decisions.

**Company:** a company is a separate legal person from its shareholders. In a company limited by shares, shareholders' liability is generally limited to the amount unpaid on their shares. Companies may have better access to capital and continuity, but must meet formal registration, reporting and governance requirements. Private and public companies differ in how shares may be offered and transferred.

**Cooperative:** owned and controlled by members who join to meet shared economic or social needs. Cooperatives may help farmers market produce, consumers obtain goods or workers organise services. Member control and service to members are central principles.

**Public enterprise:** owned or controlled by the state to provide goods or services or pursue a public policy objective. Its accountability and structure depend on the law establishing it.

| Form | Ownership | General liability position |
|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | One individual | Usually unlimited |
| Ordinary partnership | Partners | Often unlimited |
| Limited company | Shareholders | Limited under company law |
| Cooperative | Members | Depends on registered form |
| Public enterprise | State | Depends on its legal structure |

## WASSCE examination focus
Compare forms using the same criteria: ownership, capital, control, continuity and liability. A public company is not the same as a public enterprise: the former is a company whose shares may be offered to the public under applicable rules; the latter is state-owned or controlled.
