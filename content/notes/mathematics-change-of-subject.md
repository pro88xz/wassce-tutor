A formula connects quantities: $A = \pi r^2$ gives the area of a circle from its radius. But what if you know the area and need the radius? You **change the subject** of the formula: rearrange it so that $r$ stands alone: $r = \sqrt{\dfrac{A}{\pi}}$. Changing the subject uses exactly the same balance method as solving equations, but with letters instead of numbers. WASSCE sets it almost every year, often with square roots, fractions or the new subject appearing twice. This lesson works through every kind.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- rearrange a formula to make a given letter the subject
- handle formulas with brackets, fractions, powers and roots
- make the subject of a formula a letter that appears in two places
- substitute into the rearranged formula to find a value

## The idea

:::callout{type=definition}
The **subject** of a formula is the letter on its own on one side: in $v = u + at$, the subject is $v$.

To **change the subject**, undo the operations around the new subject, in **reverse order**, doing the same to both sides.
:::

:::callout{type=tip}
Ask: *"If I knew the new subject's value, what would I do to it to get the other side?"* Then undo those steps in reverse. For $v = u + at$ and the new subject $t$: you would multiply $t$ by $a$, then add $u$. So undo: **subtract $u$**, then **divide by $a$**.
:::

## Simple rearrangements

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**1. Make $t$ the subject of $v = u + at$.**

$$v - u = at \quad\Rightarrow\quad t = \frac{v - u}{a}$$

**2. Make $r$ the subject of $C = 2\pi r$.**

$$r = \frac{C}{2\pi}$$

**3. Make $x$ the subject of $y = \dfrac{3x - 5}{2}$.**

$$2y = 3x - 5 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 2y + 5 = 3x \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \frac{2y + 5}{3}$$
:::

## Powers and roots

Undo a **square** with a **square root**, and a **square root** with a **square**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**1. Make $r$ the subject of $A = \pi r^2$.**

$$r^2 = \frac{A}{\pi} \quad\Rightarrow\quad r = \sqrt{\frac{A}{\pi}}$$

**2. Make $l$ the subject of $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$.**

Divide by $2\pi$: $\dfrac{T}{2\pi} = \sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$

Square both sides: $\dfrac{T^2}{4\pi^2} = \dfrac{l}{g}$

Multiply by $g$: $$l = \frac{gT^2}{4\pi^2}$$

**3. Make $b$ the subject of $a = \sqrt{b^2 - c}$.**

$$a^2 = b^2 - c \quad\Rightarrow\quad b^2 = a^2 + c \quad\Rightarrow\quad b = \sqrt{a^2 + c}$$
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Taking the square root of each term separately.** $\sqrt{a^2 + c}$ is **not** $a + \sqrt{c}$. A square root applies to the **whole** expression. For example, $\sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5$, not $3 + 4 = 7$.
:::

## When the new subject is in a fraction's denominator

Multiply both sides by the denominator first.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Make $R$ the subject of $I = \dfrac{V}{R}$.**

$$IR = V \quad\Rightarrow\quad R = \frac{V}{I}$$

**Make $f$ the subject of $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{u} + \dfrac{1}{v}$.**

Combine the right side: $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{v + u}{uv}$

Turn both sides upside down: $$f = \frac{uv}{u + v}$$
:::

## When the new subject appears twice

This is the type that separates strong candidates. **Collect** all the terms containing the new subject on one side, **factorise** it out, then divide.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Make $x$ the subject of $ax + b = cx + d$.**

$$ax - cx = d - b \quad\Rightarrow\quad x(a - c) = d - b \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \frac{d - b}{a - c}$$

**Make $x$ the subject of $y = \dfrac{x + 2}{x - 3}$.**

1. Multiply by $(x - 3)$: $y(x - 3) = x + 2$
2. Expand: $xy - 3y = x + 2$
3. Collect $x$ terms: $xy - x = 2 + 3y$
4. Factorise: $x(y - 1) = 2 + 3y$
5. Divide: $$x = \frac{3y + 2}{y - 1}$$
:::

## Using the new formula

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Make $a$ the subject of $S = \dfrac{n}{2}(a + l)$, then find $a$ when $S = 120$, $n = 10$ and $l = 21$.**

$$2S = n(a + l) \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{2S}{n} = a + l \;\Rightarrow\; a = \frac{2S}{n} - l$$

$$a = \frac{2(120)}{10} - 21 = 24 - 21 = 3$$
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
A taxi fare in Freetown is $F = 20 + 6d$, where $d$ is the distance in kilometres. Making $d$ the subject tells you how far a given fare will take you: $d = \dfrac{F - 20}{6}$. With NLe 80, you can travel $d = \dfrac{80 - 20}{6} = 10$ km.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Make h the subject of V = πr²h."}
$$h = \frac{V}{\pi r^2}$$
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Make u the subject of v² = u² + 2as."}
$$u^2 = v^2 - 2as \quad\Rightarrow\quad u = \sqrt{v^2 - 2as}$$
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Make x the subject of p = (2x + q)/3."}
$$3p = 2x + q \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \frac{3p - q}{2}$$
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Make m the subject of k = √(m/n)."}
Square: $k^2 = \dfrac{m}{n}$, so $m = nk^2$.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Make x the subject of 3x − a = bx + 5."}
$$3x - bx = 5 + a \;\Rightarrow\; x(3 - b) = 5 + a \;\Rightarrow\; x = \frac{a + 5}{3 - b}$$
:::

:::reveal{title="6. Make t the subject of s = (t + 1)/(t − 1), then find t when s = 3."}
$$s(t - 1) = t + 1 \;\Rightarrow\; st - s = t + 1 \;\Rightarrow\; st - t = s + 1 \;\Rightarrow\; t = \frac{s + 1}{s - 1}$$

When $s = 3$: $t = \dfrac{4}{2} = 2$. (Check: $\dfrac{2 + 1}{2 - 1} = 3$ ✓)
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Subject of a formula**: the single letter on its own on one side of a formula, such as $v$ in $v = u + at$.
- **Changing the subject**: rearranging a formula so that a different letter stands alone, by doing the same inverse operations to both sides.
- **Inverse operation**: the operation that undoes another, such as subtraction for addition or squaring for a square root.
- **Algebraic formula**: a rule written in letters that connects quantities, such as $A = \pi r^2$.
- **Collecting terms**: bringing every term that contains the new subject to one side, then factorising it out, when the subject appears in two places.
- **Square root of both sides**: taking the square root of both sides to undo a square; the answer may need both the positive and the negative root.
:::

## Key points

- Undo operations in **reverse order**, doing the same to **both sides**.
- Undo a square with a **square root**; undo a root by **squaring**. A root covers the **whole** expression.
- Clear fractions by **multiplying by the denominator** first.
- If the new subject appears **twice**: collect, **factorise**, divide.
- **Check** by substituting numbers into both forms.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Change of subject appears as objective questions (*"Make $x$ the subject of…"*) and as the first part of theory questions, usually followed by a substitution. The hardest and most frequently tested type has the new subject **in two places** or **under a square root**; examiners award method marks for each correct step, so show every line.
:::
