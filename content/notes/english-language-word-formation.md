If you know the word *happy*, you already know half of *unhappy*, *happiness*, *happily* and *unhappiness*. English builds new words from old ones in a few regular ways: adding **prefixes** and **suffixes**, joining words into **compounds**, and changing a word's **class**. Learning these patterns multiplies your vocabulary, helps you work out unfamiliar words in a passage, and is tested directly in WASSCE (*"Choose the correct form of the word"*). This lesson teaches the patterns with the most useful prefixes, suffixes and spelling rules.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- identify the root, prefix and suffix of a word
- use common prefixes to change a word's meaning
- use common suffixes to change a word's class (noun, verb, adjective, adverb)
- form compounds and recognise other ways new words are made
- apply the spelling rules for adding suffixes

## Roots, prefixes and suffixes

:::callout{type=definition}
- The **root** (or base) carries the main meaning: *happy*, *agree*, *nation*.
- A **prefix** is added **before** the root and usually changes its **meaning**: ***un**happy*, ***dis**agree*.
- A **suffix** is added **after** the root and usually changes its **word class**: *happi**ness*** (noun), *nation**al*** (adjective).

*un* + *happy* + *ness* = *unhappiness*
:::

## Prefixes that change meaning

| Prefix | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **un-, in-, im-, il-, ir-, dis-, non-** | not, the opposite | *unfair, inactive, impossible, illegal, irresponsible, dishonest, non-violent* |
| **mis-** | wrongly | *misunderstand, misspell, mislead* |
| **re-** | again | *rewrite, rebuild, reconsider* |
| **pre-** | before | *preview, prehistoric, prepay* |
| **post-** | after | *postwar, postgraduate, postpone* |
| **over-** | too much | *overcrowded, overpriced, overeat* |
| **under-** | too little; below | *underpaid, underweight, underground* |
| **inter-** | between | *international, interschool, interact* |
| **sub-** | under | *submarine, subway, substandard* |
| **anti-** | against | *antibiotic, antisocial, anti-corruption* |
| **co-** | together | *co-operate, co-author, co-exist* |
| **ex-** | former | *ex-president, ex-student* |
| **multi-** | many | *multipurpose, multinational* |

:::callout{type=tip}
**Which "not" prefix?** Before *p*, *b* or *m*, use **im-** (*impossible, imbalance, immature*); before *l*, use **il-** (*illegal, illiterate*); before *r*, use **ir-** (*irregular, irrelevant*). The rest are learnt word by word: *unhappy* but *incorrect*.
:::

## Suffixes that change word class

### Making nouns

| Suffix | From | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **-tion, -sion** | verbs | *educate → education, decide → decision, inform → information* |
| **-ment** | verbs | *develop → development, govern → government, agree → agreement* |
| **-ness** | adjectives | *kind → kindness, happy → happiness, dark → darkness* |
| **-ity** | adjectives | *able → ability, equal → equality, pure → purity* |
| **-ance, -ence** | verbs / adjectives | *perform → performance, depend → dependence, patient → patience* |
| **-er, -or, -ist, -ian** | people who do | *teach → teacher, act → actor, science → scientist, music → musician* |
| **-hood, -ship** | states, relationships | *child → childhood, friend → friendship, leader → leadership* |

### Making adjectives

| Suffix | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **-ful** | full of | *hope → hopeful, care → careful, success → successful* |
| **-less** | without | *hope → hopeless, care → careless, home → homeless* |
| **-able, -ible** | can be | *read → readable, eat → edible, respond → responsible* |
| **-ous** | having the quality of | *danger → dangerous, fame → famous, courage → courageous* |
| **-al** | relating to | *nation → national, culture → cultural, agriculture → agricultural* |
| **-ive** | tending to | *act → active, create → creative, attract → attractive* |
| **-ic** | relating to | *economy → economic, history → historic, science → scientific* |
| **-y** | full of, like | *rain → rainy, dust → dusty, health → healthy* |

### Making verbs and adverbs

| Suffix | Makes | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **-ise / -ize** | verbs | *modern → modernise, organ → organise, real → realise* |
| **-en** | verbs | *wide → widen, short → shorten, strength → strengthen* |
| **-ify** | verbs | *beauty → beautify, simple → simplify, class → classify* |
| **-ly** | adverbs | *quick → quickly, careful → carefully, happy → happily* |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**One root, a whole family:**

| Noun | Verb | Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|---|---|
| success | succeed | successful | successfully |
| education | educate | educational | educationally |
| danger | endanger | dangerous | dangerously |
| beauty | beautify | beautiful | beautifully |
| strength | strengthen | strong | strongly |
| decision | decide | decisive | decisively |
:::

## Spelling rules for suffixes

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Drop a final **silent e** before a suffix beginning with a vowel | *hope → hoping, create → creative, fame → famous* |
| Keep the **e** before a consonant suffix | *hope → hopeful, care → careful, manage → management* |
| Change **y** to **i** after a consonant | *happy → happiness, beauty → beautiful, carry → carried* |
| **Double** the final consonant after a short stressed vowel | *begin → beginning, stop → stopped, run → runner* |
| Keep **y** before *-ing* | *carry → carrying, study → studying* |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The spellings examiners see wrong most often: *beginning* (not *begining*), *successful* (one *l* at the end), *government* (don't drop the *n*), *environment* (the same), *argument* (the *e* of *argue* is dropped), *truly* (no *e*), *noticeable* (the *e* stays to keep the soft *c*).
:::

## Other ways words are made

| Process | How | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Compounding** | joining two words | *classroom, blackboard, football, headmaster, sunflower, passer-by* |
| **Conversion** | using a word in a new class, unchanged | *a **text** → to **text** someone; a **phone** → to **phone*** |
| **Blending** | merging parts of two words | *smoke + fog → **smog**; breakfast + lunch → **brunch*** |
| **Clipping** | shortening a word | *examination → **exam**; telephone → **phone**; mathematics → **maths*** |
| **Acronyms** | initial letters said as a word | ***WAEC**, **NASSIT**, **AIDS**, **NATO*** |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leonean English has added its own words by these processes: *okada* (borrowed), *poda-poda* (borrowed from Krio), and acronyms such as *NASSIT* and *EDSA* that everyone says as words. In the examination, use them where they are the clearest name, but remember that WASSCE questions test **standard** English word formation.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Give the noun form: (a) decide (b) able (c) govern (d) brave"}
(a) **decision** (b) **ability** (c) **government** (d) **bravery**
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Complete with the correct form of 'care': 'The driver was ____, so the accident was his fault. He drove ____.'"}
*The driver was **careless**… He drove **carelessly**.*
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Give the opposite with the right prefix: (a) responsible (b) literate (c) patient (d) understand"}
(a) **irresponsible** (b) **illiterate** (c) **impatient** (d) **misunderstand**
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Correct the spellings: begining, sucessful, goverment, truely, enviroment"}
**beginning, successful, government, truly, environment**
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Choose the correct form: 'Farming is the main ____ activity in the district.' A. agriculture B. agricultural C. agriculturally D. agriculturist"}
**B. agricultural**: an adjective is needed before the noun *activity*.
:::

## Key points

- Words are built from a **root**, with **prefixes** (changing meaning) and **suffixes** (changing class).
- Learn the **"not" prefixes** (*un-, in-, im-, il-, ir-, dis-, non-*) and the common noun, adjective, verb and adverb suffixes.
- Apply the **spelling rules**: drop the silent *e* before vowels, *y* to *i*, double the consonant after a short stressed vowel.
- New words also come from **compounding, conversion, blending, clipping** and **acronyms**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
The objective paper asks you to choose the **correct form of a word** for a gap, which tests whether you know which suffix gives the class the sentence needs. Word formation also helps with **vocabulary** and **comprehension** questions, where you can work out an unfamiliar word from its parts. In essays, wrongly formed words and suffix spelling errors cost **Mechanical accuracy** marks.
:::
