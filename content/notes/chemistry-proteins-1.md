Fish, beans, groundnuts and eggs are body-building foods because they are rich in **proteins**. Every protein, from the keratin in your hair to the enzymes in your stomach, is a long chain of small units called **amino acids**, joined by **peptide bonds**. This lesson covers amino acids and how they link together.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the elements present in proteins and name good food sources
- describe the general structure of an amino acid and name simple examples
- explain why amino acids are amphoteric
- distinguish essential and non-essential amino acids
- describe the formation of a peptide bond by condensation
- explain the terms dipeptide, polypeptide and protein

## Proteins in food

Proteins contain **carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen**, and often **sulphur** (and sometimes phosphorus). The **nitrogen** distinguishes them from carbohydrates and fats.

**Good sources:** fish, meat, eggs, milk, beans, groundnuts, soya, cowpeas (black-eyed beans), and leafy vegetables in smaller amounts.

## Amino acids

:::callout{type=definition}
An **amino acid** is an organic compound containing both an **amino group (–NH₂)** and a **carboxyl group (–COOH)**. In the amino acids found in proteins, both groups are attached to the **same carbon atom**.
:::

General formula: **H₂N–CHR–COOH**, where **R** is a **side group** that differs from one amino acid to another. About **20** different amino acids occur in proteins.

| Amino acid | R group | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| **Glycine** (aminoethanoic acid) | –H | H₂NCH₂COOH |
| **Alanine** (2-aminopropanoic acid) | –CH₃ | H₂NCH(CH₃)COOH |
| **Serine** | –CH₂OH | H₂NCH(CH₂OH)COOH |
| **Cysteine** | –CH₂SH (contains sulphur) | H₂NCH(CH₂SH)COOH |

### Amino acids are amphoteric

The **–COOH** group is **acidic** and the **–NH₂** group is **basic**, so amino acids react with **both acids and bases**:

- with an alkali: H₂NCH₂COOH + NaOH → H₂NCH₂COONa + H₂O
- with an acid: H₂NCH₂COOH + HCl → ClH₃NCH₂COOH (i.e. ⁺H₃NCH₂COOH Cl⁻)

In solid amino acids and in neutral solution, the H⁺ of the –COOH moves to the –NH₂ group, giving a **zwitterion**, ⁺H₃N–CHR–COO⁻. This ionic form explains why amino acids are **crystalline solids** with **high melting points** that dissolve in water.

### Essential amino acids

- **Essential** amino acids **cannot be made by the body** and must come from food (for example lysine, leucine, valine, tryptophan; nine for adults).
- **Non-essential** amino acids can be made in the body from other amino acids.
- Animal proteins (fish, eggs, milk) contain all the essential amino acids. Single plant foods may lack some, so **mixing cereals and legumes** (rice and beans) gives a complete set.

## The peptide bond

![Two amino acids join: the OH of one carboxyl group and an H of the other's amino group leave as water, forming the –CO–NH– peptide bond of a dipeptide](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/peptide-bond.svg)

*Figure 1. Two amino acids form a dipeptide by condensation. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

The **–COOH** of one amino acid reacts with the **–NH₂** of another, losing a molecule of **water** (a **condensation** reaction). The new link, **–CO–NH–**, is the **peptide bond** (an **amide** link):

$$\text{H}_2\text{NCH}_2\text{COOH} + \text{H}_2\text{NCH}_2\text{COOH} \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{NCH}_2\text{CONHCH}_2\text{COOH} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

Two glycine molecules give the dipeptide **glycylglycine**.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Dipeptide** | Two amino acids joined by one peptide bond |
| **Polypeptide** | A chain of many amino acids (roughly up to 50) |
| **Protein** | One or more long polypeptide chains (often hundreds of amino acids) folded into a particular shape |

Proteins are therefore **natural condensation polymers**, with amino acids as the monomers. Because there are 20 amino acids and they can be arranged in any order, the number of possible proteins is enormous.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Water lost in forming a peptide.** How many water molecules are released when 50 amino acids join into one polypeptide chain? What is the relative molecular mass of a dipeptide made from glycine (Mᵣ 75) and alanine (Mᵣ 89)?

- A chain of n amino acids has **n − 1** peptide bonds, so 50 amino acids release **49** water molecules.
- Dipeptide: $75 + 89 - 18 = 146$.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Kwashiorkor**, a disease of young children who get enough starch but too little protein, causes swollen bellies, thin limbs and poor growth. In Sierra Leone, cheap protein sources such as **bonga fish**, groundnuts, beans and eggs, added to rice or cassava meals, provide the essential amino acids that starchy staples alone do not.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The peptide bond is **–CO–NH–**, formed from the **carboxyl group of one** amino acid and the **amino group of the next**. It is not formed between two –COOH groups or two –NH₂ groups.
:::

## Key points

- Proteins contain **C, H, O, N** (often S); the **nitrogen** sets them apart from carbohydrates and fats.
- **Amino acids**: H₂N–CHR–COOH, with an **amino** and a **carboxyl** group; about **20** occur in proteins (glycine R = H, alanine R = CH₃).
- Amino acids are **amphoteric** and exist as **zwitterions**.
- **Essential** amino acids must come from food.
- Amino acids join by **condensation**, losing water, to form **peptide bonds (–CO–NH–)**; proteins are **condensation polymers**.

## Quick check

1. Which element is present in all proteins but not in carbohydrates?
2. Name the two functional groups in an amino acid.
3. Draw (in condensed form) the peptide bond.
4. Why are amino acids described as amphoteric?

**Answers:**

1. Nitrogen.
2. The amino group (–NH₂) and the carboxyl group (–COOH).
3. –CO–NH–.
4. They contain an acidic carboxyl group and a basic amino group, so they react with both acids and bases.
