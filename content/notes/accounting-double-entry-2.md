**Ledger accounts** are classified into **personal accounts** (accounts of people and organisations the business deals with), **real accounts** (accounts of things the business owns, such as land, vehicles and stock) and **nominal accounts** (accounts of income, expenses, gains and losses). Each class has a traditional rule of debit and credit, sometimes called the **golden rules of accounting**, and the rules agree with the modern rules based on assets, liabilities, capital, income and expenses. This lesson explains the classification, the rules for each class, and how to apply them to transactions.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- classify accounts as personal, real or nominal
- state the traditional rule of debit and credit for each class
- relate the traditional rules to the modern rules
- record transactions with correct account classification
- explain the special treatment of purchases, sales and returns

## Classes of accounts

| Class | What it records | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Personal accounts** | people, firms and organisations | Kamara (customer), Sesay Wholesalers (supplier), Rokel Commercial Bank, capital and drawings (the owner) |
| **Real accounts** | things owned: tangible and intangible assets | land and buildings, motor vehicles, furniture, cash, bank, goodwill |
| **Nominal accounts** | income, expenses, gains and losses | sales, purchases, wages, rent, electricity, commission received, discount allowed |

Personal accounts can be **natural** (individuals such as Kamara), **artificial** (companies, banks, clubs) or **representative** (accounts that stand for a person, such as accrued wages owed to staff).

## The traditional rules

| Class | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| **Personal** | the **receiver** | the **giver** |
| **Real** | what **comes in** | what **goes out** |
| **Nominal** | all **expenses and losses** | all **incomes and gains** |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Apply the traditional rules (NLe).**

1. **Bought furniture for NLe 6,000 cash.** Furniture comes in (real: debit furniture); cash goes out (real: credit cash).
2. **Paid rent NLe 2,500 by cheque.** Rent is an expense (nominal: debit rent); bank money goes out (real: credit bank).
3. **Sold goods on credit to Kamara for NLe 4,000.** Kamara receives (personal: debit Kamara); sales is income (nominal: credit sales).
4. **Paid Sesay Wholesalers NLe 7,000 by cheque.** Sesay receives (personal: debit Sesay); bank money goes out (real: credit bank).
5. **Received commission of NLe 900 in cash.** Cash comes in (real: debit cash); commission is income (nominal: credit commission received).
:::

## Traditional and modern rules agree

| Account | Traditional view | Modern view | Entry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Debit cash when received | real: what comes in | asset increases | Dr |
| Credit a supplier for credit purchases | personal: the giver | liability increases | Cr |
| Debit wages | nominal: expense | expense | Dr |
| Credit sales | nominal: income | income | Cr |
| Credit capital when the owner invests | personal: the owner gives | capital increases | Cr |

Both approaches always give the **same entries**. WASSCE accepts either, as long as the entry is correct.

## Purchases, sales and returns

The movement of **goods for resale** is not recorded in a single "stock" account during the year. Instead, separate accounts are used:

| Account | Records | Entry for an increase |
|---|---|---|
| **Purchases** | goods bought for resale | Debit |
| **Sales** | goods sold | Credit |
| **Returns inwards (sales returns)** | goods returned **by customers** | Debit |
| **Returns outwards (purchases returns)** | goods returned **to suppliers** | Credit |
| **Inventory (stock)** | value of goods in hand at the **start and end** of the period only | Debit (an asset) |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Kamara returns goods worth NLe 500 that he bought on credit. Give the entries.**

Returns inwards account is **debited** NLe 500 (sales are reduced); Kamara's account is **credited** NLe 500 (he now owes less, because he is giving the goods back).
:::

:::callout{type=definition}
**Purchases** means only **goods bought for resale**.
:::

A van or a computer bought for use in the business is a **non-current asset**, debited to its own account, not to purchases.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not debit the purchases account when the business buys a motor van, shop fittings or a computer for its own use. These are **non-current assets** and have their own real accounts. Purchases are **only goods bought for resale**.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Personal accounts**: accounts of people, firms and organisations; debit the receiver, credit the giver.
- **Real accounts**: accounts of things owned, tangible and intangible assets; debit what comes in, credit what goes out.
- **Nominal accounts**: accounts of income, expenses, gains and losses; debit expenses and losses, credit income and gains.
- **Returns inwards** (sales returns): goods returned by customers.
- **Returns outwards** (purchases returns): goods returned to suppliers.
:::

## Key points

- **Personal:** debit the receiver, credit the giver. **Real:** debit what comes in, credit what goes out. **Nominal:** debit expenses and losses, credit incomes and gains.
- Traditional and modern rules give the **same entries**.
- Goods for resale use **purchases, sales, returns inwards and returns outwards** accounts.
- Assets bought for use are **non-current assets**, not purchases.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **classify accounts as personal, real or nominal**, to **state the rule for each class**, and to **identify the accounts to debit and credit**. Questions on returns inwards and returns outwards often catch candidates out: decide who is returning the goods.
:::

## Quick check

1. Classify: Rokel Commercial Bank, motor van, salaries, Kamara.
2. State the rule for real accounts.
3. Give the entries for rent paid by cheque.
4. Give the entries for goods returned to Sesay Wholesalers.
5. Why is a delivery van for the business not debited to purchases?

**Answers:**

1. Rokel Commercial Bank: personal (artificial); motor van: real; salaries: nominal; Kamara: personal (natural).
2. Debit what comes in; credit what goes out.
3. Debit rent account; credit bank account.
4. Debit Sesay Wholesalers' account; credit returns outwards account.
5. Because purchases means only goods bought for resale; the van is a non-current asset for use in the business.
