The periodic table gives chlorine a relative atomic mass of 35.5, yet no chlorine atom has a mass of 35.5. The value is an average over its isotopes. Some isotopes are also unstable: their nuclei break up and give out radiation. This lesson covers relative atomic mass and the chemistry of radioactivity.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define relative atomic mass and the atomic mass unit
- calculate relative atomic mass from isotopic abundances, and abundances from relative atomic mass
- describe the properties of alpha, beta and gamma radiation
- write and balance nuclear equations
- solve half-life problems
- state uses and dangers of radioactivity, and distinguish fission from fusion

## Relative atomic mass

:::callout{type=definition}
- The **atomic mass unit (amu or u)** is $\tfrac{1}{12}$ of the mass of one atom of **carbon-12**.
- The **relative atomic mass (Aᵣ)** of an element is the **average mass of one atom** of the element, taking account of its isotopes, compared with $\tfrac{1}{12}$ of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.
:::

Relative atomic mass has **no units**, because it is a ratio.

$$A_r = \dfrac{\sum (\text{mass} \times \%)}{100}$$

Multiply each isotope's mass number by its percentage abundance, add the results and divide by 100.

![Bar chart of chlorine isotopes: chlorine-35 at 75% and chlorine-37 at 25%, giving a relative atomic mass of 35.5](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/chlorine-isotopes.svg)

*Figure 1. The isotopes of chlorine and its relative atomic mass. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Chlorine:** ³⁵Cl 75%, ³⁷Cl 25%.

$$A_r = \dfrac{35 \times 75 + 37 \times 25}{100} = \dfrac{2625 + 925}{100} = 35.5$$

**Copper:** ⁶³Cu 69%, ⁶⁵Cu 31%.

$$A_r = \dfrac{63 \times 69 + 65 \times 31}{100} = \dfrac{4347 + 2015}{100} = 63.62 \approx 63.6$$
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Finding abundance.** Boron has isotopes ¹⁰B and ¹¹B and a relative atomic mass of 10.8. Let the percentage of ¹⁰B be $x$.

$$\dfrac{10x + 11(100 - x)}{100} = 10.8$$

$$10x + 1100 - 11x = 1080 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 20$$

So ¹⁰B is **20%** and ¹¹B is **80%**.
:::

The average always lies **closer to the more abundant isotope**: chlorine's 35.5 is nearer 35 than 37.

Isotope masses and abundances are measured with a **mass spectrometer**. It turns atoms into ions, accelerates them and separates them by mass in a magnetic field.

## Radioactivity

:::callout{type=definition}
**Radioactivity** is the **spontaneous breakdown (decay)** of unstable atomic nuclei, giving out **alpha particles, beta particles or gamma rays** and energy.
:::

- **Natural radioactivity** occurs in elements such as uranium, radium and thorium, and in isotopes such as carbon-14.
- **Artificial (induced) radioactivity** is produced by bombarding stable nuclei with particles in a reactor.

| Property | Alpha (α) | Beta (β) | Gamma (γ) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | Helium nucleus, ⁴₂He | Fast electron, ⁰₋₁e | Electromagnetic wave |
| **Charge** | +2 | −1 | 0 |
| **Relative mass** | 4 | $\tfrac{1}{1840}$ | 0 |
| **Penetrating power** | Lowest: stopped by paper or skin | Medium: stopped by a few mm of aluminium | Highest: reduced by thick lead or concrete |
| **Ionising power** | Highest | Medium | Lowest |
| **In an electric field** | Bends slightly to the **negative** plate | Bends strongly to the **positive** plate | Not deflected |

![Alpha, beta and gamma radiation passing between charged plates: alpha bends towards the negative plate, beta bends more towards the positive plate, gamma goes straight](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/radiation-deflection.svg)

*Figure 2. The three radiations in an electric field. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

### Nuclear equations

In a nuclear equation, **mass numbers balance** and **atomic numbers balance** on both sides.

- **Alpha decay:** A falls by 4, Z falls by 2.
- **Beta decay:** A is unchanged, Z rises by 1 (a neutron turns into a proton and an electron).
- **Gamma emission:** A and Z do not change.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Balancing nuclear equations.**

Alpha decay of uranium-238:

$${}^{238}_{92}\text{U} \rightarrow {}^{234}_{90}\text{Th} + {}^{4}_{2}\text{He}$$

Check: $238 = 234 + 4$ and $92 = 90 + 2$.

Beta decay of carbon-14:

$${}^{14}_{6}\text{C} \rightarrow {}^{14}_{7}\text{N} + {}^{0}_{-1}\text{e}$$

Check: $14 = 14 + 0$ and $6 = 7 + (-1)$.
:::

### Half-life

:::callout{type=definition}
The **half-life** of a radioactive isotope is the **time taken for half of the nuclei** in a sample to decay. It is fixed for each isotope and is not changed by temperature, pressure or chemical combination.
:::

![Decay curve: 80 g of a radioactive isotope falls to 40 g after one half-life, 20 g after two, 10 g after three and 5 g after four](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/half-life.svg)

*Figure 3. A decay curve: the mass halves every half-life. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

$$\text{mass left} = \text{original mass} \times \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{n}, \qquad n = \dfrac{\text{time}}{\text{half-life}}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**An isotope has a half-life of 8 days. How much of an 80 g sample is left after 24 days?**

$$n = \dfrac{24}{8} = 3 \qquad \text{mass left} = 80 \times \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^3 = 10 \text{ g}$$

**How long does 64 g take to fall to 4 g, if the half-life is 5 years?**

64 → 32 → 16 → 8 → 4 is **4 half-lives**, so the time $= 4 \times 5 = 20$ years.
:::

### Uses of radioactivity

- **Medicine:** cobalt-60 gamma rays treat cancer (radiotherapy); iodine-131 diagnoses and treats thyroid disease; radiation **sterilises** syringes and dressings.
- **Carbon dating:** the carbon-14 left in wood, bone or cloth gives its age (half-life about 5,730 years).
- **Tracers:** small amounts show leaks in pipes or how plants take up fertiliser.
- **Industry:** measuring the thickness of paper and metal sheets; smoke detectors (americium-241).
- **Energy:** nuclear power stations.
- **Agriculture:** killing insects in stored grain; producing new crop varieties.

### Dangers and precautions

Radiation damages living cells and DNA, causing burns, sickness, cancer and birth defects. **Precautions:**

- handle sources with **tongs**, never by hand
- store them in **lead containers**
- keep **time short** and **distance large**
- wear protective clothing and **film badges**
- dispose of radioactive waste safely

### Fission and fusion

| Nuclear fission | Nuclear fusion |
|---|---|
| A **heavy** nucleus (e.g. uranium-235) **splits** into two lighter nuclei when hit by a neutron | Two **light** nuclei (e.g. hydrogen isotopes) **join** to form a heavier one |
| Releases neutrons that can cause a **chain reaction** | Needs extremely high temperatures |
| Used in nuclear power stations and atomic bombs | Powers the **sun** and stars; hydrogen bomb |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
In beta decay the **atomic number increases by 1** (a neutron becomes a proton), even though an electron is given out. Many students wrongly subtract 1.
:::

## Key points

- **Relative atomic mass** is the weighted average mass of an element's atoms compared with $\tfrac{1}{12}$ of carbon-12: $A_r = \sum(\text{mass} \times \%) \div 100$.
- **Alpha**: helium nucleus, +2, least penetrating. **Beta**: electron, −1. **Gamma**: wave, no charge, most penetrating.
- In nuclear equations, **mass numbers and atomic numbers balance**.
- **Half-life**: the time for half the nuclei to decay; mass left $= \text{original} \times (\tfrac{1}{2})^n$.
- Radioactivity is used in **medicine, carbon dating, tracers and industry**, but must be handled with care.

## Quick check

1. Neon has ²⁰Ne (90%) and ²²Ne (10%). Find its relative atomic mass.
2. Complete: ${}^{226}_{88}\text{Ra} \rightarrow {}^{222}_{86}\text{Rn} + \,?$
3. A 120 g sample has a half-life of 2 hours. How much remains after 6 hours?
4. Which radiation is most penetrating, and how is it stopped?

**Answers:**

1. $\dfrac{20 \times 90 + 22 \times 10}{100} = \dfrac{2020}{100} = 20.2$
2. ${}^{4}_{2}\text{He}$, an alpha particle.
3. 6 ÷ 2 = 3 half-lives: $120 \times \tfrac{1}{8} = 15$ g.
4. Gamma rays; they are reduced by thick lead or concrete.
