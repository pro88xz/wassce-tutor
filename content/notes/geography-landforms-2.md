Besides mountains, the main **landforms** on the Earth's surface are **plateaus** (high, flat-topped land), **plains** (low, flat or gently rolling land), **valleys** (long low areas between higher ground), **escarpments** (long, steep slopes), and isolated hills called **inselbergs**. Together these make up the **relief** of a country: the shape and height of its land. This lesson explains how each landform is formed, gives African and Sierra Leone examples, and shows why relief matters for people.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define relief and describe the major relief features
- explain the types and formation of plateaus, with examples
- explain the types and formation of plains, with examples
- describe valleys, escarpments and inselbergs
- explain how relief affects human activities

## Plateaus

:::callout{type=definition}
A **plateau** is an extensive area of **high, fairly flat land**, usually with at least one steep side (an escarpment) rising above the surrounding lowland. It is sometimes called a **tableland**.
:::

| Type | Formation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Intermontane plateau** | high land enclosed by fold mountains | Tibetan Plateau (Asia) |
| **Piedmont plateau** | lies at the foot of mountains | Patagonia (South America) |
| **Continental plateau** | vast uplifted areas of old, hard rock | most of **Africa** is a high plateau |
| **Volcanic (lava) plateau** | built by sheets of runny lava | Deccan Plateau (India), Ethiopian Highlands |
| **Dissected plateau** | a plateau cut into blocks by rivers | parts of the Fouta Djallon |

**West African examples:** the **Jos Plateau** in Nigeria (tin mining, cool climate), and the **Fouta Djallon** in Guinea, the source of the Niger, Senegal and Gambia rivers, often called the "water tower of West Africa".

## Plains

:::callout{type=definition}
A **plain** is an extensive area of **low, flat or gently undulating land**, usually below about 200 m above sea level.
:::

| Type | Formation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Structural plain** | flat-lying rock layers uplifted or exposed | the Great Plains of the USA |
| **Erosional plain (peneplain)** | high land worn down almost flat by long erosion | parts of the African interior |
| **Depositional plains** | built by deposition | |
| – alluvial (flood) plain | silt deposited by rivers in flood | the Niger and Nile floodplains |
| – deltaic plain | sediment at a river mouth | the Niger Delta |
| – coastal plain | sediment deposited along the coast | the West African coastal plains |
| – lacustrine plain | a lake bed exposed after the lake dries up | around Lake Chad |

Plains are usually the most **densely populated** areas, because they are easy to farm, build on and cross with roads and railways.

## Valleys, escarpments and inselbergs

- A **valley** is a long, low area between hills or mountains, usually with a river. River valleys are V-shaped in their upper course and broad in their lower course; **rift valleys** are formed by faulting; **U-shaped** valleys by glaciers.
- An **escarpment** is a long, steep slope or cliff at the edge of a plateau or along a fault, such as the **Gambaga escarpment** in northern Ghana.
- An **inselberg** ("island mountain") is an isolated, steep-sided hill of hard rock, often granite, rising abruptly from a plain. Inselbergs are common across the savanna lands of West Africa; the bare, dome-shaped ones are called **bornhardts**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's relief rises in steps from the sea:

- a low **coastal plain** of swamps and mangroves, up to about 40 km wide
- **interior plains** and lowlands
- **plateaus** rising to about 600 m in the north and east
- **mountains** and **hills**: the **Loma Mountains** (with Bintumani, about 1,945 m), the **Tingi Hills**, and the **Freetown Peninsula mountains** near the coast

Most rivers flow from the plateaus across the plains to the Atlantic, so the land slopes generally from the north-east towards the south-west.
:::

## How relief affects human activities

| Relief | Effects on people |
|---|---|
| **Plains** | dense population; easy farming, building, roads and railways; risk of flooding on floodplains |
| **Plateaus** | cooler climate in high plateaus; grazing; minerals (tin on the Jos Plateau); rivers rising on plateaus provide hydroelectric power |
| **Mountains** | sparse population; difficult transport; forests, tourism, minerals; relief rainfall |
| **Valleys** | fertile soils and water for farming; natural routes for roads and railways; dam sites |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A plateau is **high and flat**; a plain is **low and flat**. Both are flat, so height above sea level is what distinguishes them.
:::

## Key points

- **Relief** is the shape and height of the land.
- **Plateaus:** high, flat-topped land: intermontane, piedmont, continental, volcanic, dissected. Examples: Jos Plateau, Fouta Djallon.
- **Plains:** low, flat land: structural, erosional, and depositional (alluvial, deltaic, coastal, lacustrine).
- **Escarpment:** a long, steep slope. **Inselberg:** an isolated hill rising from a plain.
- Sierra Leone rises from a **coastal plain** through **interior plains** and **plateaus** to the **Loma Mountains**.

## Quick check

1. Distinguish a plateau from a plain.
2. Name a volcanic plateau.
3. How is an alluvial plain formed?
4. What is an inselberg?
5. Why are plains usually densely populated?

**Answers:**

1. A plateau is high, flat land; a plain is low, flat land.
2. The Deccan Plateau (India) or the Ethiopian Highlands.
3. By deposition of silt by rivers in flood.
4. An isolated, steep-sided hill of hard rock rising abruptly from a plain.
5. They are easy to farm, build on and cross with roads and railways, and often have fertile soils and water.
