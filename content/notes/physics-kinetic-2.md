The kinetic theory does more than describe solids, liquids and gases: it explains why evaporation cools you, why some solids have sharp melting points, and what temperature really measures. This lesson takes the theory further.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- distinguish evaporation from boiling using the kinetic theory
- explain why evaporation causes cooling and list the factors affecting its rate
- describe cohesion and adhesion as intermolecular forces
- distinguish crystalline and amorphous solids
- relate temperature to the average kinetic energy of particles

## Temperature and molecular motion

:::callout{type=definition}
The **temperature** of a body is a measure of the **average kinetic energy** of its particles. The hotter the body, the faster its particles move on average.
:::

Not all particles move at the same speed: at any temperature some are slow and a few are very fast. Raising the temperature raises the **average** speed.

## Evaporation and why it cools

:::callout{type=definition}
**Evaporation** is the escape of the more energetic molecules from the **surface** of a liquid, at **any temperature**.
:::

Only the fastest molecules near the surface have enough energy to overcome the attraction of their neighbours and escape. Because the **fastest** molecules leave, the **average kinetic energy** of those left behind falls, so the liquid **cools**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
- Sweat evaporating from your skin cools you on a hot day in Freetown.
- Water kept in a clay pot (a "water cooler") stays cool: water seeps through the pores and evaporates from the outside surface.
- A wet cloth spread out in the sun and the wind dries much faster than one bunched up in the shade.
:::

### Factors that increase the rate of evaporation

1. **Higher temperature** — more molecules have enough energy to escape.
2. **Larger surface area** — more molecules are at the surface.
3. **Wind (moving air)** — carries escaped vapour away, so fewer molecules return.
4. **Lower humidity** — dry air takes up vapour more readily.
5. **Lower pressure** above the liquid.

## Evaporation versus boiling

| Evaporation | Boiling |
|---|---|
| occurs at **any temperature** | occurs at a **fixed temperature** (the boiling point) for a given pressure |
| takes place only at the **surface** | takes place **throughout** the liquid (bubbles form inside) |
| is a **slow**, quiet process | is **rapid** and vigorous |
| causes **cooling** of the liquid | temperature stays **constant** while heat is supplied |
| rate depends on surface area, wind, humidity | needs a continuous supply of heat |

## Intermolecular forces: cohesion and adhesion

:::callout{type=definition}
**Cohesion** is the force of attraction between molecules of the **same** substance. **Adhesion** is the force of attraction between molecules of **different** substances.
:::

- Water **wets** glass because the adhesion between water and glass is **greater** than the cohesion between water molecules. Water spreads on clean glass, and its surface in a glass tube curves **upwards** (concave meniscus).
- Mercury **does not wet** glass because its cohesion is **greater** than its adhesion to glass. Mercury forms round droplets, and its meniscus curves **downwards** (convex).

These forces explain surface tension and capillarity (next topic).

## Crystalline and amorphous solids

| Crystalline solids | Amorphous solids |
|---|---|
| particles arranged in a **regular, repeating pattern** (a lattice) | particles arranged **irregularly** |
| have **sharp melting points** | soften gradually over a range of temperatures |
| form crystals with flat faces and definite angles | have no regular shape |
| e.g. salt, sugar, ice, metals, quartz | e.g. glass, rubber, plastics, wax |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Explain, using the kinetic theory, why a bicycle tyre pumped up in the morning feels harder in the afternoon sun.**

The air inside heats up, so its molecules move faster. They strike the inner walls of the tyre **more often** and with **greater force** per collision. The pressure therefore rises, making the tyre harder (its volume can increase only slightly).
:::

## Key points

- Temperature measures the **average kinetic energy** of the particles.
- Evaporation: fastest molecules escape from the surface, so the remaining liquid cools.
- Evaporation is faster with higher temperature, larger area, wind and dry air.
- Boiling: at a fixed temperature, throughout the liquid, with bubbles.
- Cohesion (same substance) and adhesion (different substances) decide whether a liquid wets a surface.
- Crystalline solids have regular particle arrangements and sharp melting points.

## Quick check

1. Why does a liquid cool when it evaporates?
2. State two differences between evaporation and boiling.
3. Why does mercury form round droplets on glass?

**Answers:** 1. The most energetic molecules escape, lowering the average kinetic energy of those left. 2. For example: evaporation happens at any temperature, boiling at a fixed temperature; evaporation occurs at the surface, boiling throughout the liquid. 3. The cohesion between mercury molecules is greater than their adhesion to glass.
