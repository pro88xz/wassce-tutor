Bubble ethene into orange bromine water and the colour vanishes within seconds; bubble methane in and nothing happens. The difference is the **double bond**. Alkenes react mainly by **addition**: the weak π bond opens and two new atoms or groups attach, one to each carbon. These reactions turn ethene into ethanol, plastics, margarine and antifreeze.

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the uses of ethene

## Addition reactions

:::callout{type=definition}
An **addition reaction** is one in which two molecules join to form a **single product**. In alkenes, the **C=C double bond** becomes a **C–C single bond** and a new atom or group adds to each carbon.
:::

![Ethene in the centre; with hydrogen it gives ethane, with bromine 1,2-dibromoethane, with steam ethanol and with hydrogen bromide bromoethane](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/ethene-additions.svg)

*Figure 1. Four addition reactions of ethene. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

### 1. Hydrogen (hydrogenation)

$$\text{CH}_2{=}\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Ni},\ 150\,^\circ\text{C}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_3$$

A **nickel** catalyst is used (or platinum or palladium at room temperature). The same reaction **hardens vegetable oils into margarine**: hydrogen adds to some of the C=C bonds in the oil, raising its melting point.

### 2. Halogens

$$\text{CH}_2{=}\text{CH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 \rightarrow \text{CH}_2\text{BrCH}_2\text{Br}$$

The product is **1,2-dibromoethane**. The reaction is fast at room temperature, in the dark, with **no catalyst**: the **orange-brown bromine is decolourised**. Chlorine gives 1,2-dichloroethane.

With **bromine water**, the main product is **2-bromoethanol** (CH₂BrCH₂OH), but the observation is the same: rapid **decolourisation**.

### 3. Hydrogen halides

$$\text{CH}_2{=}\text{CH}_2 + \text{HBr} \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Br}$$

The product is **bromoethane**. With an unsymmetrical alkene such as propene, the hydrogen goes to the carbon that already has **more hydrogen atoms** (**Markovnikov's rule**), so the main product is **2-bromopropane**, CH₃CHBrCH₃.

### 4. Water (hydration)

$$\text{CH}_2{=}\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4} \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$$

**Steam** reacts with ethene over a **phosphoric(V) acid** catalyst at about **300 °C and 60 atm**. This is the main industrial method for making **ethanol** for solvents and fuel. (In the laboratory, ethene can be absorbed in concentrated sulphuric acid and the product then boiled with water.)

## Oxidation by potassium manganate(VII)

Shake an alkene with **cold, dilute, acidified (or alkaline) potassium manganate(VII)**. The **purple** colour disappears (with alkaline KMnO₄, a brown precipitate of MnO₂ forms). Ethene is oxidised to **ethane-1,2-diol**, which is used as **antifreeze**:

$$\text{CH}_2{=}\text{CH}_2 + [\text{O}] + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{CH}_2\text{OHCH}_2\text{OH}$$

## Addition polymerisation

Under high pressure with a catalyst, thousands of ethene molecules join together. The double bonds open and the molecules link into one long chain, **poly(ethene)** (polythene):

$$n\,\text{CH}_2{=}\text{CH}_2 \rightarrow (\text{–CH}_2\text{–CH}_2\text{–})_n$$

The small molecule (ethene) is the **monomer**; the large molecule is the **polymer**. Propene gives **poly(propene)**, and chloroethene gives **PVC**. (See the Polymers topic.)

## Tests for unsaturation

| Test | Alkene (e.g. ethene) | Alkane (e.g. ethane) |
|---|---|---|
| **Bromine water** (or bromine in tetrachloromethane) | Orange-brown colour **disappears quickly** | **No change** in the dark (slow decolourisation in sunlight, with HBr given off) |
| **Acidified KMnO₄** | Purple colour **disappears** | **Stays purple** |
| **Burning** | Luminous, slightly smoky flame | Clean, non-luminous blue flame |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Counting double bonds.** 0.1 mol of a hydrocarbon reacts with exactly 32 g of bromine (Br₂ = 160). How many C=C bonds does each molecule have?

- Moles of Br₂ $= 32 \div 160 = 0.2$ mol
- Ratio hydrocarbon : Br₂ $= 0.1 : 0.2 = 1 : 2$

Each C=C bond adds one Br₂, so each molecule has **two C=C bonds** (or one C≡C bond).
:::

## Uses of ethene

- **Poly(ethene)** for plastic bags, bottles, buckets and water tanks
- **Ethanol** (by hydration) for solvents, fuel and medicines
- **Ethane-1,2-diol** for antifreeze and polyester fibres
- **Chloroethene** (vinyl chloride) for **PVC** pipes
- **Ripening fruit** in storage rooms

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many cheap **margarines** and spreads sold in Sierra Leone are made from palm oil and other vegetable oils. Liquid oils contain many C=C bonds. Factories add hydrogen over a **nickel catalyst** to some of them. The oil becomes more saturated and therefore **solid** at room temperature: an addition reaction you can eat.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Addition gives **one product only**. When you write ethene + bromine, the product is **CH₂BrCH₂Br**, not CH₂=CHBr + HBr. Producing HBr would be **substitution**, which is what alkanes do.
:::

## Key points

- Alkenes undergo **addition**: the C=C becomes C–C and a new atom or group adds to each carbon.
- **H₂** (Ni, 150 °C) → alkane; **Br₂** (room temperature) → dibromoalkane, **decolourising** bromine; **HBr** → bromoalkane (Markovnikov); **steam** (H₃PO₄, 300 °C, 60 atm) → **ethanol**.
- Cold dilute **KMnO₄** is **decolourised**, giving ethane-1,2-diol.
- **Addition polymerisation** turns ethene into **poly(ethene)**.
- **Bromine water** and **KMnO₄** distinguish alkenes (decolourised) from alkanes (no change).

## Quick check

1. What catalyst is used to add hydrogen to ethene?
2. Describe what is seen when ethene is bubbled into bromine water.
3. Name the product of adding steam to ethene and state the catalyst.
4. Write the structure of the main product when HBr adds to propene.

**Answers:**

1. Nickel (at about 150 °C).
2. The orange-brown colour disappears quickly.
3. Ethanol; phosphoric(V) acid.
4. CH₃CHBrCH₃ (2-bromopropane).
