Punctuation is the traffic system of writing: it tells the reader when to stop, when to pause, what belongs together and who is speaking. *"Let's eat, Grandma!"* invites Grandma to dinner; *"Let's eat Grandma!"* is a very different idea. In WASSCE, punctuation errors cost marks under **Mechanical accuracy** in every essay, letter and summary, and the objective paper tests punctuation directly. This lesson covers every mark you need, with the rules, the commonest errors, and practice.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- end sentences with the correct mark
- use commas correctly in lists, after introductory elements, between clauses and around extra information
- use the apostrophe correctly for possession and contraction
- use semicolons, colons, quotation marks, hyphens, dashes and brackets
- use capital letters correctly

## End marks

| Mark | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Full stop** . | statements, commands, indirect questions, abbreviations | *The bus has left.* / *She asked where I lived.* |
| **Question mark** ? | direct questions only | *Where do you live?* |
| **Exclamation mark** ! | strong feeling, sparingly | *What a goal!* |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**A question mark after an indirect question.** *"She asked where I lived?"* is wrong: it is a statement **about** a question. Use a full stop. And **one** exclamation mark is enough: *"!!!"* has no place in an examination essay.
:::

## The comma: six main uses

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **1. Lists** | *We bought rice, oil, onions and pepper.* |
| **2. After an introductory word, phrase or clause** | ***However,** the road was closed.* / ***After the meeting,** we went home.* / ***When the rain stopped,** we left.* |
| **3. Before a coordinating conjunction joining two main clauses** | *I called him, **but** he did not answer.* |
| **4. Around extra (non-defining) information** | *My uncle, **who lives in Kenema,** is a doctor.* |
| **5. Around an appositive** (a noun renaming another) | *Mr Kamara, **our principal,** spoke first.* |
| **6. Direct address and direct speech** | ***Fatu,** come here.* / *"I'm tired**,"** she said.* |

:::callout{type=tip}
**The Oxford comma** (a comma before *and* in a list: *rice, oil, and pepper*) is optional in British English. Either style is accepted, but be **consistent**, and use it when it prevents confusion.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**The comma splice**: joining two complete sentences with only a comma. *"It was late, we went home"* is wrong. Use a full stop, a semicolon, or a comma **plus** a conjunction: *"It was late, **so** we went home."*
:::

## The apostrophe

### Possession

| Owner | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| one owner | add **'s** | *the student**'s** bag, the boss**'s** car* |
| plural owners ending in -s | add **'** only | *the students**'** bags, the teachers**'** room* |
| plural owners not ending in -s | add **'s** | *the children**'s** toys, the women**'s** market* |
| names ending in -s | **'s** (or **'**) | *James**'s** book* (or *James**'** book*) |

### Contraction (missing letters)

*it's* (it is), *don't* (do not), *can't* (cannot), *won't* (will not), *they're* (they are), *who's* (who is)

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The four apostrophe errors examiners see most:

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *The dog wagged **it's** tail.* | ***its*** tail | *its* (possessive) has **no** apostrophe |
| *Mango**'s** for sale.* | ***Mangoes*** for sale. | plurals do **not** take apostrophes |
| ***Your's** faithfully* | ***Yours*** faithfully | possessive pronouns have no apostrophe: *yours, hers, ours, theirs* |
| *The **students** bags* (many students) | *the **students'** bags* | plural possession needs the apostrophe after the *s* |
:::

:::callout{type=tip}
In formal letters, reports and argumentative essays, **avoid contractions** altogether: write *do not*, *cannot*, *it is*. In informal letters and dialogue, they are fine.
:::

## The semicolon ;

1. Joins two **closely related main clauses**: *The rain stopped**;** the sun came out.*
2. Before a **linking adverb** between two main clauses: *She studied hard**; therefore,** she passed.*
3. Separates **list items that contain commas**: *We visited Bo, in the south**;** Makeni, in the north**;** and Kenema, in the east.*

## The colon :

1. Introduces a **list**: *You will need three things**:** a pen, a ruler and a calculator.*
2. Introduces an **explanation** or **example**: *There was only one problem**:** we had no money.*
3. Introduces a **quotation** formally, and in **time** (10**:**30).

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**A colon after an incomplete clause.** *"The subjects I study are**:** English, Maths and Biology"* is wrong: what comes before a colon should be a complete sentence. Write *"I study three subjects**:** English, Maths and Biology"* or simply *"The subjects I study are English, Maths and Biology."*
:::

## Quotation marks

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| direct speech | *"We are late," said Musa.* |
| titles of short works | *the poem "The Meaning of Africa"* |
| quoting someone's words | *The minister called the project "a turning point".* |

Punctuation that belongs to the speech goes **inside** the marks: *"Are you coming?" she asked.* Single (' ') or double (" ") marks are both acceptable; be consistent.

## Hyphens, dashes and brackets

| Mark | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Hyphen** - | joins words into one compound; some prefixes; numbers | *a well-known writer, ex-president, twenty-one, a five-year plan* |
| **Dash** – | a strong break, or to add emphasis | *She had one dream – to become a doctor.* |
| **Brackets** ( ) | extra information that could be removed | *The WASSCE (West African Senior School Certificate Examination) is taken in May and June.* |

## Capital letters

| Use | Examples |
|---|---|
| the first word of a sentence and of direct speech | *She said, "**T**he bus has gone."* |
| the pronoun **I** | *Musa and **I** went.* |
| names of people, places, organisations | *Fatmata, Freetown, the Bank of Sierra Leone* |
| days, months, festivals (not seasons) | *Monday, March, Christmas, Eid* (but *the rainy season*) |
| languages, nationalities, religions | *English, Krio, Mende, Sierra Leonean, Islam, Christianity* |
| titles before names | *Doctor Koroma, President, Paramount Chief Bai Bureh* |
| main words in titles | *The Gods Are Not to Blame* |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Capitalisation errors that appear often in Sierra Leonean scripts: *"freetown"*, *"english"*, *"monday"*, *"i"* and *"sierra leonean"* in lower case, and seasons such as *"the Rainy Season"* in capitals. Proper nouns (names of particular places, days, languages, peoples) always take capitals; common nouns (*season, river, city, school*) do not, unless they are part of a name (*the Rokel River*, *Bo School*).
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Punctuate: 'my uncle who lives in kenema is a doctor'"}
*My uncle, who lives in Kenema, is a doctor.*
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Correct: 'The teachers room is near the students canteen.' (many teachers, many students)"}
*The **teachers'** room is near the **students'** canteen.*
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Correct: 'Its raining, the match has been cancelled.'"}
***It's** raining, **so** the match has been cancelled.* (or *It's raining; the match has been cancelled.*)
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Punctuate: 'what time does the bus leave asked fatu'"}
*"What time does the bus leave?" asked Fatu.*
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Add a colon correctly: 'Bring the following items a mat a bucket and a cutlass'"}
*Bring the following items**:** a mat, a bucket and a cutlass.*
:::

:::reveal{title="6. Correct: 'On monday i will visit the mende villages in the Rainy Season.'"}
*On **Monday I** will visit the **Mende** villages in the **rainy season**.*
:::

## Key points

- **End marks**: full stop for statements and indirect questions; question mark only for direct questions; one exclamation mark at most.
- **Commas**: lists, after introductions, before conjunctions joining main clauses, around extra information and appositives, in direct address and speech. **Never** join two sentences with a comma alone.
- **Apostrophes**: *'s* for one owner, *s'* for plural owners ending in *s*; none in *its, yours, hers* or plain plurals.
- **Semicolon**: joins related main clauses. **Colon**: introduces a list or explanation after a complete clause.
- **Capitals** for proper nouns, *I*, days, months, languages and nationalities, but not seasons.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Punctuation is marked in **every** piece of continuous writing under **Mechanical accuracy** (10 marks), and the objective paper includes questions on the correctly punctuated sentence. The most expensive errors are comma splices, wrong apostrophes (*it's/its*, plurals) and missing capitals on proper nouns.
:::
