The **statement of financial position** (the **balance sheet**) shows the **assets, liabilities and capital** of a business **on a particular date**, usually the last day of the financial year. It is the accounting equation set out in full: **non-current assets plus current assets** equal **capital plus non-current liabilities plus current liabilities**. Unlike the income statement, it is **not an account**: it is a statement of balances remaining after the income statement has been prepared. This lesson explains its layout and prepares one from a trial balance.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the purpose of the statement of financial position
- classify items as non-current or current assets and liabilities
- show the owner's capital with profit and drawings
- prepare a statement of financial position in vertical form
- calculate working capital and the current ratio

## Layout

| Section | Contents | Order |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-current assets** | land and buildings, motor vehicles, machinery, furniture | most permanent first |
| **Current assets** | inventory, trade receivables, prepayments, bank, cash | least liquid first |
| **Capital** | opening capital + net profit − drawings | |
| **Non-current liabilities** | long-term loans, mortgages | |
| **Current liabilities** | trade payables, accrued expenses, bank overdraft | |

Assets are listed **in order of permanence**: the items the business will keep longest come first; **cash**, the most liquid asset, comes last.

## Worked example

:::callout{type=worked-example}

Note how **every** trial balance item has been used exactly **once**: income and expenses in the income statement; assets, liabilities, capital and drawings in the statement of financial position; and the **closing inventory** appears **twice** (in cost of sales and as a current asset) because it was given outside the trial balance.

## Working capital and liquidity

$$\text{Working capital} = \text{Current assets} - \text{Current liabilities}$$

$$\text{Current ratio} = \frac{\text{Current assets}}{\text{Current liabilities}}$$

For Kargbo Stores:

- Working capital $= 45\,000 - 11\,500 = 33\,500$
- Current ratio $= \frac{45\,000}{11\,500} \approx 3.9 : 1$

A current ratio of about **2 : 1** is often regarded as comfortable; a much higher ratio may mean too much money is tied up in inventory or cash that could be used more profitably.

## Income statement and statement of financial position compared

| Income statement | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|
| covers a **period** ("for the year ended…") | shows the position **on a date** ("at 31 December…") |
| shows **income and expenses** | shows **assets, liabilities and capital** |
| finds **profit or loss** | shows **financial position** |
| is an **account** (part of the double entry) | is a **statement** of balances, not an account |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not show **drawings** as an expense in the income statement. Drawings are taken by the owner for private use: they are **deducted from capital** in the statement of financial position.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Statement of financial position** (balance sheet): a statement of the assets, liabilities and capital of a business at a date.
- **Non-current assets**: assets kept for use over more than a year, such as land and buildings, motor vehicles, machinery and furniture.
- **Current assets**: assets that change within a year, such as inventory, trade receivables, prepayments, bank and cash.
- **Current liabilities**: amounts due within a year, such as trade payables, accrued expenses and a bank overdraft.
- **Non-current liabilities**: long-term debts, such as long-term loans and mortgages.
:::

## Key points

- The **statement of financial position** shows assets, liabilities and capital **on a date**.
- **Assets** in order of permanence; **cash** last.
- **Closing capital = opening capital + net profit − drawings.**
- **Working capital = current assets − current liabilities.**
- Closing inventory appears in **both** statements.

## Quick check

1. What is the difference between the date heading of an income statement and a statement of financial position?
2. In what order are current assets listed?
3. Opening capital NLe 60,000, net profit NLe 15,000, drawings NLe 8,000. Find closing capital.
4. Current assets NLe 30,000, current liabilities NLe 12,000. Find working capital and the current ratio.
5. Why is the statement of financial position not an account?

**Answers:**

1. The income statement covers a period ("for the year ended"); the statement of financial position shows the position on one date ("as at").
2. In order of liquidity, least liquid first: inventory, trade receivables, prepayments, bank, cash.
3. $60\,000 + 15\,000 - 8\,000 = 67\,000$: NLe 67,000.
4. Working capital $= 18\,000$; current ratio $= 2.5 : 1$.
5. Because it is a list of the balances remaining in the books, not part of the double entry.
