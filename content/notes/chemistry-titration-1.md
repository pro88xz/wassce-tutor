Titration is the single most important practical skill in WASSCE Chemistry: the volumetric analysis question carries heavy marks every year. It lets you measure the exact concentration of an acid or alkali by finding the volume needed to neutralise it. This lesson covers the apparatus, the procedure, reading and recording results, and choosing the right indicator.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name the apparatus used in titration and describe how each is prepared
- describe the procedure for an acid–alkali titration
- read a burette correctly and record titration results in a table
- calculate an average titre from concordant results
- choose a suitable indicator for different acid–base titrations
- state precautions and sources of error

## Apparatus

![A burette clamped above a conical flask on a white tile, with an inset showing the burette read at the bottom of the meniscus with the eye level](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/titration-setup.svg)

*Figure 1. Titration apparatus and reading the burette. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Apparatus | Use | Rinse with |
|---|---|---|
| **Burette** (50 cm³) | Delivers measured, variable volumes of the solution in it (often the acid) | The **solution it will hold** |
| **Pipette** (25.0 cm³) | Delivers one exact, fixed volume (often the alkali) | The **solution it will deliver** |
| **Conical flask** | Holds the pipetted solution; easy to swirl without splashing | **Distilled water** only |
| **White tile** | Makes the colour change easy to see | |
| **Retort stand and clamp** | Holds the burette vertical | |
| **Funnel** | Fills the burette; **remove it** before reading | |

Rinsing the burette and pipette with distilled water only would dilute the solution. Rinsing the conical flask with the solution would add extra moles. So the conical flask is rinsed with **water**, and the burette and pipette with the **solutions they hold**.

## Procedure

1. Rinse the burette with the acid, then fill it. Run some acid through the tap to remove air from the jet. Remove the funnel and record the **initial reading**.
2. Rinse the pipette with the alkali. Use a **pipette filler** to draw the alkali up to the mark and run it into the conical flask.
3. Add **2–3 drops** of a suitable indicator.
4. Place the flask on the white tile. Run in the acid while **swirling** the flask.
5. Near the end point, add the acid **drop by drop** until the indicator **just** changes colour permanently. Record the **final reading**.
6. The first run is a **rough** (trial) titration. Repeat accurately until you have **at least two concordant titres**, which agree within **0.10 cm³**.

## Reading and recording

- Read the burette at the **bottom of the meniscus**, with your **eye level** with it.
- Record readings to **two decimal places**, ending in 0 or 5 (e.g. 22.50, 22.45).

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Titration of 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide with hydrochloric acid, using methyl orange.**

| Burette reading (cm³) | Rough | 1st | 2nd | 3rd |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Final reading | 23.10 | 45.60 | 22.45 | 44.90 |
| Initial reading | 0.00 | 23.10 | 0.00 | 22.35 |
| **Volume of acid used** | 23.10 | 22.50 | 22.45 | 22.55 |

The rough titre is not used. The accurate titres 22.50, 22.45 and 22.55 agree within 0.10 cm³.

$$\text{average titre} = \dfrac{22.50 + 22.45 + 22.55}{3} = 22.50 \text{ cm}^3$$
:::

## Choosing an indicator

The pH changes suddenly near the end point. A good indicator changes colour **within that steep part** of the curve.

![pH against volume of sodium hydroxide added to hydrochloric acid: the pH jumps from about 3 to 11 at 25 cm³, covering the colour-change ranges of both methyl orange and phenolphthalein](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/titration-curve.svg)

*Figure 2. pH change in a strong acid–strong alkali titration. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Titration | Example | Suitable indicator |
|---|---|---|
| Strong acid + strong base | HCl + NaOH | **Methyl orange or phenolphthalein** |
| Strong acid + weak base | HCl + Na₂CO₃ or NH₃ | **Methyl orange** |
| Weak acid + strong base | CH₃COOH + NaOH | **Phenolphthalein** |
| Weak acid + weak base | CH₃COOH + NH₃ | No sharp end point; no suitable simple indicator |

| Indicator | Colour in acid | Colour in alkali | End-point colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Methyl orange** | Red | Yellow | Orange |
| **Phenolphthalein** | Colourless | Pink | Faint pink (alkali in burette) or just colourless (acid in burette) |

## Precautions and sources of error

**Precautions:**

- Remove air bubbles from the burette jet.
- Remove the funnel before reading the burette.
- Read the meniscus at eye level.
- Use only 2–3 drops of indicator: indicators are weak acids or bases themselves.
- Swirl the flask constantly and add acid dropwise near the end point.
- Wash the sides of the flask with distilled water to rinse down splashes.
- Make sure the pipette tip touches the inside of the flask at the end of delivery, and do not blow out the last drop.

**Sources of error:**

- **overshooting** the end point
- **parallax** when reading
- burette or pipette **not rinsed** with the right solution
- air bubbles in the burette tap
- **impure or wet** reagents

:::callout{type=local-example}
Pharmacies and food laboratories use titration to check quality. A titration with sodium hydroxide measures how much **ethanoic acid** is in vinegar sold in Freetown markets, to check it has not been watered down. Another titration measures the acid content of palm oil, which shows how fresh it is.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do **not** include the rough titre in the average, and do not average titres that differ by more than 0.10 cm³. Average only the **concordant** values.
:::

## Key points

- Burette and pipette are rinsed with the **solutions they hold**; the conical flask with **distilled water**.
- Add indicator (2–3 drops), titrate on a **white tile**, swirl and add **dropwise** near the end point.
- Read at the **bottom of the meniscus**, to **2 decimal places**.
- Average **concordant titres** (within **0.10 cm³**), not the rough.
- **Strong acid + weak base:** methyl orange. **Weak acid + strong base:** phenolphthalein.

## Quick check

1. Why is the conical flask not rinsed with the alkali before use?
2. What is meant by concordant titres?
3. Which indicator would you use to titrate sodium carbonate with hydrochloric acid?
4. Titres were 24.10 (rough), 23.60, 23.70 and 23.65 cm³. Find the average titre.

**Answers:**

1. Extra alkali would remain in the flask, adding to the measured moles and making the titre too large.
2. Titres that agree within 0.10 cm³.
3. Methyl orange (strong acid with a weak base).
4. $(23.60 + 23.70 + 23.65) \div 3 = 23.65$ cm³.
