Pour vinegar onto baking soda and it foams up at once: ethanoic acid behaves like any other acid. It turns litmus red, fizzes with carbonates and metals, and neutralises alkalis. It also does something mineral acids cannot do: it combines with alkanols to form sweet-smelling **esters**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the typical acid reactions of ethanoic acid, with equations
- name the salts of alkanoic acids
- describe esterification as a reaction of alkanoic acids
- distinguish alkanoic acids from alkanols by simple tests
- calculate the concentration of ethanoic acid in vinegar from titration results
- state the uses of ethanoic acid and other alkanoic acids

## Reactions of ethanoic acid

![Ethanoic acid in the centre: with sodium carbonate it effervesces; with magnesium it gives magnesium ethanoate and hydrogen; with sodium hydroxide it is neutralised; with ethanol it forms ethyl ethanoate](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/ethanoic-acid-reactions.svg)

*Figure 1. Ethanoic acid shows typical acid reactions and forms esters. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

The salts of ethanoic acid are called **ethanoates** (older name: acetates). Salts of methanoic acid are **methanoates**, and so on.

### 1. Indicators

Ethanoic acid turns **blue litmus red**. Universal indicator shows **orange** (pH about 3).

### 2. Reactive metals

Magnesium, zinc and iron react, more slowly than with dilute HCl, giving **hydrogen**:

$$2\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{Mg} \rightarrow (\text{CH}_3\text{COO})_2\text{Mg} + \text{H}_2$$

### 3. Carbonates and hydrogencarbonates

**Effervescence** of carbon(IV) oxide, which turns lime water milky:

$$2\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \rightarrow 2\text{CH}_3\text{COONa} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2$$

$$\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{NaHCO}_3 \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{COONa} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2$$

This is the standard **test for a –COOH group**: alkanols do **not** fizz with carbonates.

### 4. Bases and alkalis (neutralisation)

$$\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{COONa} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

$$2\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{CuO} \rightarrow (\text{CH}_3\text{COO})_2\text{Cu} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

A solution of **sodium ethanoate** is slightly **alkaline** (salt of a weak acid and a strong base).

### 5. Esterification

With an alkanol and a few drops of **concentrated sulphuric acid** (catalyst), warmed:

$$\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

The product, **ethyl ethanoate**, has a sweet, fruity smell. Esters are covered in the next topic.

## Distinguishing ethanol and ethanoic acid

| Test | Ethanol | Ethanoic acid |
|---|---|---|
| **Blue litmus** | No change | Turns red |
| **Sodium carbonate or hydrogencarbonate** | No reaction | **Effervescence**; CO₂ turns lime water milky |
| **Sodium metal** | Fizzes (hydrogen) | Fizzes (hydrogen), more vigorously |
| **Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, warm** | Orange → green | No change |
| **Smell** | Characteristic "alcohol" smell | Sharp smell of vinegar |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Ethanoic acid in vinegar.** A 25.0 cm³ sample of vinegar was diluted to 250 cm³. Then 25.0 cm³ of the diluted solution needed 21.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ NaOH for neutralisation (phenolphthalein indicator). Find the concentration of ethanoic acid in the original vinegar in g/dm³. (CH₃COOH = 60)

- Moles of NaOH $= 0.100 \times 21.0 \div 1000 = 0.00210$ mol
- Ratio 1 : 1, so moles of acid in 25.0 cm³ of diluted solution $= 0.00210$ mol
- Diluted concentration $= 0.00210 \times 1000 \div 25.0 = 0.0840$ mol/dm³
- The vinegar was diluted 10 times: original $= 0.840$ mol/dm³
- Mass concentration $= 0.840 \times 60 = 50.4$ g/dm³ (about 5% ethanoic acid)

**Phenolphthalein** is the right indicator because this is a **weak acid–strong base** titration, with an end point above pH 7.
:::

## Uses of alkanoic acids

| Acid or salt | Use |
|---|---|
| **Ethanoic acid (vinegar)** | Flavouring and **preserving food** (pickles); cleaning kettles (removes limescale) |
| **Ethanoic acid** | Making **esters** (solvents, flavourings), cellulose ethanoate (photographic film, fibres), aspirin, poly(ethenyl ethanoate) glues and paints |
| **Methanoic acid** | Coagulating rubber latex; descaling; leather tanning |
| **Propanoic acid and its salts** | Preserving bread and animal feed (stops mould) |
| **Fatty acids** (palmitic, stearic, oleic) | Making **soaps**, candles and cosmetics |

:::callout{type=local-example}
On **rubber plantations**, collected latex is mixed with dilute **methanoic acid** (formic acid), which makes the rubber particles clump together (coagulate) into sheets for drying and export. Rubber has been grown commercially in Sierra Leone, and the same chemistry is used across West Africa.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not use methyl orange for titrating ethanoic acid with sodium hydroxide: it changes colour too early (pH 3–4.5). Use **phenolphthalein**, which changes at pH 8–10.
:::

## Key points

- Ethanoic acid shows **typical acid reactions**: indicators, **metals** (→ salt + H₂), **carbonates** (→ salt + H₂O + **CO₂**), **bases** (→ salt + H₂O); the salts are **ethanoates**.
- Fizzing with **NaHCO₃ or Na₂CO₃** is the test for **–COOH**.
- With **alkanols** and conc. H₂SO₄, alkanoic acids form **esters** (reversible).
- Titrate ethanoic acid against NaOH with **phenolphthalein**.
- Uses: **food preservation**, esters, plastics, aspirin, rubber processing, soaps.

## Quick check

1. Name the salt formed when ethanoic acid reacts with magnesium.
2. How would you show that a liquid is an alkanoic acid rather than an alkanol?
3. Write the equation for ethanoic acid and sodium hydrogencarbonate.
4. Which indicator is suitable for titrating ethanoic acid with sodium hydroxide, and why?

**Answers:**

1. Magnesium ethanoate, (CH₃COO)₂Mg.
2. Add sodium carbonate or sodium hydrogencarbonate: an alkanoic acid gives effervescence of CO₂, which turns lime water milky; an alkanol does not react.
3. CH₃COOH + NaHCO₃ → CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂.
4. Phenolphthalein: in a weak acid–strong base titration the end point is above pH 7, in phenolphthalein's range.
