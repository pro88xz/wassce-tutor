**Mining in Sierra Leone** centres on **diamonds** (Kono and Kenema), **iron ore** (Tonkolili and Marampa), **rutile** (Moyamba and Bonthe), **bauxite** (Moyamba District) and **gold**. Minerals earn most of the country's exports and have built towns, roads and railways, but mining has also damaged land and water, displaced communities and, during the civil war, helped fund the fighting. This lesson describes each mineral and weighs the economic, social and environmental effects of mining.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the mining of diamonds, iron ore, rutile, bauxite and gold in Sierra Leone
- explain how each mineral is mined and exported
- explain the positive effects of mining on Sierra Leone
- explain the negative social and environmental effects of mining
- suggest ways of reducing the harmful effects of mining

## Diamonds

- **Discovered** in **Kono** in **1930**; commercial mining began in the 1930s.
- Found mainly in **Kono**, and in parts of **Kenema** and **Bo** districts.
- Most come from **alluvial deposits** in river gravels, dug by **artisanal miners** using shovels, pans and sieves; larger companies also mine **kimberlite pipes** near **Koidu**.
- Sierra Leone's diamonds are mostly **gem quality** and very valuable. The **Star of Sierra Leone** (968.9 carats), found in 1972, is one of the largest gem diamonds ever discovered.

:::callout{type=local-example}
During the civil war (1991–2002), control of the diamond fields helped finance the fighting, and the term **"blood diamonds"** (conflict diamonds) became known worldwide. The **Kimberley Process Certification Scheme**, launched in **2003**, now requires certificates to show that diamonds traded internationally are not from conflict zones, and Sierra Leone takes part in it.
:::

## Iron ore

- **Marampa** (Port Loko District): mined from the 1930s; the ore was carried by railway to **Pepel** for export.
- **Tonkolili** (Tonkolili District): very large deposits developed from **2011**, with a railway to **Pepel** port.
- Mined by **open-cast** methods with large machines.
- Iron ore exports have risen and fallen with **world prices** and company changes, showing the risk of depending on one mineral.

## Rutile and bauxite

- **Rutile** (titanium dioxide, used in paint pigments and welding rods) and **ilmenite** are mined from mineral sands in **Moyamba and Bonthe** districts, mainly by **dredging** and dry mining. Sierra Leone has **one of the world's largest natural rutile deposits**. Exports leave through **Nitti** port.
- **Bauxite** (aluminium ore) is mined in the **Mokanji Hills** area of **Moyamba District** by open-cast methods.

## Gold

Gold is mined both by **artisanal miners** in river gravels in several districts and by companies, for example at **Baomahun** in Bo District.

## Positive effects of mining

1. **Foreign exchange:** minerals provide most of Sierra Leone's export earnings.
2. **Government revenue** from royalties, licences and taxes.
3. **Employment** for thousands of people, including artisanal miners.
4. **Infrastructure:** railways (Marampa–Pepel, Tonkolili–Pepel), roads, ports, power and water.
5. **Growth of towns** such as **Koidu**.
6. **Social amenities** such as schools and clinics built by mining companies.
7. **Development of skills** and local businesses.

## Negative effects of mining

| Social and economic | Environmental |
|---|---|
| farmland lost to pits and spoil heaps, and **farming neglected** as young people go mining | **land degradation**: open pits, spoil heaps and mined-out land |
| **displacement** of communities | **deforestation** to clear mining sites |
| **child labour** and school dropouts in artisanal mining | **water pollution** and siltation of rivers |
| **smuggling** of diamonds and loss of revenue | abandoned pits fill with water and breed **mosquitoes** |
| disputes over land and compensation | **dust and noise** from large mines |
| dependence on **world prices**; boom and bust | loss of wildlife habitats |
| historically, financing of conflict | |

## Reducing the harmful effects

1. **Land reclamation:** refilling pits and replanting mined-out land.
2. **Enforcing mining laws** and environmental impact assessments.
3. **Fair compensation** and resettlement for affected communities.
4. **Community development agreements** so that mining areas share the benefits.
5. **Banning child labour** and supporting education in mining areas.
6. **Fighting smuggling** through licensing and the Kimberley Process.
7. **Diversifying the economy** into agriculture, fishing, tourism and industry.
8. **Processing minerals locally** to add value and create jobs.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not write only about benefits or only about problems. "Assess the effects of mining" means **both**, and the best answers balance economic gains against social and environmental costs.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Rutile**: titanium dioxide, used in paint pigments and welding rods, mined from mineral sands in Moyamba and Bonthe districts.
- **Bauxite**: the ore of aluminium, mined in the Mokanji Hills of Moyamba District.
- **Marampa**: the iron-ore mine in Port Loko District, worked from the 1930s, whose ore went by railway to Pepel.
- **Tonkolili**: the very large iron-ore deposits developed from 2011, with a railway to Pepel port.
- **Siltation**: the filling of rivers with fine mud washed from mining sites.
:::

## Key points

- **Diamonds:** Kono (found 1930), mostly alluvial; kimberlite near Koidu; "blood diamonds" and the **Kimberley Process (2003)**.
- **Iron ore:** Marampa and Tonkolili, open-cast, exported through **Pepel**.
- **Rutile and ilmenite:** Moyamba and Bonthe, dredged, exported through **Nitti**; **bauxite:** Mokanji Hills, Moyamba District; **gold:** Baomahun and artisanal sites.
- Benefits: **foreign exchange, revenue, jobs, infrastructure, towns, amenities**.
- Problems: **lost farmland, displacement, child labour, smuggling, land and water damage, price dependence**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **describe the mining of a named mineral in Sierra Leone** (location, method, transport and uses), to **explain the importance of mining to the economy**, and to **explain the problems of mining and suggest solutions**. A sketch map showing mining areas and export ports earns marks.
:::

## Quick check

1. In which district were diamonds first discovered in Sierra Leone, and when?
2. Through which port is iron ore exported?
3. What is rutile used for?
4. Give two environmental effects of mining.
5. What is the purpose of the Kimberley Process?

**Answers:**

1. Kono, in 1930.
2. Pepel.
3. Paint pigments and welding rods (as a source of titanium).
4. Any two: land degradation; deforestation; water pollution and siltation; mosquito-breeding pits; dust and noise; habitat loss.
5. To certify that internationally traded diamonds are not conflict ("blood") diamonds.
