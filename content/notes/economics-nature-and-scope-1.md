## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- give at least two definitions of economics and name the economists behind them
- explain why Lionel Robbins's scarcity definition is the one most widely used
- describe economics as a social science and say what it studies

## Why economics matters to you

Every morning, a trader at Bo market decides how many bags of rice to buy, a family in Kenema decides whether to spend its money on school fees or a new roof, and the government in Freetown decides how much to spend on roads and how much on hospitals. Each of them has limited money but many things they would like to do with it.

Economics is the subject that studies how people, businesses and governments make these choices.

## Definitions of economics

Economists have defined the subject in different ways over the years. WAEC expects you to know the main ones and the person behind each.

### The wealth definition: Adam Smith

Adam Smith, often called the father of economics, wrote *An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations* (1776). He saw economics as the study of how nations produce and increase their wealth.

**Weakness:** it puts wealth at the centre and says little about people's welfare or how wealth is shared.

### The welfare definition: Alfred Marshall

Alfred Marshall described economics as the study of mankind in the ordinary business of life: how people earn an income and how they use it to improve their material well-being.

**Weakness:** it covers only *material* welfare, yet people also pay for services such as teaching and medical care, which are not material goods.

### The scarcity definition: Lionel Robbins

:::callout{type=definition}
**Economics** is the science which studies human behaviour as a relationship between ends and scarce means which have alternative uses. *(Lionel Robbins, 1932)*
:::

Robbins's definition is the one most textbooks and examiners use, because it applies to every society and every kind of choice. It has three key ideas:

| Idea in the definition | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Ends** | Human wants, which are unlimited | Food, clothing, a phone, school fees |
| **Scarce means** | The resources used to satisfy wants are limited | Money, land, time, labour |
| **Alternative uses** | Each resource can be used in more than one way | Land can grow rice or be sold for building |

Because wants are unlimited and means are scarce, people must **choose**. Economics studies how those choices are made.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not write that economics is "the study of money". Money is only one of the scarce means. Economics studies **choice** in the use of all scarce resources, including time and land.
:::

## Economics as a social science

Economics is a **social science** because it studies human behaviour in society, as sociology and political science do. It is called a *science* because economists:

- collect facts (data) about prices, output and incomes
- form theories and test them against what actually happens
- make general statements, called **economic laws**, such as the law of demand

Economic laws are less exact than the laws of physics. People do not always behave the same way, so economists add the phrase *ceteris paribus*, meaning "other things being equal". The law of demand, for example, says that when price rises, quantity demanded falls, **if nothing else changes**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
If the price of a cup of rice at the market rises, most families buy less of it. But if the harvest has failed everywhere and there is nothing else to eat, they may still buy the same amount. That is why the law of demand only holds *ceteris paribus*.
:::

## What economics studies

The subject matter of economics includes:

- **production**: how goods and services are made
- **distribution**: how income is shared among the people who help to produce
- **exchange**: how goods and services are bought and sold in markets
- **consumption**: how people use goods and services to satisfy their wants
- **the role of government**: taxes, spending, money and banking

## Key points

- Adam Smith: economics as the study of **wealth**.
- Alfred Marshall: economics as the study of **material welfare**.
- Lionel Robbins: economics as the study of **choice** between unlimited ends and scarce means with alternative uses. This is the most widely accepted definition.
- Economics is a **social science**; its laws hold *ceteris paribus*.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Questions often ask you to **define economics** and **explain the key ideas** in a definition. Name the economist, quote or paraphrase the definition accurately, then explain *ends*, *scarce means* and *alternative uses* with an example of your own. Two or three clear sentences per idea earn more marks than a long, vague paragraph.
:::

## Quick check

1. Which economist defined economics in terms of scarcity and choice?
2. Why is Marshall's welfare definition criticised?
3. What does *ceteris paribus* mean, and why do economists use it?

**Answers:** 1. Lionel Robbins. 2. It covers only material welfare and leaves out services such as teaching and health care. 3. "Other things being equal"; economic laws hold only if other influences do not change at the same time.
