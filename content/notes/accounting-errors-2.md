**Correction of errors** is done with **journal entries**: for each error you work out what **was recorded**, what **should have been recorded**, and make the entry that turns one into the other. Errors are never corrected by crossing out or rubbing out entries in the ledger; the journal gives a clear, dated **record and explanation (narration)** of every correction. This lesson shows how to write journal entries to correct each type of error, step by step.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain why errors are corrected through the journal
- use the "what was done / what should have been done" method
- write correcting journal entries with narrations for each type of error
- explain the effect of each correction on the accounts

## The method

For each error ask three questions:

1. **What was recorded?** (the wrong entries)
2. **What should have been recorded?** (the correct entries)
3. **What entry turns the first into the second?** (the correction)

Corrections are made in the **general journal** and then posted to the ledger. Every journal entry shows the account debited, the account credited, the amount and a **narration**.

## Worked examples

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Show the journal entries to correct the following errors in the books of Fofanah Ventures (NLe).**

1. **Omission:** a credit sale of 3,000 to Kamara was not recorded.
2. **Commission:** 1,200 received from Bah was credited to Bai's account.
3. **Principle:** a computer bought for 8,000 was debited to purchases.
4. **Original entry:** a credit sale of 2,450 to Conteh was recorded as 2,540.
5. **Complete reversal:** cash of 1,500 paid to Sesay was debited to cash and credited to Sesay.
6. **Compensating:** the sales account and the wages account were each overcast by 500.

## Explanations

- **Error 2 (commission):** Bai was wrongly credited, so **debit Bai** to cancel it; Bah should have been credited, so **credit Bah**.
- **Error 4 (original entry):** both Conteh and sales were recorded **90 too high** ($2\,540 - 2\,450$), so **reduce both**: debit sales 90, credit Conteh 90.
- **Error 5 (complete reversal):** cash was debited 1,500 instead of credited 1,500, so it is **3,000 wrong**. The correction is **twice the amount**: debit Sesay 3,000, credit cash 3,000.
- **Error 6 (compensating):** sales (a credit balance) is 500 too high, so **debit** sales 500; wages (a debit balance) is 500 too high, so **credit** wages 500.

## Effect on profit

Many errors change the **net profit** once corrected:

| Error | Effect of the correction on net profit |
|---|---|
| 1. Sale omitted | sales +3,000: profit **increases** 3,000 |
| 2. Commission | personal accounts only: **no effect** |
| 3. Computer in purchases | purchases −8,000: profit **increases** 8,000 |
| 4. Sale overstated | sales −90: profit **decreases** 90 |
| 5. Reversal of a payment | cash and Sesay only: **no effect** |
| 6. Compensating | sales −500 and wages −500: **no net effect** |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Small businesses in Sierra Leone often record a new freezer, generator or motorbike as "purchases" or "expenses". This **error of principle** understates profit for the year and leaves a valuable asset out of the statement of financial position. The correcting entry debits the asset account and credits the expense or purchases account.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
For a **complete reversal**, the correction is **double** the original amount, because the wrong entries must first be cancelled and then the correct entries made. Many candidates correct it with the original amount only.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Correcting journal entry**: the journal entry that turns what was wrongly recorded into what should have been recorded.
:::

## Key points

- Correct errors with **journal entries**, never by altering the ledger directly.
- Method: **what was done → what should have been done → the correcting entry**.
- **Commission:** debit the wrong person, credit the right one. **Principle:** debit the right class, credit the wrong one.
- **Complete reversal:** correct with **twice** the amount.
- Some corrections change **net profit**; corrections between personal or asset accounts do not.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **journalise the correction of errors**, with **narrations**, and often to **state the effect on net profit**. Write the account debited first, the account credited below it, and a short narration under each entry.
:::

## Quick check

1. Why are errors corrected through the journal?
2. Wages of NLe 2,000 were debited to the drawings account. Give the correcting entry.
3. A credit purchase of NLe 5,600 from Bangura was recorded as NLe 6,500. Give the correction.
4. A cash receipt of NLe 700 from Koroma was debited to Koroma and credited to cash. Give the correction.
5. Which of the corrections in questions 2–4 changes net profit?

**Answers:**

1. To give a dated record and explanation of every correction, instead of altering ledger entries.
2. Debit wages 2,000; credit drawings 2,000.
3. Debit Bangura 900; credit purchases 900.
4. Debit cash 1,400; credit Koroma 1,400 (twice the amount for a complete reversal).
5. Questions 2 (wages, an expense, increase, so profit falls) and 3 (purchases fall, so profit rises); question 4 has no effect on profit.
