A wave is a pattern moving through space and time at once. The progressive wave equation captures both in a single line, and the ripple tank lets you watch waves slowly enough to measure them.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- write and interpret the progressive wave equation
- calculate phase difference between two points on a wave
- describe the ripple tank and how a stroboscope "freezes" waves
- distinguish progressive and stationary waves

## The progressive wave equation

For a wave travelling in the $+x$ direction with amplitude $a$, period $T$ and wavelength λ, the displacement $y$ of a particle at distance $x$ at time $t$ is

$$y = a\sin 2\pi\left(\dfrac{t}{T} - \dfrac{x}{\lambda}\right) = a\sin(\omega t - kx)$$

where $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T} = 2\pi f$ and $k = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}$.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A progressive wave is described by $y = 0.05\sin(20\pi t - 0.5\pi x)$, with $y$ and $x$ in metres and $t$ in seconds. Find (a) the amplitude, (b) the frequency, (c) the wavelength, (d) the speed.**

Compare with $y = a\sin(2\pi ft - \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}x)$:

(a) $a = 0.05$ m.

(b) $2\pi f = 20\pi \Rightarrow f = 10$ Hz.

(c) $\dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} = 0.5\pi \Rightarrow \lambda = 4$ m.

(d) $v = f\lambda = 10 \times 4 = 40$ m s⁻¹.
:::

## Phase difference

Two points on a wave a distance $x$ apart have a **phase difference**

$$\phi = \dfrac{2\pi x}{\lambda}\ \text{radians} = \dfrac{x}{\lambda} \times 360°$$

- Points one wavelength apart ($x = \lambda$) are **in phase** (φ = 360°, equivalent to 0°).
- Points half a wavelength apart are **in antiphase** (φ = 180°): one is at a crest when the other is at a trough.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Two points on a wave of wavelength 2.4 m are 0.6 m apart. What is their phase difference?**

$$\phi = \dfrac{0.6}{2.4} \times 360° = 90° = \dfrac{\pi}{2}\ \text{rad}$$
:::

## Energy carried by waves

The energy carried by a wave (and its **intensity**, energy per second per unit area) is proportional to the **square of the amplitude**. Doubling the amplitude of a sound wave makes it carry four times the energy.

Waves spreading out from a point source spread their energy over an ever larger sphere, so the intensity falls with distance: $I \propto \dfrac{1}{r^2}$.

## The ripple tank

:::callout{type=definition}
A **ripple tank** is a shallow glass-bottomed tray of water used to study water waves.
:::

- A **vibrating dipper** (a straight bar for plane waves, or a small ball for circular waves) driven by a motor produces the waves.
- A **lamp above** the tank casts shadows of the waves onto a white screen below: crests act like converging lenses and show as **bright lines**, troughs as dark lines.
- **Sloping beaches** (sponge or foam) at the edges absorb the waves and stop unwanted reflections.
- A glass plate placed in the tank makes a **shallow region** to show refraction; metal barriers show reflection and diffraction.

### Freezing waves with a stroboscope

:::callout{type=definition}
A **hand stroboscope** is a disc with equally spaced slits.
:::

Looking at the waves through the spinning disc, you see them only at regular instants. If the number of views per second equals the wave frequency, each crest has moved exactly one wavelength between views, so the pattern appears **stationary ("frozen")**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A stroboscope has 12 slits. The waves first appear frozen when it turns at 2 revolutions per second. What is the frequency of the waves?**

Views per second $= 12 \times 2 = 24$, so $f = 24$ Hz.
:::

## Progressive and stationary waves

| Progressive wave | Stationary (standing) wave |
|---|---|
| the wave profile **moves**, carrying energy along | the wave profile **does not move**; no net energy transfer |
| every particle vibrates with the **same amplitude** | amplitude varies from **zero (nodes)** to maximum (antinodes) |
| neighbouring particles have different phases | all particles between two nodes are **in phase** |
| distance between successive crests = λ | distance between successive nodes = $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$ |

Stationary waves are formed when **two identical waves travel in opposite directions** and superpose — for example a wave and its reflection on a guitar string (see wave phenomena and resonance).

## Key points

- $y = a\sin 2\pi\left(\dfrac{t}{T} - \dfrac{x}{\lambda}\right)$: compare terms to read $a$, $f$ and λ.
- Phase difference $\phi = \dfrac{x}{\lambda} \times 360°$.
- Energy ∝ amplitude²; intensity from a point source ∝ 1/r².
- A stroboscope freezes waves when its flash rate equals the wave frequency.
- Stationary waves have nodes and antinodes, with nodes λ/2 apart.

## Quick check

1. Two points 1.5 m apart on a wave of wavelength 3 m: what is their phase difference?
2. In $y = 0.1\sin(100\pi t - 2\pi x)$, what is the frequency?
3. What is the distance between two adjacent nodes in a stationary wave?

**Answers:** 1. 180° (antiphase). 2. $2\pi f = 100\pi$, so $f = 50$ Hz. 3. Half a wavelength, λ/2.
