## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define utility and distinguish total, average and marginal utility
- calculate utility from a table and describe the shape of the curves
- state and explain the law of diminishing marginal utility and the paradox of value

## What is utility?

:::callout{type=definition}
**Utility** is the **satisfaction** a consumer gets from consuming a good or service. A good has utility if it can satisfy a want, whether or not it is useful or healthy.
:::

Utility is personal: a cold drink gives great satisfaction to a thirsty footballer and very little to someone who has just had three. Utility cannot be measured exactly, but economists use imaginary units called **utils** to explain consumer behaviour. This is called the **cardinal** approach.

## Total, average and marginal utility

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Total utility (TU)** is the **total satisfaction** from consuming a given quantity of a good.
- **Average utility (AU)** is total utility **per unit**: $\text{AU} = \dfrac{\text{TU}}{Q}$.
- **Marginal utility (MU)** is the **extra satisfaction** from consuming **one more unit**: $\text{MU} = \text{TU}_n - \text{TU}_{n-1}$.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Fatmata eats oranges on a hot afternoon**

| Oranges eaten | Total utility (utils) | Average utility | Marginal utility |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| 2 | 18 | 9 | 8 |
| 3 | 24 | 8 | 6 |
| 4 | 28 | 7 | 4 |
| 5 | 30 | 6 | 2 |
| 6 | 30 | 5 | 0 |
| 7 | 28 | 4 | $-2$ |

- The 3rd orange adds $24 - 18 = 6$ utils.
- Average utility of 4 oranges $= \dfrac{28}{4} = 7$ utils.
:::

## The shape of the curves

Plot TU and MU against the number of oranges:

1. **While MU is positive, TU rises**, but more and more slowly.
2. **When MU is zero, TU is at its maximum** (the 6th orange). This is the **point of satiety**: Fatmata is fully satisfied.
3. **When MU is negative, TU falls.** The 7th orange makes her feel worse (*disutility*).

The MU curve slopes **downwards** and crosses the quantity axis where TU is at its peak.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
"Diminishing marginal utility" does not mean total utility falls. Total utility keeps **rising** as long as marginal utility is positive; it is only the **addition** to satisfaction that gets smaller.
:::

## The law of diminishing marginal utility

:::callout{type=definition}
**The law of diminishing marginal utility** states that, **as a consumer consumes more and more units of a good** in a given period, **the extra satisfaction (marginal utility) from each additional unit eventually falls**, other things being equal.
:::

### Assumptions of the law

1. The units are **identical** in size and quality.
2. Consumption is **continuous**: the oranges are eaten one after another, not one today and one next month.
3. The consumer's **tastes and income do not change**.
4. The consumer is **rational**.

### Exceptions

- **Collections and hobbies**: a stamp or coin collector may gain more satisfaction from each new item that completes a set.
- **Addictive goods**: alcohol or drugs may seem to give rising satisfaction.
- **Money**: many people never feel they have enough (although each extra leone matters less to a rich person than to a poor one).

## The law and the demand curve

Because each extra unit gives less satisfaction, a consumer will buy more units **only if the price falls**. This is one reason the demand curve slopes downwards.

## The paradox of value

Water is essential for life, yet it is cheap. Diamonds are not needed for survival, yet they are very expensive. Why?

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone mines diamonds, and its rivers and taps provide water. Water is plentiful, so people consume a great deal of it and the **marginal utility of the last cup is very low**, even though its **total utility is enormous**. Diamonds are scarce, so few are consumed and the **marginal utility of one more diamond is high**. **Price reflects marginal utility, not total utility.** This explains the "diamond–water paradox".
:::

## Key points

- **Utility** = satisfaction. **TU** = total, **AU** = TU ÷ Q, **MU** = extra satisfaction from one more unit.
- When MU is **positive**, TU rises; when MU is **zero**, TU is at its **maximum**; when MU is **negative**, TU falls.
- **Law of diminishing MU**: extra satisfaction from each extra unit eventually falls.
- **Price depends on marginal utility**, which explains the diamond–water paradox.

## Quick check

1. If TU from 4 units is 40 and from 5 units is 45, what is the MU of the 5th unit?
2. What is total utility doing when marginal utility is zero?
3. Why are diamonds dearer than water?

**Answers:** 1. $45 - 40 = 5$ utils. 2. It is at its maximum. 3. Diamonds are scarce, so their marginal utility is high; water is plentiful, so the marginal utility of the last unit is low. Price reflects marginal, not total, utility.
