Fractional distillation gives far more heavy oil than the world wants and far less petrol. Refineries solve this by **cracking**: breaking big molecules into small, useful ones. Cracking also produces alkenes, the raw materials for plastics. This lesson covers cracking, reforming, the quality of petrol (octane rating) and the petrochemical industry.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define cracking and explain why it is carried out
- describe thermal and catalytic cracking and a laboratory cracking experiment
- write equations for cracking reactions
- explain reforming, knocking and octane number
- name important petrochemicals and their uses
- describe the pollution problems of petroleum use

## Cracking

:::callout{type=definition}
**Cracking** is the breaking down of **large hydrocarbon molecules** into **smaller, more useful** molecules, using heat (and often a catalyst). It always gives a smaller alkane and at least one **alkene**.
:::

**Why crack?**

1. **Demand:** there is more demand for small molecules (petrol, LPG) than fractional distillation supplies, and less demand for heavy fractions.
2. **Alkenes:** cracking produces **ethene and propene**, the raw materials for plastics, ethanol and many chemicals.
3. **Better petrol:** catalytic cracking gives branched alkanes with a higher octane number.
4. **Hydrogen** is sometimes a by-product, used to make ammonia.

**Examples:**

$$\text{C}_{10}\text{H}_{22} \rightarrow \text{C}_8\text{H}_{18} + \text{C}_2\text{H}_4$$

$$\text{C}_{14}\text{H}_{30} \rightarrow \text{C}_{10}\text{H}_{22} + 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$$

$$\text{C}_3\text{H}_8 \rightarrow \text{C}_3\text{H}_6 + \text{H}_2$$

The numbers of carbon and hydrogen atoms must balance on both sides.

| Type | Conditions | Main products |
|---|---|---|
| **Thermal cracking** | High temperature (about 800 °C), high pressure | Many alkenes |
| **Catalytic cracking** | About 500 °C, low pressure, **catalyst** of aluminium oxide and silica (zeolites) | Branched alkanes for petrol, aromatics, alkenes |
| **Steam cracking** | Naphtha or ethane with steam, about 850 °C | Mainly **ethene** and propene |

## Cracking in the laboratory

![Mineral wool soaked in liquid paraffin at the closed end of a boiling tube; broken porcelain in the middle is heated strongly; gaseous alkenes are collected over water](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/cracking-lab.svg)

*Figure 1. Cracking liquid paraffin over hot porcelain. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

- **Liquid paraffin** (a mixture of large alkanes) is soaked into mineral wool.
- **Broken porcelain** or aluminium oxide acts as the **catalyst** and is heated strongly; the paraffin is warmed so its vapour passes over the hot porcelain.
- The gas collected over water **decolourises bromine water**, showing that **alkenes** have formed.
- **Safety:** remove the delivery tube from the water **before** you stop heating, otherwise cold water is sucked back into the hot tube and cracks it (**suck-back**).

## Reforming, knocking and octane number

**Reforming** rearranges straight-chain alkanes into **branched-chain** alkanes, **cycloalkanes** and **aromatic** compounds, using a platinum catalyst. These burn more smoothly in engines.

**Knocking (pinking):** in a car engine, the petrol–air mixture should burn smoothly when the spark fires. Straight-chain alkanes may **explode too early**, giving a metallic knocking sound, wasting energy and damaging the engine.

:::callout{type=definition}
The **octane number** measures a petrol's resistance to knocking. **2,2,4-Trimethylpentane** ("iso-octane", which burns smoothly) is given **100**; **heptane** (which knocks badly) is given **0**. Petrol with an octane number of 95 knocks like a mixture of 95% iso-octane and 5% heptane.
:::

- **Branched alkanes, cycloalkanes and aromatics** have **high** octane numbers.
- **Straight-chain alkanes** have **low** octane numbers.
- **Tetraethyl lead** was once added to raise octane number. Lead is poisonous, so **leaded petrol has been phased out** worldwide; unleaded petrol uses branched alkanes, aromatics or ethanol instead.

## Petrochemicals

:::callout{type=definition}
**Petrochemicals** are chemicals made from petroleum or natural gas.
:::

| Starting material | Product | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Ethene** | Poly(ethene), ethanol, ethane-1,2-diol, PVC | Bags, bottles, solvents, antifreeze, pipes |
| **Propene** | Poly(propene) | Ropes, crates, chairs, carpets |
| **Methane** (natural gas) | Hydrogen, then ammonia | Fertilisers |
| **Methane** | Methanol | Solvents, plastics |
| **Naphtha and aromatics** | Detergents, dyes, drugs, nylon, synthetic rubber | Many everyday products |

## Pollution from petroleum

- **Carbon(IV) oxide** from burning fuels contributes to **global warming**.
- **Carbon(II) oxide** and **soot** from incomplete combustion.
- **Sulphur(IV) oxide** (from sulphur impurities) and **nitrogen oxides** (from engines) cause **acid rain**.
- **Unburnt hydrocarbons** contribute to smog.
- **Oil spills** at sea kill birds, fish and mangroves.
- **Lead** from old leaded petrol damaged health, especially children's brains.

Catalytic converters, removing sulphur at refineries, unleaded fuel and cleaner engines reduce these problems.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Completing a cracking equation.** Decane is cracked to give octane and one other product. Identify the product and test for it.

$$\text{C}_{10}\text{H}_{22} \rightarrow \text{C}_8\text{H}_{18} + \text{X}$$

Carbon: 10 − 8 = 2; hydrogen: 22 − 18 = 4. X is **C₂H₄, ethene**. It **decolourises bromine water** quickly.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Kerosene** is still widely used for lamps and cooking stoves in Sierra Leone. Kerosene mixed with cheaper or heavier oils (adulterated fuel) burns with a smoky, sooty flame and can cause lamp fires. Clean, correctly refined kerosene burns brightly with little smoke, because its molecules are within the right size range.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Cracking an alkane always gives **an alkene** as one product; you cannot crack an alkane into two alkanes only, because there would not be enough hydrogen atoms.
:::

## Key points

- **Cracking** breaks large alkanes into **smaller alkanes and alkenes**, using heat (thermal) or a catalyst (catalytic, Al₂O₃/SiO₂, about 500 °C).
- It matches supply to **demand** for petrol and makes **ethene and propene** for plastics.
- **Reforming** gives branched, cyclic and aromatic hydrocarbons.
- **Octane number** measures resistance to **knocking**: iso-octane = 100, heptane = 0; branched chains score high.
- **Petrochemicals** include plastics, fertilisers, detergents and solvents.
- Burning petroleum causes **CO₂, CO, SO₂, NOₓ and soot** pollution.

## Quick check

1. Give two reasons for cracking heavy fractions.
2. What catalyst can be used in the laboratory cracking of liquid paraffin?
3. What is meant by an octane number of 90?
4. Complete: C₁₂H₂₆ → C₈H₁₈ + 2 ____.

**Answers:**

1. Any two: to meet the demand for petrol and other small molecules; to produce alkenes (ethene, propene) for plastics and chemicals; to make higher-octane petrol.
2. Broken porcelain (or aluminium oxide).
3. The petrol knocks like a mixture of 90% 2,2,4-trimethylpentane and 10% heptane.
4. C₂H₄ (ethene).
