# Inflation, purchasing power and the monetary system

## Learning objectives
Explain inflation, calculate a simple price change and analyse how changes in purchasing power affect households and businesses.

## Meaning and measurement
Inflation is a sustained increase in the general level of prices over time. It does not mean that every individual price rises at the same rate. A consumer price index (CPI) tracks the average change in prices of a representative basket of goods and services.

A simplified inflation-rate calculation is:

**Inflation rate = (New CPI − Old CPI) ÷ Old CPI × 100**

If the CPI rises from 150 to 165, inflation = (165 − 150) ÷ 150 × 100 = **10%**.

## Causes
- **Demand-pull:** total demand rises faster than the economy's ability to supply goods and services.
- **Cost-push:** production costs such as fuel, imported inputs or wages rise and firms pass some costs to customers.
- **Monetary factors:** sustained excessive growth in money and credit relative to output can contribute to inflation.
- **Imported inflation:** depreciation or higher world prices increase the domestic cost of imports.

Several causes can operate together.

## Effects
Inflation reduces the purchasing power of money when incomes do not keep pace with prices. It can make budgeting difficult, raise business costs, reduce the real value of fixed savings and create uncertainty for investment. Borrowers with fixed-rate debt may benefit if their incomes rise while repayments remain fixed, but this is not guaranteed.

Unanticipated inflation can redistribute income between borrowers and lenders. High and unstable inflation can weaken confidence in the currency.

## Currency and confidence
A sound monetary system depends on confidence, reliable payment infrastructure and prudent monetary and fiscal management. The central bank's monetary-policy tools influence liquidity and interest rates, while commercial banks and payment providers facilitate transactions.

## WASSCE examination focus
Do not define inflation as the rise in the price of one commodity. Explain that it is a general and sustained rise. Show each step in CPI calculations.

## Quick check
1. Define inflation.
2. CPI rises from 120 to 132. Calculate the rate.
3. Distinguish demand-pull from cost-push inflation.
4. State two effects on households.

**Answers:** 1. A sustained rise in the general price level. 2. (132 − 120) ÷ 120 × 100 = 10%. 3. Demand-pull comes from excess aggregate demand; cost-push comes from rising production costs. 4. Reduced purchasing power and difficulty planning household spending.
