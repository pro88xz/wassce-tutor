Sunlight looks white, but a glass prism — or a shower of rain with the Sun behind you — splits it into a band of colours. Colour is not something objects "have"; it depends on which wavelengths of light they reflect.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe and explain dispersion of white light by a prism
- name the colours of the visible spectrum in order
- explain how a pure spectrum is produced and how colours recombine
- explain the colour of objects and the mixing of coloured lights and pigments

## Dispersion

:::callout{type=definition}
**Dispersion** is the **separation of white light into its component colours** when it passes through a prism (or another refracting medium).
:::

![A narrow beam of white light enters a glass prism and leaves as a spread of colours from red, deviated least, to violet, deviated most](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/prism-dispersion.svg)

*Figure 1. Dispersion of white light by a prism. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

**Why it happens:** white light is a mixture of colours with different wavelengths. Glass has a slightly **different refractive index for each colour**: highest for **violet** and lowest for **red**. So violet is slowed and bent **most**, and red **least**, and the colours spread out.

The order of the colours in the **visible spectrum**, from least to most deviated:

**Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Violet** (remember **ROY G BIV**).

Red has the longest wavelength (about 700 nm) and violet the shortest (about 400 nm).

## Pure and impure spectra

A spectrum produced by a prism with a wide beam is **impure**: the colours overlap. A **pure spectrum** (colours sharply separated) is obtained by:

1. using a **narrow slit** as the source of light,
2. a **converging lens** to make the light from the slit parallel before it enters the prism,
3. the prism set at **minimum deviation**,
4. a second **converging lens** to focus each colour sharply on a screen.

A **spectrometer** is an instrument built on this arrangement.

## Recombining colours

The colours of the spectrum can be recombined into white light:

- with a **second, inverted prism** placed after the first
- with **Newton's colour disc**: a disc painted in the spectrum colours appears white (or greyish) when spun rapidly, because the eye retains each colour briefly

## The rainbow

A **rainbow** forms when sunlight enters raindrops, is **refracted** (and dispersed) on entering, **totally internally reflected** at the back of the drop, and refracted again as it leaves. Each colour leaves at a slightly different angle, so you see a band of colours — **red on the outside, violet on the inside**. You see a rainbow only with the **Sun behind you** and rain in front.

:::callout{type=local-example}
During the rainy season in Freetown, rainbows often appear over the hills in the late afternoon, when the Sun is low in the west and showers are falling to the east.
:::

## Colour of objects

An opaque object appears coloured because it **reflects** some colours and **absorbs** the rest.

| Object in white light | Reflects | Absorbs |
|---|---|---|
| white paper | all colours | none |
| black cloth | none | all colours |
| red cloth | red | all other colours |
| green leaf | green | most other colours |

**Seen in coloured light:** a red shirt in **red** light looks **red**; in **green** light it looks **black**, because there is no red light for it to reflect.

A **transparent** filter **transmits** its own colour and absorbs the others: a red filter lets only red light through.

## Mixing coloured lights (additive mixing)

The **primary colours of light** are **red, green and blue**. Adding them:

| Mixture | Result |
|---|---|
| red + green | **yellow** |
| red + blue | **magenta** |
| green + blue | **cyan** |
| red + green + blue | **white** |

Two colours that add to make white are **complementary**: red and cyan, green and magenta, blue and yellow. Television and phone screens make every colour from tiny red, green and blue dots.

## Mixing paints (subtractive mixing)

Paints and dyes work by **absorbing** (subtracting) colours. Mixing yellow and cyan (blue-green) paint gives **green**, because only green is reflected by both. Mixing many paints tends towards black.

## Key points

- Dispersion: white light splits into colours because the refractive index differs for each colour.
- Order: ROYGBIV; red deviated least (longest wavelength), violet most.
- Objects reflect their own colour and absorb the rest; coloured lighting changes how they look.
- Primary colours of light: red, green, blue; red + green = yellow, all three = white.

## Quick check

1. Which colour is deviated most by a prism?
2. What colour does a blue cloth appear in red light?
3. What is the result of adding red and green light?

**Answers:** 1. Violet. 2. Black (it absorbs red and there is no blue light to reflect). 3. Yellow.
