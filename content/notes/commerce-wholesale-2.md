Supermarkets now buy straight from factories, farmers sell through cooperatives and online shops deliver direct to homes. Is the wholesaler disappearing? This lesson looks at the arguments, and at the other middlemen, the agents, who help goods and services change hands.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the reasons for the elimination of the wholesaler
- give arguments for retaining the wholesaler
- describe the types of mercantile agents: brokers, factors, del credere agents, commission agents, auctioneers and sole agents
- distinguish an agent from a principal

## Why the wholesaler may be eliminated

Goods sometimes go **directly** from the manufacturer to the retailer or consumer. The wholesaler is bypassed when:

1. **Large retailers** (supermarkets, chain stores) buy in bulk directly from manufacturers.
2. **Manufacturers open their own shops**, depots or sales branches.
3. **Branded and pre-packed goods** need less sorting, grading and breaking bulk.
4. **Retailers' cooperatives** or buying groups purchase directly in bulk.
5. **Mail order and e-commerce** let producers sell straight to consumers.
6. **Perishable goods** (fresh fish, vegetables, bread) must reach consumers quickly.
7. **Expensive or technical goods** (vehicles, machinery) need the producer's own after-sales service.
8. **Improved transport and communication** let producers reach many retailers directly.
9. **Government marketing boards** buy produce directly from farmers.

## Arguments for retaining the wholesaler

1. **Small retailers** cannot buy direct: manufacturers will not deal in small quantities.
2. The wholesaler **breaks bulk** and **gives credit**, which small retailers depend on.
3. **Storage**: the wholesaler holds stock so manufacturers and retailers do not have to.
4. **Fewer transactions**: one wholesaler links many producers with many retailers, cutting distribution costs.
5. **Risk-bearing**: the wholesaler bears the risk of price falls and damage.
6. **Market information** flows through the wholesaler in both directions.
7. **Scattered markets**: in a country with many small rural retailers, the wholesaler reaches places producers cannot.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Eliminating the wholesaler does not eliminate the functions.** When a supermarket buys direct from a factory, it must itself:

- build or rent **warehouses** to store bulk stock (storage),
- run **lorries** to collect goods (transport),
- pay for goods in bulk and wait to sell them (finance),
- bear the **risk** of unsold stock.

The supermarket has simply taken over the wholesaler's work, and its costs.
:::

## Middlemen and mercantile agents

:::callout{type=definition}
An **agent** is a person who acts on behalf of another person (the **principal**) in dealing with third parties, usually for a **commission**. A **principal** buys or sells on their own account and owns the goods.
:::

| Agent | What they do | How paid |
|---|---|---|
| **Broker** | Brings buyers and sellers together and arranges contracts; does **not** take possession of the goods (insurance, produce and stock brokers) | Brokerage (commission) |
| **Factor** | Is given **possession** of goods to sell in their own name; may sell on credit and receive payment | Factorage (commission) |
| **Del credere agent** | Sells on credit for a principal and **guarantees** that buyers will pay | A higher commission (del credere commission) |
| **Commission agent** | Buys or sells goods for a principal for a commission | Commission |
| **Auctioneer** | Sells goods publicly to the **highest bidder** | Commission |
| **Sole agent (distributor)** | Has the **exclusive right** to sell a producer's goods in an area or country | Commission or profit margin |
| **Forwarding / clearing agent** | Handles shipping, documents and customs clearance for importers and exporters | Fees |

**Advantages of using agents:** expert knowledge of markets; save the principal time; reach distant or foreign markets; reduce risk (del credere agents). **Disadvantages:** commission adds to cost; the principal depends on the agent's honesty; agents may serve several principals.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Most importers in Freetown use **clearing and forwarding agents** to handle documents and customs procedures at the port. Car and phone brands often appoint a **sole distributor** for Sierra Leone, while produce traders use **brokers** to find buyers for cocoa, coffee and cashew.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A **broker** does **not** take possession of the goods; a **factor** does. This is the most common distinction asked about agents.
:::

## Key points

- The wholesaler may be **bypassed** by large retailers, manufacturers' own outlets, cooperatives, e-commerce and branded goods.
- The wholesaler is **retained** because small retailers need **bulk-breaking, credit and storage**, and transactions are fewer.
- **Eliminating the wholesaler does not eliminate the wholesaler's functions**.
- Agents: **brokers** (no possession), **factors** (possession), **del credere agents** (guarantee payment), **commission agents, auctioneers, sole agents, clearing agents**.

## Quick check

1. Give three reasons why the wholesaler may be eliminated.
2. Give two arguments for retaining the wholesaler.
3. Distinguish a broker from a factor.
4. What is a del credere agent?

**Answers:** 1. Any three: large retailers buy direct, manufacturers' own shops, branded pre-packed goods, retail cooperatives, mail order and e-commerce, perishable goods, technical goods, better transport and communication, marketing boards. 2. Any two: small retailers need bulk-breaking and credit, storage, fewer transactions, risk-bearing, market information, scattered markets. 3. A broker brings buyers and sellers together without taking possession of the goods; a factor takes possession and sells in his own name. 4. An agent who sells on credit for a principal and guarantees that the buyers will pay, for an extra commission.
