In a busy tailoring workshop one person cuts, another sews, a third adds buttons and a fourth irons. Together they make far more shirts than four tailors each working alone. Splitting work into tasks, and people and places concentrating on what they do best, is the secret of high output. It is also why trade, and so commerce, is needed.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define division of labour and specialisation and describe the types of specialisation
- explain the advantages and disadvantages of division of labour
- explain the factors that limit division of labour
- explain why specialisation makes exchange and commerce necessary
- calculate simple measures of productivity

## Division of labour

:::callout{type=definition}
**Division of labour** is the splitting of a production process into **separate tasks**, with each worker (or group of workers) concentrating on one task.
:::

**Specialisation** is the wider idea of concentrating on one activity. Division of labour is specialisation **by process** inside one firm.

### Types of specialisation

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **By craft or occupation** | A person follows one trade | A carpenter, a mechanic, a teacher |
| **By process** (division of labour) | A worker does one stage of a product | Cutting, sewing and ironing in a garment workshop |
| **By product** | A firm makes one kind of good | A bakery that makes only bread |
| **By region (territorial)** | An area concentrates on what suits it | Kono and Tonkolili: mining; the south: oil palm; Freetown: trade and services |
| **By country (international)** | A country specialises in goods it produces best | Sierra Leone: iron ore and cocoa; other countries: machinery and fuel |

## Advantages of division of labour

1. **Higher output**: each worker becomes skilled and fast at one task.
2. **Saving of time**: no time is lost moving from one task to another.
3. **Use of machinery**: simple, repeated tasks can be mechanised.
4. **Workers use their best talents**: each does what suits them.
5. **Lower average cost**, so goods can be sold more cheaply.
6. **Easier and quicker training**: learning one task takes less time.
7. **Better quality and standard products**.

## Disadvantages of division of labour

1. **Monotony and boredom** from repeating one task.
2. **Loss of craftsmanship**: no worker makes the whole product.
3. **Interdependence**: a delay or strike in one section stops the whole process.
4. **Unemployment risk**: workers with one narrow skill find it hard to change jobs; machines may replace them.
5. **Standardised products**, with less variety.
6. **Loss of responsibility and pride** in the finished good.

## Limits to division of labour

1. **The size of the market**: if few goods can be sold, there is no point splitting the work (the most important limit).
2. **The nature of the product**: some goods, such as a carved mask or a tailored suit to order, need one person's skill throughout.
3. **The availability of capital**: splitting tasks usually needs machines and buildings.
4. **The supply of labour**: there must be enough workers to fill each task.
5. **The level of technology** and transport.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not write that division of labour "always lowers costs". It lowers costs only when **output is large enough**. A village tailor who makes a few shirts a week gains little from splitting the work among five people: the market is too small.
:::

## Specialisation, exchange and commerce

When people specialise, each produces **more of one thing** than they need and **none** of many other things. They must **exchange** their surplus for the goods they lack. Exchange needs:

- **money**, as a medium of exchange
- **markets** and **traders** to bring buyers and sellers together
- **aids to trade**: transport, warehousing, banking, insurance, communication, advertising

So specialisation **creates the need for commerce**, and commerce in turn makes deeper specialisation possible. The two grow together.

## Measuring productivity

:::callout{type=definition}
**Productivity** is output per unit of input over a period, for example output per worker per day.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A garment workshop.** Working alone, each of 5 tailors makes 4 shirts a day. When the work is divided into cutting, sewing, buttons, finishing and ironing, the same 5 workers make 35 shirts a day.

- Output before $= 5 \times 4 = 20$ shirts a day; productivity $= 20 \div 5 = 4$ shirts per worker.
- Output after $= 35$ shirts a day; productivity $= 35 \div 5 = 7$ shirts per worker.
- Increase in output $= \dfrac{35 - 20}{20} \times 100\% = 75\%$.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many motor-repair workshops ("fitting shops") in Freetown practise division of labour: one person handles engines, another electrics, another panel beating and spraying. Customers get faster, better repairs, and each mechanic becomes an expert in one area.
:::

## Key points

- **Division of labour** splits production into tasks; **specialisation** is concentrating on one activity, by craft, process, product, region or country.
- Advantages: **more output, skill, time saved, machinery, lower cost**. Disadvantages: **monotony, interdependence, loss of craft, unemployment risk**.
- It is limited mainly by **the size of the market**, and by the product, capital and labour.
- Specialisation makes **exchange**, and so **commerce**, necessary.

## Quick check

1. What is the difference between division of labour and specialisation?
2. Give two advantages and two disadvantages of division of labour.
3. What is the most important limit to division of labour?
4. Ten workers make 60 baskets a day. What is the productivity per worker?

**Answers:** 1. Division of labour splits one production process into tasks; specialisation is the wider concentration on one activity (by craft, product, region or country). 2. Advantages: any two of more output, skill, time saved, use of machines, lower cost. Disadvantages: any two of monotony, interdependence, loss of craftsmanship, unemployment risk. 3. The size of the market. 4. $60 \div 10 = 6$ baskets per worker per day.
