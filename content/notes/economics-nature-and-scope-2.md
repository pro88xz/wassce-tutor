## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- tell microeconomics and macroeconomics apart, with examples
- distinguish positive statements from normative statements
- explain the main reasons for studying economics

## Two branches: micro and macro

Economics is usually divided into two branches. The difference is the size of what is being studied.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Microeconomics** studies the behaviour of individual units in the economy: one consumer, one household, one firm or one market.

**Macroeconomics** studies the economy as a whole: total output, the general price level, unemployment, the money supply and the balance of payments.
:::

| Microeconomics asks… | Macroeconomics asks… |
|---|---|
| How does a cassava farmer decide how much to plant? | What is the total output (national income) of Sierra Leone this year? |
| Why does the price of palm oil rise in the dry season? | Why are prices in general rising (inflation)? |
| How much will a shop owner pay a new worker? | Why are many young people unable to find jobs (unemployment)? |
| How does a family share its income between food and school fees? | How should the government finance its budget? |

A simple way to remember: **micro** looks at the trees; **macro** looks at the whole forest.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Some students think microeconomics means "small businesses" and macroeconomics means "big companies". It does not. A very large firm is still a *micro* topic, because it is one unit. *Macro* is about totals and averages for the whole economy.
:::

## Positive and normative economics

Economists also separate statements of fact from statements of opinion.

:::callout{type=definition}
A **positive statement** describes what *is*, *was* or *will be*. It can be checked against facts, so it can be shown to be true or false.

A **normative statement** says what *ought to be*. It depends on values and opinions, so it cannot be proved true or false by facts alone.
:::

| Positive (can be tested) | Normative (a value judgement) |
|---|---|
| The price of a bag of rice rose last month. | The government should control the price of rice. |
| More pupils sat WASSCE this year than last year. | Every pupil ought to receive free textbooks. |
| A higher tax on cigarettes reduces the quantity bought. | Cigarettes should be taxed more heavily. |

**Clue words:** normative statements often contain *should*, *ought to*, *better*, *fair* or *unfair*.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Classify:** "If the minimum wage is raised, some small firms will employ fewer workers."

This is **positive**. It may be right or wrong, but it can be tested by looking at what firms actually do. Note that a positive statement does not have to be true: it only has to be *checkable*.
:::

## Why study economics?

Studying economics helps:

1. **individuals** to manage their income, save, borrow wisely and choose careers
2. **businesses** to decide what to produce, how to price it and how to keep costs down
3. **governments** to plan budgets, set taxes, control inflation and reduce unemployment
4. **citizens** to understand news about prices, exchange rates and the budget, and to judge government policy
5. **students** to prepare for careers in banking, accounting, business, law and public administration

:::callout{type=local-example}
When the leone falls against the US dollar, imported goods such as rice, fuel and medicines become dearer in the shops. Economics explains why this happens and what the government and the Bank of Sierra Leone can do about it.
:::

## Key points

- **Microeconomics**: individual units (a consumer, a firm, one market).
- **Macroeconomics**: the whole economy (national income, inflation, unemployment).
- **Positive** statements can be tested against facts; **normative** statements are value judgements.
- Economics helps individuals, businesses and governments make better choices.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect questions that ask you to **distinguish** micro from macro, or positive from normative, **with examples**. Give a definition of each, then at least one example of each, ideally from Sierra Leone. In objective questions, look for *should* or *ought* to spot a normative statement.
:::

## Quick check

1. Is the study of how a single bakery sets its bread price micro or macro?
2. Is "Inflation in Sierra Leone was higher last year than the year before" positive or normative?
3. Give one reason why a government needs economics.

**Answers:** 1. Micro: it studies one firm. 2. Positive: it can be checked against price data. 3. For example, to plan the budget, decide on taxes or control inflation.
