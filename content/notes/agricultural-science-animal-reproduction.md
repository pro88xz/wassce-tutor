A livestock farm grows by breeding: more kids, piglets, calves and chicks each year, and better ones than their parents. Good breeding management means knowing when an animal is ready to mate, recognising the signs of heat, caring for the pregnant female and the newborn, and choosing breeding methods that improve the herd. This lesson covers puberty and the oestrous cycle, signs of heat, mating systems and artificial insemination, gestation and parturition, care of the young, methods of breeding, and the incubation of eggs.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define puberty, oestrous cycle, heat, gestation and parturition
- state the length of the oestrous cycle and gestation period of farm animals
- describe the signs of heat and of approaching parturition
- describe mating systems, including artificial insemination
- describe the care of pregnant animals and newborn young
- explain inbreeding, line breeding, crossbreeding and upgrading
- describe the natural and artificial incubation of eggs

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Puberty**: the age at which an animal becomes able to reproduce.
- **Oestrous cycle**: the regular cycle of reproductive changes in a female, from one heat period to the next.
- **Heat (oestrus)**: the short period when the female is willing to mate and can conceive.
- **Gestation**: the period of pregnancy, from conception to birth.
- **Parturition**: the act of giving birth (calving, kidding, lambing, farrowing, kindling in rabbits).
:::

## Reproductive data

| Animal | Oestrous cycle | Length of heat | Gestation |
|---|---|---|---|
| cow | 21 days | 12–24 hours | about 283 days (9 months) |
| ewe (sheep) | 17 days | 24–36 hours | about 147 days (5 months) |
| doe (goat) | 21 days | 24–48 hours | about 150 days (5 months) |
| sow (pig) | 21 days | 2–3 days | **114 days** (3 months, 3 weeks, 3 days) |
| doe (rabbit) | no regular cycle: ovulation is triggered by mating | | about 31 days |

![Gestation periods: cow 283 days, goat 150, sheep 147, sow 114, rabbit 31 days, and 21 days of incubation for a hen's egg](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/gestation-periods.svg)

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A sow was mated on 10 March. When should the farmer expect her to farrow?**

Gestation is 114 days. Days remaining in March after the 10th: 21. April: 30 (total 51). May: 31 (total 82). June: 30 (total 112). So 112 days takes us to 30 June; 2 more days gives **2 July**.

The farmer should prepare the farrowing pen about a week earlier.
:::

## Signs of heat

- **Restlessness**, frequent bellowing or bleating, reduced appetite.
- **Mounting** other animals, and **standing still** to be mounted (the surest sign).
- **Swollen, reddened vulva** and a **clear mucus discharge**.
- Frequent urination; in cows, a drop in milk yield.
- In sows, **standing firm** when the farmer presses on her back.

## Mating systems

- **Natural mating**: the male serves the female.
  - **flock/herd mating**: the male runs with the females;
  - **pen mating**: a male is put with a selected group of females in a pen;
  - **hand mating**: the female in heat is brought to the male for a single, recorded mating.
- **Artificial insemination (AI)**: semen collected from a high-quality male is stored (often frozen) and placed in the female's reproductive tract by a trained technician.

| Advantages of AI | Disadvantages of AI |
|---|---|
| one superior male can serve **thousands** of females | needs **trained** technicians and equipment |
| no need to keep a male on the farm | semen storage needs liquid nitrogen |
| semen can be transported far and stored for years | heat must be detected accurately |
| reduces spread of diseases passed at mating | poor technique gives low conception rates |

## Pregnancy and parturition

**Care of pregnant animals:** good feeding, especially in late pregnancy (**steaming up**); clean water; gentle handling; exercise; deworming and vaccination as advised; a clean, dry place to give birth.

**Signs of approaching parturition:** the udder enlarges and fills with milk; the vulva swells and relaxes; the muscles around the tail loosen; the animal becomes restless, separates from the herd, and in sows builds a nest; a discharge from the vulva.

### Care of the newborn

- Clear mucus from the **nose and mouth** so it can breathe.
- **Dip the navel** in iodine to prevent infection.
- Make sure it suckles **colostrum** (the first milk) within the first hours: colostrum carries **antibodies** that protect the young from disease, and it is a laxative.
- Keep it **warm and dry**.
- **Piglets**: clip the needle teeth, give an **iron injection** (sow's milk lacks iron), and castrate males not kept for breeding.
- **Wean** at the right age (piglets at about 6–8 weeks).

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Delaying the first feed of colostrum.** The young animal can absorb the antibodies in colostrum only in the **first 24 hours** or so of life. A calf, kid or piglet that misses colostrum is far more likely to die of infections.
:::

## Methods of breeding

| Method | Meaning | Use and effects |
|---|---|---|
| **inbreeding** | mating **closely related** animals (brother and sister, parent and offspring) | fixes desirable traits, but can bring out harmful recessive traits and reduce vigour and fertility |
| **line breeding** | mild inbreeding, keeping animals related to one outstanding ancestor | keeps that ancestor's good traits with less risk |
| **outbreeding / outcrossing** | mating **unrelated** animals of the same breed | adds vigour; brings in new genes |
| **crossbreeding** | mating animals of **different breeds** | combines traits; offspring show **hybrid vigour** (e.g. local × exotic for hardiness plus production) |
| **upgrading (grading up)** | repeatedly mating local females (and their female offspring) to males of an improved breed | gradually raises the herd towards the improved breed |
| **selection** | choosing the best animals as parents and **culling** poor ones | steady improvement over generations |

## Incubation of eggs

**Natural incubation**: a **broody hen** sits on 10–15 eggs and hatches them in **21 days**. Local hens are good mothers.

**Artificial incubation** in an **incubator** needs:

- **temperature** about **37.5 °C**;
- **relative humidity** about 55–60%, raised near hatching;
- **turning** the eggs several times a day (until about day 18), so the embryo does not stick to the shell;
- **ventilation** for oxygen;
- **candling** (shining a light through the egg) at about days 7 and 18 to remove **infertile** eggs and dead embryos.

Choose **clean, fertile** eggs of normal shape and size, not more than about 7 days old, stored cool with the **blunt end up**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Improving village goats.** A farmer group buys or borrows a **well-grown buck** from outside the area and uses it on their West African Dwarf does, culling poor and sickly animals and keeping the best young does for breeding. Changing the buck every two years avoids **inbreeding**. Within a few years their kids grow faster while staying hardy: simple **selection and outbreeding** at work.
:::

**Oestrous cycle**: the regular cycle of reproductive changes in a female from one heat to the next. **Gestation**: the period of pregnancy from conception to birth.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. State the gestation periods of the cow, sow and goat."}

:::

:::reveal{title="3. Give four signs of heat in a cow."}
Any four: restlessness and bellowing; mounting others and standing to be mounted; swollen red vulva; clear mucus discharge; reduced appetite; drop in milk yield.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Why must newborn animals receive colostrum early?"}
Colostrum contains **antibodies** that protect against disease, and the young can absorb them only in the first day or so of life; it is also nutritious and a laxative.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. State four conditions needed in an incubator."}
Temperature about 37.5 °C; humidity about 55–60%; regular turning of eggs; good ventilation (and candling to remove infertile eggs).
:::

## Key terms to revise

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Artificial insemination** (AI): placing semen collected from a high-quality male into the female's reproductive tract.
- **Inbreeding**: mating closely related animals, such as brother and sister or parent and offspring.
- **Line breeding**: mild inbreeding that keeps animals related to one outstanding ancestor.
- **Crossbreeding**: mating animals of different breeds, which combines traits and gives hybrid vigour.
- **Upgrading** (grading up): repeatedly mating local females and their female offspring to males of an improved breed.
:::

## Key points

- **Puberty, oestrous cycle, heat, gestation, parturition**: know the definitions.
- Oestrous cycle **21 days** (cow, goat, sow), **17** (ewe). Gestation: **cow ≈ 283, goat ≈ 150, ewe ≈ 147, sow 114, rabbit ≈ 31 days**; eggs hatch in **21 days**.
- Signs of heat: **mounting, standing, swollen vulva, mucus, restlessness**.
- Mating: **natural** (flock, pen, hand) or **AI**.
- Newborns: clear airways, dip navel, **colostrum** early, warmth; iron for piglets.
- Breeding methods: **inbreeding, line breeding, outbreeding, crossbreeding, upgrading, selection**.
- Incubator: **37.5 °C, humidity, turning, ventilation, candling**.
