Sierra Leone is rich in natural resources: forests, fertile soils, rivers, fish, wildlife, minerals. People depend on them every day for food, water, firewood, building materials, medicine and income. But many are being used faster than they can recover. Forests are cleared for farms and charcoal, fish stocks are overfished, and soils are washed away. **Conservation** is the careful management of natural resources so that they meet today's needs **and** remain for future generations. This lesson explains the types of natural resources, why they need conserving, and the methods used to conserve forests, soil, water, wildlife and fish, with examples from Sierra Leone.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- distinguish renewable and non-renewable natural resources
- explain why conservation is necessary
- describe methods of conserving forests, soil, water, wildlife and fisheries
- name protected areas in Sierra Leone and explain their importance
- describe the roles of government, communities and individuals

## Natural resources

:::callout{type=definition}
A **natural resource** is anything in nature that people use.

- **Renewable** resources can be replaced by natural processes within a human lifetime **if used carefully**: forests, fish, wildlife, soil, fresh water, solar energy.
- **Non-renewable** resources form over millions of years and **cannot** be replaced once used: minerals (diamonds, gold, iron ore, rutile, bauxite), crude oil, coal.

**Conservation** is the wise use and management of natural resources so that they last, and the protection of the variety of life (**biodiversity**).
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Calling forests and fish "unlimited" because they are renewable.** A renewable resource is only renewable when it is used **more slowly than it regrows**. A forest cut faster than it regrows, or fish caught faster than they breed, will run out just like a mineral.
:::

## Why conserve?

- **Food and income**: farming, fishing and forestry support most families.
- **Water supply**: forests on hills protect the catchments of rivers and dams. The forest of the Western Area Peninsula protects the Guma Valley reservoir, which supplies much of Freetown's water.
- **Soil protection**: plant cover holds soil and prevents erosion and landslides.
- **Climate**: forests store carbon and help keep rainfall regular.
- **Biodiversity**: medicines, new crop varieties and ecological balance come from the variety of species.
- **Tourism and education**: wildlife and beautiful places attract visitors and teach.
- **Future generations** have the same right to these resources.

## Threats

| Threat | Effect |
|---|---|
| **deforestation** (farming, logging, charcoal, mining, building) | loss of habitat, soil erosion, flooding, landslides, less rainfall |
| **bush burning** | kills soil organisms, destroys humus, exposes soil to erosion |
| **overgrazing** | bare soil, erosion |
| **overhunting** (bushmeat) | species decline and local extinction |
| **overfishing** and illegal fishing methods | collapse of fish stocks |
| **mining** | pits, polluted rivers, loss of farmland |
| **pollution** | poisoned water, soil and wildlife |

## Methods of conservation

### Forests

- **Protected areas**: forest reserves and national parks where cutting is controlled.
- **Afforestation** (planting trees where there were none) and **reforestation** (replanting after cutting).
- **Selective logging**: cutting only mature trees and leaving young ones.
- **Agroforestry**: growing crops among useful trees.
- Using **fuel-efficient stoves** and alternatives to charcoal.
- **Laws and permits** for logging and charcoal production.

### Soil

- **Contour ploughing** and **ridging across slopes**, not up and down them.
- **Terracing** on steep hillsides.
- **Cover crops** (such as groundnuts and cowpeas) and **mulching** to protect the soil surface.
- **Crop rotation** and adding manure or compost to keep the soil fertile.
- **Avoiding bush burning** and leaving fallow periods long enough.
- **Windbreaks** and planting trees along rivers.

### Water

- Protecting **catchment forests** on hills.
- Preventing pollution of rivers by mining, sewage and waste.
- Harvesting rainwater; repairing leaks; using water carefully.

### Wildlife

- **National parks and sanctuaries** where hunting is banned.
- **Laws**: banning the killing of endangered species, closed hunting seasons, licences.
- **Sanctuaries** for rescue and breeding, such as for orphaned chimpanzees.
- **Education** about the value of wildlife and the risks of bushmeat (including disease).

### Fisheries

- Using nets with a **legal mesh size**, so young fish escape and grow.
- **Closed seasons** when fish breed.
- Banning destructive methods (poisons, dynamite, very fine nets).
- Keeping industrial trawlers out of the inshore zone reserved for local canoe fishers.
- Protecting **mangroves**, which are nurseries for young fish and shrimps.

## Protected areas of Sierra Leone

| Protected area | Notable for |
|---|---|
| **Gola Rainforest National Park** (east, on the border with Liberia) | one of the largest remaining areas of Upper Guinea rainforest; forest elephants, pygmy hippos, chimpanzees and hundreds of bird species |
| **Tiwai Island Wildlife Sanctuary** (Moa River) | a very high density of primates (around 11 species) and pygmy hippos; community-managed |
| **Outamba-Kilimi National Park** (north) | savanna and forest; chimpanzees, hippos and many monkey species |
| **Western Area Peninsula National Park** | forest on the hills above Freetown, protecting the city's water supply |
| **Tacugama Chimpanzee Sanctuary** (in the Western Area forest) | rescue and rehabilitation of orphaned chimpanzees, the national animal |

:::callout{type=local-example}
**The Freetown peninsula.** Houses and farms have spread up the forested hills above Freetown. Clearing this forest increases run-off, washes soil into the Guma reservoir and makes slopes unstable; the deadly 2017 mudslide at Regent showed what can happen. Protecting the Western Area Peninsula forest is therefore about **water and safety** for the capital, not only about wildlife.
:::

## Who conserves?

- **Government**: makes and enforces laws, creates protected areas, plans land use, educates.
- **Communities**: manage local forests and fisheries, set local rules (such as sacred forests and community closed seasons), and benefit from ecotourism.
- **Non-governmental organisations**: support research, tree planting and community projects.
- **Individuals**: avoid bush burning, plant trees, use water and energy carefully, dispose of waste properly, refuse to buy endangered species.

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Distinguish renewable from non-renewable resources, with two examples of each."}
Renewable resources can be replaced naturally if used carefully (forests, fish, soil, water). Non-renewable resources cannot be replaced once used up (diamonds, iron ore, gold, crude oil).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Give three methods of soil conservation."}
Any three: contour ploughing, terracing, cover crops, mulching, crop rotation, adding manure, avoiding bush burning, windbreaks.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Why are mangroves important for fisheries?"}
They are **nurseries**: young fish, shrimps and crabs shelter and feed among the roots before moving to open water. Destroying mangroves reduces catches.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Name two protected areas in Sierra Leone and one animal found in each."}
For example: Gola Rainforest National Park (forest elephant, pygmy hippo, chimpanzee); Tiwai Island (primates such as colobus monkeys, pygmy hippo); Outamba-Kilimi (chimpanzee, hippo).
:::

:::reveal{title="5. How does a legal mesh size help conserve fish?"}
Young, small fish pass through the larger mesh, survive and **reach breeding age**, so the stock is replaced.
:::

## Key points

- **Renewable** (forests, fish, soil, water) vs **non-renewable** (minerals, oil).
- Conservation keeps resources and **biodiversity** for the future.
- Threats: deforestation, bush burning, overhunting, overfishing, mining, pollution.
- Methods: protected areas, replanting, contour farming, cover crops, closed seasons, mesh sizes, laws and education.
- Protected areas include **Gola, Tiwai, Outamba-Kilimi and the Western Area Peninsula**.

Questions often ask for a named example; local examples such as Gola or Tiwai are acceptable. Give each method with a short reason for how it conserves.
