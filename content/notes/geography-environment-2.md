**Climate change** is a long-term change in the Earth's climate, especially the rise in average temperatures, known as **global warming**, caused mainly by human activities that add **greenhouse gases** to the atmosphere. **Sustainable development** is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. West Africa is among the regions most exposed to climate change, through **rising seas**, **floods**, **droughts** and **heat**. This lesson explains the greenhouse effect, the causes and effects of climate change, the responses to it, and the meaning and practice of sustainable development.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the greenhouse effect and global warming
- explain the causes of climate change
- describe the effects of climate change on West Africa and Sierra Leone
- distinguish mitigation from adaptation, with examples
- define sustainable development and describe the Sustainable Development Goals
- explain ways of conserving the environment

## The greenhouse effect

![Sunlight passes through the atmosphere and warms the ground; heat from the ground is trapped and sent back down by greenhouse gases; burning fuel and deforestation add carbon dioxide](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/greenhouse-effect.svg)

*Figure 1. The greenhouse effect and global warming. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

1. Sunlight passes through the atmosphere and warms the Earth's surface.
2. The warm Earth gives off **heat (infrared radiation)**.
3. **Greenhouse gases**, mainly **carbon dioxide (CO₂)**, **methane**, **nitrous oxide** and **water vapour**, absorb some of this heat and send it back towards the surface.
4. This natural **greenhouse effect** keeps the Earth warm enough for life.
5. Adding **more** greenhouse gases traps **more** heat, causing **global warming**.

## Causes of climate change

| Human activity | Greenhouse gas added |
|---|---|
| **Burning fossil fuels** (coal, oil, gas) in power stations, vehicles, generators and factories | carbon dioxide |
| **Deforestation** and bush burning: fewer trees absorb CO₂, and burning releases it | carbon dioxide |
| **Rice paddies, cattle and rubbish dumps** | methane |
| **Fertilisers** | nitrous oxide |
| **Cement making and industry** | carbon dioxide |

Natural factors such as volcanic eruptions and changes in the Sun's output also affect climate, but scientists agree that the warming since the mid-20th century is mainly caused by human activities.

## Effects of climate change on West Africa

1. **Rising sea levels** threaten low-lying coasts and cities such as Lagos, Banjul, Freetown and Bonthe, causing coastal erosion and flooding.
2. **Heavier, more intense rainfall** and **floods**.
3. **Longer droughts** and more unreliable rains in the Sahel, worsening desertification.
4. **Higher temperatures** and heatwaves.
5. **Lower crop yields** and food insecurity.
6. **Spread of diseases** such as malaria into new areas.
7. **Loss of biodiversity**, including damage to mangroves.
8. **Migration** and conflict over scarce land and water.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Sierra Leone** contributes very little to global greenhouse gas emissions but is highly **vulnerable** to climate change. Freetown faces more severe floods and landslides in the rainy season, and coastal communities such as **Bonthe** on Sherbro Island and parts of the Freetown Peninsula face **coastal erosion and rising seas**. Changing rainfall patterns also affect farmers who depend on rain-fed upland rice.
:::

## Responses to climate change

| Mitigation (reducing the causes) | Adaptation (coping with the effects) |
|---|---|
| using **renewable energy** (solar, hydro, wind) | **sea walls** and protecting mangroves |
| **planting trees** and protecting forests | **drought-resistant** and early-maturing crops |
| energy efficiency, cleaner vehicles and stoves | better **drainage** and early-warning systems |
| reducing gas flaring and waste burning | not building on floodplains and steep slopes |
| | **irrigation** and water storage |

**International agreements:**

- The **Kyoto Protocol** (**1997**) set targets for richer countries to cut emissions.
- The **Paris Agreement** (**2015**) commits countries to keep global warming **well below 2 °C** above pre-industrial levels and to try to limit it to **1.5 °C**.

## Sustainable development

:::callout{type=definition}
**Sustainable development** is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. This definition comes from the **Brundtland Report** (*Our Common Future*), published in **1987**.
:::

Sustainable development balances three things: **economic growth**, **social well-being** and **environmental protection**.

In **2015**, the United Nations adopted **17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)**, to be achieved by **2030**. They include ending poverty and hunger, good health, quality education, clean water and sanitation, affordable clean energy, **climate action** (Goal 13), **life below water** (Goal 14) and **life on land** (Goal 15).

## Conserving the environment

1. **Afforestation and reforestation**; protecting forest reserves and national parks.
2. **Laws and enforcement**: Sierra Leone's **Environment Protection Agency (EPA-SL)** requires environmental impact assessments for big projects.
3. **Environmental education** in schools and communities.
4. **Waste management:** reduce, reuse, recycle; proper landfills.
5. **Renewable energy** and efficient cookstoves.
6. **Sustainable farming:** agroforestry, crop rotation, contour ploughing, mulching.
7. **Controlled mining** with land reclamation.
8. **Protecting mangroves**, wetlands and fisheries.
9. **Community participation**, such as tree-planting campaigns and clean-up days.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The greenhouse effect itself is **natural and necessary**: without it the Earth would be too cold for life. The problem is the **enhanced** greenhouse effect caused by extra gases from human activity.
:::

## Key points

- **Greenhouse gases** (CO₂, methane, nitrous oxide, water vapour) trap heat; more gases cause **global warming**.
- Causes: **fossil fuels, deforestation, agriculture, industry**.
- Effects in West Africa: **rising seas, floods, droughts, heat, crop failure, disease, migration**.
- **Mitigation** cuts emissions; **adaptation** copes with effects; **Kyoto (1997)**, **Paris (2015)**.
- **Sustainable development** (Brundtland, **1987**); **17 SDGs** (2015–2030).

## Quick check

1. Name two greenhouse gases.
2. How does deforestation contribute to global warming?
3. Give three effects of climate change on West Africa.
4. Distinguish mitigation from adaptation.
5. Define sustainable development.

**Answers:**

1. Any two: carbon dioxide; methane; nitrous oxide; water vapour.
2. Fewer trees absorb carbon dioxide, and burning or rotting trees release it.
3. Any three: rising sea levels; floods; droughts; higher temperatures; lower crop yields; spread of diseases; biodiversity loss; migration and conflict.
4. Mitigation reduces the causes of climate change (cutting emissions); adaptation helps people cope with its effects.
5. Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
