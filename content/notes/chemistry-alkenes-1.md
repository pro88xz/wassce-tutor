Put an unripe banana in a closed bag with a ripe one and it ripens faster. The ripe fruit gives off **ethene**, the simplest **alkene**. Alkenes contain a carbon–carbon **double bond**, which makes them far more reactive than alkanes and makes them the starting point for plastics, ethanol and many other products.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the general formula of the alkenes and name the first members
- describe the structure and shape of the C=C double bond
- draw and name the isomers of butene, including cis–trans isomers
- describe the laboratory preparation of ethene
- state the sources of alkenes and the physical properties of ethene

## Structure and names

:::callout{type=definition}
**Alkenes** are **unsaturated hydrocarbons** that contain **one carbon–carbon double bond (C=C)**. General formula: **CₙH₂ₙ**.
:::

| Name | Molecular formula | Condensed formula | Boiling point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ethene** | C₂H₄ | CH₂=CH₂ | −104 °C |
| **Propene** | C₃H₆ | CH₃CH=CH₂ | −48 °C |
| **But-1-ene** | C₄H₈ | CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ | −6 °C |
| **But-2-ene** | C₄H₈ | CH₃CH=CHCH₃ | 1–4 °C (cis and trans) |
| **Pent-1-ene** | C₅H₁₀ | CH₂=CH(CH₂)₂CH₃ | 30 °C |

There is no "methene": a double bond needs at least **two** carbon atoms.

**The C=C bond.** The double bond is made of one strong **σ (sigma) bond** and one weaker **π (pi) bond**. The π bond breaks easily, which is why alkenes undergo **addition reactions**.

**Shape.** The two carbons of the double bond and the four atoms attached to them all lie in **one plane**, with bond angles of about **120°** (sp² hybridisation). The π bond **prevents rotation** about the C=C bond.

## Isomerism in alkenes

C₄H₈ has these alkene isomers:

| Structure | Name | Type of isomerism |
|---|---|---|
| CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ | **But-1-ene** | Position isomer of but-2-ene |
| CH₃CH=CHCH₃ (both CH₃ on the same side) | **cis-But-2-ene** | Geometric (cis) |
| CH₃CH=CHCH₃ (CH₃ groups on opposite sides) | **trans-But-2-ene** | Geometric (trans) |
| CH₂=C(CH₃)CH₃ | **2-Methylpropene** | Chain isomer |

**Cis–trans isomerism** needs **two different groups on each carbon** of the double bond. Propene (CH₃CH=CH₂) has two H atoms on one carbon, so it has no cis–trans isomers.

## Laboratory preparation of ethene

![Ethanol and excess concentrated sulphuric acid heated in a flask at 170 °C; the gas passes through sodium hydroxide solution and is collected over water](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/ethene-prep.svg)

*Figure 1. Preparing ethene by dehydrating ethanol. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

Heat **ethanol** with **excess concentrated sulphuric acid** at about **170 °C**. The acid acts as a **dehydrating agent**, removing water from ethanol:

$$\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4,\ 170\,^\circ\text{C}} \text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

- The gas is passed through **sodium hydroxide solution** to remove **sulphur(IV) oxide and carbon(IV) oxide**, formed when the acid oxidises some ethanol.
- Ethene is collected **over water**, as it is only slightly soluble.
- Broken porcelain (or sand) is added so that the mixture boils smoothly.
- If the temperature is only about 140 °C, the main product is **ethoxyethane** (an ether), not ethene.

**Alternative method:** pass **ethanol vapour over heated aluminium oxide** (or broken porcelain) at about 350 °C:

$$\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3,\ 350\,^\circ\text{C}} \text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

**Industrial source:** almost all ethene and propene are made by **cracking** naphtha or ethane from crude oil and natural gas (see the Petroleum topic).

## Physical properties of ethene

- Colourless gas with a faint sweet smell
- Slightly less dense than air
- Almost insoluble in water; soluble in organic solvents
- Burns with a **luminous, slightly smoky flame**, because it has a higher proportion of carbon than ethane

$$\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + 3\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Percentage of carbon.** Compare ethane and ethene (C = 12, H = 1).

- Ethane, C₂H₆ (Mᵣ = 30): % C $= 24 \div 30 \times 100 = 80\%$
- Ethene, C₂H₄ (Mᵣ = 28): % C $= 24 \div 28 \times 100 = 85.7\%$

The higher carbon content of ethene explains its **smokier flame**.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Traders at markets such as Dove Cot or Bo's main market sometimes ripen **bananas, plantains and mangoes** by wrapping them with a ripe fruit or storing them in closed sacks. The ripe fruit releases **ethene**, a plant hormone, which speeds up ripening of the rest. Commercial ripening rooms use controlled amounts of ethene gas.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The temperature matters in the ethanol and sulphuric acid reaction. At **170 °C** (excess acid) ethene is formed; at **140 °C** (excess ethanol) ethoxyethane is formed instead.
:::

## Key points

- **Alkenes**, CₙH₂ₙ, contain one **C=C** (a σ and a π bond); they are **unsaturated**.
- Atoms around the C=C are **planar** with angles of about **120°**; there is **no rotation** about the double bond, which allows **cis–trans isomerism**.
- **Ethene** is prepared by **dehydrating ethanol** with excess **conc. H₂SO₄ at 170 °C** (or over hot Al₂O₃); impurities are removed with NaOH(aq); collected over water.
- Industrially, alkenes come from **cracking**.
- Ethene burns with a smokier flame than ethane (higher % carbon).

## Quick check

1. Write the general formula of the alkenes.
2. What is the role of concentrated sulphuric acid in the preparation of ethene?
3. Why is the gas passed through sodium hydroxide solution?
4. Why does propene not show cis–trans isomerism?

**Answers:**

1. CₙH₂ₙ.
2. It is a dehydrating agent: it removes water from ethanol.
3. To remove the acidic impurities sulphur(IV) oxide and carbon(IV) oxide.
4. One carbon of its double bond carries two identical groups (two hydrogen atoms).
