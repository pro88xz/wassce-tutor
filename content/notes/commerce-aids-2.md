Aids to trade rarely work alone. A single delivery of imported cement to a builder in Makeni uses ships, ports, warehouses, banks, insurers, phones and lorries. This lesson follows goods through the whole chain, compares the aids, and looks at how modern technology is changing them.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain how the aids to trade work together in a real transaction
- explain the relationship between trade and aids to trade
- describe newer aids to trade such as mobile money, the internet and trade fairs
- discuss the problems facing aids to trade in Sierra Leone and suggest solutions

## The aids working together

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Importing a consignment of cement.** A builders' merchant in Makeni orders 500 bags of cement from a supplier abroad.

| Step | What happens | Aid to trade |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The merchant emails the supplier for prices and terms | Communication |
| 2 | The merchant's bank opens a letter of credit to guarantee payment | Banking |
| 3 | The cement is insured against loss at sea | Insurance |
| 4 | It is shipped to Freetown and unloaded at the Queen Elizabeth II Quay | Transport |
| 5 | It is stored in a bonded or port warehouse until customs duty is paid | Warehousing |
| 6 | A lorry carries it to Makeni, insured in transit | Transport, insurance |
| 7 | The merchant advertises on local radio and sells to builders | Advertising, retail trade |
| 8 | Builders pay by mobile money or bank transfer | Banking |

Remove any one of these aids, and the trade becomes slow, risky or impossible.
:::

## Trade and aids to trade: how they depend on each other

- **Trade creates the demand for aids**: the more goods are bought and sold, the more transport, storage, insurance and finance are needed.
- **Aids make trade possible and cheaper**: good roads, banks and communication lower the cost of trading and widen markets.
- **Aids depend on each other**: banks lend more readily on insured goods; transport firms need communication to plan deliveries; advertising needs reliable transport so that goods are in the shops when buyers come.

## Newer aids to trade

| Aid | How it helps trade |
|---|---|
| **Mobile money** (for example Orange Money and Afrimoney) | Fast, cheap payments and transfers without a bank account, even in rural areas |
| **The internet and social media** | Traders advertise on WhatsApp and Facebook, take orders online and compare prices |
| **E-banking and point-of-sale (POS) terminals** | Card and phone payments in shops; less need to carry cash |
| **Courier services** | Fast delivery of documents and small parcels |
| **Trade fairs and exhibitions** | Producers display goods to many buyers at once; new products are launched |
| **Containerisation** | Goods are packed in standard containers, reducing damage, theft and handling costs |
| **Tourism and hospitality** | Hotels and travel services support business travel and earn foreign exchange |

:::callout{type=local-example}
A pepper trader in Kambia can now take orders by phone from Freetown, receive payment by mobile money before loading, and send the goods on a commercial vehicle the same day. Ten years ago, the same trade needed a trip to the city and cash carried by hand.
:::

## Comparing the aids

| Aid | Utility created | Main beneficiaries |
|---|---|---|
| Transport | Place utility | Producers, traders, consumers |
| Warehousing | Time utility | Producers with seasonal output, wholesalers |
| Insurance | Security against loss | Traders, transporters, property owners |
| Banking | Finance and safe payment | All businesses and households |
| Advertising | Information; increased demand | Producers and consumers |
| Communication | Fast exchange of information | All traders |

## Problems facing aids to trade in Sierra Leone

1. **Poor roads**, especially in the rainy season, which raise transport costs and cause delays.
2. **Unreliable electricity**, which affects cold storage, banking systems and telecom masts.
3. **Low use of insurance**: many traders do not insure, through lack of awareness or distrust.
4. **Limited banking** in rural areas, and high interest rates on loans.
5. **High cost of advertising** for small businesses.
6. **Network failures and high data costs** for communication.
7. **Shortage of modern warehouses**, leading to waste of perishable goods.

**Solutions:** investment in roads and power; insurance awareness campaigns and micro-insurance; expansion of community banks, microfinance and mobile money; cheaper data and wider network coverage; cold stores and warehouses in producing areas; cheaper advertising through social media.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
When asked how the aids "work together", do not simply list them. Describe a **sequence**, as in the cement example, showing how each aid supports the others.
:::

## Key points

- Aids to trade **work together**; a single import may use all six.
- Trade **creates the demand** for aids, and aids **make trade possible and cheaper**.
- **Mobile money, the internet, POS terminals, couriers, trade fairs and containers** are newer aids.
- Problems in Sierra Leone include **poor roads and power, low insurance use, limited rural banking and costly communication**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::
