:::callout{type=definition}
The **Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS)** is a regional organisation founded on **28 May 1975** by the **Treaty of Lagos** to promote **economic integration**, **free movement** of people and goods, and **peace and security** in West Africa.
:::

Its headquarters, the **ECOWAS Commission**, is in **Abuja**, Nigeria. It now has **twelve members**: Benin, Cabo Verde, Côte d'Ivoire, The Gambia, Ghana, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Liberia, Nigeria, Senegal, **Sierra Leone** and Togo, after **Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger** left in **January 2025**. This lesson covers the members, aims, institutions, achievements and problems of ECOWAS.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state when and how ECOWAS was formed and name its members
- explain the aims of ECOWAS
- describe the main institutions of ECOWAS
- describe the achievements of ECOWAS
- explain the problems facing ECOWAS
- explain the benefits of ECOWAS to Sierra Leone

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **28 May 1975** | **Treaty of Lagos** signed by 15 West African states |
| **1977** | **Cabo Verde** joins, making 16 members |
| **1993** | **Revised Treaty** signed in **Cotonou**, widening ECOWAS's aims to include political cooperation and peace |
| **2000** | **Mauritania** withdraws |
| **January 2025** | **Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger** leave, having formed the **Alliance of Sahel States** after military coups |

## Aims of ECOWAS

1. Promote **economic cooperation and integration** in agriculture, industry, transport, energy, trade and finance.
2. Create a **common market** and a **customs union** with a common external tariff.
3. Allow **free movement** of people, goods, services and capital.
4. Build **regional infrastructure**: roads, power, telecommunications.
5. Raise **living standards** and promote development.
6. Maintain **peace, security and stability**, and promote **democracy and good governance**.
7. Work towards a **single currency**.

## Institutions

| Institution | Role |
|---|---|
| **Authority of Heads of State and Government** | the supreme body; meets at summits and elects a chairman |
| **Council of Ministers** | ministers who supervise the community's work |
| **ECOWAS Commission** (Abuja) | the secretariat that runs daily affairs, headed by a **President** |
| **Community Parliament** (Abuja) | representatives of member states' parliaments |
| **Community Court of Justice** (Abuja) | settles disputes and hears human-rights cases |
| **ECOWAS Bank for Investment and Development** (Lomé) | finances development projects |
| **ECOMOG** | the ceasefire monitoring group (peacekeeping force) |

## Achievements

1. **Free movement:** citizens can visit other member states **without a visa** for up to 90 days; the **ECOWAS passport** and ECOWAS identity card.
2. **Peacekeeping:** **ECOMOG** intervened in **Liberia** (from 1990) and **Sierra Leone**, where it helped restore the elected government in **1998**, and in **Guinea-Bissau**; ECOWAS forces also ensured the transfer of power in **The Gambia** in **2017**.
3. **Trade:** the **ECOWAS Trade Liberalisation Scheme** reduces tariffs on goods made in the region; a **Common External Tariff** started in **2015**.
4. **Infrastructure:** coastal and trans-Sahelian highway projects, the **West African Gas Pipeline**, and the **West African Power Pool** linking national grids, including the **CLSG** line to Sierra Leone.
5. **Democracy:** protocols on democracy and good governance; election observation missions.
6. **Health and agriculture** programmes, such as joint action against epidemics.

:::callout{type=local-example}
ECOWAS matters greatly to **Sierra Leone**: ECOMOG troops helped to end the civil war and restore the elected government in 1998; Sierra Leoneans can travel and trade across the region without visas; and regional power lines and roads link the country to its neighbours. In **June 2025**, Sierra Leone's President **Julius Maada Bio** was elected **Chairman of the ECOWAS Authority of Heads of State and Government**.
:::

## Problems of ECOWAS

1. **Political instability and coups**, such as those in Mali, Guinea, Burkina Faso and Niger, and the **withdrawal** of three members in 2025.
2. **Language differences:** English, French and Portuguese.
3. **Different currencies**: five members (Benin, Côte d'Ivoire, Guinea-Bissau, Senegal and Togo) use the **CFA franc** of the WAEMU, while the others have their own currencies; the planned single currency, the **eco**, has been repeatedly delayed.
4. **Similar products:** members export similar raw materials, so **trade between them is low**.
5. **Poor transport and communication links.**
6. **Border harassment**, illegal charges and delays despite free-movement rules.
7. **Unpaid contributions** and lack of funds.
8. **Fear of domination** by large members such as Nigeria.
9. **Loyalty to other groups** and to former colonial powers.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
ECOWAS is not the same as **West Africa**. Mauritania is in West Africa but left ECOWAS in 2000, and Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger left in 2025. Check the date of any membership list you learn.
:::

## Key points

- **ECOWAS** founded on **28 May 1975** (**Treaty of Lagos**); revised treaty **1993**; headquarters in **Abuja**.
- Now **12 members** after **Mauritania** (2000) and **Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger** (2025) left.
- Aims: **economic integration, free movement, infrastructure, peace and security, single currency**.
- Achievements: **visa-free travel, ECOMOG peacekeeping, trade scheme, common external tariff, gas pipeline, power pool**.
- Problems: **coups and withdrawals, languages, currencies, low trade, transport, border delays, funding**.

## Quick check

1. When and where was the ECOWAS treaty signed?
2. Where is the ECOWAS Commission located?
3. Name the three countries that left ECOWAS in 2025.
4. Give two achievements of ECOWAS.
5. State three problems facing ECOWAS.

**Answers:**

1. On 28 May 1975, in Lagos, Nigeria.
2. Abuja, Nigeria.
3. Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger.
4. Any two: visa-free movement; the ECOWAS passport; ECOMOG peacekeeping in Liberia and Sierra Leone; trade liberalisation; the common external tariff; the West African Gas Pipeline; the West African Power Pool.
5. Any three: coups and instability; withdrawals; language differences; different currencies; low intra-regional trade; poor transport; border harassment; unpaid contributions; fear of domination.
