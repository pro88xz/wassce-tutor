**History** is the study of **past human activities and events**, based on **evidence**, to explain **what happened, why it happened and what its results were**. Historians reconstruct the past from **sources**: **written sources** such as chronicles, letters and government records, and **unwritten sources** such as **oral tradition**, **archaeology**, **linguistics** and **artefacts**. In West Africa, where much of the past was recorded orally rather than in writing, unwritten sources are especially important. This lesson explains the meaning and importance of history and the sources historians use.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define history and explain its scope
- explain the importance of studying history
- distinguish primary from secondary sources
- describe the written and unwritten sources of West African history
- explain the strengths and weaknesses of oral tradition and archaeology

## Meaning of history

:::callout{type=definition}
**History** is the systematic study of the **past of human societies**, using **evidence** to describe and explain events, people, institutions and changes over time.
:::

History is not just a list of dates. A historian asks:

- **What** happened?
- **When** and **where** did it happen?
- **Why** did it happen (causes)?
- **What** were its **effects** (consequences)?
- **How do we know?** (evidence)

## Importance of studying history

1. It helps us **understand the present** by showing how it grew out of the past.
2. It builds **national identity and pride**, such as knowing about resistance leaders like Bai Bureh.
3. It teaches **lessons** from past successes and mistakes, such as the causes of conflict.
4. It develops **critical thinking**: weighing evidence and detecting bias.
5. It promotes **unity and tolerance** by explaining the shared past of different peoples.
6. It prepares people for careers in **law, journalism, teaching, politics, diplomacy and tourism**.
7. It **preserves culture**: traditions, languages and heritage sites.

## Primary and secondary sources

| Primary sources | Secondary sources |
|---|---|
| produced **at the time** of the events, or by eyewitnesses | produced **later**, by people who studied primary sources |
| letters, treaties, diaries, official records, photographs, artefacts, eyewitness accounts | textbooks, history books, articles, documentaries |
| first-hand, but may be biased or incomplete | wider view, but depend on the quality of the primary sources |

## Sources of West African history

![Sources of history divided into written sources (Arabic chronicles and travellers' accounts, European records, colonial archives and newspapers) and unwritten sources (oral tradition, archaeology, linguistics and place names, art and artefacts)](https://wasscetutor.com/img/history/sources-of-history.svg)

*Figure 1. Written and unwritten sources of West African history. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Source | Examples | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Oral tradition** | stories, genealogies, praise songs kept by **griots** and elders | preserves history not written down; gives the people's own view | may change over time; may glorify rulers; hard to date |
| **Archaeology** | excavations at **Kumbi Saleh**, Jenne-Jeno and Igbo-Ukwu | physical evidence; can be dated scientifically (radiocarbon) | expensive; objects do not explain themselves |
| **Arabic writings** | **al-Bakri** (1068) on Ghana; **Ibn Battuta** on Mali (1352–53); the **Tarikh al-Sudan** of Timbuktu | contemporary descriptions of Sudanese states | written by outsiders or court scholars; may be biased |
| **European records** | Portuguese accounts, ships' logs, company letters, missionary and colonial reports | detailed, dated records from the 15th century on | often biased against Africans; mainly about the coast |
| **Linguistics** | similarities between languages | shows migrations and contacts | gives no exact dates |
| **Art and artefacts** | Nok terracottas, Ife heads, Benin bronzes | show skill, religion and wealth | need other sources to explain them |
| **Ethnography, place names** | customs, institutions, town names | link present customs to the past | customs change |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Much of Sierra Leone's early history comes from **oral traditions** of the Temne, Mende, Limba, Kono and other peoples, together with **Portuguese records** from the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries and later **British colonial archives** in Freetown. Historians cross-check these sources to build a reliable picture.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not treat any single source as automatically true. Good historians **compare several sources**, ask **who** produced each one and **why**, and look for **bias**.
:::

## Key figures and terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figures and terms"}
- **Primary source**: evidence produced at the time of the events or by an eyewitness, such as a letter, a treaty, a chronicle or an artefact.
- **Secondary source**: an account produced later by people who studied the primary sources, such as a textbook or a history book.
- **Oral tradition**: history passed down by word of mouth, such as the stories, genealogies and praise songs kept by griots and elders.
- **Archaeology**: the study of the past through material remains dug from the ground, such as tools, pottery and the ruins of towns like Kumbi Saleh.
:::

## Key points

- **History** studies the human past using **evidence**: what, when, why and with what effects.
- It builds **understanding, identity, critical thinking and unity**.
- **Primary** sources date from the time; **secondary** sources are later interpretations.
- West African sources: **oral tradition, archaeology, Arabic writings, European records, linguistics, art**.
- Every source has **strengths and weaknesses**, so sources must be cross-checked.

## Quick check

1. Define history.
2. Give two differences between primary and secondary sources.
3. Who are griots?
4. State one advantage and one disadvantage of oral tradition.
5. Name one Arab writer who described a West African state.

**Answers:**

1. The systematic study of the past of human societies, using evidence to describe and explain events and changes.
2. Any two: primary sources date from the time of the events, secondary sources are produced later; primary sources are first-hand, secondary sources interpret them; examples differ (letters and treaties versus textbooks).
3. Professional oral historians and praise singers who preserve and recite a people's history, genealogies and traditions.
4. Advantage: preserves history that was never written, from the people's own view. Disadvantage: may change or exaggerate over time, and is hard to date.
5. Any one: al-Bakri; Ibn Battuta; al-Idrisi; the authors of the Tarikh al-Sudan.
