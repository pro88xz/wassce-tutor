A shop marks a phone down by 20%. Goods and services tax of 15% is added to a bill. Rice rises in price from NLe 250 to NLe 300 a cup. Every one of these needs a percentage calculation, and the fastest, most reliable tool for all of them is the **multiplier**. This lesson covers percentages of quantities, percentage change, reverse percentages and percentage error.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- find a percentage of a quantity
- express one quantity as a percentage of another
- increase or decrease a quantity by a percentage using a multiplier
- calculate percentage change and reverse percentages
- calculate percentage error

## Percentage of a quantity

$$r\% \text{ of } Q = \frac{r}{100} \times Q$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Find 35% of NLe 2,400.**

$\dfrac{35}{100} \times 2\,400 = 840$. So 35% of NLe 2,400 is NLe 840.
:::

## One quantity as a percentage of another

$$\frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}} \times 100\%$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A student scores 45 out of 60. Express this as a percentage.**

$\dfrac{45}{60} \times 100 = 75\%$.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Both quantities must be in the **same units**. 30 minutes as a percentage of 2 hours is $\tfrac{30}{120} \times 100 = 25\%$, not $\tfrac{30}{2} \times 100$.
:::

## Increase and decrease with multipliers

![Bars for 100%, 115% and 80%, showing NLe 400 increased by 15% to NLe 460 and decreased by 20% to NLe 320](https://wasscetutor.com/img/mathematics/percent-multipliers.svg)

*Figure 1. Percentage change as a single multiplication. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

- Increase by $r\%$: multiply by $\dfrac{100 + r}{100}$ (for example, $\times 1.15$ for a 15% increase).
- Decrease by $r\%$: multiply by $\dfrac{100 - r}{100}$ (for example, $\times 0.80$ for a 20% decrease).

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**(a) Increase NLe 400 by 15%. (b) Decrease NLe 400 by 20%.**

**(a)** $400 \times 1.15 = $ NLe 460 **(b)** $400 \times 0.80 = $ NLe 320
:::

### Successive changes

Multiply the multipliers. A 10% increase followed by a 10% decrease is $\times 1.1 \times 0.9 = \times 0.99$: an overall **1% decrease**, not "no change".

## Percentage change

$$\text{percentage change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100\%$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**(a) A cup of rice rises from NLe 250 to NLe 300. (b) A price falls from NLe 80 to NLe 68. Find the percentage changes.**

**(a)** $\dfrac{300 - 250}{250} \times 100 = 20\%$ increase.

**(b)** $\dfrac{68 - 80}{80} \times 100 = -15\%$, a 15% decrease.
:::

## Reverse percentages

When you know the amount **after** a change, divide by the multiplier to find the original.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**In Sierra Leone, goods and services tax (GST) is 15%. A bill including GST is NLe 690. What was the price before GST?**

Price with GST $= 1.15 \times$ original, so the original is $690 \div 1.15 = $ NLe 600.

(Taking 15% **off** NLe 690 gives NLe 586.50, which is wrong: the 15% was calculated on the original price, not on NLe 690.)
:::

## Percentage error

$$\text{percentage error} = \frac{\text{error}}{\text{actual value}} \times 100\%$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A line 5 cm long is measured as 4.8 cm. Find the percentage error.**

Error $= 5 - 4.8 = 0.2$ cm. Percentage error $= \dfrac{0.2}{5} \times 100 = 4\%$.
:::

## Key points

- $r\%$ of $Q = \dfrac{r}{100} \times Q$; part as a percentage $= \dfrac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}} \times 100$.
- **Multipliers:** increase $\times \dfrac{100 + r}{100}$, decrease $\times \dfrac{100 - r}{100}$; multiply them for successive changes.
- **Percentage change** uses the **old** value as the base.
- **Reverse percentage:** divide by the multiplier.
- **Percentage error** $= \dfrac{\text{error}}{\text{actual}} \times 100$.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect **percentage increase and decrease**, **reverse percentage** questions (prices before tax or discount), **successive percentage changes**, and **percentage error** in measurement. Read carefully which value the percentage is "of".
:::

## Practice

Try each question first, then open the answer to check your working.

**1.** Find 12.5% of NLe 6,400.

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
NLe 800.
:::

**2.** Express 18 cm as a percentage of 1.2 m.

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
$\tfrac{18}{120} \times 100 = 15\%$.
:::

**3.** Decrease NLe 2,500 by 8%.

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
$2\,500 \times 0.92 = $ NLe 2,300.
:::

**4.** After a 25% increase, a price is NLe 1,500. Find the original price.

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
$1\,500 \div 1.25 = $ NLe 1,200.
:::

**5.** The true mass of a bag is 50 kg but a scale reads 51.5 kg. Find the percentage error.

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
$\tfrac{1.5}{50} \times 100 = 3\%$.
:::
