When you deposit NLe 1,000 in a bank, the bank does not keep it all in a drawer. It lends most of it out, and that loan becomes someone else's deposit. In this way banks create money. This lesson explains how commercial banks do it, how the Bank of Sierra Leone controls them, and the other institutions of the financial system.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the functions of commercial banks
- explain how commercial banks create credit, and calculate the credit multiplier
- state the functions of a central bank and its instruments of monetary policy
- describe other financial institutions, and distinguish the money market from the capital market

## Commercial banks

:::callout{type=definition}
A **commercial bank** is a financial institution that accepts deposits from the public and lends money to individuals and businesses, aiming to make a profit.
:::

Examples in Sierra Leone include Sierra Leone Commercial Bank, Rokel Commercial Bank and several foreign-owned banks.

**Functions of commercial banks:**

1. **Accepting deposits**: current (demand) accounts, savings accounts and fixed (time) deposits.
2. **Lending**: loans, overdrafts and advances to individuals and firms.
3. **Creating credit** (explained below).
4. **Making payments**: cheques, transfers, cards and mobile banking.
5. **Agency services**: paying bills, buying shares, collecting payments for customers.
6. **Foreign exchange**: buying and selling foreign currency for traders and travellers.
7. **Safe keeping** of valuables and documents.
8. **Financial advice** to customers.

## How banks create credit

Experience shows that depositors withdraw only a small part of their deposits at any time. A bank must keep a fraction as cash reserves (the **cash reserve ratio**, set by the central bank) and can lend the rest. Each loan is spent and deposited in another bank, which lends part of it again.

![Bar chart of successive deposits with a 20 per cent reserve ratio: NLe 1,000 in the first bank, then 800, 640, 512, 410 and 328 in later banks, each keeping 20 per cent as reserves; total deposits reach NLe 5,000](https://wasscetutor.com/img/economics/credit-creation.svg)

*Figure 1. Credit creation with a 20% cash reserve ratio. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A new deposit of NLe 1,000 is made. The cash reserve ratio is 20%.**

- Bank 1 keeps NLe 200 and lends NLe 800, which is deposited in Bank 2.
- Bank 2 keeps NLe 160 and lends NLe 640, and so on.

$$\text{credit multiplier} = \dfrac{1}{\text{cash reserve ratio}} = \dfrac{1}{0.2} = 5$$

Total deposits $= 1000 \times 5 = $ **NLe 5,000**. New credit (loans) created $= 5000 - 1000 = $ **NLe 4,000**.
:::

**Limits to credit creation:** the **cash reserve ratio** (a higher ratio means less lending); the amount of **cash** banks hold; the **demand for loans** by creditworthy borrowers; the availability of **security (collateral)**; people's habit of **holding cash** instead of depositing it (cash drained out of the banking system cannot be lent again); and **central bank controls**.

## The central bank

:::callout{type=definition}
A **central bank** is the bank at the top of a country's banking system. It issues the currency, acts as banker to the government and to commercial banks, and controls the supply of money and credit. Sierra Leone's central bank is the **Bank of Sierra Leone**, established in 1964.
:::

**Functions of the central bank:**

1. **Issuing currency** (notes and coins).
2. **Banker to the government**: keeps its accounts, lends to it and manages the public debt.
3. **Banker to commercial banks**: holds their reserves and settles payments between them.
4. **Lender of last resort** to banks in difficulty.
5. **Controlling the money supply and credit** (monetary policy) to keep prices stable.
6. **Managing foreign exchange reserves** and the exchange rate.
7. **Supervising and regulating** banks and other financial institutions.
8. **Advising the government** on economic policy.

### Instruments of monetary policy

| Instrument | To reduce money and credit (fight inflation) |
|---|---|
| **Policy (bank) rate** | Raise the rate at which it lends to banks, so all interest rates rise and borrowing falls |
| **Open market operations** | Sell government securities, so money flows from banks to the central bank |
| **Cash reserve ratio** | Raise it, so banks must keep more reserves and can lend less |
| **Special deposits** | Require banks to deposit extra funds with the central bank |
| **Selective credit controls** | Direct banks to limit lending for certain purposes (for example imports of luxuries) |
| **Moral suasion** | Persuade banks to follow its advice |

To **expand** money and credit (for example in a slump), each instrument is used the other way.

## Other financial institutions

- **Development banks** lend for long-term projects in agriculture and industry.
- **Community banks and microfinance institutions** lend small sums to farmers, traders and small businesses.
- **Insurance companies** protect against risk and invest premiums.
- **Mobile money providers** let people save, pay and transfer money by phone.
- **Discount houses and finance houses** deal in short-term loans.

### Money market and capital market

| | Money market | Capital market |
|---|---|---|
| **Deals in** | Short-term loans (up to about a year) | Long-term funds (shares, bonds, long loans) |
| **Instruments** | Treasury bills, call money, commercial bills | Shares, government and company bonds, debentures |
| **Main users** | Banks, the government, large firms needing short-term cash | Firms raising capital to expand, governments funding projects |
| **Example in Sierra Leone** | Treasury bills sold by the Bank of Sierra Leone | The **Sierra Leone Stock Exchange**, set up in 2009 |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Commercial banks do not create bank **notes**; only the central bank issues currency. Banks create **credit** (deposits) by lending.
:::

## Key points

- **Commercial banks** accept deposits, lend, make payments and create **credit**.
- **Credit multiplier = 1 ÷ cash reserve ratio**; credit is limited by reserves, cash drains and the demand for loans.
- The **central bank** issues currency, banks for the government and banks, and controls money and credit.
- Monetary policy tools: **policy rate, open market operations, reserve ratio, special deposits, selective controls, moral suasion**.
- The **money market** deals in short-term funds; the **capital market** in long-term funds.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **state the functions of commercial banks** and of the **central bank**, to **explain how banks create credit** (often with a calculation), to **state the limits to credit creation**, and to **explain how the central bank controls credit**. Also common: **distinguish the money market from the capital market**. In calculations, state the multiplier formula first.
:::

## Quick check

1. A new deposit is NLe 2,000 and the reserve ratio is 25%. What are total deposits?
2. Give two functions of the Bank of Sierra Leone.
3. How does raising the cash reserve ratio reduce lending?
4. What is the difference between the money market and the capital market?

**Answers:** 1. Multiplier $= \dfrac{1}{0.25} = 4$; total deposits $= 2000 \times 4 = $ NLe 8,000. 2. Any two: issuing currency, banker to the government, banker to banks, lender of last resort, controlling credit, managing reserves, supervising banks. 3. Banks must keep more of each deposit as reserves, so they have less to lend. 4. The money market deals in short-term loans; the capital market in long-term funds such as shares and bonds.
