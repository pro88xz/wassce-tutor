A fire in a market stall can wipe out a trader's savings in an hour. Fires are rare for any one trader, but across thousands of traders some happen every year. Insurance turns that unpredictable, ruinous loss into a small, predictable payment. It is the aid to trade that removes the **hindrance of risk**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define insurance and explain how the pooling of risk works
- distinguish insurance from assurance
- explain the key terms used in insurance
- distinguish insurable from non-insurable risks
- explain the principles of insurance: utmost good faith, insurable interest, indemnity, contribution, subrogation and proximate cause
- explain the importance of insurance to business

## Meaning of insurance

:::callout{type=definition}
**Insurance** is a contract in which one party (the **insurer**) agrees, in return for a payment called a **premium**, to compensate another party (the **insured**) for a loss caused by a specified risk.
:::

### Pooling of risk

![Many traders each pay a small premium into a common fund held by the insurer; the few who suffer a loss are compensated from the fund](https://wasscetutor.com/img/commerce/pooling-of-risk.svg)

*Figure 1. The pooling of risk: the premiums of many pay the losses of the few. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Setting a premium.** 2,000 shop owners in Freetown each have a shop worth NLe 50,000. Past records show that on average **6 shops** a year are destroyed by fire.

1. Expected yearly losses $= 6 \times 50000 = $ NLe 300,000
2. Share per shop owner $= 300000 \div 2000 = $ NLe 150
3. Add 20% for the insurer's expenses and profit: $150 \times 1.2 = $ **NLe 180 premium a year**

Each owner pays NLe 180 a year, a small certain cost, instead of risking an uncertain loss of NLe 50,000. The **law of large numbers** lets the insurer predict losses accurately across many policyholders.
:::

### Insurance and assurance

| Insurance | Assurance |
|---|---|
| Covers a risk that **may or may not** happen (fire, theft, accident) | Covers an event that **will certainly** happen (death), or a fixed date |
| Compensates for the **actual loss** (indemnity) | Pays an **agreed sum** |
| Examples: fire, motor, marine, burglary | Examples: life assurance, endowment policies |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Insurer (underwriter)** | The company that accepts the risk |
| **Insured (policyholder)** | The person or firm whose risk is covered |
| **Premium** | The payment for cover, usually yearly |
| **Proposal form** | The form the person seeking insurance fills in, giving full details of the risk |
| **Cover note** | A temporary document giving cover until the policy is issued |
| **Policy** | The written contract, stating the terms and the risks covered |
| **Sum insured** | The maximum the insurer will pay |
| **Claim** | A demand by the insured for compensation after a loss |
| **Actuary** | An expert who calculates risks and premiums from statistics |
| **Insurance broker** | An agent who arranges insurance for clients with insurers, for a commission |
| **Loss adjuster / assessor** | A person who investigates a claim and assesses the loss |
| **Excess** | The first part of any loss, which the insured pays |
| **Renewal notice** | A reminder that the policy is due for renewal |

## Insurable and non-insurable risks

| Insurable risks | Non-insurable risks |
|---|---|
| Losses that can be **calculated** from past records | Losses that **cannot be calculated** |
| Fire, theft, burglary, accidents, death, damage to goods at sea | **Changes in fashion or taste**, **trade losses** from poor management or competition, **speculation** and gambling losses, a fall in demand, **war** (normally excluded), new inventions that make products out of date |

Non-insurable risks are borne by the **entrepreneur**; profit is the reward for bearing them.

## The principles of insurance

| Principle | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Utmost good faith** (*uberrimae fidei*) | Both parties must disclose **all material facts** honestly. Hiding a fact makes the contract **voidable**. | A trader who hides that the shop stores petrol cannot claim after a fire |
| **Insurable interest** | The insured must **suffer a financial loss** if the event happens; you can insure only what you have an interest in. | You can insure your own shop but not your neighbour's |
| **Indemnity** | The insured is restored to the **same financial position** as before the loss, **no better**. Insurance must not be a source of profit. (Does not apply to life assurance.) | Goods worth NLe 10,000 destroyed: the claim is NLe 10,000, not their price new |
| **Contribution** | If the same property is insured with **several insurers**, they **share** the loss; the insured cannot collect from each. | Two insurers each covering half pay half the loss each |
| **Subrogation** | After paying a claim, the insurer takes over the insured's **rights against third parties**, and any salvage. | The insurer sues the driver who caused the accident |
| **Proximate cause** | The insurer pays only if the **main (nearest) cause** of the loss is a risk covered by the policy. | A fire policy pays for fire damage, including water used to put it out, but not for theft during the fire |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Indemnity** does not apply to **life assurance**, because a human life cannot be valued in money. The agreed sum is paid instead. Contribution and subrogation follow from indemnity, so they also do not apply to life policies.
:::

## Importance of insurance to business

1. **Removes the hindrance of risk**: traders can operate knowing losses will be met.
2. **Encourages investment** and large-scale production, because risks are spread.
3. **Security for loans**: banks lend more readily on insured assets.
4. **Foreign trade**: marine insurance makes shipping goods possible.
5. **Mobilises savings**: premiums are invested in government securities, shares and property.
6. **Promotes loss prevention**: insurers advise on fire safety and security, and charge lower premiums for safer premises.
7. **Creates employment** for insurers, brokers, agents and loss adjusters.
8. **Peace of mind** for business owners and their families.

:::callout{type=local-example}
In Sierra Leone, insurance companies are licensed and supervised by the **Sierra Leone Insurance Commission (SLICOM)**. **Motor third-party insurance is compulsory**, so every vehicle on the road must carry it. Many market traders still do not insure their stock. Fires in markets therefore often ruin traders, a strong case for affordable micro-insurance.
:::

## Key points

- **Insurance** compensates for loss from a specified risk in return for a **premium**; it works by **pooling risk**.
- **Insurance** covers possible events (indemnity); **assurance** covers certain events (agreed sum).
- Only **calculable** risks are insurable; fashion changes, trade losses and gambling are not.
- Principles: **utmost good faith, insurable interest, indemnity, contribution, subrogation, proximate cause**.
