**President Ahmad Tejan Kabbah** governed Sierra Leone from **1996 to 2007**, apart from ten months in exile after the **AFRC coup of 25 May 1997**. A former **United Nations** official, he led the long search for peace through the **Abidjan Accord (1996)**, the **Lomé Peace Agreement (1999)** and the **Abuja ceasefires (2000–2001)**, with the help of **ECOMOG**, the UN mission **UNAMSIL** and **British** forces, and declared the **civil war over on 18 January 2002**. His government then rebuilt the country, set up the **Truth and Reconciliation Commission**, the **Special Court** and the **Anti-Corruption Commission**, and in **2007** handed over power peacefully to the opposition. This lesson explains **the Kabbah administration, the AFRC junta and the road to peace**.

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

## Kabbah's background and election

**Ahmad Tejan Kabbah (1932–2014)**, from **Pendembu** in Kailahun District, was an economist who had worked for the **Sierra Leone civil service** and for many years with the **United Nations Development Programme (UNDP)**. He led the **SLPP** to victory in the **1996** elections and was sworn in on **29 March 1996**.

### The Abidjan Accord (November 1996)

Signed by Kabbah and **Foday Sankoh** in **Côte d'Ivoire** on **30 November 1996**, it promised a **ceasefire**, **disarmament**, an **amnesty** and the RUF's change into a political party. It **failed** because the RUF did not disarm and fighting continued.

### The AFRC coup (25 May 1997)

Junior soldiers overthrew Kabbah and formed the **Armed Forces Revolutionary Council (AFRC)** under **Major Johnny Paul Koroma**, who was released from prison by the coup-makers. The AFRC **invited the RUF to join the government**.

**Causes:** soldiers' anger at the government's support for the **Kamajors (Civil Defence Forces)**, cuts to army **rice rations**, fears of losing their jobs, and RUF influence within the army.

**Effects:** **looting, killings and abuses** in Freetown; **schools and businesses closed**; Kabbah went into **exile in Guinea**; Sierra Leoneans resisted through **civil disobedience** (stay-at-home strikes); the international community refused to recognise the junta; **ECOWAS imposed sanctions**; the **Conakry Peace Plan (October 1997)** called for Kabbah's return.

### Kabbah's restoration (1998)

In **February 1998**, **ECOMOG**, the ECOWAS force led by **Nigeria**, drove the AFRC and RUF out of Freetown. **Kabbah returned on 10 March 1998** to a joyful welcome.

### The Freetown invasion and the Lomé Agreement (1999)

On **6 January 1999** AFRC and RUF forces **invaded Freetown**, killing thousands of civilians, burning homes and abducting children before ECOMOG drove them out. Under international pressure, Kabbah signed the **Lomé Peace Agreement** with Sankoh on **7 July 1999**:

1. **power-sharing**: RUF members received government posts, and **Sankoh** became chairman of a commission over **mineral resources**;
2. a general **amnesty**;
3. **disarmament** and the RUF's change into a political party;
4. a **UN peacekeeping force**, **UNAMSIL**, set up in **October 1999**.

### The final stages (2000–2002)

1. **May 2000:** the RUF seized **hundreds of UN peacekeepers** and advanced on Freetown. **British forces** landed (**Operation Palliser**), secured **Lungi airport** and Freetown, and helped to turn the tide. Crowds protested at Sankoh's house; he was **arrested**.
2. **2000–2001:** the **Abuja ceasefire agreements**; **UNAMSIL** grew into one of the largest UN missions in the world; **disarmament** of about **72,000** fighters.
3. **18 January 2002:** Kabbah declared the **war over** at a ceremony where weapons were burned.

## Reconstruction under Kabbah (2002–2007)

| Area | Action |
|---|---|
| **Democracy** | **May 2002** elections: Kabbah re-elected with about **70%** of the vote |
| **Justice and reconciliation** | the **Truth and Reconciliation Commission** and the **Special Court for Sierra Leone** (2002) |
| **Fighting corruption** | the **Anti-Corruption Commission** (2000) |
| **Local government** | the **Local Government Act (2004)** revived **local councils** |
| **Security** | rebuilding the **army** (with British help) and **police** |
| **Economy and services** | rebuilding roads, schools and hospitals; return of refugees; debt relief |

## The 2007 transfer of power

Kabbah stepped down after **two terms**. In the **2007 elections**, **Ernest Bai Koroma** of the **APC** defeated the SLPP candidate, and power passed **peacefully** to the opposition, a major sign of democratic recovery.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Evaluating President Kabbah**

**Achievements:** he **ended the civil war**, restored **democracy**, built **international support** (ECOWAS, UN, Britain), set up the **TRC, Special Court and Anti-Corruption Commission**, revived **local government**, and **handed over power peacefully** in 2007.

**Criticisms:** the **Lomé amnesty** and power-sharing rewarded rebels; **corruption** remained a serious problem; **youth unemployment** and poverty persisted; and progress in rebuilding services was slow.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
On **18 January 2002**, thousands gathered at **Lungi** to watch weapons collected from fighters being **burned** as Kabbah declared "**the war is over**". Sierra Leone's recovery was later held up by the UN as a model of **peacebuilding**, and UNAMSIL closed in **2005**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Kabbah was **not** in power continuously from 1996 to 2007. He was **overthrown** by the **AFRC on 25 May 1997** and spent about ten months in exile in **Guinea** before **ECOMOG** restored him in **March 1998**.
:::

## Key figures and terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figures and terms"}
- **Ahmad Tejan Kabbah**: the former UN official elected President in 1996, restored by ECOMOG in 1998, who led Sierra Leone to the end of the war.
- **AFRC** (Armed Forces Revolutionary Council): the junta led by Johnny Paul Koroma that seized power on 25 May 1997 and joined with the RUF.
- **Abidjan Accord** (November 1996): the peace agreement between the Kabbah government and the RUF, which failed.
:::

## Key points

- **Kabbah** (former UN official) elected **1996**; **Abidjan Accord (November 1996)** failed.
- **AFRC coup, 25 May 1997** (**Johnny Paul Koroma**); RUF joined the junta; Kabbah in exile in **Guinea**.
- **ECOMOG** restored Kabbah (**March 1998**); **Freetown invasion (6 January 1999)**; **Lomé Agreement (7 July 1999)**; **UNAMSIL**.
- **British intervention (May 2000)**; **Abuja ceasefires**; **disarmament**; **war over, 18 January 2002**.
- Reconstruction: **2002 elections, TRC, Special Court, Anti-Corruption Commission, local councils**; **peaceful handover (2007)**.
