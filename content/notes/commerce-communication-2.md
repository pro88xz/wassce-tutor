# Business communication: channels, records and effective messages

## Learning objectives
Select appropriate business communication channels, explain formal business documents and identify principles of effective communication.

## Communication process
Business communication transfers information between a sender and receiver. The sender encodes a message, selects a channel and sends it. The receiver interprets it and may provide feedback. Noise, unclear language, poor connectivity or cultural differences can distort the message.

A message is effective when it is accurate, complete, clear, timely and appropriate for its audience.

## Choosing a channel
- **Face-to-face meetings:** useful for negotiation, sensitive matters and immediate feedback.
- **Telephone or voice calls:** fast for urgent clarification but may leave limited written evidence.
- **Email:** suitable for formal records, attachments and messages requiring a response.
- **Letters:** useful where a formal signed communication or physical document is required.
- **Business messaging platforms:** efficient for operational updates but require access control and record-retention rules.
- **Websites and online portals:** support customer information, orders and service requests.

Sensitive commercial information should be sent only through approved channels with suitable access controls.

## Common business documents
An enquiry requests information. A quotation states proposed prices and terms. A purchase order formally requests specified goods or services. An invoice requests payment for a completed or agreed transaction. A receipt acknowledges payment. A statement of account summarises transactions and balances over a period.

These documents create a record and reduce disputes about quantities, prices, dates and payment.

## Barriers and remedies
Language barriers can be reduced through plain language and translation where needed. Technical barriers require reliable systems and backup channels. Information overload can be reduced by concise subject lines, structured messages and clear action points. Poor listening is addressed by summarising and confirming important instructions.

## WASSCE examination focus
Distinguish an invoice from a receipt: an invoice requests payment; a receipt confirms that payment has been made. When discussing communication methods, compare speed, cost, feedback and record value.

## Quick check
1. List the stages in the communication process.
2. Distinguish a quotation from a purchase order.
3. Why is email useful for business records?
4. State three qualities of effective communication.

**Answers:** 1. Sender, encoding, message/channel, receiver, decoding and feedback, with possible noise. 2. A quotation offers proposed terms; a purchase order places an order. 3. It provides a dated written record and can carry documents. 4. Clarity, accuracy, completeness, timeliness and suitability.
