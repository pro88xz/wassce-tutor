A single lens can magnify a little. Put two lenses together and you can see bacteria or the moons of Jupiter. This lesson covers the magnifying glass, the compound microscope, the astronomical telescope and the projector.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain how a magnifying glass works and calculate its magnification
- describe the compound microscope and the astronomical telescope
- calculate the magnifying power of a telescope in normal adjustment
- describe the slide projector

## The simple microscope (magnifying glass)

![A convex lens used as a magnifying glass: the object is between the lens and F, and the eye sees a virtual, upright, magnified image](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/magnifying-glass.svg)

*Figure 1. A converging lens used as a magnifying glass. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

The object is placed **between the lens and its focal point**. The lens forms a **virtual, upright, magnified** image. For the clearest view, the image is placed at the **near point** (about 25 cm, written $D$):

$$m = \dfrac{v}{u} = 1 + \dfrac{D}{f} \quad (\text{image at the near point})$$

A lens of **shorter focal length** (greater power) gives **greater magnification**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A magnifying glass of focal length 5 cm forms an image at the near point (25 cm). Find the magnification.**

$$m = 1 + \dfrac{25}{5} = 6$$
:::

Uses: reading small print, examining insects and plants, watch repairing, jewellers' loupes.

## The compound microscope

Two converging lenses of **short focal length**:

1. The **objective** (very short focal length, near the object) forms a **real, inverted, magnified** image of a tiny object placed just beyond its focal point.
2. The **eyepiece** acts as a magnifying glass on that image, giving a final **virtual, magnified** image (inverted relative to the object).

$$\text{total magnification} = m_{objective} \times m_{eyepiece}$$

A microscope with objective ×40 and eyepiece ×10 magnifies 400 times. Used in hospitals and laboratories to see cells, bacteria and the malaria parasite in blood.

## The astronomical (refracting) telescope

Two converging lenses:

1. The **objective** has a **long focal length** $f_o$ and a large diameter (to collect plenty of light). It forms a **real, inverted, diminished** image of the distant object at its focal point.
2. The **eyepiece** has a **short focal length** $f_e$ and acts as a magnifying glass on that image.

In **normal adjustment** (final image at infinity), the lenses are separated by $f_o + f_e$, and

$$\text{magnifying power} = \dfrac{f_o}{f_e}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A telescope has an objective of focal length 100 cm and an eyepiece of focal length 5 cm. Find its magnifying power and its length in normal adjustment.**

$$M = \dfrac{100}{5} = 20 \qquad \text{length} = 100 + 5 = 105\ \text{cm}$$
:::

The final image is **inverted** — no problem for stars and planets. A **terrestrial** telescope or **prism binoculars** add an extra lens or prisms to turn the image upright for viewing objects on land.

| Compound microscope | Astronomical telescope |
|---|---|
| objective: **short** focal length | objective: **long** focal length |
| object very close, just outside $f_o$ | object very far away |
| first image magnified | first image diminished |
| magnification = $m_o \times m_e$ | magnifying power = $f_o / f_e$ |

## The slide projector

A projector produces a **large, real** image on a screen.

1. A bright **lamp** with a **concave mirror** behind it sends light forward.
2. A **condenser** (two plano-convex lenses) concentrates the light evenly onto the slide.
3. The **slide** (object) is placed **between F and 2F** of the **projection lens**, **upside down**.
4. The projection lens forms a **real, magnified, inverted** image on the screen — which appears the right way up because the slide was inserted upside down.
5. A heat filter or fan protects the slide from the lamp's heat.

Moving the projection lens slightly focuses the image; moving the projector further from the screen makes the image larger.

## The camera (summary)

A converging lens forms a **real, inverted, diminished** image on the film or sensor; the object is beyond 2F. Focusing is done by **moving the lens**; the **aperture** (stop) controls the amount of light and the depth of field; the **shutter** controls the exposure time. Phone cameras work the same way with tiny lenses and electronic sensors.

## Key points

- Magnifying glass: object inside F; virtual, upright, magnified image; $m = 1 + D/f$.
- Compound microscope: short-focus objective + eyepiece; magnification $m_o \times m_e$.
- Astronomical telescope: long-focus objective + short-focus eyepiece; $M = f_o/f_e$; length $f_o + f_e$.
- Projector: slide between F and 2F, inverted; real, magnified image on the screen.

## Quick check

1. Where is the object placed for a magnifying glass?
2. A telescope has $f_o = 80$ cm and $f_e = 4$ cm. Find its magnifying power.
3. Why is the slide put into a projector upside down?

**Answers:** 1. Between the lens and its principal focus. 2. $80 \div 4 = 20$. 3. The projection lens forms an inverted image, so an inverted slide gives an upright picture on the screen.
