In ten years, from 1951 to 1961, Sierra Leone moved from a Legislative Council dominated by colonial officials to full independence. Each new constitution handed more power to elected Sierra Leoneans: first a majority in the legislature, then ministers, then a Premier and an all-Sierra Leonean cabinet. This lesson explains the constitutions of **1951**, **1956** and **1958**, which led to self-government.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the background to the 1951 constitution and the controversy it caused
- state the features of the 1951 constitution
- describe the ministerial system introduced in 1953
- state the features of the 1956 and 1958 constitutional changes
- explain how these constitutions prepared Sierra Leone for independence

## The 1951 constitution

### Background

In **1947**, Governor **Hubert Stevenson** proposed a new constitution that would bring the **Protectorate** fully into the Legislative Council and give it most of the African seats, since it held most of the population. Krio leaders in the Colony, led by **H. C. Bankole-Bright** and later the **National Council of Sierra Leone**, strongly opposed it, fearing domination by the Protectorate. After years of debate it was introduced in **1951** under Governor **George Beresford-Stooke**. It is often called the **Stevenson Constitution**.

### Features

1. An enlarged Legislative Council with an **African (unofficial) majority** for the first time.
2. **Seven members elected** from the **Colony**.
3. **Fourteen Protectorate members**, chosen **indirectly** through the **District Councils** and the **Protectorate Assembly**.
4. A smaller number of **official** and **nominated** members.
5. Africans were appointed to the **Executive Council**.
6. The Governor kept **reserved powers** and the veto.

### Results

- In the **1951** elections the newly formed **SLPP**, led by **Dr Milton Margai**, won the support of most Protectorate members and formed the government. Margai became **Leader of Government Business**.
- The constitution **united the Colony and the Protectorate** in one legislature and gave the majority of Sierra Leoneans a voice for the first time.
- It deepened the **Colony–Protectorate rivalry** in the short term.

## The ministerial system, 1953

In **1953**, elected members of the Executive Council were given charge of government departments as **ministers**, and Milton Margai became **Chief Minister** soon after. Sierra Leoneans now ran ministries, though the Governor still presided over the Executive Council and kept key powers such as defence and foreign affairs.

## The 1956 constitution and 1957 elections

1. The Legislative Council was replaced by a **House of Representatives**.
2. Most members were **directly elected** in single-member constituencies across the whole country, with a **wider franchise**.
3. **Twelve Paramount Chiefs** were **indirectly elected** as members, one for each district.
4. A few **official** and **nominated** members remained.

In the **1957** general election, 39 seats were contested directly; the **SLPP** won the largest number, and Margai continued as head of government.

## The 1958 changes

1. The Chief Minister became **Premier**, the leader of the majority in the House of Representatives.
2. The **Executive Council** was made up entirely of **Sierra Leonean ministers**, so that the country had **internal self-government**.
3. The Governor retained responsibility for a few reserved matters until independence.

These changes led directly to the **Lancaster House conference** in London in **1960** and to **independence on 27 April 1961**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**What was new in each constitution?** A quick way to remember:

- **1951**: **African majority** in the Legislative Council; Protectorate fully represented.
- **1953**: **African ministers** in charge of departments.
- **1956/1957**: **House of Representatives**; most members **directly elected**.
- **1958**: **Premier** and an **all-Sierra Leonean Executive Council** (internal self-government).
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
The debate over the 1951 constitution revealed deep divisions. Some Colony leaders argued that the Protectorate peoples, as "protected persons" rather than British subjects, should not dominate a legislature that made laws for the Colony. Protectorate leaders, led by Milton Margai and organised in the **SLPP**, argued that majority rule required the Protectorate to have most seats. The Protectorate view prevailed and shaped national politics from then on.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not say that the 1951 constitution gave Sierra Leone independence or full self-government. It gave an **African majority in the legislature**; **internal self-government** came in **1958**, and **independence** on **27 April 1961**.
:::

## Key points

- **1951 (Stevenson) constitution**: **African majority**; **7 Colony elected members**; **14 Protectorate members** chosen through District Councils and the Protectorate Assembly; opposed by Colony leaders (NCSL).
- **SLPP** won in 1951; **Milton Margai** became Leader of Government Business, then **Chief Minister**.
- **1953**: ministerial system.
- **1956**: **House of Representatives**, mostly directly elected, plus **12 Paramount Chiefs**; elections in **1957**.
- **1958**: **Premier** and **all-Sierra Leonean Executive Council**; independence followed in **1961**.

## Quick check

1. Which governor first proposed the 1951 constitution?
2. How were Protectorate members chosen under the 1951 constitution?
3. What body replaced the Legislative Council under the 1956 constitution?
4. What happened to the Executive Council in 1958?

**Answers:**

1. Governor Hubert Stevenson (in 1947).
2. Indirectly, through the District Councils and the Protectorate Assembly.
3. The House of Representatives.
4. It became made up entirely of Sierra Leonean ministers under a Premier, giving internal self-government.
