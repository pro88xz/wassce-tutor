:::callout{type=definition}
A **trial balance** is a **list of all the balances** in the ledger accounts and the cash book on a particular date, set out in two columns, **debit balances** and **credit balances**.
:::

If the double entry has been carried out correctly, the **two totals are equal**. The trial balance is therefore an **arithmetical check** on the books, and it is also the starting point for preparing the **final accounts**. This lesson explains the purpose and format of the trial balance and how to prepare one, with a full worked example.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define the trial balance and state its purposes
- identify which balances go on the debit side and which on the credit side
- prepare a trial balance from a list of balances
- find a missing figure, such as capital, from a trial balance
- explain why a balancing trial balance does not prove the books are free of errors

## Purposes of the trial balance

1. To **check the arithmetical accuracy** of the double entry: total debits should equal total credits.
2. To provide a **summary** of all the ledger balances in one place.
3. To be the **basis for preparing the final accounts**.
4. To help **locate errors** that affect the agreement of the totals.

## Which side?

![Debit balances (assets, expenses, purchases, returns inwards, discount allowed, opening inventory, drawings) and credit balances (capital, liabilities, sales, returns outwards, discount received, other income, provisions) in two columns](https://wasscetutor.com/img/accounting/trial-balance-sides.svg)

*Figure 1. Debit and credit balances in a trial balance. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

A simple test: **debit** balances are things the business **owns** or **spends** (and drawings); **credit** balances are what it **owes**, what the owner **put in**, and what it **earns**.

## Worked example

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**From the balances below, prepare the trial balance of Kargbo Stores at 31 December 2026, and find the capital (NLe).**

**Working:**

- Total debits $= 374\,000$
- Credits other than capital $= 160\,000 + 3\,000 + 1\,500 + 2\,000 + 11\,500 + 30\,000 = 208\,000$
- Capital $= 374\,000 - 208\,000 = 166\,000$

These balances are used again to prepare Kargbo Stores' final accounts in the topic on final accounts.
:::

**Points to note:**

- **Inventory** in the trial balance is the **opening** inventory: the closing inventory is found by stocktaking and is usually given **below** the trial balance.
- The **bank** balance is debit when there is money in the bank; a **bank overdraft** is a **credit** balance.
- **Carriage inwards** (on purchases) and **carriage outwards** (on sales) are both **expenses**: debit.

## Limitations of the trial balance

A trial balance can **agree** and yet the books can still contain errors, because some errors affect **both sides equally**:

- errors of **omission** (a transaction left out completely),
- errors of **commission** (posted to the wrong person's account),
- errors of **principle** (posted to the wrong class of account),
- errors of **original entry** (wrong amount in both entries),
- **complete reversal** of entries,
- **compensating** errors.

These are explained in the topic on errors.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not put **returns inwards** on the credit side because it "sounds like" income coming in. Returns inwards are **sales returns**: they **reduce sales**, so they are a **debit** balance. Returns outwards (purchases returns) are a **credit** balance.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Opening inventory**: the inventory at the start of the period; it is the inventory figure in the trial balance.
- **Debit column**: the trial balance column for assets, expenses, purchases, returns inwards, discount allowed, opening inventory and drawings.
- **Credit column**: the trial balance column for capital, liabilities, sales, returns outwards, discount received and other income.
:::

## Key points

- A **trial balance** lists all ledger balances in **debit** and **credit** columns on a given date.
- Equal totals show the double entry is **arithmetically** correct.
- **Debit:** assets, expenses, purchases, returns inwards, discount allowed, opening inventory, drawings. **Credit:** capital, liabilities, sales, returns outwards, discount received, other income.
- A missing **capital** figure is the difference between the totals.
- A balancing trial balance **does not prove** the books are error-free.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **prepare a trial balance from a list of balances**, often finding **capital** as the balancing figure, to **state the purposes of a trial balance**, and to **name errors not revealed by it**. Objective questions often ask on which side a given balance appears.
:::

## Quick check

1. What is a trial balance?
2. On which side do discount allowed and discount received appear?
3. Why is the inventory in a trial balance the opening inventory?
4. Total debit balances are NLe 250,000 and credit balances other than capital NLe 180,000. Find the capital.
5. Name two errors that a trial balance does not reveal.

**Answers:**

1. A list of all the balances in the ledger and cash book on a given date, in debit and credit columns, used to check the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry.
2. Discount allowed: debit; discount received: credit.
3. Because the closing inventory is found by stocktaking at the end of the period and has not yet been recorded in the ledger.
4. $250\,000 - 180\,000 = 70\,000$: NLe 70,000.
5. Any two: omission; commission; principle; original entry; complete reversal; compensating errors.
