*"I will come tomorrow,"* Fatu said. Later, you tell your friend: *Fatu said that she would come the next day.* Four things changed: the quotation marks disappeared, *I* became *she*, *will* became *would*, and *tomorrow* became *the next day*. That is **reported** (indirect) speech, and those changes follow rules. WASSCE tests them in the objective paper (*"Choose the option that best reports the sentence"*), and you need them in essays, summaries and reports. This lesson sets out every rule, with the exceptions that catch students.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- punctuate direct speech correctly
- change direct speech into reported speech: tenses, pronouns, time and place words
- report statements, questions, commands and requests
- recognise when the tense does **not** need to change

## Direct and reported speech

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Direct speech** gives the speaker's **exact words**, inside quotation marks: *"I am tired," said Musa.*
- **Reported (indirect) speech** gives the **meaning** of the words, without quotation marks, usually after a reporting verb: *Musa said that he was tired.*
:::

### Punctuating direct speech

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Put the spoken words inside **quotation marks** | *"We are late."* |
| Begin the spoken words with a **capital** | *She said, "**T**he bus has gone."* |
| Separate the words from the reporting clause with a **comma** (or ? or !) **inside** the marks | *"The bus has gone**,"** she said.* / *"Has it gone**?"** he asked.* |
| Start a **new line** for each new speaker | (in stories and dialogues) |

## Rule 1: the tense moves back

When the reporting verb is in the **past** (*said, told, asked*), the verbs in the spoken words usually move **one step back** in time:

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| present simple: *"I **live** in Bo."* | past simple: *She said she **lived** in Bo.* |
| present continuous: *"I **am waiting**."* | past continuous: *He said he **was waiting**.* |
| present perfect: *"I **have finished**."* | past perfect: *She said she **had finished**.* |
| past simple: *"I **saw** him."* | past perfect: *He said he **had seen** him.* |
| *will*: *"I **will** help."* | *would*: *She said she **would** help.* |
| *can*: *"I **can** swim."* | *could*: *He said he **could** swim.* |
| *may*: *"It **may** rain."* | *might*: *She said it **might** rain.* |
| *must*: *"I **must** go."* | *had to* (or *must*): *He said he **had to** go.* |

:::callout{type=tip}
The tense does **not** need to change when:

- the reporting verb is in the **present** (*says, tells*): *Musa **says** he **is** tired.*
- the statement is a **permanent truth**: *The teacher said that the earth **goes** round the sun.*
- the situation is **still true** now: *She said she **lives** in Bo* (and she still does). (Moving it back is also correct.)
:::

## Rule 2: pronouns change to fit the reporter

*"**I** will lend **you** **my** book," Ama told me.* → *Ama told me that **she** would lend **me** **her** book.*

Ask: **who** is speaking now, and **who** is being spoken about?

## Rule 3: time and place words change

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| now | then, at that time |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| yesterday | the day before, the previous day |
| tomorrow | the next day, the following day |
| last week | the week before, the previous week |
| next month | the following month |
| ago | before |
| here | there |
| this / these | that / those |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
*Direct:* *"I **will** come back **here tomorrow**," the doctor said.*

*Reported:* *The doctor said that **he would** come back **there the next day**.*

Four changes: *I → he*, *will → would*, *here → there*, *tomorrow → the next day*.
:::

## Reporting questions

1. Use a reporting verb such as **asked**, **wanted to know**, **enquired**.
2. Change the question into **statement word order** (subject before verb).
3. **No question mark.**
4. For **yes/no** questions, use **if** or **whether**; for **wh-** questions, keep the **question word**.

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| *"**Are you** coming?" she asked.* | *She asked **if I was** coming.* |
| *"**Where do you** live?" he asked me.* | *He asked me **where I lived**.* |
| *"**Why did** you leave?" the teacher asked.* | *The teacher asked **why I had left**.* |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Keeping question word order.** *"He asked me where **did I live**"* is wrong. In reported questions the word order is a **statement**: *He asked me where **I lived**.*
:::

## Reporting commands and requests

Use **told / ordered / asked / begged / advised** + object + **to** + verb. For negatives, use **not to**.

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| *"Sit down," the teacher said to us.* | *The teacher **told us to sit** down.* |
| *"Please help me," she said to him.* | *She **asked him to help** her.* |
| *"Don't touch the wires," the electrician said.* | *The electrician **warned us not to touch** the wires.* |

## Choosing the reporting verb

*Said* is fine, but a precise reporting verb shows the speaker's purpose and improves your expression:

| Purpose | Reporting verbs |
|---|---|
| a statement | said, stated, explained, announced, declared |
| a question | asked, enquired, wanted to know |
| a command | told, ordered, instructed, commanded |
| a request | asked, begged, pleaded with, urged |
| advice or warning | advised, warned, cautioned, reminded |
| a promise | promised, assured, vowed |

:::callout{type=local-example}
In Krio-influenced speech, reported questions often keep their question form: *"She asked me, am I coming?"* In standard written English, change it to *"She asked me **whether I was** coming."* This is a common error in candidates' essays and summaries.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Report: 'I am preparing for the WASSCE,' said Isatu."}
*Isatu said that she **was preparing** for the WASSCE.*
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Report: 'Have you seen my bag?' Ama asked me."}
*Ama asked me **if (whether) I had seen her bag**.*
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Report: 'Don't be late tomorrow,' the principal told the students."}
*The principal **told the students not to be late the next day**.*
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Report: 'We will visit this village next week,' the officials said."}
*The officials said that **they would visit that village the following week**.*
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Choose the best report of: 'Where are you going?' he asked her. A. He asked her where was she going. B. He asked her where she was going. C. He asked her where she is going? D. He asked where was she going?"}
**B.** Statement word order, past tense, no question mark.
:::

:::reveal{title="6. Report: 'Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius,' the teacher said."}
*The teacher said that water **boils** at 100 degrees Celsius.* (A permanent truth: the tense stays.)
:::

## Key points

- **Direct speech**: exact words in quotation marks, correctly punctuated.
- **Reported speech**: tenses usually move **back** (*is → was, will → would, has → had*), pronouns change, and time and place words change.
- **Questions**: *asked if/whether* or *asked + wh-word*, **statement word order**, no question mark.
- **Commands and requests**: *told/asked + object + (not) to + verb*.
- The tense **stays** with a present reporting verb or a permanent truth.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
The objective paper asks you to choose the **correct reported form** of a sentence, or the **correct punctuation** of direct speech. The commonest traps are question word order in reported questions, tenses that are not moved back, and time words left unchanged (*tomorrow* instead of *the next day*).
:::
