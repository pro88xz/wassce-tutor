Pick up a handful of soil from an upland farm, a riverbank and a swamp, and they feel completely different: one gritty and loose, one smooth and floury, one sticky and heavy. These differences in **soil properties** decide how well a soil drains, how much water it holds, how easy it is to dig and which crops grow well in it. This lesson explains the physical, chemical and biological properties of soil, compares sandy, clay and loamy soils, describes simple experiments to test soils, and introduces the main farming soils of Sierra Leone.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe soil texture, structure, porosity, permeability and water-holding capacity
- explain soil pH and its effect on crops
- compare sandy, clay and loamy soils
- describe experiments to determine soil composition and water retention
- describe the main agricultural soils of Sierra Leone

## Physical properties

![Sand (0.02–2 mm, gritty, drains fast), silt (0.002–0.02 mm, smooth) and clay (below 0.002 mm, sticky, holds water)](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/soil-particles.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
| Property | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **texture** | the **proportions** of sand, silt and clay particles in the soil; it feels gritty (sandy), smooth (silty) or sticky (clayey) |
| **structure** | the way particles are **grouped together** into crumbs or clods (crumb, granular, platy, blocky, columnar); a **crumb** structure is best for crops |
| **porosity** | the amount of **pore space** between particles |
| **permeability** | how easily **water passes through** the soil |
| **water-holding capacity** | how much water the soil can **hold** against gravity |
| **soil colour** | dark soils are rich in humus; red/yellow soils contain iron oxides; grey soils are often waterlogged |
| **soil temperature** | affects germination, root growth and microbial activity |
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Confusing texture and structure.** **Texture** is about particle **sizes** (sand, silt, clay) and cannot easily be changed. **Structure** is about how particles are **arranged** into crumbs; it can be improved by adding organic matter and damaged by tillage when wet or by compaction.
:::

## Chemical properties

**Soil pH** measures acidity or alkalinity on a scale of 0 to 14 (7 is neutral).

- Most crops grow best at **pH 5.5–7.0**.
- Many soils in Sierra Leone are **acidic** (pH often below 5.5) because heavy rainfall leaches out calcium and magnesium.
- Very acid soils make **phosphorus** unavailable and can release **aluminium** that damages roots.
- Acidity is corrected by **liming** (adding agricultural lime or dolomite).

Other chemical properties include the soil's **nutrient content** and its ability to hold nutrient ions on clay and humus particles (cation exchange capacity): clay and humus hold nutrients; sand holds very few.

## Biological properties

The soil teems with life: **earthworms, termites, ants, bacteria, fungi, nematodes**. They decompose organic matter into humus, recycle nutrients, fix nitrogen, and mix and aerate the soil. Some, such as nematodes and soil-borne fungi, are pests.

## Sandy, clay and loamy soils

| Feature | **Sandy soil** | **Clay soil** | **Loamy soil** |
|---|---|---|---|
| main particles | mostly sand | mostly clay | a mixture of sand, silt and clay, with humus |
| feel | gritty, loose | sticky and plastic when wet; hard and cracked when dry | soft, crumbly |
| pore size | large pores | very small pores | mixed |
| drainage and aeration | fast drainage, well aerated | poor drainage, poorly aerated, waterlogs | good drainage and aeration |
| water-holding capacity | low (dries out quickly) | high | moderate to high |
| nutrients | low; easily leached | high, but may be locked up | good |
| ease of working | easy ("light soil") | difficult ("heavy soil") | easy |
| warming | warms quickly | warms slowly | moderate |
| improved by | adding organic matter | adding organic matter, drainage, liming | maintaining organic matter |
| suits | groundnut, cassava, sweet potato, cashew | swamp rice, sugarcane | most crops |

## Simple soil experiments

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Experiment 1: Finding the components of soil by sedimentation.**

1. Put soil into a measuring cylinder or clear bottle to about one-third full.
2. Add water to about two-thirds full, shake well, and leave to settle overnight.
3. **Result:** layers form: **gravel and coarse sand at the bottom**, then fine sand, then silt, then **clay on top**; **organic matter floats** on the surface.
4. **Conclusion:** soil contains particles of different sizes; heavier particles settle first.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Experiment 2: Comparing water retention (drainage) of sand, loam and clay.**

1. Put equal amounts of dry sand, loam and clay in three funnels lined with cotton wool, over measuring cylinders.
2. Pour the same volume of water (e.g. 100 cm³) on each at the same time.
3. Measure the water that drains through after, say, 30 minutes.
4. **Typical result:** most water drains through **sand**, least through **clay**.
5. **Conclusion:** sand has the highest permeability and lowest water retention; clay has the highest water retention.
:::

Other experiments: heating a weighed sample to show **water** (mass lost on gentle heating) and **organic matter** (further mass lost on strong heating); pouring water onto a soil lump to show **air** (bubbles rise).

## Farming soils of Sierra Leone

| Soil area | Description | Main use |
|---|---|---|
| **upland soils** | well drained, often sandy or gravelly loams, leached and acidic | upland rice, cassava, groundnut, tree crops |
| **inland valley swamps** | fertile, clayey alluvial soils in valley bottoms; wet most of the year | swamp rice, vegetables in the dry season |
| **bolilands** | seasonally flooded grassland in the north, with heavy, poorly drained soils | rice, often mechanized |
| **riverine grasslands** | flooded plains along major rivers in the south | rice |
| **mangrove swamps** | salty tidal mud on the coast and estuaries (e.g. the Scarcies) | mangrove swamp rice |

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Why farmers move rice to the swamps.** On leached upland soils, rice yields are low and the land must rest for years. Inland valley swamp soils are richer, stay moist and receive nutrients washed down from the slopes, so the same land can be cropped every year with better yields. This is why government and NGOs have long promoted **swamp rice development** in Sierra Leone.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Define soil texture and soil structure."}
**Texture** is the proportion of sand, silt and clay particles in a soil. **Structure** is the way these particles are grouped together into aggregates such as crumbs.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Give three differences between sandy and clay soils."}
Any three: sandy has large particles/pores, clay small; sandy drains fast, clay slowly; sandy holds little water, clay much; sandy is easy to work, clay difficult; sandy is low in nutrients, clay high; sandy warms quickly, clay slowly.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Why is loam the best soil for most crops?"}
It has a balance of sand, silt and clay with humus, so it drains well **and** holds enough water and nutrients, is well aerated and easy to work.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. In the sedimentation experiment, which component settles last, and which floats?"}
**Clay** settles last (on top of the other layers); **organic matter** floats.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Why are many upland soils in Sierra Leone acidic, and how can this be corrected?"}
Heavy rainfall **leaches** calcium and magnesium out of the soil. Correct by **liming**.
:::

## Key points

- Physical properties: **texture, structure, porosity, permeability, water-holding capacity, colour, temperature**.
- **pH**: most crops prefer 5.5–7; leached Sierra Leone soils are often acidic; **lime** corrects acidity.
- **Sandy**: light, drains fast, poor. **Clay**: heavy, holds water, poorly aerated. **Loam**: the best balance.
- Experiments: **sedimentation** shows particle sizes; **funnels** compare drainage.
- Sierra Leone soils: **upland, inland valley swamp, boliland, riverine grassland, mangrove swamp**.
