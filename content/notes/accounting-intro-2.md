:::callout{type=definition}
**Bookkeeping** is the **recording** of financial transactions in books of account, while **accounting** goes further to **summarise, analyse and interpret** those records and report the results.
:::

Bookkeeping is the foundation; accounting is the building. The work of a business's accounts follows a repeating series of steps called the **accounting cycle**, from the source document to the final accounts. This lesson compares bookkeeping and accounting, describes the branches of accounting, and walks through the accounting cycle.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- distinguish bookkeeping from accounting
- describe the main branches of accounting
- outline the development of accounting and double entry
- describe the stages of the accounting cycle
- explain the importance of keeping proper records for a small business

## Bookkeeping and accounting

| Bookkeeping | Accounting |
|---|---|
| **records** transactions in the books | **summarises, analyses and interprets** the records |
| routine and clerical | requires judgement and professional skill |
| ends with the ledger and trial balance | goes on to the final accounts and their interpretation |
| done by a **bookkeeper** or clerk | done or supervised by an **accountant** |
| the **first stage** of accounting | the **whole process**, including bookkeeping |

## Branches of accounting

| Branch | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Financial accounting** | records transactions and prepares financial statements for owners and outside users |
| **Cost accounting** | finds the cost of making each product or providing each service |
| **Management accounting** | provides information for planning, budgeting and decisions inside the business |
| **Auditing** | independent examination of the records to give an opinion that the accounts are true and fair |
| **Taxation** | calculating tax and preparing returns for the National Revenue Authority |
| **Public sector (government) accounting** | records the income and spending of government and its agencies |
| **Forensic accounting** | investigates fraud and financial crime |

## A short history

People have kept records of goods and payments for thousands of years. The modern **double-entry system**, in which every transaction is recorded twice, was described in print by the Italian mathematician **Luca Pacioli** in **1494**, which is why he is often called the "father of accounting". Today most businesses keep their records on **computers**, but the principles are the same.

## The accounting cycle

![Six stages in a loop: a transaction evidenced by a source document, books of original entry, the ledger, the trial balance, adjustments, and the final accounts, then the next period](https://wasscetutor.com/img/accounting/accounting-cycle.svg)

*Figure 1. The accounting cycle. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| **1. Source documents** | each transaction is supported by a document: invoice, receipt, credit note, cheque counterfoil |
| **2. Books of original entry** | transactions are first listed in day books, the cash book or the journal |
| **3. Ledger** | items are posted to ledger accounts using **double entry**: a debit and an equal credit |
| **4. Trial balance** | the balances are listed to check that total debits equal total credits |
| **5. Adjustments** | accruals, prepayments, depreciation and bad debts are taken into account |
| **6. Final accounts** | the **income statement** and **statement of financial position** are prepared |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Follow one transaction through the cycle: on 5 March, Amie's Store sells goods on credit to Foday for NLe 3,000.**

1. **Source document:** Amie's Store issues a **sales invoice** for NLe 3,000.
2. **Book of original entry:** the invoice is entered in the **sales day book**.
3. **Ledger:** Foday's account is **debited** NLe 3,000 (he owes the business) and the sales account is **credited** NLe 3,000 (income earned).
4. **Trial balance:** at the month end, Foday's debit balance and the sales credit balance appear in the trial balance.
5. **Final accounts:** the NLe 3,000 is part of **sales** in the income statement, and, if unpaid, part of **trade receivables** in the statement of financial position.
:::

## Why small businesses need proper records

1. To know whether they are making a **profit**.
2. To know how much **customers owe** them and how much they **owe suppliers**.
3. To control **cash** and **stock** and spot theft.
4. To get **loans**, which need evidence of performance.
5. To pay the correct **tax** and avoid penalties.
6. To plan for the future and make **better decisions**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many market traders in Freetown and Kenema keep no written records, or mix business money with family money. When they apply for a loan from a bank or microfinance institution, they cannot prove their income. Even a simple cash book and a notebook of credit sales would let them show their profit and claim back debts.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Bookkeeping and accounting are not two names for the same job. Bookkeeping is the **recording** stage; accounting includes bookkeeping **and** the summarising, interpreting and reporting that follow.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Cost accounting**: the branch of accounting that finds the cost of making each product or providing each service.
- **Management accounting**: the branch of accounting that provides information for planning, budgeting and decisions inside the business.
- **Auditing**: independent examination of the records to give an opinion that the accounts are true and fair.
- **Forensic accounting**: the branch of accounting that investigates fraud and financial crime.
- **Accounting cycle**: the sequence from source documents to books of original entry, ledger, trial balance and final accounts.
:::

## Key points

- **Bookkeeping** records transactions; **accounting** summarises, interprets and reports them.
- Branches: **financial, cost, management, auditing, taxation, public sector, forensic**.
- Double entry was described by **Luca Pacioli** in **1494**.
- **Accounting cycle:** source documents → books of original entry → ledger → trial balance → adjustments → final accounts.
- Proper records help a business measure **profit**, control **cash and stock**, get **loans** and pay the right **tax**.

## Quick check

1. State two differences between bookkeeping and accounting.
2. What is auditing?
3. Who is often called the father of accounting, and why?
4. Put these in order: ledger, trial balance, source document, final accounts, book of original entry.
5. Give two reasons why a small trader should keep records.

**Answers:**

1. Any two: bookkeeping records while accounting also summarises and interprets; bookkeeping is clerical while accounting needs judgement; bookkeeping ends at the trial balance while accounting goes on to final accounts; bookkeeping is the first stage of accounting.
2. An independent examination of the accounting records to give an opinion on whether the accounts are true and fair.
3. Luca Pacioli, because he described the double-entry system in print in 1494.
4. Source document, book of original entry, ledger, trial balance, final accounts.
5. Any two: to know the profit; to know debts owed and owing; to control cash and stock; to obtain loans; to pay correct tax; to plan.
