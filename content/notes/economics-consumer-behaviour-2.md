## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the conditions for consumer equilibrium
- find the equilibrium combination of two goods from a table
- explain consumer surplus and how the demand curve is derived

## The consumer's problem

Every consumer has a **limited income** and **many wants**. A rational consumer tries to spend that income so as to get the **greatest possible total satisfaction**.

:::callout{type=definition}
A consumer is in **equilibrium** when they have spent their income in a way that gives **maximum total utility**, so they have no reason to change their pattern of spending.
:::

## One good

If a consumer buys only one good, they keep buying until the marginal utility of the good equals its price (measured in money):

$$\text{MU}_A = P_A$$

If the MU of another unit is worth more to them than its price, they buy it; if it is worth less, they stop.

## Two goods: the equi-marginal principle

With two goods, the consumer is in equilibrium when the **last leone spent on each good gives the same marginal utility**:

$$\dfrac{\text{MU}_A}{P_A} = \dfrac{\text{MU}_B}{P_B}$$

and all income is spent:

$$(P_A \times Q_A) + (P_B \times Q_B) = \text{Income}$$

This is the **law of equi-marginal utility** (the principle of substitution).

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Musa has NLe 18 to spend on bread rolls (NLe 2 each) and fish (NLe 4 each).**

| Unit | Bread MU | Bread MU ÷ 2 | Fish MU | Fish MU ÷ 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 | 10 | 36 | 9 |
| 2 | 16 | 8 | 32 | 8 |
| 3 | 12 | 6 | 24 | 6 |
| 4 | 8 | 4 | 20 | 5 |
| 5 | 4 | 2 | 12 | 3 |

Musa buys the unit that gives the most utility per leone each time: bread (10), fish (9), bread (8), fish (8), bread (6), fish (6).

- **3 bread rolls and 3 fish**: $\dfrac{12}{2} = \dfrac{24}{4} = 6$
- Spending: $(2 \times 3) + (4 \times 3) = 6 + 12 = \text{NLe } 18$ ✓
- Total utility: $(20 + 16 + 12) + (36 + 32 + 24) = 48 + 92 = 140$ utils
:::

Check that no other affordable combination does better:

| Combination (bread, fish) | Cost (NLe) | Total utility |
|---|---|---|
| 5 bread, 2 fish | $10 + 8 = 18$ | $60 + 68 = 128$ |
| **3 bread, 3 fish** | $6 + 12 = 18$ | **140** |
| 1 bread, 4 fish | $2 + 16 = 18$ | $20 + 112 = 132$ |

The equilibrium combination gives the highest total utility.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Equilibrium is **not** where the MU of the two goods is equal. It is where **MU divided by price** is equal. Fish gives more utility than bread at every unit, but it also costs twice as much.
:::

## Deriving the demand curve

Suppose the price of bread falls. Then $\dfrac{\text{MU}_{bread}}{P_{bread}}$ becomes **greater** than $\dfrac{\text{MU}_{fish}}{P_{fish}}$. To restore equality, Musa buys **more bread**, which lowers the MU of bread. So a **lower price leads to a larger quantity demanded**: the demand curve slopes downwards.

## Consumer surplus

:::callout{type=definition}
**Consumer surplus** is the difference between what a consumer is **willing to pay** for a good and what they **actually pay** (the market price).
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Aminata is willing to pay up to NLe 50 for a pair of school sandals, because she needs them badly. At the market in Bo they sell for NLe 35. Her consumer surplus is $50 - 35 = \text{NLe } 15$: extra satisfaction she gets "free".
:::

On a demand curve, consumer surplus is the **area below the demand curve and above the price line**. When the price falls, consumer surplus **increases**.

## Limitations of utility analysis

1. Utility **cannot be measured** in exact units.
2. Consumers are not always **rational**: habit and impulse buying matter.
3. Many goods are **indivisible** (a car, a fridge), so spending cannot be adjusted unit by unit.
4. Tastes and income **change** over time.

## Key points

- Equilibrium with one good: $\text{MU} = P$.
- With two goods: $\dfrac{\text{MU}_A}{P_A} = \dfrac{\text{MU}_B}{P_B}$, with all income spent.
- A fall in price raises $\dfrac{\text{MU}}{P}$, so more is bought: the demand curve slopes down.
- **Consumer surplus** = willing to pay − actually paid.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
A common structured question gives a table of MU for two goods with their prices and income, and asks for **the equilibrium quantities**. Work out MU ÷ P for every unit, choose the combination where the ratios are equal **and** the budget is exactly spent, then show the check. You may also be asked to **explain consumer surplus with a diagram**: shade the triangle between the demand curve and the price line.
:::

## Quick check

1. State the condition for consumer equilibrium with two goods.
2. If $\text{MU}_A / P_A$ is greater than $\text{MU}_B / P_B$, what should the consumer do?
3. A buyer is willing to pay NLe 80 for a bag and pays NLe 60. What is the consumer surplus?

**Answers:** 1. $\text{MU}_A / P_A = \text{MU}_B / P_B$, with all income spent. 2. Buy more of A and less of B until the ratios are equal. 3. NLe 20.
