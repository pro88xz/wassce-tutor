A trader from Freetown can travel by road to Conakry, Monrovia or Abidjan without a visa, using an ECOWAS travel document. In 1998, a West African force under ECOWAS drove a military junta out of Freetown. Regional bodies such as **ECOWAS**, the **Mano River Union** and the AU's **NEPAD** programme matter directly to Sierra Leone's trade, security and development. This lesson explains how they work and what they have achieved.

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

## ECOWAS

:::callout{type=definition}
The **Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS)** is a regional organisation of West African states, founded by the **Treaty of Lagos** on **28 May 1975**, to promote **economic integration** and cooperation in the region.
:::

- **Founders:** led by General **Yakubu Gowon** of Nigeria and President **Gnassingbé Eyadéma** of Togo; **15** founding members, including **Sierra Leone**.
- **Revised Treaty:** **1993** (Cotonou), adding political cooperation, peace and security, and plans for a parliament and court.
- **Headquarters:** the **ECOWAS Commission** in **Abuja**, Nigeria.
- **Membership changes:** Cape Verde joined in 1977; Mauritania left in 2000; **Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger** formally withdrew in **January 2025**, leaving **12** members.

### Aims

1. **Economic integration**: a common market and customs union.
2. **Free movement** of people, goods, services and capital.
3. **Removal of trade barriers** and a **common external tariff**.
4. Cooperation in **agriculture, industry, transport, energy and communications**.
5. A **single currency** (the planned "Eco").
6. **Peace, security** and **good governance** in the region.

### Institutions

| Institution | Role |
|---|---|
| **Authority of Heads of State and Government** | Supreme body; makes final decisions |
| **Council of Ministers** | Advises the Authority; oversees the Commission |
| **ECOWAS Commission** | Executive body that runs programmes (called the Executive Secretariat until 2007) |
| **Community Parliament** | Forum for representatives of member states |
| **Community Court of Justice** | Interprets ECOWAS law; hears human rights cases brought by citizens |
| **ECOWAS Bank for Investment and Development (EBID)** | Finances development projects |

### Achievements

- **Free movement protocol (1979)**: visa-free travel for citizens of member states.
- **ECOMOG** (ECOWAS Ceasefire Monitoring Group): intervened in **Liberia (1990)** and **Sierra Leone (1997–2000)**, restoring President Kabbah in **1998**.
- **Common external tariff** adopted in 2015.
- **Roads, energy and communications** projects, such as the West African Power Pool.
- **Defence of democracy**: sanctions on governments that seize power; the **Court of Justice** gives citizens a route to challenge rights abuses.

### Problems

- **Political instability and coups**, and the withdrawal of three Sahel states in 2025
- **Language and colonial divisions** (English-, French- and Portuguese-speaking states)
- **Failure to implement** protocols; harassment at borders
- **Poor infrastructure** and similar exports, limiting trade between members
- **Inadequate funding** and dependence on Nigeria
- Delay in the **single currency**

## The Mano River Union (MRU)

- **Founded:** **3 October 1973**, by the **Mano River Declaration** between **Liberia** and **Sierra Leone**.
- **Guinea** joined in **1980** and **Côte d'Ivoire** in **2008**.
- **Headquarters:** **Freetown**.
- **Aims:** a **customs union**, economic cooperation and development of the member states, and, since the wars of the 1990s, **peace and security** along shared borders.
- The region's civil wars in the 1990s severely disrupted the MRU, but it has been revived to tackle cross-border security, trade and health, such as the **Ebola** response.

## NEPAD and the APRM

- The **New Partnership for Africa's Development (NEPAD)** was adopted in **2001**. It is an African-led plan to end poverty and promote growth through **good governance, investment in infrastructure, agriculture, health and education**, and partnership with developed countries.
- It is now the **AU Development Agency (AUDA-NEPAD)**.
- The **African Peer Review Mechanism (APRM)**, launched in **2003**, is a voluntary process in which member states **review one another's governance**. Sierra Leone joined the APRM.

## Benefits of regional cooperation to Sierra Leone

1. **Security**: ECOMOG helped end the AFRC junta and the war.
2. **Free movement** for traders and workers.
3. **Larger markets** for Sierra Leonean goods.
4. **Joint projects** in energy, roads and communications.
5. **Collective voice** in international affairs.
6. **Legal protection** through the ECOWAS Court.
7. **Cooperation on health**, as during the Ebola epidemic.

:::callout{type=local-example}
When the **AFRC** junta seized power in **1997**, ECOWAS imposed **sanctions** and an embargo. In **February 1998**, **ECOMOG**, led by Nigerian troops, drove the junta out of Freetown, and President **Kabbah** returned in **March 1998**. ECOMOG troops stayed until UN peacekeepers took over, and many lost their lives in Sierra Leone.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**ECOMOG** was not a separate organisation; it was the **military monitoring group of ECOWAS**. Also, the **Mano River Union** was founded by **Liberia and Sierra Leone** in **1973**; Guinea and Côte d'Ivoire joined later.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **ECOWAS Commission**: the executive body that runs ECOWAS programmes, based in Abuja, Nigeria.
- **Community Court of Justice**: the ECOWAS court that interprets ECOWAS law and hears human rights cases brought by citizens.
- **Mano River Union**: the organisation founded by Liberia and Sierra Leone in 1973, joined by Guinea in 1980 and Côte d'Ivoire in 2008, with its headquarters in Freetown.
- **African Peer Review Mechanism (APRM)**: a voluntary process, launched in 2003, in which member states review one another's governance.
:::

## Key points

- **ECOWAS**: **Treaty of Lagos, 28 May 1975**; revised **1993**; Commission in **Abuja**; aims at **integration, free movement, a customs union and peace**.
- Institutions: **Authority, Council of Ministers, Commission, Parliament, Court of Justice, EBID**.
- **ECOMOG** restored **Kabbah** in **1998**; problems include **coups, divisions, poor implementation and funding**.
- **Mano River Union**: **1973**, Liberia and Sierra Leone; Guinea **1980**; Côte d'Ivoire **2008**; HQ **Freetown**.
- **NEPAD (2001)** and the **APRM (2003)** promote development and good governance.

## Quick check

1. When was ECOWAS founded, and by what treaty?
2. What was ECOMOG?
3. Which two countries founded the Mano River Union?
4. What is the African Peer Review Mechanism?

**Answers:**

1. On 28 May 1975, by the Treaty of Lagos.
2. The ECOWAS Ceasefire Monitoring Group, the military force ECOWAS used in Liberia and Sierra Leone; it restored President Kabbah in 1998.
3. Liberia and Sierra Leone, in 1973.
4. A voluntary process under the AU (launched in 2003) in which African states review one another's governance.
