**Sierra Leone's road to independence** from **1951 to 1961** was peaceful and **constitutional**. Under **Dr (later Sir) Milton Margai** and the **SLPP**, Africans became **ministers (1953)**, the **House of Representatives** replaced the Legislative Council (**1956**, with elections in **1957**), and Margai became **Premier (1958)**. At the **Lancaster House Constitutional Conference** in London in **1960**, a **United Front** of parties agreed the terms of independence, but **Siaka Stevens** refused to sign and founded the **All People's Congress (APC)**. Sierra Leone became independent on **27 April 1961**. This lesson explains **the stages and challenges of Sierra Leone's road to independence**.

## Constitutional stages

| Year | Change |
|---|---|
| **1951** | **Stevenson Constitution**: African majority; SLPP leads government business |
| **1953** | **Ministerial system**: African members put in charge of government departments |
| **1954** | **Milton Margai** becomes **Chief Minister** |
| **1956** | new constitution: the **House of Representatives** replaces the Legislative Council, with most members **directly elected** and **twelve paramount chiefs** |
| **1957** | general election; the SLPP wins |
| **1958** | Margai becomes **Premier**; the **Executive Council** becomes all Sierra Leonean (internal self-government) |
| **1960** | **Lancaster House** conference agrees independence |
| **27 April 1961** | **independence** |

## The disturbances of 1955–1956

1. **February 1955:** a **general strike** in Freetown by workers demanding higher wages led to **riots**, looting and deaths.
2. **1955–1956:** riots broke out in the **north** against **paramount chiefs**, who were accused of **extortion, forced labour and heavy local taxes**.
3. The **Cox Commission** of inquiry blamed the abuses of chiefs and recommended reforms; some chiefs were removed.

These events showed the **economic hardship** of ordinary people and the need to **reform chieftaincy**, and they made the government more careful.

## The SLPP split and the PNP

After the **1957** election, **Albert Margai**, Milton's younger brother, challenged Milton for the party leadership. When Milton kept power, Albert and **Siaka Stevens** left the SLPP and in **1958** formed the **People's National Party (PNP)**, which attracted younger, more radical members.

## The Lancaster House conference (1960)

In **April–May 1960** a conference in **London** settled the terms of independence. Sierra Leone's delegation was a **United Front** of almost all parties, led by **Milton Margai**, formed to show unity.

**Main decisions:**

1. Independence on **27 April 1961**.
2. A **parliamentary system** with the **Queen** as head of state, represented by a **Governor-General**, and a **Prime Minister**.
3. Membership of the **Commonwealth**.
4. A **defence agreement** with Britain.

**Siaka Stevens refused to sign** the agreement, because:

1. he demanded **elections before independence**, so that the people could choose who would lead the new state;
2. he opposed the **defence agreement** with Britain, which he saw as a threat to full sovereignty.

On his return he formed the **Elections Before Independence Movement**, which became the **All People's Congress (APC)** in **1960**. Its support came mainly from the **north** and from **Freetown** workers and youth.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Why Sierra Leone's independence was peaceful**

1. **Britain** had accepted gradual self-government after 1945.
2. Sierra Leone had a long tradition of **constitutional politics**, from the 1863 Legislative Council to the NCBWA.
3. **Milton Margai** was a **moderate**, cautious leader who worked with Britain, the chiefs and other parties.
4. There were **few white settlers**, so there was no settler resistance as in Kenya or Rhodesia.
5. The **United Front** brought most parties together.
:::

## Independence, 27 April 1961

1. On **27 April 1961** the **Union Jack** was lowered and the **green, white and blue** flag of Sierra Leone raised.
2. The **Duke of Kent** represented the Queen at the ceremonies.
3. **Sir Milton Margai** became the first **Prime Minister**; **Sir Maurice Dorman** became the first **Governor-General**.
4. Just before independence, the government declared a **state of emergency** and **detained Siaka Stevens and other APC leaders**, who had called for protests.
5. Sierra Leone joined the **Commonwealth** and, in **September 1961**, the **United Nations**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Independence Day, **27 April**, is celebrated every year as a public holiday in Sierra Leone, with parades, lantern processions and cultural events. The national flag's colours stand for **green** (agriculture and natural resources), **white** (unity and justice) and **blue** (the harbour of Freetown and the hope for peace).
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Siaka Stevens did **not** oppose independence itself. He opposed the **timing** (he wanted **elections before independence**) and the **defence agreement** with Britain.
:::

## Key figures and terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figures and terms"}
- **Lancaster House conference** (1960): the conference in London, attended by a United Front of Sierra Leone's parties, that agreed the terms of independence.
- **APC** (All People's Congress): the party founded by Siaka Stevens in 1960 after he refused to sign the independence agreement.
- **PNP** (People's National Party): the party formed in 1958 by Albert Margai and Siaka Stevens.
:::

## Key points

- Stages: **ministers (1953)**, **Chief Minister (1954)**, **House of Representatives (1956/57)**, **Premier and internal self-government (1958)**.
- **1955–56** strikes and riots led to the **Cox Commission** and chieftaincy reform.
- **Albert Margai and Siaka Stevens** formed the **PNP (1958)**.
- **Lancaster House (1960)**: **United Front**; parliamentary system; Commonwealth; defence agreement.
- **Stevens refused to sign**; founded the **APC (1960)**.
- **Independence: 27 April 1961**; **Sir Milton Margai** Prime Minister.
