**Africa's climate and vegetation** are arranged in belts that are roughly **symmetrical on either side of the Equator**: **equatorial** climate and **rainforest** in the Congo Basin and along the Guinea coast; **tropical wet-and-dry** climate and **savanna** to the north and south; **hot desert** in the **Sahara** and the **Kalahari and Namib**; and **Mediterranean** climate at the far north and south-west tips. Highlands such as East Africa are cooler. **Population** is very unevenly spread: dense along the Nile, in West Africa, around the East African lakes and in South Africa's industrial areas, and sparse in deserts and dense forests. This lesson explains the pattern.

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

## Climate and vegetation belts

![Climate and vegetation belts of Africa from north to south: Mediterranean, hot desert, savanna, equatorial rainforest at the Equator, then savanna, hot desert and Mediterranean again, with East Africa's highlands cooler](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/africa-climate-belts.svg)

*Figure 1. Africa's climate and vegetation belts on either side of the Equator (simplified). WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Climate | Where | Features | Vegetation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Equatorial** | Congo Basin, Gulf of Guinea coast | hot (about 27 °C) all year; rain in every month, often over 2,000 mm; high humidity | **tropical rainforest** |
| **Tropical wet-and-dry (savanna)** | north and south of the rainforest | hot; one long rainy season in summer and a dry season in winter | **savanna** grassland with trees |
| **Semi-arid** | Sahel; edges of the Kalahari | short, unreliable rains | short grass, thorn scrub |
| **Hot desert** | **Sahara**; **Kalahari** and **Namib** | very little rain; very hot days, cold nights | almost none; plants at oases |
| **Mediterranean** | north coast (Morocco to Tunisia) and the **Cape** of South Africa | hot, dry summers; mild, wet winters | evergreen shrubs and trees (olive, cork oak), now largely farms and vineyards |
| **Highland (tropical montane)** | Ethiopian and East African highlands | cooler with height; more rain on windward slopes | montane forest and grassland |
| **Warm temperate east coast** | south-east coast of South Africa | warm, rain all year | forest and grassland |

### Factors that affect the climate of Africa

1. **Latitude:** most of Africa is in the tropics, so temperatures are high; the belts repeat north and south of the Equator.
2. **Altitude:** highlands are cooler (Nairobi is cooler than Mombasa).
3. **Distance from the sea:** coastal areas are more humid and have smaller temperature ranges.
4. **Ocean currents:** the cold **Canary** and **Benguela** currents chill the air off the west coast and help to create the coastal deserts (the Namib); the warm **Mozambique** current brings rain to the south-east coast.
5. **Prevailing winds** and the movement of the **ITCZ**.
6. **Relief:** windward slopes get relief rain; lee sides are drier.

## Population of Africa

Africa has about **1.5 billion people** and the **youngest population** of any continent. Its population is growing fast and becoming more urban.

| Densely populated areas | Reasons |
|---|---|
| **Nile valley and delta** (Egypt) | water and fertile silt for irrigation in a desert |
| **West Africa**, especially **southern Nigeria** and the Guinea coast | rainfall, fertile soils, cash crops, oil, large cities |
| **Lake Victoria basin** and the **East African highlands** | fertile volcanic soils, reliable rain, cooler healthier climate |
| **Rwanda and Burundi** | fertile highlands, reliable rain |
| **South Africa's** Gauteng region | mining (gold), industry, cities |
| **Mediterranean coast** of the Maghreb | mild climate, farming, ports |

| Sparsely populated areas | Reasons |
|---|---|
| **Sahara**, **Kalahari** and **Namib** | lack of water, extreme heat |
| parts of the **Congo Basin** rainforest | dense forest, disease, poor communications |
| areas infested by **tsetse fly** | sleeping sickness in people and cattle |
| very steep, high mountains | cold, rugged land |

The largest cities include **Lagos**, **Cairo** and **Kinshasa**, each with well over ten million people in their wider urban areas.

## Main economic activities by region

| Region | Activities |
|---|---|
| Equatorial forest | cocoa, oil palm, rubber, timber, subsistence farming |
| Savanna | cattle, groundnuts, cotton, grains, wildlife tourism (Serengeti, Kruger) |
| Deserts | pastoral nomadism, oasis farming, oil and gas (Algeria, Libya), uranium (Niger) |
| Mediterranean | citrus fruits, grapes, olives, wheat, tourism |
| Highlands | tea and coffee (Kenya, Ethiopia), dairy, horticulture |
| Mineral regions | copper (Zambia, DR Congo), gold and platinum (South Africa), diamonds (Botswana), oil (Nigeria, Angola) |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The deserts of Africa are not on the Equator. They lie near the **tropics** (about 20–30° north and south), where dry, sinking air gives little rain. The **Equator** has the **wettest** climate.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Population distribution**: the way people are spread over an area; in Africa it is very uneven.
- **Highland climate** (tropical montane): the cooler climate of the Ethiopian and East African highlands.
- **Nile valley**: the valley and delta of the Nile, whose water and fertile silt allow farming in the desert.
:::

## Key points

- Climate belts are roughly **symmetrical about the Equator**: **equatorial → savanna → semi-arid → desert → Mediterranean**.
- Factors: **latitude, altitude, distance from the sea, ocean currents, winds and the ITCZ, relief**.
- Cold **Canary** and **Benguela** currents help create west-coast deserts.
- Population: dense in the **Nile valley**, **West Africa**, **East African highlands and lakes**, **South Africa's** industrial areas; sparse in **deserts**, dense **forests** and **tsetse** areas.

## Quick check

1. Why are Africa's climate belts similar north and south of the Equator?
2. Describe the Mediterranean climate.
3. How do cold ocean currents help to form deserts?
4. Give two reasons why the Nile valley is densely populated.
5. Name two sparsely populated areas of Africa.

**Answers:**

1. Because the Equator crosses the middle of the continent, so latitude and the movement of the sun and the ITCZ produce matching belts on each side.
2. Hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters.
3. They cool the air above them, so it holds little moisture and gives little rain to the coast.
4. Any two: water from the Nile; fertile silt; irrigation; Cairo and other large cities; long history of settlement.
5. Any two: the Sahara; the Kalahari; the Namib; parts of the Congo Basin; tsetse-infested areas; high mountains.
