**Africa** is the world's **second-largest continent**, covering about **30 million km²** and home to **54 countries** and about **1.5 billion people**. It stretches from about **37°N** to **35°S** and is crossed almost through its middle by the **Equator**, so most of it lies in the **tropics**. Africa is largely a **plateau**, high in the east and south and lower in the north and west, broken by the **Great Rift Valley**, and its great rivers are the **Nile**, **Congo**, **Niger** and **Zambezi**. This lesson covers the location, size, relief and drainage of Africa.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the location, size and extent of Africa
- describe the main relief regions of Africa, including High and Low Africa
- describe the Great Rift Valley and the highest mountains
- describe the major rivers and lakes of Africa
- explain the importance of the rivers and lakes
- draw a sketch map of the physical features of Africa

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Area** | about **30 million km²**, about a fifth of the world's land |
| **Latitude** | about **37°N** (northern Tunisia) to **35°S** (Cape Agulhas, South Africa) |
| **Longitude** | about **17°W** (Cap Vert, Senegal) to **51°E** (Ras Hafun, Somalia) |
| **Countries** | **54**; the largest by area is **Algeria**, the most populous is **Nigeria** |
| **Population** | about **1.5 billion**, the fastest-growing of any continent |
| **Surrounding waters** | **Mediterranean Sea** (north), **Atlantic Ocean** (west), **Indian Ocean** (east), **Red Sea** (north-east) |
| **Lines crossing it** | the **Equator**, the **Tropic of Cancer** and the **Tropic of Capricorn** |

Africa is joined to Asia only by the narrow **Isthmus of Suez**, through which the **Suez Canal** (opened **1869**) was cut. Its largest island is **Madagascar**.

## Relief

Most of Africa is a vast **plateau** of very old rocks.

| Region | Description |
|---|---|
| **High Africa** | the **east and south**, mostly over **1,000 m**: East African Plateau, Ethiopian Highlands, South African Plateau |
| **Low Africa** | the **north and west**, mostly **below 1,000 m**: the Sahara, West Africa, the Congo Basin |
| **Fold mountains** | the **Atlas Mountains** in the north-west (Toubkal, 4,167 m) and the **Cape Ranges** in the far south |
| **Volcanic mountains** | **Kilimanjaro (5,895 m)**, the highest in Africa, and **Mount Kenya (5,199 m)**; **Mount Cameroon** in the west |
| **Block mountains** | the **Ruwenzori** on the Uganda–DR Congo border |
| **Escarpments** | the **Drakensberg** on the edge of the South African Plateau |
| **Basins** | the **Congo**, **Chad** and **Kalahari** basins |
| **Deserts** | the **Sahara** (the largest hot desert in the world), the **Kalahari** and the **Namib** |
| **Coastal plains** | generally narrow, with few natural harbours |

### The Great Rift Valley

:::callout{type=definition}
The **East African Rift Valley** is a long trough formed where the crust has been **pulled apart** and blocks have sunk between parallel faults.
:::

It runs from the **Red Sea** through **Ethiopia** and **Kenya** to **Mozambique**, with a western branch through Uganda, DR Congo and Tanzania. It contains deep lakes such as **Tanganyika** and **Malawi**, volcanoes and steep escarpments. **Lake Assal** in Djibouti, about **155 m below sea level**, is the lowest point in Africa.

## Drainage

| River | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Nile** | about **6,650 km**, the **longest river in Africa**; the **White Nile** flows from **Lake Victoria** and the **Blue Nile** from **Lake Tana** in Ethiopia, meeting at **Khartoum**; flows north through Egypt to a delta on the Mediterranean; the **Aswan High Dam** (completed 1970) formed **Lake Nasser**; the **Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam** on the Blue Nile was inaugurated in **2025** |
| **Congo** | about **4,700 km**, the second longest in Africa and second in the world by volume of water; crosses the Equator twice; huge hydroelectric potential at **Inga Falls** |
| **Niger** | about **4,180 km**, the main river of West Africa (see the West Africa lesson) |
| **Zambezi** | flows east to the Indian Ocean; **Victoria Falls** on the Zambia–Zimbabwe border; **Kariba** and **Cahora Bassa** dams |
| **Orange** | flows west across South Africa to the Atlantic |
| **Limpopo** | flows east to the Indian Ocean |

| Lake | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Victoria** | the **largest lake in Africa**, shared by Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania; source of the White Nile |
| **Tanganyika** | a Rift Valley lake, one of the deepest lakes in the world |
| **Malawi (Nyasa)** | a Rift Valley lake |
| **Chad** | shallow, shrinking lake in the Sahel |
| **Volta** | one of the largest man-made lakes, behind the Akosombo Dam |

**Features of African rivers:** many have **waterfalls and rapids** where they drop off the plateau near the coast, which limits navigation but gives great **hydroelectric potential**; many have **seasonal flow**; several have **deltas** (Nile, Niger) or **estuaries**.

**Importance:** irrigation (Nile valley), hydroelectric power, fishing, transport on navigable stretches, water supply, fertile floodplains and tourism (Victoria Falls).

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Africa's rivers are **not** easy highways into the interior. Waterfalls and rapids near the coast, where rivers drop off the plateau, blocked ships for centuries; this is one reason the interior was explored late by outsiders.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **High Africa**: the east and south of Africa, mostly over 1,000 m: the East African Plateau, the Ethiopian Highlands and the South African Plateau.
- **Low Africa**: the north and west of Africa, mostly below 1,000 m: the Sahara, West Africa and the Congo Basin.
- **Fold mountains**: mountains formed when layers of rock are pushed up into folds, such as the Atlas Mountains.
:::

## Key points

- Africa: **second-largest continent**, about **30 million km²**, **54 countries**, about **1.5 billion people**; from about **37°N to 35°S**, crossed by the **Equator**.
- Mostly **plateau**: **High Africa** (east and south) and **Low Africa** (north and west).
- **Kilimanjaro (5,895 m)** is the highest point; **Lake Assal** (−155 m) the lowest; the **Great Rift Valley** runs from the Red Sea to Mozambique.
- Rivers: **Nile** (longest), **Congo** (largest flow), **Niger**, **Zambezi**, **Orange**; lakes **Victoria**, **Tanganyika**, **Malawi**, **Chad**.

## Quick check

1. Name the highest mountain in Africa and give its height.
2. Distinguish High Africa from Low Africa.
3. How was the Great Rift Valley formed?
4. Name the longest river in Africa and the sea it flows into.
5. Which lake is the source of the White Nile?

**Answers:**

1. Kilimanjaro, 5,895 m.
2. High Africa is the east and south, mostly over 1,000 m; Low Africa is the north and west, mostly below 1,000 m.
3. By faulting: the crust was pulled apart and a block of land sank between parallel faults.
4. The Nile, which flows into the Mediterranean Sea.
5. Lake Victoria.
