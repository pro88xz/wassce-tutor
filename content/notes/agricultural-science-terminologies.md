Read any agricultural science question and you meet special words: arable land, perennial crops, agronomy, a farrowing sow, a hybrid, a cash crop. Examiners expect you to use these words correctly, and many questions begin with "Define…". This lesson collects the key terms of agriculture in one place, grouped by area, so you can learn them and use them accurately.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define the basic terms used in agriculture
- use crop, animal, soil and economic terms correctly
- distinguish between terms that are often confused
- apply the terms to examples from Sierra Leone

## General terms

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Agriculture**: the cultivation of crops and the rearing of animals for food, raw materials and income, including the processing and marketing of the products.
- **Farm**: an area of land, with its buildings, used for growing crops or rearing animals.
- **Farmer**: a person who cultivates crops or rears animals.
- **Subsistence agriculture**: farming mainly to feed the farmer's own family, with little left to sell.
- **Commercial agriculture**: farming mainly to produce for sale and profit.
- **Mechanisation**: the use of machines such as tractors, ploughs and harvesters in farming.
:::

## Crop terms

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Agronomy**: the branch of agriculture that deals with crop production and soil management.
- **Horticulture**: the growing of fruits, vegetables and ornamental plants.
- **Annual crop**: a crop that completes its life cycle in one year or less, such as rice, maize and groundnut.
- **Biennial crop**: a crop that completes its life cycle in two years, such as cabbage grown for seed.
- **Perennial crop**: a crop that lives and produces for more than two years, such as cocoa, coffee, oil palm and cashew.
- **Cash crop**: a crop grown mainly for sale, such as cocoa, coffee and oil palm.
- **Food crop**: a crop grown mainly to be eaten, such as rice and cassava.
- **Arable land**: land that is suitable for growing crops.
- **Yield**: the quantity of produce obtained from a given area of land.
:::

## Animal terms

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Animal husbandry**: the care, breeding and management of farm animals.
- **Livestock**: animals kept on a farm for food, products or work, such as cattle, goats, sheep, pigs and poultry.
- **Breed**: a group of animals of the same species with similar, inherited characteristics.
- **Gestation period**: the length of pregnancy in an animal, from fertilisation to birth.
- **Parturition**: the act of giving birth in farm animals.
- **Culling**: removing weak, sick, old or unproductive animals from a herd or flock.
:::

The names for giving birth differ by animal: a cow **calves**, a sow **farrows**, a goat **kids**, a ewe **lambs**.

## Soil and land terms

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Soil fertility**: the ability of a soil to supply crops with nutrients in the right amounts and proportions.
- **Soil erosion**: the removal of the top layer of soil by water, wind or human activity.
- **Fallow**: land left uncultivated for a period so that its fertility can recover.
- **Irrigation**: the artificial supply of water to crops.
:::

## Breeding and improvement terms

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Hybrid**: the offspring of a cross between two different varieties or breeds.
- **Variety**: a group of plants within a species with distinct, inherited characteristics, such as a rice variety.
- **Pest**: any organism that damages crops, stored produce or animals.
:::

## Terms that are often confused

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Annual / perennial | An annual lives one season; a perennial lives and yields for many years |
| Cash crop / food crop | A cash crop is grown mainly to sell; a food crop mainly to eat (some, like rice, are both) |
| Yield / output | Yield is produce per unit area (bags per acre); output is the total produced |
| Variety / breed | Variety is used for plants; breed for animals |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not call every useful plant a "cash crop". A crop is a cash crop when it is grown **mainly for sale**. Rice grown to feed the family is a food crop; rice grown on a large farm to sell in Freetown is grown as a cash crop.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's main **perennial cash crops** are cocoa, coffee, oil palm and cashew, grown mostly in the Eastern and Southern provinces. Its main **annual food crops** are rice (the staple), cassava, groundnut, maize and sweet potato. Many farmers practise **bush fallow**, leaving land to rest so its **fertility** can recover.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Using the terms.** "A farmer at Rokupr grows an improved rice variety on 2 acres and harvests 40 bags. He sells 30 bags and keeps 10 for his family."

- The rice is an **annual** crop and a **food crop**, but here mostly grown for sale, so he is practising **commercial** farming.
- His **yield** is $40 \div 2 = 20$ bags per acre.
- The improved **variety** was developed by plant breeders.
:::

## Key points

- Agricultural terms fall into groups: **general**, **crop**, **animal**, **soil** and **breeding** terms.
- **Annual**, **biennial** and **perennial** describe a crop's life span; **cash** and **food** crops describe its purpose.
- **Yield** is output per unit area; **fallow** land rests to regain **fertility**.
- **Variety** is for plants, **breed** for animals; a **hybrid** comes from crossing two of them.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Many WASSCE Agricultural Science questions begin with **"Define…"** or **"Explain the following terms"**. Give a precise definition in one sentence, then add an example, ideally from Sierra Leone. Learn the pairs that are often confused, such as annual and perennial, and yield and output.
:::

## Quick check

1. Define agronomy.
2. Give two examples of perennial crops.
3. What is culling?
4. What is the difference between yield and output?

**Answers:** 1. The branch of agriculture that deals with crop production and soil management. 2. Any two of cocoa, coffee, oil palm, cashew, citrus. 3. Removing weak, sick, old or unproductive animals from a herd or flock. 4. Yield is produce per unit area; output is the total quantity produced.
