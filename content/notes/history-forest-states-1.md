**Oyo** and **Benin** were two of the most powerful **forest and woodland kingdoms** of West Africa, both in present-day **Nigeria**. **Oyo**, a **Yoruba** kingdom ruled by the **Alaafin**, built its power on **cavalry**, trade and a famous system of **checks and balances** between the king and the **Oyo Mesi** council, before collapsing in the early nineteenth century. **Benin**, ruled by the **Oba**, reached its height under **Oba Ewuare** in the fifteenth century, traded with the **Portuguese** from the 1480s, and became world-famous for its **bronze and brass art**, before it was conquered by Britain in **1897**. This lesson explains the rise, government and decline of both kingdoms.

## The Oyo Empire

**Origins:** Yoruba tradition traces Oyo's founding to **Oranmiyan**, a prince of **Ile-Ife**, the sacred city regarded as the cradle of the Yoruba. Oyo lay in the **northern savanna woodland** of Yorubaland, where horses could survive.

**Factors in its rise (17th–18th centuries):**

1. A strong **cavalry** army, using horses obtained from the north.
2. Control of **trade routes** between the savanna and the coast, including the Atlantic slave trade through ports such as Porto-Novo.
3. An effective system of **government** and provincial control.
4. Tribute from conquered states, including **Dahomey**, which paid tribute to Oyo for much of the 18th century.

### Government: checks and balances

| Institution | Role |
|---|---|
| **Alaafin** | the king, sacred ruler and head of government |
| **Oyo Mesi** | council of seven chiefs, led by the **Bashorun**; chose the Alaafin and advised him; could **reject** a bad Alaafin, who was then expected to take his own life |
| **Ogboni** | a powerful society of elders and religious leaders that checked the **Oyo Mesi** and could mediate between the Alaafin and the council |
| **Are-Ona-Kakanfo** | the commander of the army, often stationed away from the capital so he could not seize power |
| **Ilari** | royal messengers and agents who collected tribute and kept watch on provinces |

This system meant that **no single person or group** held unchecked power.

**Decline (late 18th to early 19th century):**

1. **Struggles** between the Alaafin and the Oyo Mesi, especially under the Bashorun **Gaha** in the 18th century.
2. **Revolt of Afonja**, the Are-Ona-Kakanfo, at **Ilorin** (about 1817), who allied with Fulani jihadists.
3. The **Fulani jihad**, which made Ilorin part of the Sokoto Caliphate.
4. **Revolts** of provinces and tributaries, including Dahomey.
5. Changes in the Atlantic trade and rivals on the coast.

**Old Oyo** was abandoned around **1835**, and a new Oyo was founded further south.

## The Benin Kingdom

**Government:** the **Oba** was a sacred ruler with great power, assisted by chiefs: the **Uzama** (hereditary kingmakers), **palace chiefs** and **town chiefs**. Succession passed to the Oba's eldest son.

**Height of power:** **Oba Ewuare the Great** (15th century) rebuilt **Benin City** with walls and moats, expanded the kingdom by conquest and strengthened royal power.

**Economy:** farming, crafts and trade; after the **Portuguese** arrived in the **1480s**, Benin traded **pepper, ivory, cloth** and later **slaves** for **brass manillas, cloth and guns**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Benin's **guild of bronze and brass casters**, working for the Oba, produced plaques and heads that recorded the kingdom's history, rulers and its dealings with the Portuguese. After the British **punitive expedition of 1897**, thousands of these **Benin Bronzes** were taken to Europe. Some museums have now begun to return them to Nigeria.
:::

**Fall of Benin:** British trading interests and a clash in **1897**, in which British officials travelling to Benin City were killed, led to a **punitive expedition** that captured and burned Benin City and exiled **Oba Ovonramwen** to Calabar.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The **Benin Kingdom** was in present-day **southern Nigeria**, not in the modern Republic of Benin, which was formerly called **Dahomey** and took its name from the **Bight of Benin** in 1975.
:::

## Key points

- **Oyo:** Yoruba empire founded by Oranmiyan of Ife; power from **cavalry**, **trade** and tribute.
- Government: **Alaafin**, **Oyo Mesi** (led by the Bashorun), **Ogboni**, **Are-Ona-Kakanfo**: checks and balances.
- Decline: internal struggles, **Afonja's revolt at Ilorin**, the **Fulani jihad**; Old Oyo abandoned **c. 1835**.
- **Benin:** ruled by the **Oba**; height under **Ewuare**; trade with the **Portuguese**; famous **bronzes**; conquered by Britain in **1897**.

2. What was the role of the Oyo Mesi?
3. How did the Ogboni check the Oyo Mesi?
4. Name two causes of the decline of Oyo.
5. What happened to Benin City in 1897?

**Answers:**

1. The Alaafin.
2. It chose and advised the Alaafin and could reject a bad Alaafin.
3. It was a society of elders and religious leaders that could oppose and mediate between the Oyo Mesi and the Alaafin, so the council could not abuse its power.
4. Any two: struggles between the Alaafin and the Oyo Mesi; Afonja's revolt at Ilorin; the Fulani jihad; provincial revolts; changes in trade.
5. It was captured and burned by a British punitive expedition, and the Oba was exiled.
