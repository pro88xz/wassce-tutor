There are three **types of rocks**: **igneous rocks**, formed when molten magma or lava cools and hardens (for example granite and basalt); **sedimentary rocks**, formed from layers of sediment that are pressed and cemented together (for example sandstone, limestone and coal); and **metamorphic rocks**, formed when existing rocks are changed by heat and pressure (for example marble, slate and gneiss). Over millions of years each type can turn into the others in the **rock cycle**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a rock and a mineral
- describe how igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic rocks are formed, with examples
- distinguish intrusive and extrusive igneous rocks
- classify sedimentary rocks by how they formed
- describe the rock cycle
- explain the economic importance of rocks, with Sierra Leone examples

## Rocks and minerals

:::callout{type=definition}
A **mineral** is a naturally occurring substance with a definite chemical composition, such as quartz, feldspar or mica. A **rock** is a natural, solid mass made of one or more minerals. Granite, for example, is made of quartz, feldspar and mica.
:::

## Igneous rocks

**Igneous** rocks (from the Latin for "fire") form from **molten rock** that cools and solidifies.

| Intrusive (plutonic) | Extrusive (volcanic) |
|---|---|
| magma cools **slowly underground** | lava cools **quickly at the surface** |
| **large crystals** (coarse-grained) | **small or no crystals** (fine-grained or glassy) |
| granite, gabbro, diorite | basalt, andesite, obsidian, pumice |

**Characteristics:** hard and crystalline; no layers; **no fossils**; usually not porous.

:::callout{type=local-example}
The mountains of the **Freetown Peninsula** are made of a dark, heavy igneous rock called **gabbro**, part of a huge body of magma that cooled underground millions of years ago and was later exposed by erosion. Quarries around Freetown crush similar hard rock into chippings for roads and concrete.
:::

## Sedimentary rocks

Sedimentary rocks form from **sediments**: fragments of other rocks, remains of living things, or minerals that settle out of water. The sediments are laid down in layers (**strata**), then **compacted** and **cemented** over long periods.

| How formed | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Mechanically formed** (from rock fragments) | sandstone (sand), conglomerate (pebbles), shale and clay (mud) |
| **Organically formed** (from plants and animals) | limestone and chalk (shells), coal (plants), coral |
| **Chemically formed** (from minerals in solution) | rock salt, gypsum, potash, ironstone |

**Characteristics:** in **layers**; often contain **fossils**; often porous and softer than igneous rocks.

## Metamorphic rocks

**Metamorphic** rocks ("changed form") form when existing rocks are altered by great **heat**, **pressure**, or both, without melting completely, for example near intrusions of magma or deep in mountain belts.

| Original rock | Metamorphic rock |
|---|---|
| limestone | **marble** |
| sandstone | **quartzite** |
| shale or clay | **slate** |
| slate | **schist** |
| granite | **gneiss** |
| coal | **graphite** |

**Characteristics:** very hard; minerals often in **bands** or flat layers (foliation); any fossils are usually destroyed.

## The rock cycle

![The rock cycle: magma cools to igneous rock; weathering, erosion and deposition make sedimentary rock; heat and pressure make metamorphic rock; melting returns any rock to magma](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/rock-cycle.svg)

*Figure 1. How the three types of rock change into one another. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

1. **Magma** cools and hardens into **igneous** rock.
2. **Weathering and erosion** break any rock into sediments, which are **deposited**, **compacted** and **cemented** into **sedimentary** rock.
3. **Heat and pressure** change igneous or sedimentary rock into **metamorphic** rock.
4. **Melting** turns any rock back into magma, and the cycle continues.

## Comparing the three types

| | Igneous | Sedimentary | Metamorphic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Formed by** | cooling of magma or lava | deposition, compaction and cementing of sediments | heat and pressure on existing rock |
| **Texture** | crystalline | in layers (strata) | banded, very hard |
| **Fossils** | none | common | rare (usually destroyed) |
| **Examples** | granite, basalt | sandstone, limestone, coal | marble, slate, gneiss |

## Economic importance of rocks

1. **Building materials:** granite, gravel and sand for houses and roads; limestone for **cement**; clay for bricks.
2. **Minerals:** diamonds (from kimberlite), iron ore, gold and **rutile** (titanium ore in sands) are mined from rocks.
3. **Fuels:** coal, petroleum and natural gas occur in sedimentary rocks.
4. **Soils:** weathered rock forms the soils on which farming depends.
5. **Water:** porous sedimentary rocks store **groundwater** for wells and boreholes.
6. **Tourism and landscape:** rock landforms such as mountains and waterfalls attract visitors.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's mining economy rests on its rocks: **diamonds** in Kono, **iron ore** in Tonkolili and Marampa, **rutile** in Moyamba and Bonthe, **bauxite** near Mokanji, and **gold** in several districts. Iron ore and bauxite are themselves the products of long weathering of older rocks in a hot, wet climate.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not confuse **intrusive** and **extrusive**. Intrusive rocks cool **inside** the crust (slowly, large crystals: granite); extrusive rocks cool **outside**, at the surface (quickly, small crystals: basalt).
:::

## Key points

- **Igneous:** cooled magma or lava; crystalline, no fossils; intrusive (granite) or extrusive (basalt).
- **Sedimentary:** layered sediments; often fossils; mechanical (sandstone), organic (limestone, coal), chemical (rock salt).
- **Metamorphic:** changed by heat and pressure; limestone → marble, shale → slate, granite → gneiss.
- **Rock cycle:** cooling → weathering and deposition → heat and pressure → melting.
- Rocks provide **building materials, minerals, fuels, soils and groundwater**.

## Quick check

1. What is the difference between a rock and a mineral?
2. Why does granite have larger crystals than basalt?
3. Name one organically formed sedimentary rock.
4. What metamorphic rock forms from limestone?
5. Give two economic uses of rocks in Sierra Leone.

**Answers:**

1. A mineral is a natural substance with a definite composition; a rock is a solid mass of one or more minerals.
2. Granite cooled slowly underground, giving crystals time to grow; basalt cooled quickly at the surface.
3. Limestone, chalk, coal or coral.
4. Marble.
5. Any two: building stone and chippings; diamonds from kimberlite; iron ore; rutile; bauxite; gold; clay for bricks; sand and gravel for construction.
