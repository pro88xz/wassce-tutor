Health is more than not being ill: the World Health Organization defines it as a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being. **Disease** is any condition that stops the body or mind from working normally. Sierra Leone has faced some of the most serious diseases in the world, from malaria every rainy season to cholera in 2012, Ebola in 2014–2016, and Lassa fever in the east. Knowing what causes each disease, how it spreads and how it can be prevented saves lives. This lesson classifies diseases, studies the most important infectious diseases in detail, explains the life cycle of the malaria parasite, and describes non-infectious diseases and the body's defences.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- classify diseases as infectious or non-infectious, with examples
- define pathogen, vector, host and carrier
- describe the cause, transmission, symptoms, prevention and treatment of major diseases
- describe the life cycle of *Plasmodium*
- describe deficiency, hereditary and lifestyle diseases
- outline the body's natural defences

## Classifying diseases

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Infectious (communicable) diseases** are caused by **pathogens** (disease-causing organisms) and can spread from person to person, or from animals or vectors: malaria, cholera, TB, HIV, Ebola.
- **Non-infectious diseases** are not caught from others:
  - **deficiency**: lack of a nutrient (kwashiorkor, rickets, scurvy, goitre, anaemia)
  - **hereditary**: inherited genes (sickle cell disease, haemophilia)
  - **lifestyle and degenerative**: linked to diet, smoking, alcohol, inactivity or age (high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes, heart disease, lung cancer)
  - **mental illness**
:::

:::callout{type=definition}
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **pathogen** | an organism that causes disease (virus, bacterium, fungus, protozoan, worm) |
| **vector** | an organism that carries a pathogen from one host to another (e.g. the *Anopheles* mosquito) |
| **host** | the organism a pathogen lives in |
| **carrier** | a person who carries a pathogen without showing symptoms, but can pass it on |
| **reservoir** | an animal population in which a pathogen lives naturally (e.g. rats for Lassa virus) |
| **epidemic** | a sudden rise in cases in an area; **endemic**: always present in an area |
:::

## Ways diseases spread

- **Air (droplets)**: coughing and sneezing: TB, measles, influenza, COVID-19.
- **Contaminated water and food** (faecal–oral route): cholera, typhoid, dysentery, roundworm.
- **Vectors**: mosquitoes (malaria, yellow fever), blackflies (river blindness), tsetse flies (sleeping sickness), houseflies (carry germs onto food).
- **Body fluids and blood**: sexual contact, shared needles and razors, transfusion, mother to child: HIV, hepatitis B, Ebola.
- **Contact with animals or their waste**: Lassa fever (rat urine and droppings), rabies (dog bites).
- **Skin contact or contaminated water**: ringworm; bilharzia (larvae enter through the skin in snail-infested water); hookworm (larvae enter bare feet).

## Major infectious diseases

| Disease | Pathogen | Spread by | Main signs | Prevention and treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **malaria** | *Plasmodium* (protozoan) | bite of infected female *Anopheles* mosquito | fever, chills, sweating, headache, anaemia | insecticide-treated nets, draining stagnant water, indoor spraying; treat with **ACTs** |
| **cholera** | *Vibrio cholerae* (bacterium) | water and food contaminated with faeces | sudden, severe watery diarrhoea, vomiting, dehydration | safe water, latrines, hand washing; **oral rehydration solution**; vaccine |
| **typhoid** | *Salmonella typhi* (bacterium) | contaminated food and water | long fever, abdominal pain, weakness | safe water and food, hygiene; antibiotics; vaccine |
| **tuberculosis (TB)** | *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (bacterium) | droplets in air | cough for over 2 weeks, blood in sputum, weight loss, night sweats | **BCG** vaccine, ventilation; **6-month** course of drugs |
| **HIV/AIDS** | human immunodeficiency virus | unprotected sex, infected blood and needles, mother to child | weakened immunity; repeated infections (AIDS) | abstinence, faithfulness, condoms, testing, sterile needles; **antiretroviral therapy** (no cure) |
| **Ebola virus disease** | Ebola virus | blood and body fluids of sick or dead people; infected animals | fever, weakness, vomiting, diarrhoea, bleeding | isolation, protective clothing, **safe burials**, hand washing; vaccine |
| **Lassa fever** | Lassa virus | food and surfaces contaminated by **rat** urine and droppings; body fluids | fever, sore throat, weakness, sometimes bleeding | rat-proof food storage, clean homes; early treatment (ribavirin) |
| **measles** | measles virus | droplets in air | fever, rash, cough, red eyes | **measles vaccine** |
| **river blindness** | *Onchocerca* (worm) | bite of blackfly near fast rivers | itching, skin nodules, blindness | ivermectin (mass treatment), control of blackflies |
| **bilharzia (schistosomiasis)** | *Schistosoma* (flatworm) | larvae from water snails enter skin | blood in urine or stool | avoid infested water, latrines; praziquantel |
| **hookworm** | hookworm | larvae enter **bare feet** from soil contaminated with faeces | anaemia, tiredness | wear shoes, use latrines; deworming |

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Ebola and safe burials.** During the 2014–2016 epidemic, Sierra Leone recorded about 14,000 cases and nearly 4,000 deaths. The body of a person who has died of Ebola is highly infectious, so traditional washing and touching of the dead spread the virus. **Safe and dignified burials**, carried out by trained teams with protective equipment and respect for the family's wishes, were one of the measures that ended the epidemic.
:::

## Malaria in detail

![The malaria life cycle: an infected female Anopheles injects sporozoites; they multiply in the liver; merozoites invade red blood cells, which burst every 2–3 days causing fever; a mosquito takes up gametocytes; they fuse in its gut and form new sporozoites in its salivary glands](https://wasscetutor.com/img/biology/malaria-cycle.svg)

- Only the **female** *Anopheles* bites (she needs blood to make eggs); she breeds in **stagnant water** and bites mainly at **night**.
- The regular bursting of red blood cells releases parasites and wastes, causing the **cycles of fever and chills**, and destroys red cells, causing **anaemia**.
- Children under five and pregnant women are at greatest risk.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**"Malaria is caused by mosquitoes."** The **cause** (pathogen) is the protozoan *Plasmodium*. The mosquito is the **vector** that carries it. Examiners mark this distinction.
:::

:::callout{type=tip}
**Malaria control targets each stage:** nets and repellents stop the **bite**; draining puddles, clearing gutters and larvicides stop **breeding**; indoor spraying kills **adult mosquitoes**; ACTs kill the **parasite** in the blood; prompt testing finds cases early.
:::

## Non-infectious diseases

| Disease | Cause |
|---|---|
| **kwashiorkor** | lack of **protein** (swollen belly and legs, thin reddish hair) |
| **marasmus** | lack of **energy and protein** (very thin, wasted) |
| **rickets** | lack of **vitamin D** or calcium (soft, bent bones) |
| **scurvy** | lack of **vitamin C** (bleeding gums) |
| **night blindness** | lack of **vitamin A** |
| **goitre** | lack of **iodine** (swollen thyroid) |
| **anaemia** | lack of **iron** (also caused by malaria and hookworm) |
| **sickle cell disease** | inherited (genotype SS) |
| **hypertension, type 2 diabetes** | linked to diet (salt, sugar, fat), obesity, inactivity |
| **lung cancer** | strongly linked to **smoking** |

## The body's defences

- **Skin**: a barrier; sweat and oils contain chemicals that kill microbes.
- **Mucus and cilia** in the airways trap and sweep out microbes and dust.
- **Stomach acid** kills microbes in food.
- **Tears and saliva** contain the enzyme lysozyme.
- **Blood clotting** seals wounds.
- **White blood cells**: **phagocytes** engulf microbes; **lymphocytes** make **antibodies** and give **immunity**.

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Distinguish between a pathogen and a vector, using malaria as an example."}
The **pathogen** causes the disease: *Plasmodium*. The **vector** carries it between hosts: the female *Anopheles* mosquito.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Name the cause and two methods of preventing cholera."}
Cause: the bacterium *Vibrio cholerae*. Prevention: any two of safe (boiled or chlorinated) drinking water, proper latrines, hand washing with soap, food hygiene, vaccination.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. How is Lassa fever spread, and how can it be prevented?"}
By food or surfaces contaminated with the urine or droppings of infected rats, and by body fluids of sick people. Prevent by storing food in rat-proof containers, keeping homes clean, and taking care with sick people's body fluids.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Why does malaria cause anaemia?"}
The parasites multiply inside **red blood cells** and burst them, so the number of red cells falls.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Give the cause of (a) kwashiorkor, (b) rickets, (c) goitre."}
(a) lack of protein (b) lack of vitamin D or calcium (c) lack of iodine.
:::

## Key points

- **Infectious** diseases are caused by **pathogens**; **non-infectious** ones by deficiency, inheritance, lifestyle or age.
- Spread by **air, water and food, vectors, body fluids, animals, contact**.
- Know the **cause, spread, signs, prevention and treatment** of malaria, cholera, typhoid, TB, HIV, Ebola, Lassa fever and measles.
- *Plasmodium* is the **pathogen**; the female *Anopheles* is the **vector**.
- Deficiency diseases: **kwashiorkor, marasmus, rickets, scurvy, goitre, anaemia**.
- Defences: **skin, mucus, acid, enzymes, clotting, white blood cells**.
