English has **24 consonant sounds**, written with 21 consonant letters, and once again the spelling and the sound often disagree. *Ch* sounds different in *church*, *chemistry* and *machine*; *th* is two different sounds in *thin* and *this*; the *-ed* at the end of *walked*, *played* and *wanted* is pronounced three different ways. This lesson teaches all 24 consonants with their symbols, the sounds Sierra Leonean speakers most often change, the rules for *-ed* and *-s* endings, consonant clusters and silent letters: everything the Oral English questions test.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name and recognise the 24 consonant sounds and their symbols
- tell voiced from voiceless consonants
- pronounce *-ed* and *-s* endings correctly and explain the rule
- identify consonant clusters and silent letters
- avoid the consonant substitutions common in West African English

## The 24 consonants

:::callout{type=definition}
A **consonant** is a sound made by **blocking or narrowing** the air somewhere in the mouth or throat: with the lips (/p/, /m/), the tongue and teeth (/θ/), the tongue and the roof of the mouth (/k/), and so on.

A consonant is **voiced** if your vocal cords vibrate (put your fingers on your throat and say /z/: you feel a buzz), and **voiceless** if they don't (say /s/: no buzz).
:::

### The voiceless–voiced pairs

| Voiceless | Example | Voiced | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| /p/ | **p**en, ha**pp**y | /b/ | **b**ed, ru**bb**er |
| /t/ | **t**ea, wal**ked** | /d/ | **d**og, play**ed** |
| /k/ | **c**at, **k**ey, **ch**emistry, que**ue** | /g/ | **g**o, **gh**ost |
| /f/ | **f**an, **ph**one, lau**gh** | /v/ | **v**an, o**f** |
| /θ/ | **th**in, **th**ink, ma**th**s | /ð/ | **th**is, **th**ey, mo**th**er |
| /s/ | **s**it, **c**ity, gla**ss** | /z/ | **z**oo, i**s**, dog**s** |
| /ʃ/ | **sh**oe, na**ti**on, ma**ch**ine, **s**ugar | /ʒ/ | mea**s**ure, vi**s**ion, plea**s**ure, bei**ge** |
| /tʃ/ | **ch**urch, wa**tch**, na**t**ure | /dʒ/ | **j**udge, a**g**e, sol**d**ier |

### The other eight (all voiced)

| Symbol | Example words |
|---|---|
| /m/ | **m**an, su**mm**er, cli**mb** |
| /n/ | **n**o, **kn**ife, **gn**at |
| /ŋ/ | si**ng**, thi**n**k, fi**ng**er |
| /h/ | **h**at, **wh**o |
| /l/ | **l**eg, ba**ll** |
| /r/ | **r**ed, **wr**ite |
| /w/ | **w**et, **wh**ite, q**u**een |
| /j/ | **y**es, **u**se (/juːz/), b**eau**ty |

:::callout{type=tip}
The symbol /j/ is the **y** sound in *yes*, not the *j* in *judge* (that is /dʒ/). And /ŋ/ is the single sound at the end of *sing*: there is no separate /g/ in *singer*, though there is one in *finger* (/ˈfɪŋɡə/).
:::

## Sounds Sierra Leonean speakers often change

:::callout{type=local-example}
These substitutions are natural in Krio and in much West African English. In the examination you must recognise the **standard** sounds:

| Standard sound | Often said as | Examples to practise |
|---|---|---|
| /θ/ (*thin*) | /t/ | *thin/tin, three/tree, thought/taught, both/boat* |
| /ð/ (*this*) | /d/ | *this/dis, they/day, then/den, breathe/breed* |
| /ʒ/ (*measure*) | /ʃ/ or /dʒ/ | *measure, pleasure, television, usual, garage* |
| final clusters | dropped consonant | *test* (not *tes*), *desk* (not *des*), *asked* /ɑːskt/ (not *aks*) |
| /ʃ/ and /tʃ/ | swapped | *shoe/chew, share/chair, wash/watch* |
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Hearing the spelling.** In *"Thomas"* and *"Thames"* the *th* is just /t/. In *"thyme"* it is also /t/. But in *"think"* it is /θ/ and in *"that"* it is /ð/. Do not assume every *th* is the same sound.
:::

| Spelling | Sounds | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **ch** | /tʃ/, /k/, /ʃ/ | **ch**urch / **ch**emistry, **ch**aracter / ma**ch**ine, **ch**ef |
| **c** | /k/, /s/ | **c**at, **c**ome / **c**ity, **c**entre |
| **g** | /g/, /dʒ/, /ʒ/ | **g**o / **g**iant, a**g**e / bei**g**e |
| **s** | /s/, /z/, /ʃ/, /ʒ/ | **s**it / i**s**, ro**s**e / **s**ugar / mea**s**ure |
| **th** | /θ/, /ð/, /t/ | **th**ink / **th**is / **Th**omas |
| **gh** | /f/, /g/, silent | lau**gh** / **gh**ost / ni**gh**t |

## The -ed ending: three sounds

| Rule | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| after **/t/ or /d/** | /ɪd/ (an extra syllable) | want**ed**, need**ed**, start**ed**, decid**ed** |
| after a **voiceless** sound (/p/, /k/, /f/, /s/, /ʃ/, /tʃ/, /θ/) | /t/ | walk**ed**, stopp**ed**, wash**ed**, laugh**ed**, watch**ed** |
| after a **voiced** sound (vowels and /b/, /g/, /v/, /z/, /m/, /n/, /l/, /r/…) | /d/ | play**ed**, rain**ed**, lov**ed**, begg**ed**, call**ed** |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
*Which word has its **-ed** pronounced /ɪd/? A. jumped B. waited C. cried D. washed*

**B. waited**: the verb ends in /t/, so *-ed* adds a syllable: /ˈweɪtɪd/. *Jumped* and *washed* end in /t/, *cried* in /d/.
:::

## The -s ending: three sounds

The same logic applies to plural nouns, possessives and verbs with *he/she/it*:

| Rule | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| after **hissing sounds** (/s/, /z/, /ʃ/, /ʒ/, /tʃ/, /dʒ/) | /ɪz/ (an extra syllable) | bus**es**, rose**s**, wish**es**, church**es**, judge**s** |
| after other **voiceless** sounds | /s/ | cat**s**, book**s**, cup**s**, laugh**s** |
| after **voiced** sounds | /z/ | dog**s**, bag**s**, day**s**, pen**s**, girl**s** |

## Consonant clusters

:::callout{type=definition}
A **consonant cluster** is two or more consonant **sounds** together with no vowel between them: **str**eet (/str/), **spl**ash (/spl/), te**xts** (/ksts/), si**xth** (/ksθ/).
:::

Count **sounds**, not letters: *ph* in *photo* is one sound (/f/), and *ck* in *back* is one sound (/k/), so neither is a cluster.

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Which word begins with /θ/? A. those B. thought C. Thomas D. then"}
**B. thought**. *Those* and *then* begin with /ð/; *Thomas* with /t/.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Which word contains /ʒ/? A. sure B. pressure C. leisure D. issue"}
**C. leisure** (/ˈleʒə/). *Sure*, *pressure* and *issue* contain /ʃ/.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. In which word is -ed pronounced /t/? A. played B. needed C. kicked D. called"}
**C. kicked**: the verb ends in the voiceless /k/. *Played* and *called* take /d/; *needed* takes /ɪd/.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. In which word is the plural ending pronounced /ɪz/? A. books B. boxes C. bags D. hats"}
**B. boxes**: *box* ends in /s/ (the x is /ks/), a hissing sound, so the ending adds a syllable.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Which word has a silent letter? A. bomb B. bump C. band D. bulb"}
**A. bomb**: the final *b* is silent.
:::

:::reveal{title="6. Which word has the same 'ch' sound as 'chef'? A. chair B. machine C. school D. choose"}
**B. machine**: /ʃ/. *Chair* and *choose* have /tʃ/; *school* has /k/.
:::

## Key points

- English has **24 consonant sounds**; many come in **voiceless–voiced pairs**.
- /θ/ and /ð/ (*thin*, *this*), /ʒ/ (*measure*) and **final clusters** are the sounds most often changed in West African speech.
- **-ed**: /ɪd/ after /t, d/; /t/ after voiceless sounds; /d/ after voiced sounds.
- **-s**: /ɪz/ after hissing sounds; /s/ after voiceless; /z/ after voiced.
- Count **sounds**, not letters, for clusters, and learn the common **silent letters**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect questions asking which word contains a given **consonant sound** or symbol, which word has the same sound as **underlined letters**, how an **-ed** or **-s** ending is pronounced, and which word has a **silent letter** or a **cluster**. Many distractors are chosen because their **spelling** matches but their **sound** does not, so always say the word in your head.
:::
