*"If it rains, the match will be cancelled."* *"If I were the President, I would build more schools."* *"If you had studied, you would have passed."* Each sentence imagines a condition and its result, but each talks about a different kind of situation: a real possibility, an unreal dream, a past that cannot be changed. These are the **conditionals**, and mixing up their tenses is one of the commonest grammar errors in WASSCE scripts. This lesson teaches all four types, mixed conditionals, and the alternatives to *if*.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- form and use the zero, first, second and third conditionals
- choose the right conditional for real, unreal and past situations
- use *were* correctly in the second conditional
- form mixed conditionals
- use *unless*, *provided that*, *as long as* and inversion

## The structure

:::callout{type=definition}
A conditional sentence has two clauses:

- the **if-clause** (the condition): *If it rains,*
- the **main clause** (the result): *the match will be cancelled.*

Either can come first. When the *if*-clause comes first, put a **comma** after it: *If it rains, the match will be cancelled.* / *The match will be cancelled if it rains.*
:::

## The four types

| Type | Situation | If-clause | Main clause | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Zero** | always true; facts, science | present simple | present simple | *If you heat ice, it **melts**.* |
| **First** | a real possibility in the future | present simple | **will** + verb | *If it **rains**, we **will stay** indoors.* |
| **Second** | unreal or unlikely, now or in the future | past simple | **would** + verb | *If I **had** a car, I **would drive** to Bo.* |
| **Third** | unreal past: it didn't happen | past perfect | **would have** + past participle | *If you **had studied**, you **would have passed**.* |

### Zero conditional: facts

> *If you **mix** blue and yellow, you **get** green.*
> *If water **reaches** 100 °C, it **boils**.*

*If* can be replaced by *when* without changing the meaning.

### First conditional: real future possibilities

> *If you **study** hard, you **will pass** the WASSCE.*
> *If the lorry **comes** early, we **will reach** Kenema before dark.*

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**"Will" in the if-clause.** *"If it **will rain**, we will stay indoors"* is wrong. The if-clause takes the **present** tense even though it refers to the future: *"If it **rains**…"*
:::

### Second conditional: unreal or unlikely

> *If I **won** the lottery, I **would build** a hospital in my village.* (I probably won't.)
> *If I **were** you, I **would apologise**.* (I am not you.)

:::callout{type=tip}
With *I, he, she, it*, formal English uses **were**, not *was*, in the second conditional: *"If I **were** the principal…"*, *"If she **were** here…"* This is often tested. *"If I was…"* is common in speech but marked wrong in the examination.
:::

### Third conditional: the unreal past

> *If the driver **had slowed** down, the accident **would not have happened**.* (He didn't slow down; it happened.)
> *If I **had known** about the test, I **would have revised**.* (I didn't know; I didn't revise.)

## Mixed conditionals

Sometimes a past condition has a **present** result, or a present situation explains a past result:

| Mix | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| past condition → present result | *if* + past perfect, *would* + verb | *If I **had taken** the job, I **would be** rich now.* |
| present condition → past result | *if* + past simple, *would have* + past participle | *If I **were** braver, I **would have spoken** up yesterday.* |

## Alternatives to "if"

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **unless** | if … not | ***Unless** you hurry, you will miss the bus.* (= If you don't hurry…) |
| **provided (that) / providing** | only if | *You can go **provided that** you finish your work.* |
| **as long as** | only if | *I'll lend you my book **as long as** you return it.* |
| **in case** | as a precaution | *Take an umbrella **in case** it rains.* |
| **otherwise** | if not | *Leave now; **otherwise**, you'll be late.* |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Double negative with "unless".** *"Unless you **don't** study, you will fail"* is wrong: *unless* already means *if not*. Write *"**Unless** you study, you will fail."*
:::

### Inversion (formal)

In formal writing, *if* can be dropped and the subject and auxiliary inverted:

| With *if* | Inverted |
|---|---|
| *If I **had** known…* | ***Had I** known…* |
| *If you **should** need help…* | ***Should you** need help…* |
| *If I **were** you…* | ***Were I** you…* |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Conditionals are natural in essays about change in Sierra Leone: *"If the government **built** more feeder roads, farmers **would sell** more of their produce."* (second: a hope) / *"If the drainage **had been cleared**, the floods **would not have destroyed** so many homes."* (third: a regret about the past). Choosing the right type shows control of tense, which earns accuracy marks.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Complete: 'If you ____ (put) your hand in fire, it burns.'"}
**put** (zero conditional: a general truth).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Complete: 'If she ____ (pass) the WASSCE, she will go to Fourah Bay College.'"}
**passes** (first conditional).
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Correct: 'If I was the principal, I will ban phones.'"}
*If I **were** the principal, I **would** ban phones.* (second conditional)
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Complete: 'If we ____ (leave) earlier, we would have caught the bus.'"}
**had left** (third conditional).
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Rewrite with 'unless': 'If you don't apologise, she won't forgive you.'"}
***Unless you apologise**, she won't forgive you.*
:::

:::reveal{title="6. Rewrite with inversion: 'If I had known you were ill, I would have visited you.'"}
***Had I known** you were ill, I would have visited you.*
:::

## Key points

- **Zero**: present + present (facts). **First**: present + *will* (real future).
- **Second**: past + *would* (unreal or unlikely); use **were** for all persons.
- **Third**: past perfect + *would have* + past participle (unreal past).
- **Never** put *will* or *would* in the *if*-clause.
- **Unless** = *if not* (no extra negative); **provided that, as long as, in case, otherwise**; formal **inversion** (*Had I known…*).

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Structure questions test the **verb form** in one clause of a conditional: the classic traps are *will* in the if-clause, *was* instead of *were*, and the wrong form in the third conditional (*would had*, *would have went*). In essays, consistent conditionals show accurate control of tense, which counts under **Mechanical accuracy**.
:::
