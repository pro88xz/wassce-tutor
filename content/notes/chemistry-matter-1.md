Everything around you, from the air you breathe to the rice in your bowl, is **matter**. Chemists explain the behaviour of matter by imagining it made of tiny particles: atoms, molecules and ions. This lesson introduces those particles and the three ways they come together: as elements, compounds and mixtures.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define matter and describe the evidence that it is made of particles
- distinguish atoms, molecules and ions
- distinguish elements, compounds and mixtures, and metals from non-metals
- state the differences between compounds and mixtures
- distinguish physical changes from chemical changes

## Matter and its particles

:::callout{type=definition}
**Matter** is anything that has **mass** and occupies **space** (has volume). It exists as **solid, liquid or gas**.
:::

The **particulate theory of matter** says matter is made of very small particles that are always moving.

**Evidence for particles:**

- **Diffusion:** the smell of fried fish or perfume spreads through a room, because particles move and mix.
- **Dissolving:** sugar "disappears" in tea because its particles spread out between the water particles.
- **Brownian motion:** pollen grains or smoke specks jiggle randomly, pushed by invisible moving particles.

| Particle | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Atom** | The smallest particle of an element that can take part in a chemical reaction | Na, Fe, C |
| **Molecule** | The smallest particle of an element or compound that can exist on its own, made of two or more atoms joined together | O₂, H₂, H₂O, CO₂ |
| **Ion** | An atom or group of atoms carrying an electric charge | Na⁺, Cl⁻, SO₄²⁻, NH₄⁺ |

## Elements, compounds and mixtures

![Particle diagrams: an element has one kind of atom; a compound has atoms of different elements joined in a fixed ratio; a mixture has different substances not joined together](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/particles-elements-compounds.svg)

*Figure 1. Elements, compounds and mixtures at the particle level. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=definition}
- An **element** is a substance that **cannot be split into simpler substances** by chemical means. It contains only one kind of atom.
- A **compound** is a substance formed when two or more elements are **chemically combined in a fixed ratio by mass**.
- A **mixture** contains two or more substances **not chemically combined**, in any proportion.
:::

### Metals and non-metals

| Metals | Non-metals |
|---|---|
| Shiny (lustrous) | Usually dull |
| Good conductors of heat and electricity | Poor conductors (except graphite) |
| Malleable and ductile | Brittle when solid |
| Usually high melting points | Usually low melting points |
| Sonorous (ring when struck) | Not sonorous |
| Form **basic** oxides | Form **acidic** or neutral oxides |
| Form **positive** ions | Form **negative** ions (or share electrons) |
| Examples: Fe, Al, Cu, Na, Au | Examples: C, O, N, S, Cl |

Some elements, such as silicon, have properties of both and are called **metalloids**.

### Compounds and mixtures compared

| Compound | Mixture |
|---|---|
| Elements **chemically combined** | Substances **not chemically combined** |
| **Fixed composition** (water is always 2 H : 1 O) | **Variable composition** (any amount of salt in water) |
| Properties **differ** from those of its elements | Each part keeps its **own properties** |
| Separated only by **chemical** means | Separated by **physical** means (filtration, distillation) |
| Formation usually gives out or takes in **heat** | Usually little or no heat change on mixing |
| **Sharp, fixed** melting and boiling points | Melts and boils over a **range** |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Iron and sulphur.** Iron filings and yellow sulphur powder are mixed.

- **Mixture:** a magnet pulls out the iron, and both keep their properties.
- **Heated:** the mixture glows and forms black **iron(II) sulphide**, FeS, a **compound**.
- **After heating:** a magnet no longer picks out the iron, the product is not yellow, and it reacts with dilute acid to give **hydrogen sulphide**, which smells of rotten eggs. Iron alone gives hydrogen, which has no smell.

The new properties show a chemical change has made a compound.
:::

## Physical and chemical changes

| Physical change | Chemical change |
|---|---|
| **No new substance** forms | **New substance(s)** form |
| Usually **easily reversed** | Usually **not easily reversed** |
| Mass of substance unchanged | Total mass is conserved, but substances change |
| Little energy change | Often large energy change (heat, light) |
| Examples: melting ice, dissolving salt, boiling water, magnetising iron, crushing stones | Examples: burning charcoal, rusting, cooking an egg, fermenting palm wine, souring of milk |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Making **gari** involves both kinds of change. **Grating and pressing** the cassava are physical changes. **Fermentation**, which sours the pulp and breaks down poisonous cyanide compounds, is a chemical change. **Roasting** dries it (physical) and partly cooks the starch (chemical).
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Dissolving is a physical change**, even though the solid "disappears". Evaporating the water gets the salt back unchanged. Students often wrongly call it a chemical change.
:::

## Key points

- **Matter** has mass and occupies space; it is made of moving particles: **atoms, molecules and ions**.
- **Elements** have one kind of atom; **compounds** have elements chemically combined in a **fixed ratio**; **mixtures** are not chemically combined.
- **Metals** are shiny conductors that form positive ions and basic oxides; **non-metals** form negative ions and acidic oxides.
- **Physical changes** make no new substance; **chemical changes** make new substances.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
A favourite question is **"State four differences between a compound and a mixture"**; another is **classifying changes as physical or chemical** with reasons. Expect to define atom, molecule and ion with examples, and to compare metals with non-metals.
:::

## Quick check

1. Define a molecule and give one example.
2. Is air an element, a compound or a mixture? Give a reason.
3. Give two differences between a compound and a mixture.
4. Is the rusting of iron a physical or a chemical change? Why?

**Answers:**

1. The smallest particle of a substance that can exist on its own, made of two or more atoms joined together, e.g. O₂ or H₂O.
2. A mixture: its gases are not chemically combined and its composition varies.
3. Any two: chemically combined or not; fixed or variable composition; properties differ from or match the components; separated by chemical or physical means; sharp or range of melting point.
4. Chemical: a new substance (hydrated iron(III) oxide) forms and the change is not easily reversed.
