## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- present economic data in tables
- draw and read bar charts, pie charts and line graphs
- choose the right kind of chart for a set of data

## Why economists use tools

Economists work with large amounts of information: prices, output, incomes, population. Tables, charts and graphs make this information easier to read, compare and explain. WAEC expects you to **construct** these and to **interpret** them.

## Tables

A table arranges data in rows and columns. A good table has:

- a clear **title**
- **headings** for every column, with **units** (bags, NLe, %)
- the **source** of the data where it is known

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Rice sold by a shop in one week**

| Day | Bags sold |
|---|---|
| Monday | 12 |
| Tuesday | 8 |
| Wednesday | 10 |
| Thursday | 6 |
| Friday | 14 |
| **Total** | **50** |

From the table we can see at a glance that Friday was the busiest day and Thursday the quietest.
:::

## Bar charts

A **bar chart** shows quantities as bars of equal width; the **height** (or length) of each bar shows the value.

- Use it to **compare** separate items or periods, such as sales on different days.
- Leave equal gaps between the bars.
- Label both axes and start the value axis at zero.

A **component (subdivided) bar chart** splits each bar into parts, for example a family's spending each year divided into food, rent and transport.

## Pie charts

A **pie chart** is a circle divided into sectors. Each sector shows a **share of the whole**.

Because a full circle is 360°, each item's angle is:

$$\text{angle} = \dfrac{\text{value of the item}}{\text{total}} \times 360^\circ$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
A household spends NLe 600 a month:

| Item | Spending (NLe) | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| Food | 300 | $\dfrac{300}{600} \times 360^\circ = 180^\circ$ |
| Rent | 150 | $\dfrac{150}{600} \times 360^\circ = 90^\circ$ |
| Transport | 90 | $\dfrac{90}{600} \times 360^\circ = 54^\circ$ |
| School fees | 60 | $\dfrac{60}{600} \times 360^\circ = 36^\circ$ |
| **Total** | **600** | **360°** |

Always check that your angles add up to 360°.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Students often draw a pie chart without first working out the angles, or forget to check the total. Show your working in a table like the one above: examiners award marks for the calculation as well as the drawing.
:::

## Line graphs

A **line graph** joins plotted points with a line. It is best for showing how something **changes over time**, such as the price of rice month by month.

- Time goes on the **horizontal (x) axis**.
- The variable being measured goes on the **vertical (y) axis**.
- A rising line shows an increase; a falling line shows a decrease; a steep line shows fast change.

### Graphs of relationships

Economists also use graphs to show how two variables are related. A **demand curve**, for example, plots price against quantity demanded. When one variable rises as the other falls, the line slopes **downwards** (a negative or inverse relationship). When both rise together, it slopes **upwards** (a positive or direct relationship).

## Choosing the right tool

| You want to show… | Use |
|---|---|
| Exact figures for reference | A table |
| A comparison between separate items | A bar chart |
| Shares of a total | A pie chart (or a component bar chart) |
| A change over time | A line graph |
| The relationship between two variables | A graph with both variables on the axes |

## Key points

- Every table, chart and graph needs a **title**, **labelled axes or headings** and **units**.
- Pie chart angle = value ÷ total × 360°.
- Use bar charts to compare, pie charts for shares and line graphs for trends.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Data questions are common. You may be asked to **construct** a pie chart or bar chart from a table, or to **read** a graph and explain a trend. Use a ruler and protractor, label everything, give a title and show calculations. When interpreting, quote figures from the data ("sales rose from 6 bags on Thursday to 14 on Friday") rather than describing in general terms.
:::

## Quick check

1. A firm's total costs are NLe 2,000, of which wages are NLe 500. What angle would wages take in a pie chart?
2. Which chart is best for showing how the price of fuel changed over twelve months?
3. What does a downward-sloping line on a graph tell you about two variables?

**Answers:** 1. $\dfrac{500}{2000} \times 360^\circ = 90^\circ$. 2. A line graph. 3. They have an inverse relationship: as one rises, the other falls.
