Preparing an **adjusted cash book and bank reconciliation statement** is done in two steps. **First**, update the cash book with items that appear **only on the bank statement** (bank charges, standing orders, direct debits, direct credits, dishonoured cheques) and correct any **errors** in the cash book, to find the **adjusted cash book balance**. **Second**, prepare the **bank reconciliation statement**, which explains the remaining **timing differences** (unpresented cheques and uncredited lodgements) between the adjusted balance and the bank statement. This lesson works through a complete WASSCE-style question.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- decide which items go in the adjusted cash book and which in the reconciliation statement
- prepare an adjusted cash book
- prepare a bank reconciliation statement from the adjusted balance
- reconcile from the bank statement balance as a check
- deal with an overdraft in the reconciliation

## Worked example

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**On 31 March 2026 the bank column of the cash book of Koroma Enterprises showed a debit balance of NLe 18,400, but the bank statement showed a credit balance of NLe 20,600. On comparing them:**

1. Cheques paid to Sesay (NLe 3,200) and Bah (NLe 1,750) had not been presented.
2. Cash of NLe 2,600 paid in on 31 March had not been credited by the bank.
3. Bank charges of NLe 350 appeared only on the bank statement.
4. A standing order for insurance, NLe 1,200, appeared only on the bank statement.
5. Kamara paid NLe 2,400 directly into the bank account (direct credit), not yet in the cash book.
6. A cheque of NLe 1,000 from Bah had been dishonoured, shown only on the bank statement.

**Required:** (a) the adjusted cash book; (b) the bank reconciliation statement.
:::

**Step 1: sort the items**

| Item | Goes in |
|---|---|
| 1. Unpresented cheques, 4,950 | reconciliation statement |
| 2. Uncredited lodgement, 2,600 | reconciliation statement |
| 3. Bank charges, 350 | cash book (credit) |
| 4. Standing order, 1,200 | cash book (credit) |
| 5. Direct credit, 2,400 | cash book (debit) |
| 6. Dishonoured cheque, 1,000 | cash book (credit) |

**(a) Adjusted cash book (bank column)**

| Dr | NLe | Cr | NLe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Balance b/d | 18,400 | Bank charges | 350 |
| Kamara | 2,400 | Insurance | 1,200 |
| | | Bah (dishonoured) | 1,000 |
| | | Balance c/d | 18,250 |
| **Total** | **20,800** | **Total** | **20,800** |
| Balance b/d | 18,250 | | |

**Working:** $18\,400 + 2\,400 - 350 - 1\,200 - 1\,000 = 18\,250$

The dishonoured cheque is credited back to the cash book and **debited to Bah's account**: Bah owes the money again.

**(b) Bank reconciliation statement at 31 March 2026**

| | NLe |
|---|---|
| Balance as per adjusted cash book | 18,250 |
| Add: unpresented cheques (3,200 + 1,750) | 4,950 |
| | 23,200 |
| Less: uncredited lodgement | (2,600) |
| **Balance as per bank statement** | **20,600** |

The statement agrees with the bank statement balance of NLe 20,600 ✓.

**Check from the other direction:**

$20\,600 - 4\,950 + 2\,600 = 18\,250$ = adjusted cash book ✓

## Dealing with an overdraft

When the cash book shows a **credit** balance (an overdraft), treat it as a **negative** number:

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**The adjusted cash book shows an overdraft of NLe 3,000. Unpresented cheques are NLe 1,800 and uncredited lodgements NLe 700. Find the bank statement balance.**

$-3\,000 + 1\,800 - 700 = -1\,900$

The bank statement shows an **overdraft** (a **debit** balance) of NLe 1,900.
:::

## The adjusted balance in the final accounts

The **adjusted cash book balance** (NLe 18,250 above), **not** the bank statement balance, is the figure for **cash at bank** in the statement of financial position, because it includes all items the business has paid or received.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not put **unpresented cheques** or **uncredited lodgements** in the adjusted cash book: they are **already** in the cash book; the bank has simply not caught up. They belong only in the **reconciliation statement**. WAEC examiners have reported candidates losing marks for carrying the **wrong balance** from the adjusted cash book into the statement.
:::

## Key points

- **Step 1:** adjust the cash book for **bank-only items** (charges, standing orders, direct debits, direct credits, dishonoured cheques) and **errors**.
- **Step 2:** reconcile the **adjusted** balance: **add unpresented cheques, deduct uncredited lodgements**.
- Treat an **overdraft** as a negative balance.
- The **adjusted cash book balance** goes into the statement of financial position.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
"**Prepare the adjusted cash book and a bank reconciliation statement**" is one of the most frequently set Paper 2 questions. Sort the items first, balance the adjusted cash book, then start the statement from the **adjusted** balance and finish exactly on the bank statement figure.
:::

## Quick check

1. Which of these go in the adjusted cash book: bank charges, unpresented cheques, a direct credit?
2. The cash book shows NLe 9,000; bank charges are NLe 150 and a standing order NLe 600 is unrecorded. Find the adjusted balance.
3. From an adjusted balance of NLe 8,250, add unpresented cheques of NLe 1,300 and deduct an uncredited lodgement of NLe 900. Find the bank statement balance.
4. Why is a dishonoured cheque credited in the cash book?
5. Which balance appears as cash at bank in the statement of financial position?

**Answers:**

1. Bank charges and the direct credit.
2. $9\,000 - 150 - 600 = 8\,250$: NLe 8,250.
3. $8\,250 + 1\,300 - 900 = 8\,650$: NLe 8,650.
4. Because the money recorded as received has been taken back by the bank, so the bank balance is reduced and the customer owes the amount again.
5. The adjusted cash book balance.
