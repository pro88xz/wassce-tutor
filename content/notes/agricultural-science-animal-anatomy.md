To feed, breed and keep animals healthy, a farmer needs to know how their bodies work. Why can a goat live on grass and leaves while a pig needs grain? Why must chickens have grit? How does a hen form an egg every day? This lesson covers the parts of the body systems of farm animals that matter most in farming: the **digestive systems** of ruminants, non-ruminants and poultry, the **reproductive systems** of male and female animals and of the hen, and the **normal signs of health** that tell a farmer when an animal is sick.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the ruminant stomach and explain rumination
- compare digestion in ruminants, non-ruminants and poultry
- describe the digestive system of the fowl
- name the parts and functions of the male and female reproductive systems
- describe the reproductive system of the hen and how an egg is formed
- state the normal temperature, pulse and breathing rates of farm animals

## Digestion in ruminants

![The ruminant stomach: food passes from the oesophagus into the rumen, where microbes ferment cellulose; the reticulum forms the cud, which is brought up and rechewed; then the omasum absorbs water and the abomasum (true stomach) secretes enzymes and acid](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/ruminant-stomach.svg)

| Chamber | Function |
|---|---|
| **rumen** (paunch) | the largest chamber; **microbes** (bacteria and protozoa) **ferment cellulose** into volatile fatty acids that the animal absorbs for energy; microbes also make protein and B vitamins |
| **reticulum** (honeycomb) | forms food into balls (**cud**) that are returned to the mouth; traps heavy objects such as nails |
| **omasum** (manyplies) | absorbs water from the food |
| **abomasum** (true stomach) | secretes **hydrochloric acid and enzymes**; digests protein as in other animals |

:::callout{type=definition}
**Rumination** (chewing the cud): the ruminant swallows grass quickly, then later, while resting, brings the cud back up, **chews it again** thoroughly and swallows it. This breaks down the tough fibre so microbes can work on it.
:::

**Why ruminants can live on grass:** the rumen microbes digest **cellulose**, which no mammal can digest with its own enzymes, and they make protein from simple nitrogen sources such as **urea**. Ruminants also have no upper incisor teeth: they grip grass between their lower incisors and a hard **dental pad**.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Saying ruminants have "four stomachs".** They have **one stomach with four chambers**. Only the **abomasum** works like the stomach of a pig or a human.
:::

## Digestion in non-ruminants (pigs, rabbits)

Non-ruminants have a **simple stomach** like humans. They cannot digest much fibre, so they need **concentrated** feed: grains, roots, protein meals and kitchen waste. **Rabbits** have a large **caecum** where some fibre is fermented; they eat some of their own soft droppings to absorb the nutrients made there.

## The digestive system of the fowl

![The fowl's digestive system: beak, crop (stores and softens), proventriculus (enzymes and acid), gizzard (grinds with grit), small intestine (digests and absorbs), caeca and large intestine (water absorbed), cloaca](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/fowl-digestive.svg)

- **Beak**: picks up food; birds have **no teeth**.
- **Crop**: a bag in the neck that **stores and softens** food.
- **Proventriculus**: the glandular stomach; secretes **enzymes and acid**.
- **Gizzard**: a thick muscular organ that **grinds** food with **grit** (small stones) the bird swallows.
- **Small intestine**: digestion and absorption.
- **Caeca** (two blind pouches) and **large intestine**: some fermentation and water absorption.
- **Cloaca**: the common opening for droppings, urine (white urates) and eggs.

**Practical point:** birds kept indoors need **grit** (or shell) in the feed; without it, the gizzard cannot grind whole grains properly.

## Reproductive systems

### Male (bull, ram, buck, boar)

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **testes** (in the scrotum) | produce **sperm** and the male hormone testosterone; the scrotum keeps them cooler than the body |
| **epididymis** | stores and matures sperm |
| **vas deferens** (sperm duct) | carries sperm to the urethra |
| **accessory glands** (seminal vesicles, prostate, Cowper's glands) | produce fluid that carries and feeds sperm (semen) |
| **penis** | deposits semen in the female |

### Female (cow, ewe, doe, sow)

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **ovaries** | produce **eggs (ova)** and the hormones oestrogen and progesterone |
| **oviducts** (Fallopian tubes) | where **fertilisation** takes place |
| **uterus** (womb) | where the embryo develops; in pigs and rabbits it has long horns holding many young |
| **cervix** | the neck of the uterus; sealed during pregnancy |
| **vagina** and **vulva** | receive the penis at mating; the birth canal |

### The hen's reproductive system and egg formation

The hen has only **one functional ovary and oviduct** (the left). The yolk is released from the ovary and passes along the oviduct, which forms the rest of the egg in about **25 hours**:

| Part of oviduct | What happens |
|---|---|
| **infundibulum** | catches the yolk; **fertilisation** occurs here if the hen has mated |
| **magnum** | adds the **albumen** (egg white) |
| **isthmus** | adds the **shell membranes** |
| **uterus (shell gland)** | adds the **shell** (calcium carbonate) and colour; the longest stage |
| **vagina** and **cloaca** | the egg is laid |

A hen lays eggs **without a cock**; the cock is needed only for **fertile** eggs that will hatch. Layers need plenty of **calcium** in their feed for strong shells.

## Signs of health and normal values

A healthy animal is alert, eats and drinks well, moves freely, has a smooth shiny coat or feathers, bright eyes, normal droppings and normal breathing. Measuring vital signs helps spot disease early.

| Animal | Temperature (°C) | Pulse (beats/min) | Breathing (breaths/min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| cattle | about 38.5 | 40–80 | 10–30 |
| sheep and goats | about 39–39.5 | 70–90 | 12–30 |
| pig | about 39 | 60–80 | 10–20 |
| chicken | about 41–42 | 200–400 | 15–40 |

Temperature is taken with a thermometer in the **rectum** (cloaca in birds).

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Why goats survive the dry season.** In the long dry season, when grass is brown and scarce, village goats survive by **browsing** shrubs and tree leaves and eating dry crop residues. Their rumen microbes digest this fibrous food that a pig could not live on. Feeding goats a little **groundnut haulm** or cassava leaves in the dry season boosts their protein and keeps them in condition.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Name the four chambers of the ruminant stomach and state one function of each."}
Rumen: microbial fermentation of cellulose. Reticulum: forms the cud; traps heavy objects. Omasum: absorbs water. Abomasum: secretes acid and enzymes (true stomach).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. What is rumination?"}
The process in which a ruminant brings swallowed food (cud) back to the mouth, chews it again and re-swallows it.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. State the functions of the crop and the gizzard in the fowl."}
Crop: stores and softens food. Gizzard: grinds food with the help of grit.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. In which part of the hen's oviduct are (a) the albumen, (b) the shell added?"}
(a) magnum (b) uterus (shell gland).
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Give the functions of the testes and the ovaries."}
Testes: produce sperm and testosterone. Ovaries: produce eggs (ova) and the hormones oestrogen and progesterone.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Ruminant**: an animal, such as a cow, goat or sheep, with a four-chambered stomach that chews the cud.
- **Rumen**: the largest stomach chamber of a ruminant, where microbes ferment cellulose into fatty acids the animal absorbs for energy.
- **Reticulum**: the stomach chamber that forms food into balls of cud, which are returned to the mouth.
- **Omasum**: the stomach chamber that absorbs water from the food.
- **Abomasum**: the true stomach of a ruminant, which secretes hydrochloric acid and enzymes.
- **Oviduct**: the tube from the ovary to the uterus, where fertilisation takes place.
:::

## Key points

- Ruminant stomach: **rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum**; microbes digest cellulose; **rumination** rechews the cud.
- Non-ruminants have a **simple stomach** and need concentrated feed.
- Fowl: **beak, crop, proventriculus, gizzard (grit), intestines, caeca, cloaca**.
- Male: **testes, epididymis, vas deferens, accessory glands, penis**. Female: **ovaries, oviducts, uterus, cervix, vagina**.
- Hen: one ovary and oviduct; **infundibulum, magnum, isthmus, uterus, vagina**; an egg takes about 25 hours.
- Know the **normal temperature, pulse and breathing** rates.
