A looking glass, a car's side mirror, a periscope on a submarine and a dentist's little mirror all follow the same two laws of reflection. Learn them, and every mirror question becomes geometry.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the laws of reflection and distinguish regular and diffuse reflection
- describe the characteristics of the image in a plane mirror
- locate images by ray diagrams
- find the number of images in inclined mirrors and the effect of rotating a mirror
- explain the periscope and other uses of plane mirrors

## The laws of reflection

:::callout{type=definition}
1. The **angle of incidence** equals the **angle of reflection** ($i = r$).
2. The **incident ray**, the **reflected ray** and the **normal** at the point of incidence all lie in the **same plane**.
:::

Angles are measured from the **normal** (the line at 90° to the mirror), not from the mirror surface.

### Regular and diffuse reflection

- **Regular (specular)** reflection: a smooth, polished surface (a mirror, still water) reflects a parallel beam as a parallel beam, giving clear images.
- **Diffuse** reflection: a rough surface (paper, a wall) scatters the light in many directions. Each ray still obeys the laws of reflection, but the normals point in different directions. Diffuse reflection is what lets us see non-luminous objects from any angle.

## The image in a plane mirror

![A point object O in front of a plane mirror; rays reflect into the eye and appear to come from an image I behind the mirror, at the same distance d as the object](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/plane-mirror-image.svg)

*Figure 1. The image appears as far behind the mirror as the object is in front. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

The image formed by a plane mirror is:

1. **Virtual** (cannot be formed on a screen; the rays only appear to come from it)
2. **Upright** (erect)
3. **The same size** as the object
4. **As far behind** the mirror as the object is in front
5. **Laterally inverted** (left and right are swapped)

:::callout{type=local-example}
"AMBULANCE" is printed in mirror writing on the front of ambulances so that drivers ahead read it correctly in their rear-view mirrors. That is lateral inversion put to work.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A girl stands 3 m in front of a plane mirror. (a) How far is she from her image? (b) She walks 1 m towards the mirror. How far is she now from her image?**

(a) Image is 3 m behind the mirror: $3 + 3 = 6$ m.

(b) She is 2 m from the mirror; the image is 2 m behind it: $2 + 2 = 4$ m. (Moving 1 m closer brings her 2 m closer to her image.)
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
To see your **whole body** in a plane mirror, the mirror needs to be only **half your height**, whatever your distance from it.
:::

## Rotating a mirror

If a mirror is rotated through an angle θ while the incident ray stays fixed, the **reflected ray turns through 2θ**. This is used in sensitive meters (the mirror galvanometer) to magnify small rotations.

## Images in inclined mirrors

Two plane mirrors at angle θ to each other form multiple images:

$$n = \dfrac{360°}{\theta} - 1$$

| Angle between mirrors | Number of images |
|---|---|
| 90° | 3 |
| 60° | 5 |
| 45° | 7 |
| 0° (parallel) | infinitely many |

This is the principle of the **kaleidoscope** (mirrors at 60°).

## Uses of plane mirrors

- **Periscope**: two plane mirrors at **45°**, facing each other, in a tube. Light turns through 90° at each mirror, so you can see over a wall or crowd (or above water from a submarine). The image is **upright**, the same size and virtual.
- Looking glasses and dressing mirrors; the **rear-view mirror** inside a car
- Kaleidoscopes and decorations
- In **sextants** and **mirror galvanometers**
- The "infinite" corridor in barbers' shops with mirrors on opposite walls

## Key points

- Laws: $i = r$; incident ray, reflected ray and normal are in one plane.
- Plane-mirror image: virtual, upright, same size, same distance behind, laterally inverted.
- Rotating a mirror by θ turns the reflected ray by 2θ.
- Number of images in mirrors at θ: $n = \dfrac{360}{\theta} - 1$.
- Periscope: two mirrors at 45°.

Objective items on lateral inversion, mirror rotation (2θ) and image distances are very common.

## Quick check

1. A ray strikes a plane mirror at 30° to the mirror surface. What is the angle of reflection?
2. How many images are formed by two mirrors inclined at 72°?
3. A mirror is turned through 15°. Through what angle does the reflected ray turn?

**Answers:** 1. The angle of incidence (from the normal) is 60°, so the angle of reflection is 60°. 2. $\dfrac{360}{72} - 1 = 4$. 3. 30°.
