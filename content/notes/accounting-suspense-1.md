:::callout{type=definition}
A **suspense account** is a **temporary account** opened to hold the **difference** when the trial balance totals do not agree, so that the trial balance can be made to balance and the final accounts prepared while the errors are being found.
:::

When the errors are discovered, each is corrected through the journal, with one side of the correction going to the suspense account, until the **suspense account is closed** with a nil balance. Only errors that **affect the agreement of the trial balance** pass through the suspense account. This lesson explains when and how a suspense account is opened and which errors it holds.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a suspense account and explain why it is opened
- decide on which side the suspense balance appears
- identify which errors are corrected through the suspense account
- explain where an uncleared suspense balance appears in the final accounts

## Opening a suspense account

When the trial balance does not agree:

1. Find the **difference** between the debit and credit totals.
2. Enter it in a **suspense account** on the side that is **short**, so the totals agree.

| If the trial balance shows | Suspense balance |
|---|---|
| **credit** total larger than debit total | **debit** suspense (added to the debit column) |
| **debit** total larger than credit total | **credit** suspense (added to the credit column) |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A trial balance shows debits of NLe 89,600 and credits of NLe 90,300. Open the suspense account.**

Difference $= 90\,300 - 89\,600 = 700$. The debit side is short, so suspense is **debited** NLe 700.

| Difference in TB | 700 | | |

Both trial balance columns now total NLe 90,300.
:::

## Which errors go through suspense?

| Error | Through suspense? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal, compensating | **No** | debits and credits were equal; the trial balance agreed |
| One side of an entry missing | **Yes** | only one side needs correcting |
| Entry on the wrong side of one account only | **Yes** | one account is wrong by twice the amount |
| Casting error in one account or book | **Yes** | one total is wrong |
| Balance extracted wrongly into the trial balance | **Yes** | the trial balance itself is wrong |
| Different amounts on the two sides | **Yes** | the sides are unequal |

**A simple test:** if correcting the error needs an entry in **only one** real account, the other side of the correcting entry goes to **suspense**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Wages of NLe 900 paid in cash were entered in the cash book but not posted to the wages account. How is this corrected?**

Only the wages debit is missing. The correction is: **debit wages 900, credit suspense 900**. The cash book is already correct.
:::

## The suspense account in the final accounts

If the final accounts must be prepared **before** all errors are found, any remaining suspense balance is shown in the **statement of financial position**:

- a **debit** balance among the **current assets**,
- a **credit** balance among the **current liabilities**.

This is a temporary measure; the account should be cleared as soon as the errors are found.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not pass errors of omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal or compensating errors through the suspense account. They did not cause the trial balance difference, so their corrections do not touch suspense.
:::

## Key points

- A **suspense account** temporarily holds a **trial balance difference**.
- It is entered on the **short** side: credit total larger → **debit** suspense.
- Only errors that **affect the agreement** of the trial balance are corrected through suspense.
- If a correction needs only **one** real account, the other side goes to **suspense**.
- An uncleared balance appears in the **statement of financial position**.

Expect to **explain the purpose of a suspense account**, to **open it with the correct balance**, and to **say which errors pass through it**. Most suspense questions go on to require journal entries and the completed suspense account; see the next lesson.
:::

## Quick check

1. What is a suspense account?
2. Debits total NLe 52,400 and credits NLe 51,900. What is the suspense balance and on which side?
3. Would an error of commission be corrected through the suspense account? Why?
4. The sales day book was undercast by NLe 300. Give the correcting entry.
5. Where does an uncleared debit suspense balance appear?

**Answers:**

1. A temporary account that holds the difference in a trial balance until the errors causing it are found and corrected.
2. Difference $= 500$; the credit side is short, so a credit suspense balance of NLe 500.
3. No, because it affected debits and credits equally and did not cause a difference in the trial balance.
4. Debit suspense 300; credit sales 300.
5. In the statement of financial position, among the current assets.
