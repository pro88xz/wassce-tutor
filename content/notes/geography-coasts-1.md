:::callout{type=definition}
**Coastal landforms** are the features shaped by the sea where the land meets the ocean.
:::

**Landforms of coastal erosion** include **cliffs, wave-cut platforms, headlands and bays, caves, arches, stacks and stumps**; **landforms of coastal deposition** include **beaches, spits, bars, tombolos and lagoons**. They are made by waves, which erode the coast in some places and deposit sand and shingle in others. This lesson explains how waves work and how each landform is formed.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe how waves form and distinguish constructive from destructive waves
- explain the processes of marine erosion and longshore drift
- explain the formation of cliffs, wave-cut platforms, headlands, bays, caves, arches, stacks and stumps
- explain the formation of beaches, spits, bars, tombolos and lagoons
- describe the coast of Sierra Leone and West Africa and the problem of coastal erosion

## Waves

Waves are formed by **wind** blowing over the sea. Their size depends on the **strength of the wind**, **how long** it blows and the **fetch**, the distance of open water it blows over. As a wave reaches shallow water it slows, steepens and **breaks**: the rush of water up the beach is the **swash**, and its return is the **backwash**.

| Constructive waves | Destructive waves |
|---|---|
| low, gentle, widely spaced | high, steep, close together |
| strong swash, weak backwash | weak swash, strong backwash |
| **deposit** material, building beaches | **erode** and remove material |

## Marine erosion

| Process | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Hydraulic action** | the force of waves compresses air in cracks; when the wave retreats, the air expands explosively and weakens the rock |
| **Abrasion (corrasion)** | waves hurl sand and pebbles at the cliff, wearing it away |
| **Attrition** | pebbles knock against each other, becoming smaller and rounder |
| **Solution (corrosion)** | sea water dissolves rocks such as limestone |

## Landforms of erosion

*Figure 1. Landforms of coastal erosion and deposition. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

**Cliffs and wave-cut platforms:** waves attack the base of the land between high and low tide, cutting a **wave-cut notch**. The overhanging rock above collapses, and the **cliff** retreats inland. The flat, rocky surface left behind, exposed at low tide, is a **wave-cut platform**.

**Headlands and bays:** where bands of hard and soft rock meet the coast, the soft rock is eroded quickly into **bays**, while the hard rock is left jutting out as **headlands**.

**Cave → arch → stack → stump:**

1. Waves attack a crack or joint in a headland by hydraulic action and abrasion, enlarging it into a **cave**.
2. Two caves on opposite sides of the headland meet, or one cuts right through, forming an **arch**.
3. The roof of the arch is weakened and collapses, leaving an isolated pillar of rock: a **stack**.
4. The stack is worn down at its base until only a low **stump** remains, covered at high tide.

## Longshore drift

When waves approach the beach at an **angle**, the swash carries material up the beach at that angle, but the backwash pulls it straight back down the slope under gravity. The material therefore zigzags **along** the coast. This movement is **longshore drift**, and it supplies material for many depositional landforms.

## Landforms of deposition

| Landform | Formation |
|---|---|
| **Beach** | sand and shingle deposited by constructive waves, especially in sheltered **bays** |
| **Spit** | a long, narrow ridge of sand or shingle built out from the land by longshore drift, where the coast changes direction or at a river mouth; its end may curve inwards |
| **Bar** | a spit that grows right across a bay, joining two headlands |
| **Lagoon** | the area of calm water trapped behind a bar |
| **Tombolo** | a ridge of sand that joins an island to the mainland |
| **Sand dunes** | sand from the beach blown inland by onshore winds |

:::callout{type=local-example}
The **Freetown Peninsula** has a classic coastline of rocky **headlands** separating sandy **bays**, with beaches such as **Lumley**, **Tokeh** and **River No. 2**. Much of the rest of Sierra Leone's coast is low and fringed with **mangrove** swamps and creeks. Along the West African coast, longshore drift has built long **sand bars** enclosing **lagoons**, such as the Lagos Lagoon in Nigeria and the Keta Lagoon in Ghana.
:::

### Coastal erosion and its control

Rising sea levels, sand mining from beaches and the clearing of mangroves are speeding up coastal erosion in parts of West Africa. It can be reduced by:

- **sea walls** and **rock armour** (large boulders) to absorb wave energy
- **groynes**, low walls built out to sea, which trap sand moving by longshore drift
- **banning sand mining** from beaches
- **replanting mangroves**, whose roots trap sediment and break waves
- **beach nourishment** (adding sand)

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A **stack** is an isolated pillar of rock left when an **arch** collapses. A **stump** is the low remnant of a stack. Keep the sequence: crack → cave → arch → stack → stump.
:::

## Key points

- **Constructive** waves build beaches; **destructive** waves erode.
- Marine erosion: **hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, solution**.
- Erosion landforms: **cliffs, wave-cut platforms, headlands and bays, caves, arches, stacks, stumps**.
- **Longshore drift** moves material along the coast in a zigzag.
- Deposition landforms: **beaches, spits, bars, lagoons, tombolos, sand dunes**.
- Coastal erosion is controlled by **sea walls, groynes, rock armour, mangrove planting** and banning sand mining.

## Quick check

1. What is the fetch of a wave?
2. Distinguish constructive from destructive waves.
3. Put in order: stump, cave, stack, arch.
4. What is longshore drift?
5. How is a lagoon formed?

**Answers:**

1. The distance of open water over which the wind blows to form the wave.
2. Constructive waves are low with a strong swash and build beaches; destructive waves are high with a strong backwash and erode.
3. Cave, arch, stack, stump.
4. The zigzag movement of sand and shingle along a coast when waves approach at an angle.
5. A bar of sand grows across a bay, trapping calm water behind it.
