The **vegetation zones of West Africa** run in belts roughly parallel to the coast: **mangrove swamps** on the coast, **tropical rainforest** inland, then **Guinea savanna**, **Sudan savanna** and **Sahel savanna**, and finally the **Sahara desert**. The belts follow rainfall, which decreases northwards. Vegetation is enormously important to people, but human activities, especially farming, logging, charcoal burning and bush fires, have destroyed much of it. This lesson describes the belts, the value of vegetation and how it can be conserved.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the vegetation belts of West Africa and explain their pattern
- describe the Guinea, Sudan and Sahel savanna
- explain the economic and environmental importance of vegetation
- explain the causes and effects of deforestation and bush burning
- describe ways of conserving vegetation, with Sierra Leone examples

## The vegetation belts

![Vegetation belts from the Gulf of Guinea northwards: mangrove swamp, tropical rainforest (over 1,500 mm), Guinea savanna, Sudan savanna, Sahel savanna and Sahara desert (under 250 mm)](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/vegetation-belts-wa.svg)

*Figure 1. Vegetation belts of West Africa (simplified). WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Belt | Rainfall and dry season | Vegetation | Examples of plants |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mangrove swamp** | coastal, tidal | dense salt-tolerant trees with stilt roots | red and white mangroves |
| **Tropical rainforest** | over 1,500 mm; short dry season | tall evergreen forest, layered | mahogany, iroko, obeche, oil palm |
| **Guinea savanna** | 1,000–1,500 mm; dry season 4–5 months | tall grass (up to 3 m) with many trees; forest along rivers (gallery forest) | shea, locust bean, isoberlinia |
| **Sudan savanna** | 500–1,000 mm; dry season 6–8 months | shorter grass with scattered trees | baobab, acacia, doum palm |
| **Sahel savanna** | 250–500 mm; dry season 8–9 months | short, tufted grass and thorny shrubs | acacia, thorn scrub |
| **Desert** | under 250 mm | very sparse xerophytes; oases | date palm, cacti-like plants |

**Why the belts form:** going north, **rainfall decreases**, the **dry season lengthens** and the **temperature range increases**, so only plants able to survive longer droughts can grow. Human activity has pushed the belts' boundaries: much rainforest has become **derived savanna** after repeated clearing and burning.

## Importance of vegetation

**Economic:**

1. **Timber** for building, furniture and export.
2. **Fuelwood and charcoal**, the main cooking fuel for most households.
3. **Food:** fruits, nuts, leaves, bush meat, honey.
4. **Raw materials:** palm oil, rubber, shea butter, gum arabic, raffia, cane.
5. **Medicines** from herbs, bark and roots.
6. **Grazing** for cattle, sheep and goats in the savanna.
7. **Tourism** in parks and reserves.

**Environmental:**

1. **Protects the soil** from erosion by binding it with roots and breaking the force of rain.
2. **Adds humus** to the soil, keeping it fertile.
3. **Affects rainfall and humidity** through transpiration.
4. **Habitat** for wildlife and a store of biodiversity.
5. **Stores carbon**, slowing climate change.
6. **Protects water catchments**, keeping rivers flowing.

## Human impact on vegetation

| Activity | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Shifting cultivation and bush fallowing** with shorter fallows | forest replaced by farm bush and grassland |
| **Logging** (legal and illegal) | loss of valuable hardwoods and forest cover |
| **Charcoal burning and fuelwood cutting** | clearing around towns |
| **Bush burning** for farming and hunting | destroys young trees, kills soil organisms, exposes soil |
| **Mining** | clears forest and leaves pits and spoil heaps |
| **Settlement and roads** | permanent clearing |
| **Overgrazing** | loss of grass cover, leading to desertification in the Sahel |

**Effects of deforestation:** soil erosion and loss of fertility; floods and landslides; drying of streams; loss of wildlife and medicinal plants; reduced rainfall locally; more carbon dioxide in the atmosphere.

## Conserving vegetation

1. **Forest reserves and national parks** that protect forest by law.
2. **Afforestation and reforestation:** planting trees on bare or cleared land.
3. **Controlling logging** with licences, quotas and replanting.
4. **Agroforestry:** growing trees together with crops.
5. **Banning or controlling bush burning.**
6. **Alternative fuels**, such as improved stoves and gas, to reduce charcoal use.
7. **Education** of communities on the value of forests.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone protects important forests in national parks and reserves:

- **Gola Rainforest National Park** (declared in **2011**) in the east, one of the largest remaining blocks of Upper Guinea rainforest, home to forest elephants and pygmy hippos
- **Tiwai Island Wildlife Sanctuary** on the Moa River, famous for its primates
- **Outamba-Kilimi National Park** in the north, with savanna, forest and river habitats
- the **Western Area Peninsula National Park**, protecting the forested mountains that feed Freetown's water supply, under pressure from settlement and charcoal burning

Community forest projects around Gola link conservation with income, for example through cocoa grown under forest shade.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Savanna does not always have the same grass and trees. Grass is **tallest and trees most numerous** in the wetter **Guinea savanna**, and grass gets **shorter and trees fewer** northwards into the Sudan and Sahel savanna.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Mangrove swamp**: salt-tolerant trees growing on coastal tidal mud along estuaries and creeks.
- **Guinea savanna**: tall grass with scattered trees, where rainfall is 1,000–1,500 mm and the dry season lasts 4–5 months.
- **Sudan savanna**: shorter grass with fewer trees, where rainfall is 500–1,000 mm and the dry season lasts 6–8 months.
- **Sahel savanna**: short grass and thorn scrub, where rainfall is 250–500 mm and the dry season lasts 8–9 months.
- **Deforestation**: the clearing of forest by farming, logging, charcoal burning, mining, settlement and roads.
:::

## Key points

- West African belts: **mangrove → rainforest → Guinea savanna → Sudan savanna → Sahel savanna → desert**, following **decreasing rainfall**.
- Vegetation provides **timber, fuel, food, raw materials, medicine, grazing** and protects **soil, water and wildlife**.
- Threats: **farming, logging, charcoal, bush burning, mining, overgrazing**.
- Conservation: **parks and reserves, afforestation, controlled logging, agroforestry, fire control, alternative fuels, education**.
- Sierra Leone: **Gola Rainforest National Park** (2011), **Tiwai Island**, **Outamba-Kilimi**, **Western Area Peninsula National Park**.

## Quick check

1. Name the vegetation belts of West Africa from the coast to the Sahara.
2. Why does vegetation change northwards across West Africa?
3. State three economic uses of the rainforest.
4. Give two effects of deforestation.
5. Name two protected areas in Sierra Leone.

**Answers:**

1. Mangrove swamp, tropical rainforest, Guinea savanna, Sudan savanna, Sahel savanna, desert.
2. Rainfall decreases and the dry season lengthens northwards.
3. Any three: timber; fuelwood and charcoal; food; palm oil, rubber and other raw materials; medicines; tourism.
4. Any two: soil erosion; floods and landslides; loss of wildlife; reduced rainfall; drying of streams; more carbon dioxide.
5. Any two: Gola Rainforest National Park; Tiwai Island Wildlife Sanctuary; Outamba-Kilimi National Park; Western Area Peninsula National Park.
