The essay is the biggest single question in Paper 2: **50 marks**, half the paper. Most students write it the way they think an essay should be written. Strong students write it the way it is **marked**. This lesson opens up the WAEC marking scheme for continuous writing, shows what earns and loses marks under each heading, and takes one weak paragraph and rebuilds it into a strong one so you can see the difference.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the four headings of the essay marking scheme and the marks for each
- explain what examiners reward and penalise under each heading
- improve a paragraph's content, organisation, expression and accuracy
- plan your writing time so the essay gets the attention its marks deserve

## The marking scheme

:::callout{type=definition}
WAEC marks every piece of continuous writing (essay, letter, speech, article, report, story) under four headings:

| Heading | Marks | The examiner asks |
|---|---|---|
| **Content** | 10 | Are the ideas relevant, sufficient and well developed? Is every part of the question answered? |
| **Organisation** | 10 | Is the format right? Is there an introduction, clear paragraphs, a logical order, links, a conclusion? |
| **Expression** | 20 | Is the vocabulary precise and varied? Are the sentences varied and fluent? Is the tone right for the reader? |
| **Mechanical accuracy** | 10 | Are grammar, spelling and punctuation correct? |
:::

**Expression is worth twice as much as anything else.** That is good news: it is the part that improves most with practice.

## Content (10): relevant, sufficient, developed

### What earns marks

- **Relevance**: every paragraph answers the question as set.
- **Completeness**: if the question has two parts (*causes and solutions*, *advantages and disadvantages*), both are answered.
- **Development**: each idea is **explained** and **illustrated**, not just stated.

### What loses marks

- writing about the **topic** in general instead of the **question** asked
- a list of many undeveloped points
- memorised material that does not fit the question

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Question:** *Write a letter to your friend telling them about the changes in your school since they left.*

| Thin content | Developed content |
|---|---|
| We have a new library. We have a new principal. We have new rules. | The biggest change is the library. The old storeroom behind the science block has been painted, fitted with shelves and filled with donated books, and every student can now borrow two books a week. I have already read three novels this term, which I could never have afforded to buy. |

The developed version makes **one** point and makes it **real**: where, what, how it works, why it matters.
:::

## Organisation (10): the shape of the writing

Examiners look for:

1. **The correct format** for the type of writing: addresses and close for a letter, a vocative for a speech, a title and writer's name for an article. A missing part costs marks here.
2. **An introduction** that leads the reader into the topic.
3. **Paragraphs**, each with **one** main idea, in a sensible order.
4. **Linking words** that guide the reader: *firstly, moreover, however, as a result, on the other hand, finally*.
5. **A conclusion** that rounds the piece off.

## Expression (20): where good writing earns its reward

| What examiners reward | Weaker | Stronger |
|---|---|---|
| **Precise vocabulary** | The market was very crowded. | The market heaved with traders and shoppers. |
| **Varied sentences** | I woke up. I went to school. I was late. | I woke late, rushed through the rain to school, and arrived just as the gate was closing. |
| **Varied openings** | He… He… He… | Breathless, he… / At the gate, he… / Only then did he… |
| **Suitable register** | *"Hey Principal, wassup?"* | *"Dear Sir, I am writing to…"* |
| **Fluency** | ideas jump and repeat | ideas flow from one to the next |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Memorised "big English".** *"In this contemporary dispensation of our nation's socio-economic milieu, it is pertinent to note…"* Used without meaning, this impresses no one, and it often contains errors. **Clear, correct, lively English scores higher than decorated nonsense.**
:::

## Mechanical accuracy (10): the cost of errors

Each error in grammar, spelling or punctuation can cost you. These are the most common:

| Error | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Subject-verb agreement | *The number of students **are** rising.* | *The number of students **is** rising.* |
| Tense shift | *He came in and **sits** down.* | *He came in and **sat** down.* |
| Comma splice | *It was late, we went home.* | *It was late, **so** we went home.* |
| Confused words | ***Their** going to Bo.* | ***They're** going to Bo.* |
| Capital letters | *freetown, monday, english* | *Freetown, Monday, English* |
| Apostrophes | *the student**s** bag* (one student) | *the student**'s** bag* |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Krio shapes the everyday English of many Sierra Leoneans. That is natural in speech, but in the examination write **standard English**:

| Spoken pattern | Standard English |
|---|---|
| *I am coming* (meaning *I'll be back soon*) | I will be back soon. |
| *He has been sick since.* | He has been sick **for a long time** / **since Monday**. |
| *Me and him went.* | **He and I** went. |
| *I don't know whether he will come or not.* | I don't know whether he will come. |
:::

## One paragraph, rebuilt

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Question:** *Write an article for your school magazine on the dangers of drug abuse among young people.*

**Weak paragraph (as written by a candidate):**

> *drug abuse is very bad for the youths in our society today, it destroy there health and there future. Many youths take it. They become mad and they steal. Drug abuse is very bad.*

**Examiner's view:**

- **Content**: two points (health, crime) but neither is developed; *"very bad"* is repeated.
- **Organisation**: no topic sentence; it ends where it began.
- **Expression**: vague (*"very bad"*, *"mad"*), repetitive, flat sentences.
- **Accuracy**: no capital at the start, a comma splice, *destroy* (agreement), *there* (should be *their*) twice.

**Rebuilt paragraph:**

> *The first and most obvious danger of drug abuse is the damage it does to the body and mind. Substances such as kush, the cheap synthetic drug now sold on many street corners, can cause confusion, violent mood swings and lasting mental illness. Young people who were once bright and hard-working drop out of school, and families who cannot afford treatment watch helplessly as their sons and daughters waste away. Once health is gone, a young person's future often goes with it.*

It makes **one** point (damage to health), explains it, gives a **local, concrete** example, shows the **effect**, and ends with a sentence that links to the next idea (the future).
:::

## Length and time

The question paper states the minimum length (it has been **450 words** in recent years). Far less loses content and organisation marks; far more gives you more chances to make errors. Aim to pass the minimum comfortably with **developed** points, not repetition.

Because the essay is half the paper, give it about **half your writing time**, including five minutes to plan and five minutes to check.

## Key points

- **Content 10, Organisation 10, Expression 20, Mechanical accuracy 10**: 50 marks in all.
- **Content**: answer every part of the question with developed, relevant ideas.
- **Organisation**: correct format, introduction, one idea per paragraph, links, conclusion.
- **Expression**: precise words, varied sentences, the right tone; no empty "big English".
- **Accuracy**: check agreement, tense, punctuation, spelling and capitals.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
You choose **one** essay from several options, so choose the one you can **develop** best, not the one that sounds most impressive. Examiners mark the whole piece against all four headings. A well-organised, accurate essay with clear, developed ideas scores well even without rare vocabulary.
:::

## Quick check

:::reveal{title="1. Which heading carries the most marks?"}
**Expression**, with **20** marks.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. A letter has no address. Which heading loses marks?"}
**Organisation**, because format is part of organisation.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Correct: 'Each of the students have their own uniform.'"}
**Each of the students has his or her own uniform.** (*Each* is singular.)
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Why is a list of eight undeveloped points weaker than four developed paragraphs?"}
Content rewards **development**: explanation, examples and effects. A bare list shows little thought, and it usually earns low organisation and expression marks too.
:::
