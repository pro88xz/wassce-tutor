Supermarkets on Wilkinson Road, chain stores in several towns, phone shops selling on credit and online stores delivering by motorbike: large-scale retailing is growing fast in Sierra Leone. This lesson covers the main types of large-scale retailers, their merits and problems, and the modern trends changing how goods are sold.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe department stores, supermarkets, chain (multiple) stores and other large-scale retailers
- state the advantages and disadvantages of each
- describe mail order, online selling, vending machines and hire purchase
- explain modern trends in retailing

## Large-scale retailers

### Department stores

:::callout{type=definition}
A **department store** is a large shop divided into **separate departments**, each selling a different class of goods (clothing, furniture, electronics, cosmetics) under one roof and one management.
:::

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Wide variety under one roof: "one-stop shopping" | High running costs, so prices are often high |
| Comfortable surroundings and extra services (cafés, delivery) | Usually located in city centres, far from many homes |
| Economies of scale in buying | Impersonal service |
| Each department can be managed by a specialist | Little or no credit to ordinary customers |

### Supermarkets

:::callout{type=definition}
A **supermarket** is a large **self-service** store selling mainly food and household goods, with goods displayed on open shelves and paid for at checkout counters.
:::

**Features:** self-service; wide range of branded and packaged goods; prices clearly marked; checkout counters; often car parking; cash, card and mobile money payment.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Customers choose freely and compare prices | No credit; little personal service |
| Lower prices from bulk buying | Impulse buying may lead customers to overspend |
| Saves time: quick shopping | High capital and running costs |
| Clean, hygienic and well organised | Shoplifting risk |
| Fewer staff needed per customer | Located mainly in towns |

### Chain stores (multiple shops)

:::callout{type=definition}
**Chain stores (multiple shops)** are a group of similar shops, usually ten or more, owned and controlled by **one firm**, selling similar goods at the same prices, with the same appearance, in different towns or areas.
:::

**Advantages:** bulk buying and lower prices; standard quality and prices everywhere; risks spread across many branches; central advertising; brand recognition. **Disadvantages:** branch managers have little freedom; stock may not suit every local area; impersonal service; heavy central costs.

### Other forms of retailing

| Form | How it works | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mail order** | Customers choose from catalogues or adverts and goods are sent by post or courier | Shop from home; reaches remote buyers | Cannot inspect goods; delivery delays and costs |
| **Online (internet) stores** | Customers order through a website or app; pay online or on delivery | Open 24 hours; wide choice; easy price comparison | Fraud risk; need internet and delivery services |
| **Vending machines** | Machines sell drinks, snacks or airtime when coins, notes or cards are inserted | Sells 24 hours; no sales staff | Breakdowns, vandalism, limited range |
| **Discount stores** | Sell at low prices with few services | Cheap goods | Limited service and comfort |
| **Franchises** | A firm (franchisee) uses another's brand and methods for a fee | Known brand; proven system | Fees; little independence |

### Hire purchase and credit sales

Many large retailers sell expensive goods (motorbikes, phones, refrigerators, furniture) on **hire purchase**: the buyer pays a **deposit** and then **instalments**, takes the goods at once, and becomes the owner only after the **last instalment**. (See *Legal Aspects of Business* for the law and calculations.)

## Modern trends in retailing

1. **Self-service**, which cuts staff costs and lets customers browse freely.
2. **Branding and packaging**: goods sold under brand names in attractive, informative packs.
3. **Electronic payment**: cards, POS terminals and **mobile money** replace cash.
4. **Online shopping and delivery**, including selling through WhatsApp and Facebook.
5. **Barcodes and computerised stock control**, so shops reorder automatically.
6. **After-sales service and guarantees** on electrical goods.
7. **Shopping malls**: many shops in one building with parking and security.
8. **Loyalty cards and promotions** to keep customers returning.
9. **Longer opening hours**, including weekends and holidays.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Freetown's supermarkets and shopping centres now sit alongside the traditional markets. Many shops accept mobile money, and small sellers advertise on WhatsApp status and deliver by *okada* or tricycle. Large and small retailers increasingly use the same digital tools.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A **chain store** is not the same as a **department store**. A chain store is **many similar shops** in different places under one owner; a department store is **one large shop** with many departments.
:::

## Key points

- **Department stores**: many departments under one roof; **supermarkets**: self-service food and household goods; **chain stores**: many similar branches under one owner.
- Large retailers gain from **bulk buying and economies of scale** but offer **less personal service and credit**.
- **Mail order, online stores, vending machines, discount stores and franchises** are other forms.
- Trends: **self-service, branding, electronic and mobile payment, online selling, barcodes, malls**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **describe the features** of a supermarket, department store or chain store, to **state their advantages and disadvantages**, to **compare large and small retailers**, and to **explain modern trends in retailing**. Define each type in one sentence before listing its features.
:::
