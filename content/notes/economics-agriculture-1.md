Most families in Sierra Leone depend on farming, yet the country still imports much of the rice it eats. Agriculture feeds the nation, employs most of its people and earns foreign exchange, but it is held back by problems WASSCE expects you to explain, and to solve.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the importance of agriculture to West African economies
- describe the main farming systems and land tenure systems
- explain the problems facing agriculture in West Africa
- suggest measures to improve agricultural production

## Why agriculture matters

:::callout{type=definition}
**Agriculture** is the growing of crops and the rearing of animals (including fishing and forestry) for food, raw materials and income.
:::

| Contribution | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Food** | Feeds a growing population and reduces costly food imports |
| **Employment** | Employs the majority of Sierra Leone's workers, mostly as smallholders |
| **Raw materials** | Supplies industry: palm kernels for oil mills, cocoa, rubber, timber, cotton |
| **Foreign exchange** | Exports such as cocoa and coffee earn dollars to pay for imports |
| **Government revenue** | Export duties and taxes on agricultural trade |
| **Market for industry** | Farmers buy tools, fertiliser, clothing and other goods |
| **Capital for development** | Savings from farm incomes can be invested in other sectors |

## Farming systems

| System | Features | Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Subsistence farming** | Small plots, family labour, hand tools; grown mainly for the family | Feeds the family; low cost | Low output; little surplus to sell |
| **Commercial farming** | Grown mainly for sale; larger scale | Higher income; uses modern methods | Needs capital; market risk |
| **Shifting cultivation / bush fallow** | Land farmed for a few years, then left to recover | Restores soil fertility naturally | Needs lots of land; wasteful as population grows |
| **Mixed farming** | Crops and animals on the same farm | Manure for crops, crop waste for animals; spreads risk | Needs more skill and management |
| **Plantation agriculture** | Large estates, one cash crop, hired labour | High output and quality for export | Needs huge capital; often foreign-owned |
| **Mechanised farming** | Tractors and machines | Large output, less labour | Expensive; needs spare parts and skills |

### Land tenure

**Land tenure** is the system by which land is owned and used.

- **Communal or family ownership**: land belongs to the community or extended family and is allocated by chiefs or family heads. This is common in the provinces of Sierra Leone.
- **Individual (freehold) ownership**: one person owns the land outright, as in much of the Western Area.
- **Leasehold**: land is rented for a fixed period, often to investors.

Communal tenure gives everyone access to land, but it can **discourage investment** (a farmer may not plant tree crops on land he does not own), makes land **hard to use as security for loans** and leads to **fragmented plots** and disputes.

## Problems of agriculture in West Africa

1. **Simple tools and methods**: hoes and cutlasses limit the area a family can farm.
2. **Lack of credit**: farmers cannot borrow to buy seed, fertiliser or machines, partly because they lack collateral.
3. **Land tenure problems**: fragmentation, disputes and insecure rights.
4. **Poor storage and processing**: much of the harvest rots or is eaten by pests after harvest.
5. **Poor transport**: bad feeder roads raise costs and cut farmers off from markets.
6. **Pests, diseases and weather**: floods, drought and late rains destroy crops.
7. **Ageing farmers and youth migration**: young people leave for towns.
8. **Poor marketing and low prices**: middlemen take a large share; prices swing widely (see the next lesson).
9. **Low use of improved seeds and fertiliser**, and weak **extension services** to teach new methods.
10. **Illiteracy** among farmers, which slows the spread of new ideas.

## Improving agriculture

| Problem | Measure |
|---|---|
| Lack of credit | Agricultural banks, cooperatives and microfinance; loans on easy terms |
| Simple methods | Mechanisation on suitable land; tractor-hire services |
| Low yields | Improved seeds, fertiliser (sometimes subsidised), research |
| Ignorance of new methods | Extension services and farmer training |
| Storage losses | Silos, drying floors, processing centres |
| Poor roads | Building and maintaining feeder roads |
| Weather | Irrigation and swamp development for dry-season farming |
| Marketing | Cooperatives, guaranteed prices, better market information |
| Youth migration | Make farming profitable and attractive; rural amenities |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone has large areas of **inland valley swamps** suited to rice, yet much rice is imported. The **Sierra Leone Agricultural Research Institute (SLARI)** develops improved rice and cassava varieties, and the government's **Feed Salone** programme (launched in 2023) aims to raise food production and cut rice imports through mechanisation, irrigation and support for farmers.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
When asked for "measures to improve agriculture", do not just list "give farmers money". Explain the **mechanism**: credit lets farmers buy fertiliser and improved seed, which raises yields per acre.
:::

## Key points

- Agriculture provides **food, jobs, raw materials, foreign exchange and revenue**, and a market for industry.
- Farming systems range from **subsistence and shifting cultivation** to **plantations and mechanised farming**.
- **Communal land tenure** gives access to land but can discourage investment and borrowing.
- Problems include **simple tools, lack of credit, poor storage and transport, pests and weather, and poor marketing**; each has a matching remedy.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **explain the importance of agriculture** to your country, **describe the problems of agriculture** in West Africa and **suggest measures** to solve them, and to **compare farming systems** or explain **land tenure**. Match each problem with a specific solution, and use Sierra Leonean crops and examples.
:::

2. Why may communal land tenure discourage investment?
3. State two causes of low agricultural output in West Africa.
4. How does irrigation help farmers?

Farmers may not invest in land they do not own, and cannot use it as security for loans. 3. Any two: simple tools, lack of credit, poor storage, poor transport, pests and diseases, bad weather, land tenure problems, ageing farmers. 4. It provides water in the dry season, so farmers can grow more than one crop a year and are protected from drought.
