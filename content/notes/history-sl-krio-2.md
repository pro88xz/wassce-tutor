:::callout{type=definition}
The **Krio** are the people of Sierra Leone descended from the four groups who settled in Freetown between **1787 and the 1860s**: the **Black Poor**, the **Nova Scotians**, the **Jamaican Maroons**, and, above all, the **Liberated Africans (recaptives)**.
:::

They developed a distinct culture with the **Krio language**, Christian and Muslim faith, and a strong love of **Western education**. Through **Fourah Bay College (1827)** and mission schools, Freetown became the "**Athens of West Africa**", and Krio teachers, clergy, doctors, lawyers and traders worked all along the West African coast. Leading Krio included **Bishop Samuel Ajayi Crowther**, **Dr James Africanus Horton** and **Sir Samuel Lewis**. This lesson explains **the origin of the Krio people, their culture and their contribution to Sierra Leone and West Africa**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the origin of the Krio people
- describe the main features of Krio culture
- explain the role of education and Fourah Bay College
- describe the contributions of leading Krio to Sierra Leone and West Africa
- explain the Krio diaspora along the West African coast
- explain why Krio influence declined in the late nineteenth century

## Origin of the Krio

![Four groups joined to form the Krio: the Black Poor from London (1787), the Nova Scotians (1792), the Jamaican Maroons (1800) and the Liberated Africans (1808 to the 1860s), the largest group. Krio culture combined the Krio language, mostly Christian religion with Muslim Krio (Aku), and education and work as teachers, clergy, lawyers, doctors and traders](https://wasscetutor.com/img/history/krio-origins.svg)

*Figure 1. Where the Krio came from. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

At first the **settlers** (Nova Scotians and Maroons) looked down on the **recaptives**, and the two groups lived apart. But the recaptives soon **prospered** through farming, trade and education, **intermarried** with settlers, and adopted many settler customs. By the middle of the nineteenth century the two groups had merged into a single people, the **Krio**.

## Features of Krio culture

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **Language** | **Krio**, an English-based creole with words from **Yoruba**, Portuguese, French and other African languages; today it is the **lingua franca** of Sierra Leone |
| **Religion** | most Krio are **Christians** (Anglican, Methodist and others); a Muslim community, the **Aku** (Oku), descended mainly from **Yoruba Muslim recaptives**, lives at **Fourah Bay** and **Fula Town** |
| **Education** | high value placed on schooling and professions |
| **Customs** | **awujoh** feasts for the ancestors; Yoruba-derived societies and masquerades such as the **Hunting societies** and **Ojeh**; Western-style dress such as the **kabaslot** gown |
| **Names and homes** | English family names, and **board houses** with verandas, built in a style influenced by the Caribbean and North America |
| **Food** | dishes such as **fufu**, **cassava leaves** and **potato leaves** plasas, and Yoruba-derived foods such as **agidi**, **olele** and **akara** |

## Education: the "Athens of West Africa"

| Institution | Founded | Importance |
|---|---|---|
| **Fourah Bay College** | **1827**, by the CMS | first Western-style college in West Africa; its first student was **Samuel Ajayi Crowther**; affiliated to **Durham University** in **1876**, so it could award British degrees |
| **CMS Grammar School** | **1845** | first secondary school in West Africa |
| **Annie Walsh Memorial School** | **1849** | one of the first girls' secondary schools in West Africa |
| **Methodist Boys' High School** | **1874** | trained many professionals |

## Leading Krio and recaptives

| Person | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Samuel Ajayi Crowther (about 1809–1891)** | Yoruba recaptive; first student of Fourah Bay College; translated the Bible into Yoruba; **first African Anglican bishop (1864)** |
| **James Africanus Beale Horton (1835–1883)** | army surgeon and writer; argued in *West African Countries and Peoples* (1868) for **self-government** for West Africa, an early nationalist idea |
| **Sir Samuel Lewis (1843–1903)** | lawyer and politician; **first Mayor of Freetown (1895)**; among the first Africans to be **knighted (1896)** |
| **Adelaide Casely-Hayford (1868–1960)** | educator and campaigner; founded a **girls' vocational school** in Freetown (1923) |
| **I. T. A. Wallace-Johnson (1894–1965)** | journalist, trade unionist and nationalist (see the topic on nationalism) |

## The Krio diaspora

From the 1830s and 1840s, many Krio and recaptives **left Freetown** to work and trade along the coast:

1. **Nigeria:** recaptives returned to **Lagos** and **Abeokuta**, where they were called **"Saro"**; they included traders, clergy and teachers, and their descendants included nationalists such as **Herbert Macaulay** (Crowther's grandson).
2. **Gold Coast, The Gambia, Fernando Po, Cameroon** and elsewhere: Krio served as **clerks, teachers, missionaries, doctors and traders** for colonial governments, missions and firms.
3. They spread **Christianity, Western education and the English language** across West Africa.

## Decline of Krio influence

In the late nineteenth century Krio influence declined because:

1. **Racist attitudes** grew among Europeans; Africans were pushed out of **senior posts** in the civil service, church and medicine (for example, the **West African Medical Staff**, created in **1902**, excluded African doctors).
2. **European and Lebanese firms** took over much of the trade.
3. After the **Protectorate (1896)**, the colonial government paid more attention to the hinterland and the chiefs.
4. In the **Hut Tax War (1898)** many Krio traders and missionaries in the Protectorate were killed, and **mistrust** grew between the Colony and the Protectorate.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Krio is now spoken by almost everyone in Sierra Leone as a **first or second language**, even though the Krio people themselves are a small minority. It is the language of markets, music, radio and everyday life across all ethnic groups, a lasting contribution of the Krio to national unity.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not say that the Krio are "Europeans" or "not Africans". The Krio are **Africans**, descended mainly from **African recaptives and settlers of African descent**, who created a new African culture in Freetown.
:::

## Key figures and terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figures and terms"}
- **Athens of West Africa**: the name given to Freetown for its schools and college, which educated people from all over West Africa.
- **Saro**: the name given in Nigeria to Krio and recaptives from Sierra Leone who settled in Lagos and Abeokuta.
- **Aku**: Krio of Yoruba descent, many of them Muslims.
:::

## Key points

- The **Krio** descend from the **Black Poor, Nova Scotians, Maroons** and, above all, the **Liberated Africans**.
- Culture: **Krio language**, Christianity and **Aku Muslims**, education, **awujoh**, distinctive dress, food and houses.
- **Fourah Bay College (1827)**, **CMS Grammar School (1845)**, **Annie Walsh (1849)**: Freetown the "**Athens of West Africa**".
- Leading figures: **Crowther, Horton, Samuel Lewis, Adelaide Casely-Hayford, Wallace-Johnson**.
- The **Krio diaspora** ("Saro" in Nigeria) spread education and Christianity; influence declined with **racism**, competition and the **Protectorate**.

**Answers:**

1. The Black Poor, the Nova Scotians, the Jamaican Maroons and the Liberated Africans.
2. Muslim Krio, descended mainly from Yoruba Muslim recaptives, living at Fourah Bay and Fula Town.
3. In 1827; Samuel Ajayi Crowther.
4. Because of its many schools and Fourah Bay College, which educated Africans from all over West Africa.
5. Krio and recaptives who moved from Sierra Leone to Lagos and Abeokuta in Nigeria.
