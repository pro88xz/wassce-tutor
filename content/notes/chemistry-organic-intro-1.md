More than ninety per cent of all known compounds contain carbon: the fuels in cars and kitchens, plastics, medicines, soap, palm oil and every molecule in your body. **Organic chemistry** is the chemistry of carbon compounds. Its huge size becomes manageable once you learn to sort compounds into families called **homologous series**, each defined by a **functional group**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define organic chemistry and name the main sources of organic compounds
- explain why carbon forms so many compounds
- define a homologous series and state its characteristics
- identify the functional groups of the main homologous series
- distinguish molecular, empirical, structural and displayed formulas
- distinguish saturated and unsaturated, aliphatic and aromatic compounds

## What organic chemistry is

:::callout{type=definition}
**Organic chemistry** is the study of the compounds of **carbon**, except a few simple ones (carbon oxides, carbonates, hydrogencarbonates and cyanides), which are treated as inorganic.
:::

**Sources of organic compounds:**

- **petroleum** (crude oil) and **natural gas**: the main source today
- **coal**
- **plants and animals**: oils and fats (palm oil), sugar, starch, cellulose, proteins, rubber
- **synthesis** in laboratories and factories

## Why carbon forms so many compounds

1. **Tetravalency:** carbon has four outer electrons and forms **four covalent bonds**.
2. **Catenation:** carbon atoms join to each other in **long chains, branches and rings**, more than any other element.
3. **Multiple bonds:** carbon forms **single, double and triple** bonds (C–C, C=C, C≡C).
4. **Bonds with other elements:** strong bonds with hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, halogens and sulphur.
5. **Isomerism:** the same formula can have several different structures.

## Homologous series

:::callout{type=definition}
A **homologous series** is a family of organic compounds with the **same functional group** and **general formula**, in which each member differs from the next by a **CH₂** group.
:::

**Characteristics of a homologous series:**

1. All members have the **same general formula** (e.g. alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂).
2. Successive members differ by **–CH₂–** (relative molecular mass 14).
3. They have the **same functional group**, so similar **chemical properties**.
4. **Physical properties change gradually** (boiling point and density rise with chain length).
5. They can be **prepared by similar methods**.

:::callout{type=definition}
A **functional group** is the atom or group of atoms that gives a homologous series its **characteristic chemical properties**.
:::

| Series | Functional group | General formula | First member |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alkanes** | C–C (single bonds only) | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | Methane, CH₄ |
| **Alkenes** | C=C | CₙH₂ₙ | Ethene, C₂H₄ |
| **Alkynes** | C≡C | CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ | Ethyne, C₂H₂ |
| **Alkanols** (alcohols) | –OH (hydroxyl) | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH | Methanol, CH₃OH |
| **Alkanoic acids** | –COOH (carboxyl) | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH | Methanoic acid, HCOOH |
| **Alkanoates** (esters) | –COO– | RCOOR′ | Methyl methanoate |
| **Halogenoalkanes** | –X (Cl, Br, I) | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁X | Chloromethane, CH₃Cl |

## Types of formula

| Formula | Shows | Ethanol |
|---|---|---|
| **Molecular** | Actual number of each atom | C₂H₆O |
| **Empirical** | Simplest whole-number ratio | C₂H₆O (here the same) |
| **Structural (condensed)** | How atoms are grouped | CH₃CH₂OH |
| **Displayed** | **Every** atom and **every** bond | H–C–C–O–H with all H's drawn (Figure 1) |

## Classifying organic compounds

| Saturated | Unsaturated |
|---|---|
| Only **single** C–C bonds | At least one **C=C or C≡C** bond |
| Alkanes | Alkenes, alkynes |
| Undergo **substitution** | Undergo **addition**; decolourise bromine water |

- **Aliphatic** compounds have open chains (alkanes, alkenes, alkanols).
- **Aromatic** compounds contain a **benzene ring** (C₆H₆), a stable ring of six carbon atoms. Benzene compounds are found in dyes, drugs and plastics.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Palm oil**, used in nearly every Sierra Leonean kitchen, is a mixture of organic compounds called **triglycerides** (esters). Its red colour comes from carotene, another organic compound and the source of vitamin A. Through soap making, margarine and biodiesel, it links organic chemistry to local industry.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Not every carbon compound is organic. **Carbon(IV) oxide, carbonates and cyanides** are studied as **inorganic** compounds.
:::

## Key points

- **Organic chemistry** is the chemistry of carbon compounds (except oxides, carbonates and cyanides).
- Carbon forms millions of compounds through **tetravalency, catenation, multiple bonds and isomerism**.
- A **homologous series** has the **same general formula and functional group**, members differing by **CH₂**, with similar chemical and gradually changing physical properties.
- Functional groups: **C=C** (alkenes), **C≡C** (alkynes), **–OH** (alkanols), **–COOH** (alkanoic acids), **–COO–** (esters).
- **Saturated** = single bonds only (substitution); **unsaturated** = C=C or C≡C (addition).

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **define a homologous series** and **list its characteristics**, to **name functional groups** and identify the series of given compounds, to **give general formulas**, and to **explain why carbon forms so many compounds**. Writing displayed formulas correctly earns marks in many organic questions.
:::

## Quick check

1. State three characteristics of a homologous series.
2. What is the functional group of the alkanols?
3. Write the general formula of the alkenes.
4. Distinguish saturated from unsaturated hydrocarbons.

**Answers:**

1. Any three: same general formula; same functional group; successive members differ by CH₂; similar chemical properties; gradual change in physical properties; similar methods of preparation.
2. The hydroxyl group, –OH.
3. CₙH₂ₙ.
4. Saturated hydrocarbons have only single C–C bonds; unsaturated ones contain at least one C=C or C≡C bond.
