The **land of West Africa**, with its belts of **desert, savanna and forest**, its great rivers such as the **Niger**, and its long **Atlantic coast**, strongly shaped its **history**. The open **savanna** favoured **trade, horses and large empires** such as Ghana, Mali and Songhai; the **forest** supported smaller but rich **kingdoms** such as Asante and Benin; the **Sahara** linked West Africa to North Africa through the **trans-Saharan trade**; and the **coast** brought contact with **Europeans** from the fifteenth century. This lesson explains how geography influenced the history of West Africa.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the location and main physical regions of West Africa
- explain how the savanna favoured trade and large states
- explain how the forest influenced states and economies
- explain the role of the Sahara, the rivers and the coast in West African history
- explain the effect of disease, especially the tsetse fly, on history

## West Africa's physical setting

West Africa lies between the **Sahara Desert** in the north and the **Atlantic Ocean and Gulf of Guinea** in the south and west. Its main zones from north to south are:

| Zone | Features | Historical importance |
|---|---|---|
| **Sahara (desert)** | very dry; oases | a barrier, but crossed by **camel caravans** in the trans-Saharan trade |
| **Sahel and Sudan savanna** | grassland, open country | easy movement, **horses**, cattle; **trading cities** and **empires** grew here |
| **Guinea savanna** | taller grass and trees | farming; links between forest and Sahel |
| **Tropical forest** | dense trees, heavy rain | **kola**, gold, palm products; **forest kingdoms**; tsetse fly limited horses |
| **Coast** | mangroves, lagoons, harbours | fishing; salt; **European trading forts** from the 15th century |

## How geography shaped history

1. **Savanna and empires:** the open grasslands allowed **cavalry** to move quickly, so rulers could conquer and control large areas. The great **Sudanese empires** (Ghana, Mali, Songhai) and the **Hausa states** and **Kanem-Bornu** grew in the savanna.
2. **The Sahara and trade:** the desert was crossed by **camel caravans** linking West Africa with **North Africa**, bringing **salt, cloth, horses and Islam** south and taking **gold, kola and slaves** north. Cities such as **Timbuktu** and **Gao** grew at the "ports" of the desert.
3. **The River Niger:** a highway for **canoe transport** and a source of water and fish; many cities and states grew along it (Djenné, Timbuktu, Gao).
4. **The forest:** rich in **gold** (Asante) and **kola nuts**, it supported strong kingdoms, but **dense vegetation** and the **tsetse fly**, which kills horses and cattle, made large cavalry armies impossible.
5. **The coast:** from the fifteenth century, Europeans arrived by **sea**. Coastal peoples became **middlemen** in the Atlantic trade, and coastal states such as **Dahomey** rose in power.
6. **Minerals:** **gold** from Bambuk, Bure and the Akan forest drew traders across the Sahara and later to the coast (the "Gold Coast").

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's position on the Atlantic coast, with one of the largest natural harbours in Africa at the mouth of the Sierra Leone River, made it a regular stopping place for European ships from the fifteenth century. This same harbour later made Freetown the base for the Royal Navy's anti-slavery squadron and a major port.
:::

## Disease and history

The **tsetse fly**, found in the forest and wetter savanna, carries **sleeping sickness**, deadly to horses and cattle. This:

- limited the use of **horses** in the forest, so forest states relied on **foot soldiers**;
- set a southern limit to the **cavalry empires** of the savanna;
- later slowed **European penetration** inland, together with **malaria**, which earned West Africa the name "the white man's grave".

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not describe the Sahara only as a barrier. For more than a thousand years it was also a **bridge**, crossed by organised caravans that linked West Africa to North Africa and the Mediterranean world.
:::

## Key figures and terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figures and terms"}
- **Sahel**: the belt of dry grassland on the southern edge of the Sahara, where horses, trade and trading cities helped large empires grow.
- **Oasis**: a fertile place in the desert with water, where trans-Saharan caravans could rest and take on supplies.
- **Tsetse fly**: an insect of the forest zone that spreads disease to horses and cattle, so forest states could not rely on cavalry.
:::

## Key points

- Zones: **desert, Sahel and Sudan savanna, Guinea savanna, forest, coast**.
- **Savanna:** horses, trade, cities and **large empires**.
- **Sahara:** crossed by **camel caravans** in the trans-Saharan trade.
- **Forest:** gold, kola, **forest kingdoms**; **tsetse fly** limited horses.
- **Coast:** **European contact** and the **Atlantic trade** from the 15th century.

## Quick check

1. Why did large empires develop in the savanna?
2. How did the tsetse fly affect forest states?
3. Name two goods that crossed the Sahara from north to south.
4. Why was the River Niger important to Sudanese states?
5. Why did Europeans first make contact with coastal peoples?

**Answers:**

1. The open grassland allowed easy movement, cavalry and trade, so rulers could conquer and control large areas.
2. It killed horses and cattle, so forest states could not use large cavalry armies and relied on foot soldiers.
3. Any two: salt; cloth; horses; copper; books; glassware.
4. It provided transport, water and fish, and many trading cities grew along it.
5. Because they arrived by sea, and inland travel was difficult because of disease and dense forest.
