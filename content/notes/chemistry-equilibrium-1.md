Most reactions you have met so far go one way: once wood has burned, it cannot "unburn". But many reactions can run **both forwards and backwards**. In a closed container they settle into a balance where both directions continue at the same rate. Understanding this **dynamic equilibrium** is the key to making ammonia, sulphuric acid and many other industrial chemicals efficiently.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- distinguish reversible from irreversible reactions, with examples
- explain what is meant by dynamic equilibrium
- state the characteristics of a system at equilibrium
- interpret graphs of concentration and rate against time as equilibrium is reached
- describe simple reversible reactions that show colour changes

## Reversible and irreversible reactions

:::callout{type=definition}
A **reversible reaction** is one that can go in **both directions**: the products can react to re-form the reactants. It is shown by the sign **⇌**.
:::

| Irreversible (one way) | Reversible |
|---|---|
| Burning charcoal, cooking an egg, rusting | Heating hydrated copper(II) sulphate |
| Neutralisation of a strong acid by a strong alkali | Thermal decomposition of ammonium chloride |
| Most precipitation reactions | Haber process (ammonia), Contact process (SO₃) |

**Examples of reversible reactions:**

1. **Hydrated copper(II) sulphate:**

$$\text{CuSO}_4\cdot5\text{H}_2\text{O(s)} \rightleftharpoons \text{CuSO}_4\text{(s)} + 5\text{H}_2\text{O(l)}$$

Heating drives off water: blue → white. Adding water reverses it: white → blue, with heat given out.

2. **Ammonium chloride:**

$$\text{NH}_4\text{Cl(s)} \rightleftharpoons \text{NH}_3\text{(g)} + \text{HCl(g)}$$

On heating it decomposes; the gases recombine as white solid on the cool upper part of the test tube.

3. **Making ammonia:**

$$\text{N}_2\text{(g)} + 3\text{H}_2\text{(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3\text{(g)}$$

## Dynamic equilibrium

When a reversible reaction takes place in a **closed system** (nothing can enter or leave):

1. At first, only the **forward** reaction occurs, quickly.
2. As products build up, the **backward** reaction starts and speeds up, while the forward reaction slows (reactants are used up).
3. Eventually **the forward and backward rates become equal**. From then on, the **concentrations stay constant**.

![Concentration against time: reactant concentration falls and product concentration rises until both become constant at equilibrium](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/equilibrium-time.svg)

*Figure 1. Concentrations level off when equilibrium is reached. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=definition}
**Dynamic equilibrium** is the state of a reversible reaction in a closed system in which the **rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the backward reaction**, so the **concentrations of reactants and products remain constant**.
:::

It is called **dynamic** because both reactions **are still happening**; they simply cancel each other out. Think of someone walking up a downward-moving escalator at the same speed: they stay in one place, but are still moving.

### Characteristics of a system at equilibrium

| Characteristic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Closed system** | Equilibrium can be set up only if no substance escapes |
| **Rates equal** | Forward rate = backward rate |
| **Concentrations constant** | But **not necessarily equal**; there may be much more reactant than product, or the reverse |
| **Dynamic** | Particles keep reacting in both directions |
| **Reached from either side** | The same equilibrium mixture forms whether you start with reactants or with products (same conditions) |
| **Macroscopic properties constant** | Colour, pressure and density stop changing |
| **Affected by conditions** | Changing concentration, pressure or temperature shifts the position (next lesson) |

## Seeing equilibrium: colour changes

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Chromate and dichromate.** In solution:

$$2\text{CrO}_4^{2-}\text{(aq)} + 2\text{H}^+\text{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}\text{(aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{O(l)}$$

The left side (CrO₄²⁻) is yellow and the right side (Cr₂O₇²⁻) is orange.

- Add **acid** (more H⁺): the solution turns **orange**, as the equilibrium shifts to the right.
- Add **alkali** (which removes H⁺): it turns **yellow**, as the equilibrium shifts to the left.

The change can be reversed again and again, which shows the reaction is reversible.
:::

Another example: brown **nitrogen(IV) oxide**, NO₂, is in equilibrium with colourless **dinitrogen tetroxide**, N₂O₄:

$$2\text{NO}_2\text{(g, brown)} \rightleftharpoons \text{N}_2\text{O}_4\text{(g, colourless)}$$

A sealed tube of the mixture is darker brown when hot and paler when cold.

:::callout{type=local-example}
A bottle of soft drink is a closed system at equilibrium. Carbon(IV) oxide gas above the drink is in equilibrium with CO₂ dissolved in it. While the cap is on, the dissolving and escaping happen at equal rates and the drink stays fizzy. Opening the bottle breaks the closed system: gas escapes, the equilibrium can no longer hold, and the drink slowly goes flat.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
At equilibrium the concentrations are **constant**, not **equal**. An equilibrium mixture may contain mostly products, mostly reactants, or anything between.
:::

## Key points

- **Reversible reactions** (⇌) can go in both directions; examples: CuSO₄·5H₂O ⇌ CuSO₄ + 5H₂O, NH₄Cl ⇌ NH₃ + HCl, N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃.
- In a **closed system**, a reversible reaction reaches **dynamic equilibrium**: forward rate = backward rate.
- At equilibrium, concentrations are **constant but not necessarily equal**; reactions continue in both directions.
- Equilibrium can be reached **from either side**.
- Colour changes (chromate/dichromate, NO₂/N₂O₄) show equilibria shifting.

## Quick check

1. What does the sign ⇌ mean in an equation?
2. Why is chemical equilibrium described as dynamic?
3. State two characteristics of a system at equilibrium.
4. What colour change is seen when acid is added to potassium chromate solution?

**Answers:**

1. The reaction is reversible: it can go forwards and backwards.
2. Both the forward and backward reactions continue to happen, at equal rates.
3. Any two: closed system; forward and backward rates equal; concentrations constant; reachable from either side; macroscopic properties constant.
4. Yellow to orange.
