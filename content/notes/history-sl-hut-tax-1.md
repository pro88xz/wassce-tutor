:::callout{type=definition}
The **Hut Tax War of 1898** was the armed rising of the peoples of the new **Sierra Leone Protectorate** against British rule, sparked by the **hut tax** introduced by **Governor Frederic Cardew** from **1 January 1898**.
:::

In the north, the Temne chief **Bai Bureh of Kasseh** led a skilful **guerrilla war** from **February to November 1898**; in the south, a more violent **Mende rising** broke out at the end of **April 1898**, in which **missionaries, Krio traders and officials** were killed. The British used troops of the **West India Regiment** and the **Frontier Police** to crush both risings. This lesson explains the **causes of the Hut Tax War**, the **role of Bai Bureh** and the **course of the war**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the hut tax and how it was collected
- explain the causes of the Hut Tax War
- describe Bai Bureh and his leadership of the northern rising
- explain why Bai Bureh was able to resist for so long
- describe the Mende rising in the south
- explain how the British suppressed the risings

## The hut tax

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Introduced by** | **Governor Frederic Cardew** |
| **Start** | **1 January 1898** |
| **Rate** | **5 shillings** a year for smaller houses and **10 shillings** for larger ones |
| **Collection** | by **chiefs**, under the supervision of **District Commissioners** and the **Frontier Police** |
| **Purpose** | to make the Protectorate **pay for its own administration** |

## Causes of the Hut Tax War

| Cause | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **The tax itself** | people saw a tax on their homes as **rent for land that already belonged to them**, and as a sign of **lost independence** |
| **Poverty** | many could not find money to pay; trade was depressed |
| **Brutal collection** | the **Frontier Police**, many from other areas or former slaves, **flogged and humiliated chiefs** who could not pay, and looted property |
| **Loss of chiefs' powers** | the **Protectorate Ordinance** reduced chiefs' courts, banned slave dealing and some customs, and made chiefs subordinate to DCs |
| **Fear for land** | the Ordinance gave the government power over **"waste" land**; rumours spread that the British would seize the land |
| **Anger at the Krio and missionaries** | they were seen as agents of the colonial government and its new rules |
| **Secret societies** | the **Poro** helped to organise resistance in the south |

## Bai Bureh and the northern rising

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figure"}
**Bai Bureh** (also called **Kebalai**) was the ruler of **Kasseh**, near **Port Loko**, and an experienced war leader who had fought in earlier wars in the north, sometimes as an ally of the British.
:::

**Course of the northern rising:**

1. In **February 1898** the District Commissioner at **Karene**, **Captain Wilfred Sharpe**, tried to force chiefs at **Port Loko** to collect the tax and arrested some who refused.
2. The government ordered the arrest of **Bai Bureh**, accusing him of encouraging resistance. He refused to surrender, and fighting began.
3. For months Bai Bureh's warriors **ambushed** British columns marching between **Port Loko and Karene**, firing from **hidden stockades** along the narrow forest paths and then disappearing.
4. The British brought in more troops of the **West India Regiment**, burned towns and destroyed farms to cut off his support.
5. Bai Bureh was finally **captured on 11 November 1898**.

## Why Bai Bureh resisted for so long

1. **Guerrilla tactics:** ambushes, hit-and-run attacks and the use of **stockades** along forest paths.
2. **Knowledge of the land:** his fighters knew the forests, swamps and hills.
3. **Popular support:** many Temne and Loko chiefs and people supported him and supplied food and fighters.
4. **Good leadership and discipline:** he was an experienced commander.
5. **Weapons:** his men had guns obtained through trade.
6. **British difficulties:** the rainy season, disease and long supply lines slowed British columns.

## The Mende rising in the south

At the end of **April 1898**, a separate and more violent rising broke out among the **Mende** and neighbouring peoples in the south.

1. It was **organised secretly**, partly through the **Poro society**, and began almost at the same time in many places.
2. Fighters attacked **anyone linked with the colonial government or Western ways**: **missionaries**, **Krio traders**, government officials, police, and Africans in Western dress.
3. Among those killed were **American United Brethren missionaries** at **Rotifunk** and many Krio in towns such as **Bonthe**, **Taiama** and elsewhere; estimates of those killed run to **hundreds**.
4. British troops and the Frontier Police **suppressed** the rising by **late 1898 and early 1899**, burning towns and **executing** many people found guilty of murder.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Comparing the two risings**

The **northern rising** was led by **one known leader**, Bai Bureh, and was mainly a **military campaign** against British forces. The **Mende rising** had **no single leader**, was organised secretly through societies and chiefs, and was directed at **Krio, missionaries and anyone seen as part of colonial rule**, so it was much more violent towards civilians. Both, however, had the **same main causes**: the hut tax, police abuses and the loss of chiefs' power and land.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Bai Bureh is honoured as one of **Sierra Leone's national heroes**. Even British officers admired his skill, and newspapers in Britain wrote about him. His image has appeared on Sierra Leone's banknotes, and his resistance is remembered as an example of courage against colonial injustice.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not say that Bai Bureh led the **whole** Hut Tax War. He led the **northern (Temne) rising**. The **Mende rising in the south** was a separate movement, organised differently and led by many local chiefs and war leaders.
:::

## Key points

- **Hut tax**: introduced by **Governor Cardew** from **1 January 1898**; **5s or 10s** a year per house.
- Causes: the **tax**, **poverty**, **Frontier Police abuses**, **loss of chiefs' powers**, **fear for land**, anger at **Krio and missionaries**.
- **Bai Bureh of Kasseh** led the **northern rising** (Feb–Nov 1898) with **guerrilla tactics**; captured **11 November 1898**.
- The **Mende rising** (from late April 1898) killed **missionaries, Krio traders and officials**; suppressed with force.

1. Who introduced the hut tax, and when did it start?
2. What were the rates of the hut tax?
3. Give four causes of the Hut Tax War.
4. Who was Bai Bureh?
5. Give three reasons why Bai Bureh resisted for so long.
6. Who were the main targets of the Mende rising?

**Answers:**

1. Governor Frederic Cardew; from 1 January 1898.
2. 5 shillings a year for smaller houses and 10 shillings for larger houses.
3. Any four: the tax itself; poverty; brutal collection by the Frontier Police; loss of chiefs' powers; fear that land would be taken; anger at Krio and missionaries; organisation by secret societies.
4. The Temne ruler of Kasseh, near Port Loko, who led the northern rising against the British in 1898.
5. Any three: guerrilla tactics; knowledge of the land; popular support; good leadership; guns; British difficulties with rain, disease and supply.
6. Missionaries, Krio traders, government officials, police and others linked with colonial rule.
