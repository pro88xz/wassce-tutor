The faster you drive, the less time a journey takes. The cost of printing has a fixed setting-up charge plus an amount per copy. The area of a circle depends on the square of its radius. These are **inverse**, **partial** and **power** variations. With direct variation from the previous lesson, they complete the topic. The method is always the same: write the equation, find the constant, then use it.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- solve problems on inverse variation
- solve problems on joint variation, including combinations of direct and inverse
- solve problems on partial variation
- solve problems where one quantity varies as a power or root of another
- recognise each type of variation from its graph

## The four types at a glance

![Graphs of direct variation (a line through the origin), inverse variation (a falling curve), partial variation (a line with a y-intercept) and y varying as x squared (a parabola)](https://wasscetutor.com/img/mathematics/variation-graphs.svg)

*Figure 1. The shapes of the four kinds of variation. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Statement | Equation |
|---|---|
| $y$ varies directly as $x$ | $y = kx$ |
| $y$ varies inversely as $x$ | $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$ |
| $y$ varies jointly as $x$ and $z$ | $y = kxz$ |
| $y$ varies directly as $x$ and inversely as $z^2$ | $y = \dfrac{kx}{z^2}$ |
| $y$ is partly constant and partly varies as $x$ | $y = a + kx$ |
| $y$ varies as the square of $x$ | $y = kx^2$ |
| $y$ varies as the square root of $x$ | $y = k\sqrt{x}$ |

**Method:** (1) write the equation; (2) substitute the given pair of values to find the constant(s); (3) write the full equation; (4) use it.

## Inverse variation

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**$y$ varies inversely as $x$, and $y = 6$ when $x = 4$. Find $y$ when $x = 8$.**

$y = \dfrac{k}{x}$, so $6 = \dfrac{k}{4}$ and $k = 24$. The equation is $y = \dfrac{24}{x}$. When $x = 8$, $y = 3$.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
**The time for a journey from Freetown to Makeni varies inversely as the average speed. At 60 km/h it takes 3 hours. How long does it take at 90 km/h?**

$t = \dfrac{k}{v}$: $3 = \dfrac{k}{60}$, so $k = 180$ (the distance in km). At 90 km/h, $t = \dfrac{180}{90} = 2$ hours.
:::

## Joint variation

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**(a) $y$ varies jointly as $x$ and $z$, and $y = 24$ when $x = 2$ and $z = 3$. Find $y$ when $x = 5$ and $z = 2$.**

$y = kxz$: $24 = k(2)(3)$, so $k = 4$. Then $y = 4(5)(2) = 40$.

**(b) $y$ varies directly as $x$ and inversely as the square of $z$. $y = 6$ when $x = 3$ and $z = 2$. Find $y$ when $x = 5$ and $z = 4$.**

$y = \dfrac{kx}{z^2}$: $6 = \dfrac{3k}{4}$, so $k = 8$. Then $y = \dfrac{8 \times 5}{16} = 2.5$.
:::

## Partial variation

Partial variation has **two constants**, so you need **two pairs** of values and simultaneous equations.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**The cost $C$ of printing $n$ copies of a school magazine is partly constant and partly varies as $n$. 100 copies cost NLe 3,500 and 300 copies cost NLe 7,500. Find the cost of 500 copies.**

$C = a + bn$:

$$a + 100b = 3\,500 \qquad a + 300b = 7\,500$$

Subtracting: $200b = 4\,000$, so $b = 20$ and $a = 3\,500 - 2\,000 = 1\,500$.

$C = 1\,500 + 20n$. For 500 copies: $C = 1\,500 + 10\,000 = $ NLe 11,500.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
In partial variation, doubling $n$ does **not** double $C$: 200 copies cost NLe 5,500, not NLe 7,000. The fixed part ($a = 1\,500$) does not change.
:::

## Powers and roots

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**(a) $y$ varies as the square of $x$, and $y = 12$ when $x = 2$. Find $y$ when $x = 5$.**

$y = kx^2$: $12 = 4k$, so $k = 3$. Then $y = 3 \times 25 = 75$.

**(b) $y$ varies as the square root of $x$, and $y = 6$ when $x = 9$. Find $y$ when $x = 25$.**

$y = k\sqrt{x}$: $6 = 3k$, so $k = 2$. Then $y = 2 \times 5 = 10$.
:::

## Key points

- Translate the words into an equation first; every variation has a **constant $k$**.
- **Inverse:** $y = \tfrac{k}{x}$ (product constant). **Joint:** $y = kxz$.
- **Partial:** $y = a + kx$ needs **two pairs** of values.
- **Powers and roots:** $y = kx^2$, $y = k\sqrt{x}$, and so on.
- Graphs: direct is a line **through** the origin; partial is a line **not** through it; inverse is a falling curve.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **find the constant and the equation** of a variation and use it, especially **joint variation with an inverse square**, **partial variation from two pairs of values**, and **inverse variation in speed–time or workers–days contexts**. Always write the equation connecting the variables; it carries marks.
:::

## Practice

Try each question first, then open the answer to check your working.

**1.** $p$ varies inversely as $q$; $p = 10$ when $q = 3$. Find $p$ when $q = 5$.

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
$k = 30$, so $p = 6$.
:::

**2.** $y \propto x^2$ and $y = 50$ when $x = 5$. Find $y$ when $x = 3$.

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
$k = 2$, so $y = 18$.
:::

**3.** $m$ varies jointly as $n$ and the square root of $t$; $m = 24$ when $n = 3$ and $t = 4$. Find $m$ when $n = 5$ and $t = 9$.

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
$24 = k(3)(2)$, $k = 4$; $m = 4(5)(3) = 60$.
:::

**4.** $C = a + bn$; $C = 900$ when $n = 10$ and $C = 1\,400$ when $n = 20$. Find $a$ and $b$.

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
$10b = 500$, $b = 50$; $a = 900 - 500 = 400$.
:::

**5.** Which graph is a straight line that does not pass through the origin?

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
Partial variation ($y = a + kx$ with $a \ne 0$).
:::
