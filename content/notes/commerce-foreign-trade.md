# Foreign trade: imports, exports and balance of trade

## Learning objectives
Distinguish imports from exports, explain why countries trade internationally and calculate a visible balance of trade.

## Meaning
Foreign trade is the exchange of goods and services between parties in different countries. It is also called international or external trade. An **import** is a good or service purchased from another country; an **export** is a good or service sold to another country.

Countries trade because natural resources, climate, skills, technology, capital and production costs differ. International trade gives consumers access to products not available locally, expands markets for domestic producers, encourages specialisation and can earn foreign exchange. It can also expose businesses to exchange-rate changes, shipping delays, foreign competition and external economic shocks.

## Visible and invisible trade
Visible trade refers to physical goods crossing national borders, such as machinery, fuel, minerals or agricultural products. Invisible trade refers to services and other non-physical transactions, such as tourism, shipping, insurance, banking and professional services. International trade includes both.

## Balance of trade
The balance of trade is the difference between the value of a country's exports and imports of goods over a stated period. In a simplified visible-trade calculation:

**Balance of trade = value of visible exports − value of visible imports**

If exports exceed imports, there is a trade surplus. If imports exceed exports, there is a trade deficit. If they are equal, trade is balanced.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
A country exports goods worth US$460 million and imports goods worth US$520 million. Balance of trade = 460 − 520 = −US$60 million. The country has a visible trade deficit of US$60 million for the period.
:::

A deficit is not automatically proof that an economy is failing. Its significance depends on what is imported, how imports are financed, whether imported machinery supports future production and the wider balance of payments. The balance of payments is broader because it includes goods, services, income, transfers and financial flows.

## Sierra Leone context
Sierra Leone exports minerals, agricultural products and fish, while importing goods such as fuel, machinery, medicines and manufactured products. Improving infrastructure, product quality, export diversification and market access can help domestic producers compete. Imported equipment may support future production even though it raises imports in the short term.

## WASSCE examination focus
Show the subtraction clearly, state the period and interpret the sign. Do not confuse balance of trade, which focuses on goods in the usual syllabus calculation, with balance of payments, which records a wider range of international transactions.
