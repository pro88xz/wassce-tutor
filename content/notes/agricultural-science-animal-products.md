Animals produce meat, milk, eggs, fish, hides and skins, and many by-products. All of these are **perishable**: meat and fish spoil within hours in a hot climate, milk turns sour, and badly handled eggs crack and rot. Careful handling, hygiene and processing keep products safe to eat, make them last longer and add value, from the smoked fish of Tombo to the leather of a pair of sandals. This lesson covers slaughter and meat processing, milk and its products, eggs, fish preservation, hides and skins, and the by-products of animals.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the hygienic slaughter of farm animals and meat inspection
- describe methods of preserving meat and fish
- state the composition of milk and describe hygienic milking and pasteurisation
- describe the structure, quality testing and storage of eggs
- describe the processing of hides and skins
- name animal by-products and their uses

## Meat

### Slaughter

1. **Rest** the animal and withhold feed (but not water) for about 12–24 hours before slaughter.
2. **Inspect** the live animal (ante-mortem inspection): only healthy animals should be slaughtered.
3. **Stun** (where practised) and **bleed** thoroughly by cutting the main blood vessels of the neck (good bleeding helps the meat keep).
4. **Flay**: remove the skin carefully, without cutting it.
5. **Eviscerate**: remove the internal organs without bursting the gut, which would contaminate the meat.
6. **Inspect** the carcass and organs (post-mortem inspection) for disease such as tuberculosis, liver flukes and cysts; condemn unfit meat.
7. **Wash**, split and chill the carcass.

All this should be done in a clean **abattoir** or slaughter slab, with clean water, clean tools and protective clothing.

### Meat preservation

| Method | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **refrigeration and freezing** | low temperature slows microbes | cold rooms, freezers |
| **smoking** | dries the meat and adds chemicals that kill microbes | smoked bushmeat and fish |
| **drying** | removes water | dried meat strips |
| **salting** and **curing** | salt draws water out of microbes | salted meat, bacon |
| **canning** | heating in sealed cans kills microbes | corned beef |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Buying meat with no inspection.** Meat from sick animals can carry diseases such as **anthrax, tuberculosis and tapeworms** to people. Meat should come from an inspected slaughter facility, and pork and beef should be **cooked thoroughly**.
:::

## Milk

### Composition

Cow's milk is about **87% water**, **3.5–4% fat** (butterfat), **3.3% protein** (mainly casein), **4.8% lactose** (milk sugar) and **0.7% minerals** (especially calcium), plus vitamins.

### Hygienic milking

Clean the milking place; wash and dry the udder; milker with clean hands and clothes; check the first squirts in a **strip cup** for clots (mastitis); use clean, covered stainless or plastic containers; **cool** milk quickly; keep milk from cows treated with antibiotics separate for the withdrawal period.

### Pasteurisation and products

**Pasteurisation** heats milk enough to kill harmful bacteria without boiling it:

- **low temperature, long time**: about **63 °C for 30 minutes**; or
- **high temperature, short time**: about **72 °C for 15 seconds**; then cool quickly.

**Milk products:** butter and ghee (from the fat), cheese, **yoghurt** and sour milk (fermented by lactic acid bacteria), powdered and evaporated milk.

## Eggs

![The structure of a hen's egg: shell, shell membranes, air cell at the blunt end, albumen, yolk with the germinal disc, and the chalazae holding the yolk](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/egg-structure.svg)

### Egg quality

- **Candling**: shining a light through the egg in a dark place shows cracks, blood spots, the size of the **air cell** (larger in older eggs) and fertility.
- **Freshness test**: a fresh egg **sinks** and lies flat in water; a stale egg stands up or **floats** because its air cell has grown.
- **Grading** by size, cleanliness and shell quality.

### Handling and storage

Collect eggs at least three times a day; do not wash dirty eggs in cold water (wipe or wash in warm water if necessary); store in a **cool** place, **blunt end up** (keeps the air cell in place and the yolk centred); sell quickly; avoid sharp temperature changes.

## Fish

Fish spoils faster than meat. Traditional methods are widely used along the coast:

- **Smoking**: in **ovens (bandas)** over wood fires; dries the fish and preserves it for weeks. **Improved ovens** use less wood, produce less smoke for the processors (mostly women), and give cleaner fish.
- **Sun-drying** and **salting**.
- **Icing and freezing** for fresh fish sales.
- **Fish meal**: low-grade fish and waste dried and ground for **animal feed**.

## Hides and skins

**Hides** come from large animals (cattle); **skins** from small ones (goats, sheep).

1. **Flay** carefully, without cuts or holes (cuts lower the value).
2. **Cure** to stop decay: **salting** (wet or dry salt) or **air-drying** in the shade on a frame (not on the ground or in direct sun).
3. **Tan**: treat with chemicals (or traditional plant tannins) to turn the raw skin into **leather** that will not rot.

Leather is used for shoes, sandals, bags, belts, drums and upholstery.

## By-products

| By-product | Use |
|---|---|
| **blood** | blood meal (feed protein, fertiliser) |
| **bones** | bone meal (calcium and phosphorus in feed and fertiliser), glue |
| **horns and hooves** | buttons, combs, hoof and horn meal (fertiliser) |
| **feathers** | feather meal, pillows, fly whisks |
| **manure** | fertiliser, biogas |
| **offal** | food (liver, kidneys, tripe) |
| **fats** | soap, cooking |

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Tombo fish smokers.** At Tombo and other fishing towns, women buy the catch from canoes and smoke it in large ovens. Smoked bonga and other fish then travel inland to markets across Sierra Leone, where fresh fish could never reach in good condition. Improved, fuel-efficient smoking ovens cut firewood use and the smoke the women breathe, while giving a more even, higher-value product.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. List the steps in the slaughter of a goat."}
Rest and fast the animal; ante-mortem inspection; stun (where practised) and bleed; flay; eviscerate; post-mortem inspection; wash, split and chill.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. State the approximate composition of cow's milk."}
About 87% water, 3.5–4% fat, 3.3% protein, 4.8% lactose, 0.7% minerals.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. What is pasteurisation, and why is it done?"}
Heating milk to about 63 °C for 30 minutes (or 72 °C for 15 seconds) and cooling it quickly, to **kill harmful bacteria** without boiling, so the milk is safe and keeps longer.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. How can you tell a fresh egg from a stale one using water?"}
A fresh egg **sinks** and lies flat; a stale egg stands upright or **floats**, because its air cell has enlarged.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Distinguish between hides and skins, and name the three steps in processing them."}
**Hides** come from large animals (cattle), **skins** from small animals (goats, sheep). Steps: flaying, curing (salting or drying), tanning.
:::

## Key points

- Slaughter: **rest, inspect, bleed well, flay, eviscerate cleanly, inspect carcass**.
- Preserve meat and fish by **cold, smoking, drying, salting, canning**.
- Milk: mostly water, with fat, protein (casein), lactose, minerals; **hygienic milking**; **pasteurise** at 63 °C/30 min or 72 °C/15 s.
- Eggs: **candle**, **float test**, store cool **blunt end up**.
- Hides and skins: **flay, cure, tan** → leather.
- By-products: **blood, bone, feather and horn meals, manure, fats**.
