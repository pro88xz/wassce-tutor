Crack an egg into a hot pan and the clear, runny egg white turns solid and white within seconds. Nothing has been added: the heat has destroyed the **shape** of the protein molecules. A protein's job depends entirely on its shape. This lesson covers how protein chains fold, the types and functions of proteins, how they are tested and hydrolysed, and what denatures them.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the primary, secondary and tertiary structure of proteins
- distinguish fibrous and globular proteins and state functions of proteins
- describe the tests for proteins
- describe the hydrolysis of proteins and the identification of amino acids by chromatography
- explain denaturation and name the agents that cause it
- explain why enzymes are affected by temperature and pH

## Structure of proteins

![Primary structure as a chain of amino acids, secondary as a helix, tertiary as a folded 3-D shape, and a denatured protein as an unfolded chain](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/protein-structure.svg)

*Figure 1. Levels of protein structure, and what denaturation does. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Level | Description | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | The **sequence** (order) of amino acids in the chain | Peptide bonds (covalent) |
| **Secondary** | The chain coils into a **helix** or folds into **pleated sheets** | **Hydrogen bonds** between –CO– and –NH– groups |
| **Tertiary** | The coiled chain folds into a precise **3-D shape** | Hydrogen bonds, ionic attractions, **S–S (disulphide) bridges** |

The **primary structure** decides how the chain folds, and the **shape** decides what the protein can do. Changing even one amino acid can change the shape (as in sickle-cell haemoglobin).

## Types and functions of proteins

| Type | Shape and properties | Examples and functions |
|---|---|---|
| **Fibrous** | Long, insoluble fibres; strong | **Keratin** (hair, nails, feathers), **collagen** (skin, tendons, bone), silk |
| **Globular** | Compact, rounded; soluble in water | **Enzymes** (amylase, pepsin), **haemoglobin** (carries oxygen), **albumin** (egg white), **insulin** (hormone), antibodies |

**Functions:** growth and repair of tissues, **enzymes** (biological catalysts), transport (haemoglobin), hormones, defence (antibodies), and energy when carbohydrates and fats run short.

## Tests for proteins

![The biuret test gives violet; Millon's reagent gives a brick-red precipitate; concentrated nitric acid gives yellow; heated egg white coagulates](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/protein-tests.svg)

*Figure 2. Tests for proteins. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Test | Method | Positive result |
|---|---|---|
| **Biuret test** | Add sodium hydroxide solution, then a few drops of **dilute copper(II) sulphate** | **Violet (purple)** colour; detects **peptide bonds** |
| **Millon's test** | Add Millon's reagent and warm | **Brick-red (pink) precipitate** |
| **Xanthoproteic test** | Add **concentrated nitric acid** and warm (care!) | **Yellow** colour, turning **orange** with ammonia |
| **Heat** | Heat a protein solution such as egg white | **Coagulates** (white solid) |

Concentrated nitric acid also stains skin yellow, because skin contains protein.

## Hydrolysis of proteins

Proteins are hydrolysed back to **amino acids** by:

- boiling with **concentrated hydrochloric acid** (about 6 mol/dm³) for many hours, or
- **enzymes** (proteases) at body temperature: **pepsin** in the stomach (acid conditions), **trypsin** from the pancreas (alkaline conditions)

$$\text{protein} + \text{water} \rightarrow \text{amino acids}$$

**Identifying the amino acids:** the mixture is separated by **paper chromatography**. The paper is then sprayed with **ninhydrin** and warmed: each amino acid shows as a **purple spot**, identified by comparing its **Rf value** with known amino acids.

## Denaturation

:::callout{type=definition}
**Denaturation** is the **permanent change in the 3-D shape** of a protein, caused by breaking the hydrogen bonds and other forces that hold the shape. The peptide bonds (primary structure) are **not** broken, but the protein loses its function and often **coagulates**.
:::

**Denaturing agents:**

- **heat** (above about 40–60 °C): cooking eggs, meat and fish
- **strong acids and alkalis** (change of pH): milk curdling when it sours
- **heavy-metal ions** (Pb²⁺, Hg²⁺, Ag⁺, Cu²⁺): this is why they are poisonous
- **alcohol** (ethanol) and other organic solvents: why ethanol is an antiseptic
- **ultraviolet radiation**, strong shaking, concentrated salts

**Enzymes** are globular proteins. They work fastest at an **optimum temperature** (about **37 °C** in humans) and an **optimum pH**. Above the optimum temperature, the enzyme is denatured and its **active site** changes shape, so the reaction stops.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Explaining results.** An enzyme solution is tested at 20 °C, 37 °C and 70 °C. The rate is moderate at 20 °C, fastest at 37 °C, and zero at 70 °C. When the 70 °C sample is cooled back to 37 °C, it still does not work. Explain.

- From 20 °C to 37 °C, particles move faster and collide more often, so the rate rises.
- At 70 °C the enzyme is **denatured**: its shape (active site) is destroyed.
- Denaturation is **permanent**, so cooling does not restore activity.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Fishermen and traders along the Sierra Leone coast preserve **bonga and herring** by **smoking and salting**. Heat from the smoke and high salt concentrations **denature the enzymes** of the fish and of spoilage bacteria, so the fish keeps for weeks without a fridge. The same idea explains why boiling milk delays souring.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Denaturation is not hydrolysis. **Denaturation** changes the **shape** but leaves the chain of amino acids intact. **Hydrolysis** breaks the **peptide bonds** to give free amino acids.
:::

## Key points

- **Primary** structure = amino acid **sequence**; **secondary** = helix or sheet (**hydrogen bonds**); **tertiary** = 3-D folding (H-bonds, ionic, **S–S** bridges).
- **Fibrous** proteins (keratin, collagen) are insoluble and structural; **globular** proteins (enzymes, haemoglobin, albumin) are soluble and functional.
- Tests: **biuret** (violet), **Millon's** (brick-red), **xanthoproteic** (yellow, conc. HNO₃), **heat** (coagulates).
- **Hydrolysis** (conc. HCl or pepsin/trypsin) → **amino acids**, identified by chromatography with **ninhydrin**.
- **Denaturation** (heat, pH, heavy metals, alcohol) destroys the **shape** and function, not the peptide bonds.

## Quick check

1. What colour shows a positive biuret test?
2. Name two agents that denature proteins.
3. What are the products of complete hydrolysis of a protein?
4. Why do heavy-metal salts act as poisons?

**Answers:**

1. Violet (purple).
2. Any two: heat, strong acids, strong alkalis, heavy-metal ions, alcohol, UV radiation.
3. Amino acids.
4. Heavy-metal ions denature proteins, including enzymes, so the body's reactions stop.
