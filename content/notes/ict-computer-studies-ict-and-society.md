:::callout{type=definition}
**ICT (information and communication technology)** is the use of computers, telecommunication networks, the Internet and related devices to collect, store, process and communicate information. **ICT and society** is the study of how these technologies change the way people learn, work, trade, govern and live together.
:::

ICT now touches almost every part of life in Sierra Leone: results are checked online, money moves by phone, farmers get prices by SMS and patients in distant towns can speak to doctors by video. This lesson describes the **uses of ICT in society** sector by sector, explains terms such as **e-learning**, **e-commerce**, **e-government**, **telemedicine** and **teleworking**, weighs the **positive and negative effects of ICT** (including the **digital divide**, automation and e-waste), and looks at **careers in ICT**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the uses of ICT in education, health, business, banking, agriculture, government and other areas
- explain e-learning, e-commerce, e-government, telemedicine and teleworking
- state the positive effects of ICT on individuals and society
- explain the negative effects of ICT, including unemployment, the digital divide, misinformation and e-waste
- suggest ways of reducing the digital divide and using ICT responsibly
- name careers in ICT and what each involves

:::callout{type=definition}
**E-learning:** learning through electronic media, such as online lessons, videos, quizzes and virtual classrooms, often away from a classroom.

**E-commerce:** buying and selling goods and services over the Internet or mobile networks, including online payment.

**E-government:** the use of ICT by government to give information and services to citizens and businesses, such as online forms, tax payment and digital identity records.

**Telemedicine:** the use of telecommunications, such as video calls and shared electronic records, to give medical advice and care at a distance.

**Teleworking (telecommuting):** working from home or another place away from the office, keeping in touch through the Internet, email and video calls.

**Video conferencing:** a live meeting between people in different places using cameras, microphones and the Internet.
:::

| Sector | How ICT is used |
|---|---|
| **Education** | e-learning and online lessons; projectors and computer labs; school records and results processing; online registration for exams and universities |
| **Health** | electronic patient records; telemedicine; scanners and monitors in hospitals; SMS reminders for vaccinations; tracking disease outbreaks |
| **Banking and finance** | ATMs; mobile money; online and mobile banking; electronic funds transfer; card payments |
| **Business and commerce** | e-commerce; point-of-sale terminals and barcodes; stock control; accounting software; online advertising |
| **Agriculture** | weather forecasts and market prices by SMS and radio; GPS mapping of farms; mobile payments to farmers |
| **Government** | e-government services; national ID and voter registers; online tax payment; census data |
| **Communication and media** | phone calls, messaging, social media, online news, radio and TV production |
| **Transport** | GPS navigation; online ticket booking; vehicle tracking; traffic-light control |
| **Security and law** | CCTV cameras; fingerprint and face recognition; police databases |
| **Entertainment** | music and video streaming, games, digital photography |

## Positive effects of ICT

- **Faster communication** with people anywhere, at low cost.
- **Access to information and learning**: libraries of lessons, books and news on a phone.
- **Better services**: banking, health and government services reach people far from towns.
- **Higher productivity**: work is done faster and more accurately, with fewer errors.
- **New jobs and businesses**: phone repair, cyber cafés, app development, online selling, mobile money agents.
- **Financial inclusion**: people with no bank account can save and pay by mobile money.
- **Safer societies**: CCTV, biometrics and quicker emergency communication.

## Negative effects of ICT

:::callout{type=definition}
**Automation:** the use of computers and machines to do work that people once did by hand, such as ticket machines and robots on assembly lines.

**Digital divide:** the gap between people who have access to ICT and the skills to use it and people who do not, such as between towns and rural areas or rich and poor families.

**Misinformation:** false or misleading information that spreads, often quickly on social media; when it is spread on purpose to deceive, it is called disinformation.

**Electronic waste (e-waste):** thrown-away electronic devices, such as old phones, computers, batteries and chargers, which can release harmful substances if they are not recycled safely.
:::

- **Unemployment**: automation replaces some jobs, such as typists and filing clerks, and workers must retrain.
- **The digital divide**: people without electricity, devices, network coverage or skills are left behind.
- **Misinformation and fake news** spread quickly and can cause panic, conflict or harm to health.
- **Loss of privacy** and **cybercrime**, such as fraud and identity theft (see the lessons on ethics and on cybersecurity).
- **Over-use and addiction**: too much time on phones and games harms study, sleep and family life.
- **Social isolation**: online contact can replace face-to-face friendship.
- **Health problems** from poor posture and long hours at screens (see the lesson on practical computer skills).
- **E-waste** that pollutes soil and water when burnt or dumped.

### Reducing the digital divide

- extending **electricity** and **network coverage** to rural areas
- **community ICT centres** and school computer labs open to the public
- cheaper **devices and data**, and free access to educational sites
- teaching **digital literacy** in schools and communities
- content and apps in **local languages** such as Krio

:::callout{type=definition}
**Digital literacy:** the ability to find, judge, use and create information safely and effectively with digital technology.

**Green computing:** using ICT in ways that reduce harm to the environment, such as saving energy, using devices for longer, and recycling e-waste safely.
:::

## Careers in ICT

| Career | What the person does |
|---|---|
| **Software developer** | designs, writes and tests programs and apps |
| **Systems analyst** | studies an organisation's needs and plans computer systems to meet them |
| **Network administrator** | sets up, runs and secures an organisation's network |
| **Database administrator** | designs, protects and backs up databases |
| **Web designer / developer** | creates and maintains websites |
| **Computer technician** | assembles, repairs and maintains computers and phones |
| **Cybersecurity analyst** | protects systems and data from attack |
| **Data analyst** | turns large amounts of data into useful information |
| **Graphic designer** | creates images, logos and layouts with graphics software |
| **ICT teacher / trainer** | teaches people to use computers and software |

:::callout{type=local-example}
**ICT changing a farmer's day.** A cassava farmer near Moyamba checks the next week's rain forecast on her phone, sees the price buyers are paying in Freetown before deciding where to sell, receives payment by mobile money, and watches a short video on controlling cassava pests, all from one basic smartphone.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Giving only advantages.** Questions on ICT and society usually want **both sides**. When asked for effects, give positive and negative effects, each with a clear example.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Explain e-commerce and give one example."}
**Buying and selling goods and services over the Internet or mobile networks**, with online payment. Example: ordering a phone from an online shop and paying by card or mobile money.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. State three uses of ICT in health care."}
Any three: **electronic patient records**; **telemedicine** (advice by video); **scanners and monitors**; **SMS reminders**; **tracking disease outbreaks**; ordering and tracking **medicines**.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. What is the digital divide? Give two ways of reducing it."}
The **gap between people who have access to ICT and the skills to use it and those who do not**. Ways (any two): extend **electricity and network coverage**; **community ICT centres**; cheaper **devices and data**; **digital literacy** teaching; **local-language** content.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. State three negative effects of ICT on society."}
Any three: **unemployment** through automation; the **digital divide**; **misinformation**; **loss of privacy** and **cybercrime**; **addiction** and over-use; **social isolation**; **health problems**; **e-waste**.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Name two careers in ICT and state what each person does."}
Any two, such as: **network administrator**, sets up and secures networks; **software developer**, writes programs; **computer technician**, repairs computers; **database administrator**, manages databases.
:::

## Key points

- **ICT** is used in **education, health, banking, commerce, agriculture, government, communication, transport, security and entertainment**.
- E-terms: **e-learning, e-commerce, e-government, telemedicine, teleworking, video conferencing**.
- Positive effects: faster **communication**, **information** for all, better **services**, **productivity**, new **jobs**, **financial inclusion**.
- Negative effects: **unemployment** (automation), **digital divide**, **misinformation**, loss of **privacy**, **cybercrime**, **addiction**, **isolation**, **e-waste**.
- Reduce the divide with **coverage, community centres, cheap data, digital literacy and local-language content**; practise **green computing**.
