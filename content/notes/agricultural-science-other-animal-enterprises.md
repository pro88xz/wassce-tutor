Not every livestock enterprise needs much land or money. **Fish farming** in a small pond, **bee-keeping** at the edge of a farm, and **rabbits**, **snails** or **grasscutters** in pens behind the house can all produce food and income on a small scale. They also take pressure off wild fish stocks and wild bushmeat. This lesson describes aquaculture, apiculture, rabbit production, snail farming (heliciculture) and grasscutter rearing, with the key management practices for each.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the establishment and management of a fish pond
- explain integrated fish farming
- describe the organisation of a honeybee colony and the management of an apiary
- describe the management of rabbits, snails and grasscutters
- state the importance and problems of these enterprises

## Aquaculture (fish farming)

:::callout{type=definition}
**Aquaculture** is the rearing of fish and other aquatic animals under controlled conditions.
:::

Common species: **tilapia** (fast-breeding, eats plankton and plant matter) and **African catfish** (*Clarias*; hardy, tolerates low oxygen, fast-growing).

![An earthen fish pond in cross-section: inlet with a screen at the shallow end, water deepening to about 1.5 m at the outlet end with a drain pipe, and dykes planted with grass](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/fish-pond.svg)

### Establishing a pond

- **Site**: a reliable supply of **clean water** (stream or spring) all year; **clay or clay-loam** soil that holds water; a **gentle slope** so the pond can be filled and drained by gravity; not liable to flooding; secure.
- **Construction**: dig and compact the pond; build strong **dykes** (banks) and plant grass on them; fit an **inlet** with a **screen** (to keep out wild fish and predators) and an **outlet** that can drain the pond completely; make the bottom slope from about 0.5 m deep at the inlet to about 1–1.5 m at the outlet.
- **Liming** the pond bottom if acidic, then **fertilising** with manure to grow **plankton**, the natural food of young fish.

### Management

1. **Stock** with healthy **fingerlings** at the right density (e.g. 2–3 tilapia per m²).
2. **Feed** with supplementary feeds (rice bran, groundnut cake, pelleted fish feed) once or twice daily, plus pond fertilisation.
3. **Manage water**: keep the level steady; watch for fish gasping at the surface in the early morning (low oxygen); add fresh water.
4. **Control** weeds, predators (birds, snakes, frogs), and **thieves**.
5. **Harvest** at about **5–6 months** by draining the pond or seining (netting); sell or process (smoke) the fish.

### Integrated fish farming

Fish farming can be combined with other enterprises: **poultry or pig houses** over or beside the pond, whose droppings fertilise the water; **rice–fish** farming in swamps; and vegetables on the dykes, watered from the pond.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Overstocking and overfeeding.** Too many fish or too much uneaten feed uses up the **oxygen** in the water as it decays, and fish die suddenly, often at dawn. Stock at the recommended density and feed only what the fish eat in a few minutes.
:::

## Apiculture (bee-keeping)

### The honeybee colony

| Caste | Number | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **queen** | one | the only fertile female; lays all the eggs (up to about 2000 a day) |
| **drones** | a few hundred | males; mate with a new queen; do no work |
| **workers** | tens of thousands | sterile females: collect **nectar** and **pollen**, make honey and wax, build comb, feed the young, guard and clean the hive |

### Hives and management

- **Hives**: traditional (log, pot or basket), **Kenya top-bar hive** (simple, cheap, bars on which bees build comb), and **Langstroth** (frame) hive.
- **Site (apiary)**: near flowering plants and water; shaded; away from homes, roads and livestock; hives raised on stands with **oil or grease on the legs** against ants.
- **Equipment**: **smoker** (calms the bees), **protective clothing** (veil, gloves, overall, boots), hive tool, knife, bucket.
- **Harvesting**: in the evening or early morning, using smoke; take only **ripe, capped** honey comb, leaving enough for the bees.
- **Products**: **honey**, **beeswax** (candles, polish, cosmetics), propolis, pollen.

**Importance of bees for crops:** bees **pollinate** many crops, including oil palm, citrus, mango, cashew, cucumber and pumpkin, raising yields.

## Rabbit production

- **Housing**: wooden or wire **hutches** raised off the ground, well ventilated, protected from rain, sun and predators.
- **Feeding**: grasses, leaves (e.g. cassava, sweet potato leaves in moderation), kitchen vegetables, plus pellets or a concentrate mix; clean water.
- **Breeding**: does are mated from about 5–6 months; **gestation about 31 days**; provide a **nest box** with dry grass a few days before kindling; litters of 6–8; wean at about 6 weeks.
- **Health**: watch for coccidiosis, ear mites (mange) and diarrhoea; keep hutches clean and dry.

## Snail farming (heliciculture)

The **giant African land snail** (*Achatina*) is popular, high in protein and low in fat.

- **Snailery**: shaded pens or boxes with moist, loose soil; netting or a lid to stop escape; protection from ants, rats and other predators.
- **Feeding**: leaves (pawpaw, cocoyam, cassava), fruits, and **calcium** (crushed shells, limestone) for strong shells.
- **Conditions**: keep soil **moist** but not waterlogged, cool and shaded; snails become inactive in dry heat.
- **Breeding**: snails are **hermaphrodites**; they lay clutches of eggs in the soil.

## Grasscutter (cane rat) rearing

:::callout{type=definition}
The **grasscutter** (*Thryonomys swinderianus*) is a highly prized bushmeat.
:::

Rearing it in cages or pens with grass (elephant grass, guinea grass), cassava, groundnuts and other feed produces the meat **without hunting** in the wild, which also reduces the risk of disease from wild bushmeat.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Inland valley swamps and fish ponds.** Some farmers in Sierra Leone have built small fish ponds in or beside their inland valley swamps, using the same water channels as the rice. The ponds give fish for the family and the market, and the pond water and mud fertilise the rice and vegetables around them. It is a good example of **integrated farming**: one resource serving several enterprises.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. State four factors to consider in siting a fish pond."}
Any four: reliable clean water supply; clay soil that holds water; gentle slope for filling and draining; not liable to flooding; security; access to roads and markets.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Why is a fish pond fertilised with manure?"}
To encourage the growth of **plankton**, the natural food of young fish.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Name the three castes of honeybees and give the role of each."}
**Queen**: lays the eggs. **Drones**: mate with a new queen. **Workers**: collect nectar and pollen, make honey and wax, care for the young, guard and clean the hive.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. What is the function of a smoker in bee-keeping?"}
Smoke **calms the bees** (they gorge on honey and are less likely to sting), making it safer to open the hive.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Give two reasons why grasscutter rearing is encouraged."}
Provides valued meat and income; reduces hunting of wild animals; lowers the risk of diseases from wild bushmeat; needs little land (any two).
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Fingerlings**: young fish used to stock a pond.
- **Plankton**: tiny plants and animals in water, the natural food of young fish.
- **Dyke**: a bank around a fish pond.
- **Apiculture** (bee-keeping): keeping bees for honey and wax.
- **Drones**: male honey bees; a colony has a few hundred.
- **Heliciculture** (snail farming): the rearing of snails for food.
:::

## Key points

- **Aquaculture**: tilapia and catfish; ponds need **water, clay soil, gentle slope**; screen the inlet; fertilise for plankton; avoid overstocking; harvest at 5–6 months.
- **Integrated farming** links ponds with poultry, pigs, rice and vegetables.
- **Bees**: **queen, drones, workers**; top-bar or frame hives; smoker and protective clothing; honey and wax; bees **pollinate** crops.
- **Rabbits**: raised hutches; gestation about 31 days. **Snails**: moist, shaded pens; calcium. **Grasscutters**: meat without hunting.
