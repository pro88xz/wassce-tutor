A single outbreak of disease can wipe out a farmer's flock in days: **Newcastle disease** can kill most of the chickens in a village, **African swine fever** can destroy a whole pig farm, and **PPR** kills many goats and sheep each year. Most of these losses can be prevented with good housing, hygiene, feeding, vaccination and early action. This lesson explains the signs of ill health, the causes and spread of animal diseases, the major diseases and parasites of farm animals in Sierra Leone, and the principles of prevention and control.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- recognise the signs of good health and ill health in farm animals
- classify animal diseases by their causes
- explain how diseases are spread
- describe major viral, bacterial and protozoan diseases of farm animals
- describe internal and external parasites and their control
- describe the general methods of preventing and controlling disease

## Signs of health and ill health

| Healthy animal | Sick animal |
|---|---|
| alert, active, moves with the group | dull, isolated, lies down a lot |
| eats and drinks well; ruminants chew the cud | loss of appetite; stops chewing the cud |
| smooth, shiny coat; bright, clear eyes | rough coat; watery or dull eyes; discharge from eyes or nose |
| normal droppings and urine | diarrhoea, blood in droppings, constipation |
| normal temperature, pulse and breathing | fever; rapid or laboured breathing; coughing |
| good production (growth, eggs, milk) | sudden fall in production; weight loss |

## Causes of disease

| Cause | Examples |
|---|---|
| **viruses** | Newcastle disease, Gumboro disease, fowl pox, PPR, foot-and-mouth disease, African swine fever, rabies |
| **bacteria** | anthrax, tuberculosis, brucellosis, mastitis, fowl typhoid, pneumonia |
| **protozoa** | **trypanosomiasis**, **coccidiosis**, babesiosis (redwater) |
| **fungi** | ringworm, aflatoxin poisoning from mouldy feed |
| **internal parasites** | roundworms, tapeworms, liver flukes |
| **external parasites** | ticks, mites (mange), lice, fleas, tsetse flies |
| **nutritional deficiencies** | rickets, anaemia, milk fever |
| **poisoning** | toxic plants, pesticides, mouldy feed |
| **injuries and stress** | wounds, overcrowding, heat stress |

## How diseases spread

- **Direct contact** with sick animals.
- **Contaminated feed, water, litter and equipment**.
- **Air** (coughing, sneezing).
- **Vectors**: ticks, tsetse flies, mosquitoes, flies.
- **People**: on shoes, clothes, hands and vehicles moving between farms.
- **New animals** brought in without quarantine; **wild birds and animals**.
- From mother to young.

## Major diseases

| Disease | Animals | Cause | Main signs | Prevention and control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Newcastle disease** | poultry | virus | gasping, twisted neck, green diarrhoea, sudden deaths, drop in eggs | **vaccination**; quarantine; hygiene |
| **Gumboro disease** | young chickens | virus | depression, whitish diarrhoea, deaths at 3–6 weeks | vaccination |
| **coccidiosis** | poultry, rabbits | protozoan | **bloody diarrhoea**, ruffled feathers, deaths | dry litter; coccidiostats in feed or water |
| **fowl pox** | poultry | virus | wart-like scabs on comb and wattles | vaccination; mosquito control |
| **PPR** (peste des petits ruminants) | goats, sheep | virus | fever, eye and nose discharge, mouth sores, diarrhoea, pneumonia, high death rate | **vaccination**; isolation |
| **trypanosomiasis** | cattle, goats, sheep, pigs | protozoan (*Trypanosoma*) spread by **tsetse flies** | anaemia, weakness, weight loss, fever | **trypanotolerant breeds (N'Dama)**; tsetse control; drugs |
| **foot-and-mouth disease** | cattle, goats, sheep, pigs | virus | blisters on mouth and feet, lameness, drooling | vaccination; movement control |
| **African swine fever** | pigs | virus | high fever, red skin, sudden deaths of most pigs | **no vaccine**: strict biosecurity; do not feed uncooked swill; quarantine; slaughter of infected herds |
| **anthrax** | cattle, goats, sheep | bacterium | sudden death, dark blood from body openings | vaccination; **never open the carcass**; burn or bury deeply |
| **mastitis** | cows, goats | bacteria | hot, swollen, painful udder; clots in milk | milking hygiene; antibiotics |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Opening the carcass of an animal suspected of anthrax.** Anthrax bacteria form **spores** when exposed to air, which survive in soil for decades and can infect people and animals. Report sudden deaths to a veterinary officer, and never open, skin or eat such a carcass.
:::

## Parasites

**Internal parasites (endoparasites)**: roundworms in the gut, tapeworms, liver flukes. They cause weight loss, pot belly, diarrhoea, anaemia (pale eyelids), rough coat and poor growth.

**Control:** regular **deworming** with anthelmintics; **rotational grazing** (worm larvae die on rested pasture); clean, dry housing; keeping animals off wet, snail-infested areas (liver fluke).

**External parasites (ectoparasites)**: ticks, mites, lice, fleas and biting flies. They suck blood, cause irritation, damage hides, and spread diseases (ticks spread babesiosis; tsetse spread trypanosomiasis).

**Control:** **dipping** or **spraying** with acaricides; hand-picking ticks; clean housing; burning or clearing bush near pens.

## Prevention and control of disease

:::callout{type=definition}
**Prevention** (cheaper and better than cure):

1. **Vaccination** on schedule.
2. **Good housing**: dry, ventilated, not overcrowded, easy to clean.
3. **Balanced feeding** and **clean water**.
4. **Hygiene and biosecurity**: footbaths with disinfectant at doors; clean equipment; limit visitors; clean and disinfect houses between batches ("all in, all out").
5. **Quarantine** new animals for 2–3 weeks before mixing.
6. **Control of parasites and vectors**.
7. Buying healthy stock from reliable sources.

**Control** when disease appears:

1. **Isolate** sick animals at once.
2. Call a **veterinary officer**; treat with the correct drugs.
3. **Cull** and dispose of dead animals properly (burn or bury deeply).
4. **Disinfect** houses and equipment.
5. **Restrict movement** of animals and people; report notifiable diseases.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A typical vaccination plan for broilers (follow the advice of your veterinary officer and the vaccine label).**

| Age | Vaccine | Method |
|---|---|---|
| day 1–7 | Newcastle disease (e.g. Hitchner B1 or Lasota) | eye drop or drinking water |
| about day 10–14 | Gumboro disease | drinking water |
| about day 21 | Newcastle disease booster | drinking water |
| about day 21–28 | Gumboro booster (where advised) | drinking water |

Water vaccines must be used in **clean water with no chlorine**, and birds are given water only after a short period without it, so that they drink the vaccine quickly.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Village chickens and Newcastle disease.** Newcastle disease sweeps through village chicken flocks, especially in the dry season, killing most of the birds. Community programmes that train **village vaccinators** to give a cheap, heat-tolerant Newcastle vaccine every few months have greatly reduced these losses, giving families more chickens to eat and sell.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. State five signs of ill health in a goat."}
Any five: dullness and isolation; loss of appetite; not chewing the cud; rough coat; discharge from eyes or nose; diarrhoea; fever; rapid breathing; weight loss.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Name the cause and vector of trypanosomiasis, and one control method."}
Cause: the protozoan *Trypanosoma*. Vector: the **tsetse fly**. Control: trypanotolerant breeds such as N'Dama; tsetse control; drugs.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Give two signs and two control measures of coccidiosis in poultry."}
Signs: bloody diarrhoea; ruffled feathers; droopiness; deaths. Control: keep litter dry; coccidiostats in feed or water; avoid overcrowding (any two of each).
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Distinguish between endoparasites and ectoparasites, with an example of each."}
**Endoparasites** live inside the host (roundworms, tapeworms, liver flukes). **Ectoparasites** live on the outside (ticks, mites, lice).
:::

:::reveal{title="5. List five ways of preventing disease on a livestock farm."}
Any five: vaccination; good housing; balanced feeding and clean water; hygiene and footbaths; quarantine of new animals; parasite and vector control; buying healthy stock.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Notifiable disease**: a disease, such as anthrax or foot-and-mouth disease, that must be reported to the veterinary authorities.
- **Endoparasites**: parasites that live inside the animal, such as roundworms, tapeworms and liver flukes.
- **Ectoparasites**: parasites that live on the outside of the animal, such as ticks, mites, lice and fleas.
- **Trypanosomiasis**: a protozoan disease of cattle, goats, sheep and pigs spread by tsetse flies.
- **Newcastle disease**: a virus disease of poultry, prevented by vaccination.
- **Quarantine**: keeping new or sick animals apart so they do not spread disease.
- **Vaccination**: giving a vaccine to protect animals against a disease.
:::

## Key points

- Know the signs of **health** and **ill health**.
- Causes: **viruses, bacteria, protozoa, fungi, parasites, deficiencies, poisons, injuries**.
- Spread by **contact, feed and water, air, vectors, people, new animals**.
- Key diseases: **Newcastle, Gumboro, coccidiosis, PPR, trypanosomiasis, foot-and-mouth, African swine fever, anthrax, mastitis**.
- **Deworm** for internal parasites; **dip or spray** for ticks and mites.
- **Prevention**: vaccination, housing, feeding, biosecurity, quarantine. **Control**: isolate, treat, cull, disinfect, restrict movement.
