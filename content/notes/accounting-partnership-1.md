:::callout{type=definition}
A **partnership** is an association of **two to twenty persons** who carry on business together with a view to **profit**.
:::

Its accounts differ from a sole trader's in two ways: the net profit is shared through a **partnership appropriation account**, using the terms of the **partnership agreement** (profit-sharing ratio, interest on capital, partners' salaries, interest on drawings), and each partner has a **capital account** and usually a **current account**. This lesson explains the partnership agreement, the rules that apply without one, and how to prepare the appropriation and current accounts.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a partnership and state the contents of a partnership agreement
- state the rules that apply when there is no agreement
- prepare a partnership appropriation account
- distinguish fixed from fluctuating capital accounts
- prepare partners' current accounts

## The partnership agreement

:::callout{type=definition}
A **partnership agreement** (deed of partnership) is a written or oral agreement setting out the partners' rights and duties.
:::

It usually covers:

1. the **capital** each partner contributes;
2. the **profit-sharing ratio** (PSR);
3. **interest on capital** and its rate;
4. **partners' salaries**;
5. **interest on drawings**;
6. **interest on partners' loans**;
7. the rules for **admission, retirement and death** of partners, and for settling disputes.

## Rules when there is no agreement

Where the partners have not agreed otherwise, the usual legal rules (based on the **Partnership Act 1890**, which WASSCE follows) apply:

1. Profits and losses are shared **equally**.
2. **No interest on capital**.
3. **No salaries** for partners.
4. **No interest on drawings**.
5. **Interest at 5% a year** on any **loan** a partner makes to the firm beyond their capital.

## Capital and current accounts

| Fixed capital method | Fluctuating capital method |
|---|---|
| capital accounts stay at the **agreed amount** | all items go through the **capital account** |
| a separate **current account** records salary, interest, profit share and drawings | no current account |
| capital changes only if more capital is introduced or withdrawn | capital changes every year |

**Partners' current accounts (fixed capital method)**

| | Kamara | Sesay |
|---|---|---|
| **Credit:** interest on capital | 6,000 | 4,000 |
| **Credit:** salary | | 8,000 |
| **Credit:** share of profit | 19,800 | 13,200 |
| **Total credits** | **25,800** | **25,200** |
| **Debit:** drawings | 15,000 | 12,000 |
| **Debit:** interest on drawings | 600 | 400 |
| **Total debits** | **15,600** | **12,400** |
| **Balance c/d (credit)** | **10,200** | **12,800** |

Both partners have **credit balances**: the firm owes them these amounts, shown under capital in the statement of financial position. A **debit** balance would mean the partner has drawn more than their share.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Partners' salaries** and **interest on capital** are **appropriations of profit**, not expenses: they go in the **appropriation account**, not the income statement. Only interest on a partner's **loan** is an expense.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Appropriation account**: the account that shares a partnership's net profit, after interest on drawings, partners' salaries and interest on capital, in the profit-sharing ratio.
- **Current account**: a partner's account recording their share of profit, interest, salary and drawings.
- **Interest on capital**: interest allowed to partners on the capital they have put in, an appropriation of profit.
- **Interest on drawings**: interest charged to partners on their drawings, which adds to profit to be shared.
- **Profit-sharing ratio** (PSR): the ratio in which partners share residual profit or loss.
:::

## Key points

- A **partnership agreement** sets out capital, PSR, interest, salaries and other rules.
- Without an agreement: **equal shares, no interest on capital or drawings, no salaries, 5% on partners' loans**.
- **Appropriation:** net profit + interest on drawings − salaries − interest on capital = **residual profit**, shared in the **PSR**.
- **Current accounts** record each partner's share, interest, salary and drawings.

## Quick check

1. State three items found in a partnership agreement.
2. In the absence of an agreement, how are profits shared?
3. Is a partner's salary an expense? Explain.
4. Residual profit NLe 24,000 is shared 2 : 1. Find each share.
5. What does a debit balance on a partner's current account mean?

**Answers:**

1. Any three: capital contributed; profit-sharing ratio; interest on capital; salaries; interest on drawings; interest on loans; rules for admission, retirement and disputes.
2. Equally.
3. No: it is an appropriation of profit, shown in the appropriation account.
4. NLe 16,000 and NLe 8,000.
5. That the partner has drawn more than their share of profit, salary and interest; the partner owes the firm.
