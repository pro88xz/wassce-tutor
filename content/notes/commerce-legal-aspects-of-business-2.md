# Consumer protection, business records and resolving commercial disputes

## Learning objectives
Explain consumer protection, identify useful business records and compare methods of resolving commercial disputes.

## Consumer protection
Consumer protection aims to ensure that buyers receive accurate information, safe products and fair treatment. Important concerns include misleading descriptions, unsafe goods, unfair contract terms, defective products, false pricing and failure to honour applicable warranties or remedies.

Businesses should provide truthful product information, display prices clearly, issue receipts, keep complaint records and handle personal data responsibly. Specific legal rights and enforcement procedures depend on the jurisdiction and current legislation.

## Business records
Reliable records support accountability and help resolve disputes:
- purchase orders show what was ordered;
- delivery notes record goods delivered;
- invoices request payment;
- receipts acknowledge payment;
- contracts record agreed obligations;
- stock records show inventory movements;
- correspondence records instructions and complaints.

Records should be accurate, dated, securely stored and accessible only to authorised staff. Retention periods should follow applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

## Resolving disputes
**Negotiation** allows parties to seek a direct settlement. **Mediation** uses an independent neutral person to help parties reach their own agreement. **Arbitration** involves an arbitrator who hears the dispute and makes a decision that may be binding under the agreement and applicable law. **Litigation** takes the dispute to court for determination.

The appropriate route depends on urgency, cost, confidentiality, relationship preservation, complexity and enforceability. A written contract can specify a dispute-resolution process, but it cannot remove mandatory legal rights.

## Ethical commercial conduct
Fair dealing requires accurate advertising, honest weights and measures, safe handling, respect for agreed terms and proper treatment of customers and suppliers. Ethical conduct reduces complaints and supports long-term reputation.

## WASSCE examination focus
Distinguish mediation from arbitration: a mediator facilitates agreement; an arbitrator decides the dispute. In consumer questions, link the business practice to the protection it provides.

## Quick check
1. State four examples of consumer-protection concerns.
2. Why should a business issue receipts?
3. Distinguish mediation from arbitration.
4. Give three records that can help resolve a delivery dispute.

**Answers:** 1. Unsafe goods, misleading claims, unfair terms, false pricing and defective products. 2. A receipt proves payment and records transaction details. 3. A mediator helps parties agree; an arbitrator makes a decision. 4. Purchase order, delivery note, invoice, contract and correspondence.
