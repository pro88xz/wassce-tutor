How much fencing does a farm need? How far does a bicycle wheel travel in one turn? How long is the curved edge of a round table? These are questions about **length** and **perimeter**, and WASSCE sets them as practical problems: plots of land, wheels, running tracks, and shapes made of rectangles and circles. This lesson covers perimeters of polygons, the circumference of a circle, arc length, composite shapes and Pythagoras' theorem for missing lengths.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- find the perimeter of polygons and composite shapes
- find the circumference of a circle and the length of an arc
- find the perimeter of a sector
- use Pythagoras' theorem to find missing lengths
- convert between units of length

## Units of length

| | |
|---|---|
| $10$ mm $= 1$ cm | $100$ cm $= 1$ m |
| $1000$ mm $= 1$ m | $1000$ m $= 1$ km |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Mixing units.** A rectangle $2$ m by $50$ cm has perimeter $2(2 + 0.5) = 5$ m, **not** $2(2 + 50) = 104$. Convert everything to the same unit **first**.
:::

## Perimeter of polygons

:::callout{type=definition}
The **perimeter** is the total distance around the **outside** of a shape. Add the lengths of all the outside edges.

- Rectangle: $P = 2(l + b)$
- Square: $P = 4s$
:::

### Composite shapes

![An L-shaped plot of land: 20 m along the bottom, 15 m on the left, 12 m along the top, a 6 m step down, 8 m across, and 9 m on the right; it divides into rectangles A and B](https://wasscetutor.com/img/mathematics/composite-plot.svg)

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Find the perimeter of the L-shaped plot.**

Go round the outside: $20 + 9 + 8 + 6 + 12 + 15 = 70$ m.

**Check the missing lengths:** the top ($12$) and the step ($8$) together equal the bottom: $12 + 8 = 20$ ✓. The right side ($9$) and the step down ($6$) equal the left side: $9 + 6 = 15$ ✓.
:::

## The circumference of a circle

:::callout{type=definition}
$$C = \pi d = 2\pi r$$

$\pi \approx 3.14$ or $\tfrac{22}{7}$. Use the value the question gives.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A bicycle wheel has a radius of 35 cm. How far does it travel in 100 complete turns? (Take $\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$.)**

One turn $= 2\pi r = 2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 35 = 220$ cm.

100 turns $= 22\,000$ cm $=$ **220 m**.
:::

## Arc length and the perimeter of a sector

![A sector of a circle with radius r and angle θ at the centre; arc length = (θ/360) × 2πr, sector area = (θ/360) × πr², and the perimeter of the sector = arc + 2r](https://wasscetutor.com/img/mathematics/sector-arc.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
An arc with angle $\theta$ at the centre is the fraction $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$ of the whole circumference:

$$\text{arc length} = \frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$$

The perimeter of a **sector** is the arc **plus two radii**: $\;P = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r + 2r$
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A sector has radius 14 cm and angle $90°$. Find its perimeter. ($\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$)**

Arc $= \tfrac{90}{360} \times 2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 14 = \tfrac14 \times 88 = 22$ cm.

Perimeter $= 22 + 14 + 14 =$ **50 cm**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Forgetting the two radii.** The perimeter of a sector is not just the arc. Its boundary also includes the two straight edges.
:::

## Finding the angle or radius from an arc

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**An arc of length 11 cm subtends an angle of $45°$ at the centre. Find the radius. ($\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$)**

$$11 = \frac{45}{360} \times 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times r = \frac{1}{8} \times \frac{44}{7} r = \frac{11}{14} r$$

$$r = 11 \times \frac{14}{11} = 14 \text{ cm}$$
:::

## Pythagoras' theorem

:::callout{type=definition}
In a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse $c$ (the longest side, opposite the right angle):

$$c^2 = a^2 + b^2$$

Common whole-number triples: $(3, 4, 5)$, $(5, 12, 13)$, $(8, 15, 17)$, $(7, 24, 25)$, and their multiples such as $(6, 8, 10)$.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A ladder 6.5 m long leans against a wall with its foot 2.5 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach?**

$$h = \sqrt{6.5^2 - 2.5^2} = \sqrt{42.25 - 6.25} = \sqrt{36} = 6 \text{ m}$$
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
A farmer in Kono fences a rectangular rice plot 40 m by 25 m, with a gate 3 m wide. Fencing needed $= 2(40 + 25) - 3 = 130 - 3 = $ **127 m**. If wire costs NLe 18 per metre, the fence costs $127 \times 18 = $ **NLe 2 286**.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Find the circumference of a circle of diameter 21 cm (π = 22/7)."}
$C = \pi d = \tfrac{22}{7} \times 21 = 66$ cm.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. A circular running track has circumference 440 m. Find its radius (π = 22/7)."}
$2\pi r = 440 \Rightarrow r = \dfrac{440 \times 7}{44} = 70$ m.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Find the perimeter of a sector of radius 7 cm and angle 120° (π = 22/7)."}
Arc $= \tfrac{120}{360} \times 2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 7 = \tfrac13 \times 44 = 14\tfrac23$ cm. Perimeter $= 14\tfrac23 + 14 = 28\tfrac23$ cm.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. A right-angled triangle has legs 9 cm and 12 cm. Find its perimeter."}
Hypotenuse $= \sqrt{81 + 144} = \sqrt{225} = 15$. Perimeter $= 9 + 12 + 15 = 36$ cm.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. A semicircular flower bed has diameter 14 m. Find its perimeter (π = 22/7)."}
Curved edge $= \tfrac12 \times \pi d = \tfrac12 \times 44 = 22$ m. Perimeter $= 22 + 14 = 36$ m.
:::

## Key points

- **Perimeter** is the distance round the outside; convert to **one unit** first.
- **Circumference**: $C = \pi d = 2\pi r$.
- **Arc length** $= \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$; **sector perimeter** = arc $+ 2r$.
- **Pythagoras**: $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$; learn the common triples.
- In composite shapes, **find missing lengths** before adding.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect questions on circumferences (wheels, tracks), arc lengths and sector perimeters (often with $\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$ chosen to give whole numbers), composite perimeters, and Pythagoras inside larger problems. The commonest losses are forgetting the radii of a sector, mixing units, and using the wrong value of $\pi$.
:::
