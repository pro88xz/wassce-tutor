Fluorine, chlorine, bromine and iodine form a family, the **halogens** ("salt-makers"). They look very different, from a pale gas to a shiny black solid, but they react in very similar ways with steady trends. This lesson covers the group trends, hydrogen chloride and hydrochloric acid, tests for halide ions, and the uses of halogen compounds, including the iodised salt that protects against goitre.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the physical properties of the halogens and their trends
- explain the trend in reactivity down group 7, with displacement reactions
- describe the preparation and properties of hydrogen chloride and hydrochloric acid
- describe tests for chloride, bromide and iodide ions
- state the uses of halogens and their compounds

## The halogens: group 7

All halogens have **seven outer electrons**. They gain one electron to form **halide ions** (F⁻, Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻) and exist as **diatomic molecules** (F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂).

| Halogen | State at room temperature | Colour | Melting point (°C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fluorine**, F₂ | Gas | Pale yellow | −220 |
| **Chlorine**, Cl₂ | Gas | Greenish-yellow | −101 |
| **Bromine**, Br₂ | Liquid | Red-brown (gives orange vapour) | −7 |
| **Iodine**, I₂ | Solid | Shiny grey-black (gives violet vapour); **sublimes** | 114 |

**Trends down the group:**

| Property | Trend down the group | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Melting and boiling points | **Increase** | Larger molecules have stronger van der Waals forces |
| Colour | Gets **darker** | |
| Atomic radius | **Increases** | More shells |
| Electronegativity | **Decreases** | Outer electrons further from the nucleus |
| **Reactivity (oxidising power)** | **Decreases** | A new electron is attracted less strongly by a more distant, better-shielded nucleus |

**Fluorine is the most reactive** non-metal of all.

## Displacement reactions

A **more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive one** from a solution of its halide.

| | KCl(aq) | KBr(aq) | KI(aq) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chlorine water** | No change | **Orange** (Br₂ formed) | **Brown** (I₂ formed) |
| **Bromine water** | No change | No change | **Brown** (I₂ formed) |
| **Iodine solution** | No change | No change | No change |

$$\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{Br}^- \rightarrow 2\text{Cl}^- + \text{Br}_2$$

$$\text{Br}_2 + 2\text{I}^- \rightarrow 2\text{Br}^- + \text{I}_2$$

The halogen is **reduced** (it is the oxidising agent); the halide ion is **oxidised**. Iodine turns **starch solution blue-black**, which makes the iodine easy to detect.

## Hydrogen chloride and hydrochloric acid

**Laboratory preparation:** warm **sodium chloride** with **concentrated sulphuric acid**:

$$\text{NaCl(s)} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\text{(l)} \rightarrow \text{NaHSO}_4\text{(s)} + \text{HCl(g)}$$

Dry it with concentrated sulphuric acid and collect by **downward delivery** (it is denser than air and very soluble in water).

**Properties of hydrogen chloride gas:**

- colourless, choking gas that **fumes in moist air**
- **extremely soluble** in water: the **fountain experiment** shows this; with litmus in the water, the fountain turns red
- forms **dense white fumes** of ammonium chloride with ammonia: NH₃ + HCl → NH₄Cl
- dry HCl gas (or HCl in methylbenzene) shows **no acidic properties**; it ionises only in water

:::callout{type=definition}
**Hydrochloric acid** (HCl in water) is a **strong acid**.
:::

It reacts with metals, bases and carbonates. A concentrated solution is about 36% HCl.

**Uses of hydrochloric acid:**

- cleaning rust from metal before galvanising ("pickling")
- making chlorides
- processing food (gelatine) and leather
- in the stomach: hydrochloric acid helps digestion and kills germs in food

## Tests for halide ions

Add **dilute nitric acid**, then **silver nitrate solution**. The nitric acid removes carbonate ions, which would also give a precipitate.

| Ion | Precipitate | Effect of adding aqueous ammonia |
|---|---|---|
| **Chloride**, Cl⁻ | **White** (AgCl); darkens in light | **Dissolves** |
| **Bromide**, Br⁻ | **Cream** (AgBr) | Slightly soluble (dissolves in concentrated ammonia) |
| **Iodide**, I⁻ | **Yellow** (AgI) | **Insoluble** |

$$\text{Ag}^+\text{(aq)} + \text{Cl}^-\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{AgCl(s)}$$

## Uses of halogens and their compounds

| Substance | Use |
|---|---|
| **Fluorides** | **Toothpaste** and some water supplies (strengthen tooth enamel) |
| **PTFE** (Teflon) | Non-stick cooking pans |
| **Chlorine** and **bleach** | Water treatment, disinfection, bleaching |
| **PVC** | Pipes, cables, flooring |
| **Bromine compounds** | Fire retardants; formerly in leaded petrol |
| **Silver bromide** | Photographic film (decomposes in light) |
| **Iodine** | **Tincture of iodine** antiseptic (iodine in ethanol) |
| **Potassium iodide / iodate** | **Iodised salt**, preventing goitre |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Lack of **iodine** in the diet causes **goitre** (swelling of the thyroid gland) and can harm children's development. Sierra Leone, like many countries, promotes **iodised salt**: common salt with a little potassium iodate added. Buy iodised salt, store it covered and add it near the end of cooking, because iodine is slowly lost from salt left open in humid air.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Down group 7, reactivity **decreases**, the opposite of group 1. Chlorine displaces iodine from iodides, but iodine **cannot** displace chlorine from chlorides.
:::

## Key points

- Halogens have **7 outer electrons**, form **X⁻ ions** and **diatomic molecules**.
- Down the group: **darker**, **higher melting points**, **less reactive**. F₂ and Cl₂ are gases, Br₂ a liquid, I₂ a solid that sublimes.
- A **more reactive halogen displaces** a less reactive one from its halide (Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂).
- **HCl:** NaCl + conc. H₂SO₄; fumes in moist air; very soluble (fountain); white fumes with NH₃.
- **Halide test:** HNO₃ then AgNO₃: **white** (Cl⁻), **cream** (Br⁻), **yellow** (I⁻).
- Uses: **fluoride toothpaste, water treatment, PVC, AgBr film, iodine antiseptic, iodised salt**.

## Quick check

1. Why does reactivity decrease down group 7?
2. What is seen when chlorine water is added to potassium iodide solution? Write an ionic equation.
3. Why is dilute nitric acid added before silver nitrate in the test for halides?
4. Give one use of iodine or its compounds.

**Answers:**

1. The atoms get larger with more shielding, so an incoming electron is attracted less strongly.
2. The solution turns brown as iodine forms: Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂.
3. To remove carbonate ions, which would also give a white precipitate with silver nitrate.
4. Any one: tincture of iodine (antiseptic); iodised salt (prevents goitre); testing for starch.
