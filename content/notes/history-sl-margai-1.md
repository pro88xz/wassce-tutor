**Sir Milton Margai's government (1961–1964)** led Sierra Leone through its first years of independence. A cautious, consensus-seeking leader known as "**Pa**", Margai kept the country **peaceful and united**, worked closely with **paramount chiefs**, joined the **United Nations**, the **Commonwealth** and the **Organisation of African Unity**, and laid foundations for development, including the **Bank of Sierra Leone** and the national currency, the **leone (1964)**, and **Njala University College**. His government also faced **diamond smuggling**, a growing **APC opposition** and complaints of favouritism. When he died on **28 April 1964**, his brother **Albert Margai** succeeded him. This lesson explains **Sierra Leone at independence and the achievements and problems of Sir Milton Margai's government**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the character and style of Sir Milton Margai's leadership

## Government at independence

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Head of state** | **Queen Elizabeth II**, represented by a **Governor-General** |
| **Head of government** | the **Prime Minister**, leader of the majority in Parliament |
| **Parliament** | the **House of Representatives**, with elected members and **paramount chief** members |
| **Courts** | final appeals to the **Privy Council** in London |
| **Rights** | a **Bill of Rights** protected fundamental freedoms |

## Margai's style of leadership

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figure"}
**Sir Milton Margai (1895–1964)** was a **medical doctor** from **Gbangbatoke** in Moyamba District.
:::

He was:

1. **Moderate and cautious**, preferring gradual change and good relations with Britain.
2. **Consensus-seeking**: he formed a **broad government** and brought rivals into his party.
3. **Close to the chiefs**, whose support gave the SLPP strength in the provinces.
4. **Tolerant of opposition**: although APC leaders were detained in 1961, he generally allowed the press and opposition to operate.
5. **Fatherly**: people called him "**Pa**", and he was respected for his honesty and simple life.

## Achievements of Milton Margai's government

1. **Peaceful independence** and the **smooth transfer of power** from Britain.
2. **National unity**: he eased the **Colony–Protectorate divide**, bringing Krio and provincial leaders into government.
3. **Democracy**: the **1962 general election** was held under the independence constitution, and the SLPP won.
4. **Economic institutions**: the **Bank of Sierra Leone** was established and the **leone** replaced the West African pound in **1964**.
5. **Education**: expansion of schools and the founding of **Njala University College (1964)**, alongside Fourah Bay College.
6. **Infrastructure**: roads, the extension of electricity and water supplies, and health services.
7. **Agriculture**: support for cash crops and rice through the **Produce Marketing Board**.

## Foreign relations

1. **United Nations**: joined on **27 September 1961**.
2. **Commonwealth**: joined at independence in **1961**.
3. **OAU**: Sierra Leone was a **founding member** at Addis Ababa in **1963**, and supported the moderate **Monrovia Group**.
4. **Non-alignment**: Margai kept friendly ties with the West, especially **Britain** and the **USA**, while avoiding Cold War blocs.
5. **Good neighbourliness** with **Guinea** and **Liberia**.

## Problems of the government

1. **Diamond smuggling**: illegal mining and smuggling in **Kono** cost the state large revenues.
2. **Opposition**: the **APC** grew strong in the **north** and in **Freetown**; it won control of **Freetown City Council** in **1964**, with **Siaka Stevens** as mayor.
3. **Accusations of favouritism**: critics said the SLPP favoured the **south and east** and the **Mende**.
4. **Dependence on minerals and foreign aid**, with few industries.
5. **Unemployment** among young people who moved to Freetown.
6. **Succession**: there was no clear plan for who would follow the ageing Prime Minister.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Evaluating Sir Milton Margai**

**Strengths**: he led Sierra Leone to independence **peacefully**, kept the country **united and stable**, respected **parliamentary democracy**, and built key national institutions such as the **Bank of Sierra Leone** and **Njala**.

**Weaknesses**: his government relied heavily on **chiefs**, did little to stop **diamond smuggling**, and allowed regional and ethnic tensions to grow. When he died suddenly in **1964**, the **succession** was disputed, which opened the way to the crises of the following years.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sir Milton Margai is honoured as Sierra Leone's **founding father**. The **Milton Margai Technical University** (formerly Milton Margai College of Education and Technology) in Freetown, and the **Milton Margai School for the Blind**, carry his name, recalling his lifelong concern for education and health.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
At independence Sierra Leone was **not yet a republic**. The **Queen** remained head of state, represented by a **Governor-General**, until Sierra Leone became a **republic on 19 April 1971**.
:::

## Key points

- At independence: **Queen** as head of state, **Governor-General**, **Prime Minister**, **Parliament**, appeals to the **Privy Council**.
- **Milton Margai**: moderate, consensus-seeking, close to chiefs, called "**Pa**".
- Achievements: **peaceful independence, unity, 1962 election, Bank of Sierra Leone and the leone (1964), Njala University College (1964)**.
- Foreign relations: **UN (1961)**, **Commonwealth**, founding member of the **OAU (1963)**, non-alignment.
- Problems: **diamond smuggling, APC opposition, regional favouritism, dependence**, unclear **succession**.
- Died **28 April 1964**; succeeded by **Albert Margai**.
