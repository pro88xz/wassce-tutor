**Sierra Leone's foreign policy** since independence in 1961 has been guided by **non-alignment**, **good neighbourliness**, **African unity**, the **peaceful settlement of disputes** and support for the **United Nations**. Sierra Leone joined the **United Nations** and the **Commonwealth** in **1961**, was a **founding member** of the **Organisation of African Unity (1963)**, the **Mano River Union (1973)** and **ECOWAS (1975)**, and belongs to the **African Union**, the **Non-Aligned Movement** and the **Organisation of Islamic Cooperation**. It hosted the **OAU summit of 1980**, has sent **peacekeepers** abroad, and served on the **UN Security Council** for **2024–2025**. This lesson explains **the objectives of Sierra Leone's foreign policy and its role in international organisations**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define foreign policy and state the objectives of Sierra Leone's foreign policy
- explain the factors that shape Sierra Leone's foreign policy
- describe Sierra Leone's membership of international organisations
- explain the benefits Sierra Leone gains from these organisations
- describe Sierra Leone's contributions to the international community

## Meaning and objectives of foreign policy

:::callout{type=definition}
**Foreign policy** is the set of principles and actions a country follows in its relations with other countries and international organisations, in order to protect and promote its **national interests**.
:::

**Objectives of Sierra Leone's foreign policy:**

1. To **protect the sovereignty** and territorial integrity of Sierra Leone.
2. To promote **economic development** through trade, investment and aid.
3. To promote **peace and security**, especially in West Africa.
4. To support **African unity** and the **liberation** of Africa from colonialism and apartheid.
5. To maintain **good relations with neighbours**, especially **Guinea** and **Liberia**.
6. To follow **non-alignment**, avoiding the blocs of the Cold War.
7. To support the **United Nations** and international law, including **human rights**.

## Factors that shape Sierra Leone's foreign policy

| Factor | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Small size and limited power** | Sierra Leone works through **international organisations** to make its voice heard |
| **Economic needs** | dependence on **mineral exports**, aid and investment |
| **Geography** | neighbours **Guinea** and **Liberia**; conflicts across borders affected Sierra Leone directly |
| **History** | colonial ties with **Britain**; the experience of the **slave trade** and the **civil war** |
| **Leaders' views** | each government's ideas shaped policy, such as **Stevens's** hosting of the OAU summit |
| **Religion and culture** | both Christian and Muslim communities; ties with the Islamic world |

## Benefits of membership

1. **Peace and security:** **ECOWAS (ECOMOG)**, the **UN (UNAMSIL)** and **Britain** helped to end the civil war.
2. **Development aid and loans:** from the **UN agencies**, the **World Bank**, the **African Development Bank** and the **Islamic Development Bank**.
3. **Trade and free movement:** ECOWAS allows **free movement** of citizens and promotes regional trade.
4. **A voice in world affairs:** small states gain influence by working together.
5. **Debt relief:** international support helped Sierra Leone receive major **debt relief** in the 2000s.
6. **Health emergencies:** international support during the **Ebola outbreak (2014–2016)**.

## Sierra Leone's contributions

1. **Pan-African and liberation support:** Sierra Leone supported **African liberation movements** and opposed **apartheid**.
2. **Hosting the OAU summit (1980):** President **Stevens** became **OAU chairman**.
3. **Peacekeeping:** after rebuilding its army, Sierra Leone sent troops to **UN and AU missions**, including in **Darfur (Sudan)**, **Somalia (AMISOM)**, **Lebanon** and **Mali**.
4. **UN reform:** President **Ernest Bai Koroma** coordinated the AU's **Committee of Ten (C-10)** on reform of the UN Security Council.
5. **Justice:** the **Special Court for Sierra Leone** set examples in international law, including the conviction of **Charles Taylor** and judgments on **child soldiers** and **forced marriage**.
6. **Regional leadership:** in **2025** President **Julius Maada Bio** became **Chairman of the ECOWAS Authority of Heads of State and Government**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's experience of war and recovery made it a voice for **peacebuilding** in international meetings. It helped to found the **g7+**, a group of countries affected by conflict, which argues for better ways of supporting fragile states, and its peacekeepers have served in several UN missions.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not confuse the **Mano River Union** (founded in **1973** by Sierra Leone and Liberia, later joined by Guinea and Côte d'Ivoire) with **ECOWAS** (founded in **1975** with fifteen West African states). The Mano River Union is a smaller sub-regional body.
:::

## Key points

- Foreign policy aims: **sovereignty, development, peace, African unity, good neighbourliness, non-alignment, support for the UN**.
- Shaped by **small size, economic needs, geography, history, leaders, culture**.
- Memberships: **UN and Commonwealth (1961)**, **OAU (1963) / AU (2002)**, **Mano River Union (1973)**, **ECOWAS (1975)**, **NAM**, **OIC**.
- Benefits: **peace (ECOMOG, UNAMSIL)**, aid, trade, voice, debt relief, health support.
- Contributions: **OAU summit (1980)**, **peacekeeping**, **C-10**, the **Special Court**, the **UN Security Council (2024–2025)**, **ECOWAS chair (2025)**.
