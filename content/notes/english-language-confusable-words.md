*"The new rules will **effect** everyone."* *"I am **loosing** my patience."* *"He **lay** the book on the table."* Each sentence contains one of the word pairs that trip up students, teachers and even newspaper writers every day. In WASSCE, they cost marks twice: in the objective paper, where you must choose the right word for a gap, and in your essays, where every confusion counts against you. This lesson groups the most common confusables, explains the difference simply, and gives you memory tricks and practice.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- tell apart the most common pairs of confusable words
- use memory tricks to choose correctly under exam pressure
- avoid the errors that examiners see most often in essays
- answer gap-filling questions on confusable words

## Pairs that sound alike

| Pair | Meaning and use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **affect / effect** | *affect* is usually a **verb** (to influence); *effect* is usually a **noun** (a result) | The rain **affected** the harvest. The **effect** was a poor harvest. |
| **accept / except** | *accept* = to receive or agree; *except* = apart from | She **accepted** the gift. Everyone came **except** Musa. |
| **advice / advise** | *advice* is the **noun** (/s/ sound); *advise* is the **verb** (/z/ sound) | My father **advised** me. I took his **advice**. |
| **practice / practise** | *practice* is the **noun**; *practise* is the **verb** (British spelling) | We **practise** daily. Football **practice** is at four. |
| **principal / principle** | *principal* = head of a school, or main; *principle* = a rule or belief | The **principal** spoke. Honesty is my **principle**. |
| **stationary / stationery** | *stationary* = not moving; *stationery* = paper, pens, envelopes | The bus was **stationary**. Buy **stationery** for school. |
| **complement / compliment** | *complement* = to complete or go well with; *compliment* = praise | The sauce **complements** the rice. She **complimented** my essay. |
| **lose / loose** | *lose* (/uːz/) = to stop having; *loose* (/uːs/) = not tight | Don't **lose** your phone. This shirt is **loose**. |
| **then / than** | *then* = at that time, next; *than* = used in comparisons | She is taller **than** me. We ate, **then** we left. |
| **their / there / they're** | *their* = belonging to them; *there* = in that place; *they're* = they are | **They're** leaving **their** bags over **there**. |
| **its / it's** | *its* = belonging to it; *it's* = it is / it has | **It's** raining. The dog wagged **its** tail. |
| **whose / who's** | *whose* = belonging to whom; *who's* = who is / who has | **Who's** coming? **Whose** pen is this? |

:::callout{type=tip}
**Memory tricks:**

- **A**ffect is an **A**ction (verb); **E**ffect is an **E**nd result (noun).
- The princi**pal** is your **pal**; a princip**le** is a ru**le**.
- Station**e**ry: **e** for **e**nvelopes. Station**a**ry: **a** for st**a**nding still.
- Compl**i**ment: **I** like praise. Compl**e**ment: it compl**e**tes.
- **Its** and **it's**: if you can say *it is*, use the apostrophe.
:::

## Verb pairs that are easy to mix up

### lie / lay

| Verb | Meaning | Present | Past | Past participle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **lie** | to rest flat (no object) | lie, lying | **lay** | lain |
| **lay** | to put something down (needs an object) | lay, laying | **laid** | laid |
| **lie** | to tell an untruth | lie, lying | **lied** | lied |

> *I **lie** on the mat every afternoon. Yesterday I **lay** there for an hour.*
> *Please **lay** the books on the table. She **laid** them neatly.*
> *He **lied** about his age.*

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
*"I was **laying** in bed"* is wrong: you are not putting anything down. It should be *"I was **lying** in bed."* Hens **lay** eggs; tired students **lie** down.
:::

### rise / raise

| Verb | Meaning | Forms | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **rise** | to go up (no object) | rise, rose, risen | The sun **rises**. Prices **rose**. |
| **raise** | to lift something (needs an object) | raise, raised, raised | **Raise** your hand. They **raised** the fees. |

### borrow / lend

You **borrow from** someone; someone **lends to** you.

> *Can I **borrow** your pen?* (I take it.) *Can you **lend** me your pen?* (You give it.)

:::callout{type=local-example}
In Krio, one word, *borrow*, covers both directions (*"Borrow me your pen"*). In standard English this is wrong: say *"**Lend** me your pen"* or *"Can I **borrow** your pen?"* This error appears often in Sierra Leonean candidates' essays.
:::

### Other pairs to master

| Pair | Difference | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **bring / take** | *bring* = towards the speaker; *take* = away | **Bring** your book here. **Take** this letter to the office. |
| **learn / teach** | you *learn* from a teacher; a teacher *teaches* you | Mr Koroma **taught** us; we **learnt** a lot. (not *"he learnt us"*) |
| **say / tell** | *tell* needs a person as object; *say* doesn't | She **told me** the news. She **said** it was late. |
| **fewer / less** | *fewer* with countable nouns; *less* with uncountable | **fewer** students, **less** water |
| **between / among** | *between* two; *among* more than two | **between** Bo and Kenema; **among** the students |
| **beside / besides** | *beside* = next to; *besides* = in addition | Sit **beside** me. **Besides** Maths, I like Biology. |
| **economic / economical** | *economic* = about the economy; *economical* = saving money | **economic** growth; an **economical** car |
| **historic / historical** | *historic* = very important in history; *historical* = about the past | a **historic** election; **historical** documents |

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Choose: 'The drought had a serious (affect / effect) on food prices.'"}
**effect**: a noun, the result.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Correct the sentence: 'Can you borrow me your bicycle?'"}
**Can you lend me your bicycle?** (or *Can I borrow your bicycle?*)
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Choose: 'There were (less / fewer) candidates this year (then / than) last year.'"}
**fewer** (candidates are countable) and **than** (a comparison).
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Correct: 'The principle advised us to practice everyday.'"}
*The **principal** advised us to **practise every day**.* (Three corrections: *principal*, the verb *practise*, and *every day* as two words, meaning *each day*.)
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Choose: 'The hen has (laid / lain) three eggs, and now she is (lying / laying) in the shade.'"}
**laid** (put down eggs) and **lying** (resting).
:::

:::reveal{title="6. Choose: 'Our teacher (learnt / taught) us how to write a summary.'"}
**taught**: a teacher teaches; students learn.
:::

## Key points

- Sound-alike pairs: **affect/effect, accept/except, advice/advise, practice/practise, principal/principle, stationary/stationery, complement/compliment, lose/loose, then/than, their/there/they're, its/it's**.
- Verb pairs: **lie/lay**, **rise/raise**, **borrow/lend**, **bring/take**, **learn/teach**, **say/tell**.
- **Fewer** with countable nouns, **less** with uncountable; **between** two, **among** more.
- Use the **memory tricks** when you are unsure under pressure.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
The objective paper tests confusables through **gap-filling** (*choose the word that best completes the sentence*), and essays are marked down for them under **Mechanical accuracy** and **Expression**. The pairs above are among the commonest errors in candidates' scripts. Learn them until the right choice is automatic.
:::
