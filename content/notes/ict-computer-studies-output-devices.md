:::callout{type=definition}
An **output device** is any hardware device that presents the results of processing from a computer to the user, as text, pictures, sound or movement, such as a monitor, a printer, a projector or a speaker.
:::

The computer processes data, but you only see the result because an **output device** shows it: a picture on a screen, a printed result slip, music from a speaker or a phone vibrating. This lesson describes the **types of output devices and their functions**: soft copy and hard copy, monitors and screen resolution, projectors, the **types of printers** (impact and non-impact), plotters, sound output and devices that are both input and output.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define an output device and give examples
- distinguish soft copy output from hard copy output
- describe the types of monitors and explain pixels and resolution
- classify printers as impact or non-impact, with examples, advantages and disadvantages
- describe plotters, projectors, speakers and other output devices
- choose a suitable output device for a given task

## Soft copy and hard copy

:::callout{type=definition}
**Soft copy:** output that is shown on a screen or heard through a speaker; it is not permanent and cannot be held in the hand.

**Hard copy:** output printed on paper or another material; it is permanent and can be carried and read without a computer.
:::

| Soft copy | Hard copy |
|---|---|
| on a **screen** or as **sound** | on **paper** or film |
| not permanent; lost when the device is off | permanent |
| easy to change and to share electronically | cannot be changed once printed |
| needs a device to view | can be read anywhere |
| produced by monitors, projectors, speakers | produced by printers and plotters |

## Monitors (screens)

:::callout{type=definition}
**Monitor:** an output device that displays text, pictures and video from the computer on a screen; also called a visual display unit (VDU).

**Pixel:** short for picture element, the smallest dot of light on a screen; every picture on a screen is made of pixels.

**Resolution:** the number of pixels a screen can display, written as width × height, such as 1920 × 1080; more pixels give a sharper picture.
:::

| Type of monitor | How it works | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **CRT** (cathode ray tube) | an electron beam lights a glass screen | old type; heavy, bulky, uses much power |
| **LCD** (liquid crystal display) | liquid crystals let light through, lit from behind | flat, light, uses less power |
| **LED** (light-emitting diode) | an LCD lit by LEDs | thinner, brighter and more efficient than older LCDs |
| **OLED** (organic LED) | each pixel makes its own light | very thin, deep black colours; used in many smartphones |

A **touch screen** is both an **input** and an **output** device: it shows the output and you touch it to give input.

**Screen size** is measured **diagonally**, from one corner to the opposite corner, in inches, such as a 15.6-inch laptop screen.

## Projectors

A **multimedia (data) projector** shows the computer's output on a large screen or a wall so that a whole class or meeting can see it. It is used for lessons, presentations, film shows and church services.

## Printers

:::callout{type=definition}
**Printer:** an output device that produces a hard copy of text and pictures on paper.

**Impact printer:** a printer that makes characters by striking an inked ribbon against the paper.

**Non-impact printer:** a printer that forms characters without striking the paper, using ink sprays, toner and laser light, or heat.
:::

![Types of printers: printers are impact or non-impact. Impact printers, such as dot-matrix, daisy-wheel and line printers, strike an inked ribbon against the paper; they are noisy but can print carbon copies. Non-impact printers, such as inkjet, laser, thermal and 3D printers, do not strike the paper; they are quiet and give high-quality output](https://wasscetutor.com/img/ict/types-of-printers.svg)

### Impact printers

- **Dot-matrix printer:** a print head with a grid of small pins strikes the ribbon, so each character is made of dots. Cheap to run and can print **multi-part (carbon) forms**, such as invoices and receipts, but noisy and of low quality.
- **Daisy-wheel printer:** a wheel of "petals", each carrying one character, strikes the ribbon; letter-quality text but slow, and cannot print pictures. It is now rarely used.
- **Line printer:** prints a whole line at a time; very fast, used with mainframes for large reports.

### Non-impact printers

- **Inkjet printer:** sprays tiny drops of liquid ink onto the paper. Cheap to buy, good for colour photographs, but the ink cartridges are expensive and printing is slower than a laser printer.
- **Laser printer:** a laser beam draws the page on a drum; powdered **toner** sticks to it and is fused onto the paper by heat. Fast, quiet and high quality, with a low cost per page; used in offices and schools.
- **Thermal printer:** uses heat on special heat-sensitive paper. Used for **till receipts**, ATM slips and bus or parking tickets.
- **3D printer:** builds a solid object layer by layer from plastic or other material, from a computer design.

| | Impact printers | Non-impact printers |
|---|---|---|
| How they print | strike a ribbon on the paper | no striking: ink, toner or heat |
| Noise | noisy | quiet |
| Quality | lower | high |
| Speed | mostly slower (except line printers) | mostly faster |
| Carbon copies | **can** print multi-part forms | cannot |
| Examples | dot-matrix, daisy-wheel, line | inkjet, laser, thermal, 3D |

:::callout{type=definition}
**Plotter:** an output device that draws high-quality, large-size drawings, such as building plans, maps and engineering designs, using pens or ink jets.
:::

**Printer speed** is measured in **pages per minute (ppm)** for laser and inkjet printers and **characters per second (cps)** for dot-matrix printers. **Print quality** is measured in **dots per inch (dpi)**.

## Sound and other output

- **Speakers** and **headphones (earphones)** output sound: music, voices and alerts.
- **Speech synthesisers** turn text into spoken words, helping people who are blind or who cannot read the screen.
- **Actuators** are motors and switches controlled by a computer, such as those that open an automatic door or move a robot arm.
- **Buzzers, lights (LEDs) and vibration** give simple signals, such as a phone vibrating for a message.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Output devices at work in Sierra Leone.** A cyber café prints passport-photo pages and CVs on an **inkjet** or **laser** printer; a supermarket till prints receipts on a **thermal** printer; a school shows lessons with a **projector**; and the result-checker website shows your WASSCE result as **soft copy** that you can then print as a **hard copy**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Calling a laser printer an impact printer because it is a "heavy" printer.** Whether a printer is impact or non-impact depends only on whether it **strikes** the paper. Laser printers never strike the paper, so they are non-impact.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Define an output device and give three examples."}
A hardware device that **presents the results** of processing to the user. Examples: monitor, printer, projector, speaker, plotter, headphones (any three).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. State two differences between soft copy and hard copy."}
Any two: soft copy is on a **screen** or heard, hard copy is **printed**; soft copy is **not permanent**, hard copy is **permanent**; soft copy can be **edited and shared** easily, hard copy cannot be changed; soft copy needs a **device** to view it.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Classify these printers as impact or non-impact: laser, dot-matrix, inkjet, line, thermal."}
**Impact:** dot-matrix, line. **Non-impact:** laser, inkjet, thermal.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Which type of printer is used to print invoices with carbon copies, and why?"}
A **dot-matrix** (impact) printer, because its pins **strike** the paper hard enough to make copies on the sheets underneath.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. What is meant by a screen resolution of 1920 × 1080?"}
The screen shows **1920 pixels across** and **1080 pixels down**, about 2 million pixels in all; the more pixels, the sharper the picture.
:::

## Key points

- An **output device** presents processed results: **soft copy** (screen, sound) or **hard copy** (print).
- Monitors: **CRT, LCD, LED, OLED**; pictures are made of **pixels**; **resolution** = pixels across × down.
- **Impact** printers (dot-matrix, daisy-wheel, line) strike a ribbon: noisy, can print carbon copies.
- **Non-impact** printers (inkjet, laser, thermal, 3D) do not strike the paper: quiet, high quality.
- **Plotters** draw large plans and maps; **projectors** show output on a big screen; **speakers** output sound.
- A **touch screen** is both an input and an output device.
