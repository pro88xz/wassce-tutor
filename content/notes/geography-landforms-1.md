# Major landforms: mountains, plateaus and plains

## Learning objectives
You should be able to define major landform categories, describe their characteristics and explain the principal processes responsible for their formation.

## What is a landform?
A landform is a natural physical feature of the Earth's surface with a recognisable shape and origin. Landforms develop through internal processes such as tectonic movement and volcanism, and external processes such as weathering, erosion, transportation and deposition. Their present form reflects the interaction of these processes over time.

## Mountains
Mountains are areas of high relief that rise prominently above surrounding land. They may form through:
- **Fold mountain building:** compression causes rock layers to buckle and fold, often where tectonic plates converge.
- **Faulting:** blocks of crust move along faults; uplifted blocks may form block mountains and downthrown blocks may form rift valleys.
- **Volcanic activity:** repeated eruptions build volcanic cones or plateaus.
- **Erosion:** resistant rock may remain as residual mountains after surrounding weaker material is removed.

Mountains influence rainfall, temperature, drainage, transport and settlement. Steep slopes may limit farming and construction but provide water resources, minerals and tourism opportunities.

## Plateaus
A plateau is an extensive area of relatively elevated land with a broad, comparatively level or gently rolling surface. Plateaus may form through uplift of large crustal blocks, volcanic lava accumulation or long-term erosion that leaves a high surface. Their edges may be marked by steep escarpments.

Plateaus can support grazing, mining and settlement, but dissected surfaces and steep margins may make transport difficult. Rivers flowing across plateaus may cut deep valleys and gorges.

## Plains
A plain is a broad area of low relief with generally gentle slopes. Plains may be formed by river deposition, marine deposition, glacial deposition or prolonged erosion that reduces relief. Alluvial plains develop where rivers deposit sediment across low-lying areas; coastal plains may develop through marine deposition or changes in sea level.

Plains often favour agriculture, transport and settlement because slopes are gentle and construction is easier. However, floodplains may be exposed to flooding, waterlogging and seasonal changes in river level.

## Relief and human activity
Relief affects accessibility, land use, settlement density and the cost of infrastructure. Gentle terrain often supports dense transport networks, while rugged mountains may require tunnels, bridges and winding roads. Relief also affects local climate: air forced to rise over mountains cools, which can produce orographic rainfall on windward slopes and drier conditions on leeward slopes.

## WASSCE examination focus
Give a precise definition and explain formation. Do not define a plateau merely as a flat area; mention its elevated position. Distinguish mountains by high relief, plateaus by elevated extensive surfaces and plains by low relief and gentle slopes.

## Quick check
1. Define a plateau.
2. State two processes that can form mountains.
3. Give two possible origins of plains.
4. Explain one way relief affects transport.
