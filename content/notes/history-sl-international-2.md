**Sierra Leone's relations with its neighbours and partners** have shaped its history since independence. With **Liberia** and **Guinea** it shares borders, peoples and the **Mano River Union**, founded in **1973**; but the **Liberian civil war** spilled into Sierra Leone in **1991**, and a dispute with Guinea over the border town of **Yenga** followed the war. Beyond West Africa, Sierra Leone's main partners include **Britain**, its former colonial power, which intervened militarily in **2000** and helped rebuild the army; the **United States**; **China**, which has funded stadiums, roads and buildings; the **European Union**; and the **IMF and World Bank**. This lesson explains **Sierra Leone's relations with its neighbours, the Mano River Union, and its main international partners**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe Sierra Leone's relations with Guinea and Liberia
- explain the aims, achievements and problems of the Mano River Union
- describe Sierra Leone's relations with Britain since independence
- describe its relations with the USA, China and other partners
- explain the role of international financial institutions in Sierra Leone
- explain how international help supported recovery from war and Ebola

## Relations with Guinea and Liberia

| Neighbour | Relations |
|---|---|
| **Guinea** | close ties since independence; Guinea sent **troops to protect Siaka Stevens (1971)**; hosted **Momoh (1992)** and **Kabbah (1997–98)** in exile and received many **refugees**; but a dispute arose over **Yenga**, a border town in **Kailahun District** occupied by Guinean troops after the war, which the two governments have worked to settle by negotiation |
| **Liberia** | shared peoples (**Vai, Kissi, Gola, Mende**) and the **Mano River** border; **Charles Taylor's** support for the **RUF** helped to start and prolong the civil war; since 2003, peace in both countries has brought renewed cooperation |

## The Mano River Union

![Map of the Mano River Union: Sierra Leone and Liberia, founders in 1973; Guinea, which joined in 1980; and Côte d'Ivoire, which joined in 2008; with the secretariat in Freetown and the Mano River on the Sierra Leone–Liberia border](https://wasscetutor.com/img/history/mano-river-union-map.svg)

*Figure 1. The Mano River Union. WASSCE Tutor map.*

The **Mano River Union (MRU)** was founded on **3 October 1973** by the **Mano River Declaration** between **Sierra Leone** (President Siaka Stevens) and **Liberia** (President William Tolbert). **Guinea** joined in **1980** and **Côte d'Ivoire** in **2008**. Its secretariat is in **Freetown**.

**Aims:**

1. To promote **economic cooperation** and a **customs union** among members.
2. To develop **joint projects** in transport, energy, agriculture and industry.
3. Later, to promote **peace and security** along the members' borders.

**Achievements:** a **customs union** framework; the **Mano River Bridge** linking Sierra Leone and Liberia; training institutions; cooperation on **border security** and on fighting **Ebola**.

**Problems:** **civil wars** in Liberia and Sierra Leone in the 1990s **paralysed** the Union; **lack of funds**; **political mistrust** between leaders; and overlap with **ECOWAS**.

## Relations with Britain

1. **Colonial and Commonwealth ties**: English is the official language, and the legal and education systems follow British models.
2. **Aid and trade**: Britain has long been a major **aid donor** and trading partner.
3. **Military intervention (2000)**: **Operation Palliser** helped to save Freetown and end the war.
4. **Rebuilding security**: the **International Military Advisory and Training Team (IMATT)** retrained the army; British support helped rebuild the police.
5. **Ebola**: Britain led the international response in Sierra Leone in **2014–2015**.

## Other partners

| Partner | Relations |
|---|---|
| **United States** | aid for health, education, democracy and food security; home of a large **Sierra Leonean diaspora**; historic links through the **Amistad** and the **Gullah** |
| **China** | diplomatic relations since **1971**; built the **National Stadium**, the **Youyi Building** (government offices) and many **roads**; investment in mining and infrastructure |
| **European Union** | major development aid for roads, energy, education and governance |
| **Nigeria and Ghana** | Nigeria led **ECOMOG**; both are partners in ECOWAS; many Sierra Leoneans studied and worked there |
| **Arab and Islamic countries** | aid and loans through the **Islamic Development Bank**; links through the OIC |

## International financial institutions

The **International Monetary Fund (IMF)** and **World Bank** have played major roles:

1. **Structural adjustment** programmes in the 1980s and 1990s required cuts in spending and changes in prices, which caused hardship.
2. After the war they supported **reconstruction** and **debt relief** under the **Heavily Indebted Poor Countries (HIPC)** initiative, which cancelled much of Sierra Leone's debt in **2006**.
3. They continue to fund programmes in **health, education, energy and roads**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**International help in two crises**

**The civil war (1991–2002):** ECOWAS (ECOMOG), the UN (UNAMSIL, one of its largest missions), Britain (Operation Palliser and IMATT) and donors helped to **end the war and rebuild the state**.

**The Ebola epidemic (2014–2016):** nearly **4,000** Sierra Leoneans died. Britain, the USA, China, Cuba, the African Union, the World Health Organization and many charities sent **health workers, treatment centres and funds**. Both crises showed how much Sierra Leone depends on **international cooperation**, and how it can also **contribute** lessons to the world.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many families in Sierra Leone have relatives in **Guinea** or **Liberia**, and markets such as those at **Koindu** in Kailahun District, near where the three countries meet, have long brought traders together across the borders. This is why peace and cooperation in the **Mano River** region matter so much to Sierra Leone.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not describe Sierra Leone's foreign relations only in terms of **aid**. Relations also involve **trade, security, shared peoples, diplomacy** and Sierra Leone's own **contributions**, such as peacekeeping and regional leadership.
:::

## Key points

- **Guinea**: troops in 1971, refuge for leaders and refugees, the **Yenga** border dispute; **Liberia**: shared peoples, **Taylor's** role in the war, later cooperation.
- **Mano River Union**: founded **3 October 1973** (Sierra Leone, Liberia); **Guinea 1980**, **Côte d'Ivoire 2008**; secretariat in **Freetown**; hit hard by the wars.
- **Britain**: Commonwealth ties, aid, **Operation Palliser (2000)**, **IMATT**, Ebola response.
- **USA, China, EU, Nigeria, Islamic partners**; **IMF and World Bank**: structural adjustment, **HIPC debt relief (2006)**.
- International help was vital in the **civil war** and the **Ebola epidemic**.
