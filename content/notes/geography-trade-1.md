:::callout{type=definition}
**International trade** is the exchange of goods and services between countries.
:::

It happens because natural resources, climate, skills and technology are **unevenly distributed**, so each country gains by **specialising** in what it produces best and buying what others produce more cheaply. A country's **balance of trade** is the value of its **exports minus its imports**: a surplus when exports are greater, a deficit when imports are greater. This lesson explains why countries trade, the importance and problems of trade in West Africa, and Sierra Leone's main exports and imports.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define international trade and distinguish it from internal trade
- explain the reasons for international trade
- explain the balance of trade, the balance of payments and the terms of trade
- describe the main exports and imports of Sierra Leone and West Africa
- explain the importance and problems of international trade in West Africa
- describe efforts to increase trade within West Africa and Africa

## Internal and international trade

| Internal (home) trade | International (foreign) trade |
|---|---|
| within one country | between countries |
| one currency | different currencies; exchange needed |
| no customs duties | customs duties, quotas, documents |
| shorter distances | longer distances, often by sea or air |

## Reasons for international trade

1. **Uneven distribution of natural resources**: Sierra Leone has iron ore and diamonds but no oil wells.
2. **Differences in climate**: tropical countries grow cocoa and coffee; temperate ones grow wheat.
3. **Specialisation**: each country produces what it is best at, so total output rises.
4. **Differences in skills, capital and technology**: West Africa imports machinery and vehicles.
5. **Surplus production** that must be sold abroad.
6. **Variety** of goods for consumers.

## Balance of trade and balance of payments

![Two pairs of bars: when exports are greater than imports there is a trade surplus; when imports are greater than exports there is a trade deficit](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/balance-of-trade.svg)

*Figure 1. A favourable and an unfavourable balance of trade. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=definition}
The **balance of trade** is the difference between the value of a country's **visible exports** (goods) and **visible imports**. The **balance of payments** is the record of **all** money flows between a country and the rest of the world, including goods, **services** (tourism, shipping, insurance), investment, loans and remittances.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**In one year a country exports goods worth 1.2 billion US dollars and imports goods worth 1.8 billion US dollars. Find its balance of trade.**

Balance of trade $=$ exports $-$ imports $= 1.2 - 1.8 = -0.6$ billion US dollars.

The country has a **trade deficit** (an unfavourable balance) of 0.6 billion US dollars.
:::

The **terms of trade** compare the prices a country receives for its exports with the prices it pays for its imports. West African countries often suffer **poor terms of trade**: they export raw materials whose prices are low and unstable, and import manufactured goods whose prices keep rising.

## Exports and imports

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Sierra Leone** exports mainly **minerals**: **iron ore**, **diamonds**, **rutile**, **ilmenite**, **bauxite** and **gold**, together with **cocoa**, **coffee**, **cashew** and **fish**. It imports **rice and other foods**, **petroleum products**, **machinery**, **vehicles**, **building materials**, **medicines** and manufactured goods. Because it imports more than it exports, Sierra Leone usually has a **trade deficit**.
:::

| Country | Main exports |
|---|---|
| **Nigeria** | crude oil and natural gas (by far the largest share), cocoa |
| **Ghana** | gold, cocoa, crude oil |
| **Côte d'Ivoire** | cocoa (the world's largest producer), petroleum products, gold, cashew, rubber |
| **Guinea** | bauxite, gold |
| **Liberia** | iron ore, gold, rubber |
| **Senegal** | fish, phosphates and fertilisers, groundnuts, gold, petroleum products |

## Importance of international trade

1. **Foreign exchange** to pay for imports.
2. **Goods not produced at home** (machinery, fuel, medicines).
3. **Markets** for surplus production.
4. **Government revenue** from customs duties.
5. **Jobs** in ports, transport, banking and trading.
6. **Better relations** between countries.
7. **Transfer of technology** and ideas.

## Problems of trade in West Africa

1. **Dependence on a few primary products**, such as oil or cocoa.
2. **Unstable world prices** for raw materials.
3. **Poor terms of trade.**
4. **Tariffs and quotas** in rich countries on processed goods.
5. **Little trade between West African countries**: they produce similar goods and their transport routes lead to the coast, not to each other.
6. **Different currencies** and exchange problems.
7. **Poor transport and communication** links.
8. **Smuggling** and border delays.

## Promoting trade within Africa

- **ECOWAS** (founded **1975**) aims at free movement of goods and people and runs a **Trade Liberalisation Scheme** to remove tariffs on West African goods.
- The **African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA)** began trading on **1 January 2021**, aiming to create a single market across Africa.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The **balance of trade** covers **goods only**. The **balance of payments** is wider and includes services, investment, loans and remittances. Do not use the two terms as if they mean the same thing.
:::

## Key points

- International trade arises from **uneven resources, climate, specialisation and technology**.
- **Balance of trade** = visible exports − visible imports; surplus or deficit.
- West Africa exports **raw materials** and imports **manufactured goods**, giving poor **terms of trade**.
- Sierra Leone exports **iron ore, diamonds, rutile, bauxite, gold, cocoa, coffee, cashew**; imports **rice, fuel, machinery, vehicles**.
- **ECOWAS (1975)** and **AfCFTA (2021)** aim to increase trade within Africa.

## Quick check

1. Define international trade.
2. Give two reasons why countries trade.
3. A country's exports are worth 500 million US dollars and its imports 650 million US dollars. Find its balance of trade.
4. Name three main exports of Sierra Leone.
5. Why is trade between West African countries small?

**Answers:**

1. The exchange of goods and services between countries.
2. Any two: uneven distribution of resources; differences in climate; specialisation; differences in skills and technology; surplus production; variety.
3. $500 - 650 = -150$: a trade deficit of 150 million US dollars.
4. Any three: iron ore; diamonds; rutile; ilmenite; bauxite; gold; cocoa; coffee; cashew; fish.
5. They produce similar goods, transport links lead to the coast rather than to each other, and tariffs, currencies and border delays get in the way.
