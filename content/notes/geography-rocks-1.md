The **layers of the Earth** are the **crust**, the **mantle**, the **outer core** and the **inner core**. The crust is the thin rocky skin we live on; beneath it the mantle of hot, slowly flowing rock reaches down to about 2,900 km; and at the centre is a core of iron and nickel, liquid on the outside and solid on the inside. This lesson describes each layer, how scientists know they exist, and why the structure matters for earthquakes, volcanoes and minerals.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name and describe the layers of the Earth in order from the surface
- state the approximate depth, state and composition of each layer
- distinguish continental crust (sial) from oceanic crust (sima)
- explain the terms lithosphere, asthenosphere and discontinuity
- explain how earthquake waves reveal the Earth's interior

## The four layers

*Figure 1. The internal structure of the Earth. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Layer | Depth | State | Composition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crust** | surface to 5–70 km | solid | silicate rocks: granite, basalt |
| **Mantle** | to about 2,900 km | solid but able to flow very slowly | rocks rich in iron and magnesium |
| **Outer core** | about 2,900–5,100 km | **liquid** | iron and nickel |
| **Inner core** | about 5,100–6,370 km | **solid** (because of enormous pressure) | iron and nickel; about 5,500 °C |

### The crust

The crust is the thinnest layer, like the skin on a mango. It is of two kinds:

| Continental crust (**sial**) | Oceanic crust (**sima**) |
|---|---|
| mainly **si**lica and **al**uminium | mainly **si**lica and **ma**gnesium |
| 30–70 km thick, under the continents | 5–10 km thick, under the oceans |
| lighter (less dense), granite-type rocks | denser, basalt-type rocks |
| very old in places | younger, constantly formed and destroyed |

### The mantle

The mantle makes up about **84%** of the Earth's volume. It is solid rock, but so hot that over millions of years it flows slowly. **Convection currents** in the mantle, rising where it is hottest and sinking where it is cooler, are thought to drive the movement of the plates of the crust.

### The core

The core is mainly **iron and nickel**, which is why it is sometimes called **NiFe**. Movements in the liquid outer core generate the Earth's **magnetic field**, which makes a compass work and shields the Earth from harmful radiation from the Sun.

## Lithosphere, asthenosphere and discontinuities

- The **lithosphere** is the rigid outer shell: the crust plus the top of the mantle, about 100 km thick. It is broken into **plates**.
- The **asthenosphere** is the soft, partly molten layer of the upper mantle beneath it, on which the plates move.
- A **discontinuity** is a boundary where earthquake waves suddenly change speed:
  - the **Mohorovičić discontinuity (Moho)** separates the crust from the mantle
  - the **Gutenberg discontinuity** separates the mantle from the core

## How do we know?

No one has drilled through the crust. The deepest borehole, in Russia, reached only about 12 km. Scientists study the interior by:

1. **Earthquake (seismic) waves:** primary (P) waves pass through solids and liquids; secondary (S) waves pass only through solids. S-waves do not pass through the outer core, which shows it is **liquid**. Waves bend and change speed at each boundary.
2. **Volcanic eruptions**, which bring up material from the mantle.
3. **Meteorites**, thought to be made of the same material as the early Earth.
4. **The Earth's density and magnetism:** the whole Earth is much denser than surface rocks, so the centre must be very dense (iron and nickel).

:::callout{type=local-example}
The diamonds of **Kono District** were carried up from more than 150 km below the surface, from the mantle, in narrow pipes and dykes of a volcanic rock called **kimberlite**. Diamonds form only under the immense pressure of the mantle, so every Sierra Leonean diamond is a piece of evidence about the Earth's interior.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The inner core is **solid**, even though it is the hottest part, because the pressure there is so great. The **outer** core is the liquid layer.
:::

## Why the structure matters

- **Plate movements** on the asthenosphere cause earthquakes, volcanoes and mountain building.
- **Minerals** such as diamonds, gold and iron ore are brought nearer the surface by movements of magma and the crust.
- **Geothermal energy** uses heat from inside the Earth, for example at Olkaria in Kenya.

## Key points

- Layers: **crust** (5–70 km) → **mantle** (to ~2,900 km) → **outer core** (liquid) → **inner core** (solid), ~6,370 km to the centre.
- Continental crust is **sial** (thick, light); oceanic crust is **sima** (thin, dense).
- **Lithosphere** = crust + upper mantle, broken into plates; **asthenosphere** = soft layer beneath.
- **Moho** (crust–mantle) and **Gutenberg** (mantle–core) discontinuities.
- **Seismic waves**, especially S-waves failing to cross the outer core, reveal the structure.

## Quick check

1. Name the layers of the Earth from the surface to the centre.
2. Which layer is liquid?
3. State two differences between sial and sima.
4. What is the Moho?
5. Name the two main metals in the Earth's core.

**Answers:**

1. Crust, mantle, outer core, inner core.
2. The outer core.
3. Any two: sial is continental, sima oceanic; sial is silica and aluminium, sima silica and magnesium; sial is thicker; sial is less dense; sial is older.
4. The Mohorovičić discontinuity, the boundary between the crust and the mantle.
5. Iron and nickel.
