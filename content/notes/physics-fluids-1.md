Why does a sharp knife cut better than a blunt one? Why are the walls of a dam thicker at the bottom? Why do your ears pop when a plane takes off? Pressure answers all three — and pressure in liquids and air is one of the most examined topics in WASSCE Physics.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define density and relative density and calculate them
- define pressure and calculate pressure in solids and liquids
- use $p = h\rho g$ and explain the properties of liquid pressure
- explain atmospheric pressure, the mercury barometer and the manometer
- state Pascal's principle and apply it to hydraulic machines

## Density and relative density

:::callout{type=definition}
**Density** is mass per unit volume: $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$, in **kg m⁻³** (or g cm⁻³; 1 g cm⁻³ = 1000 kg m⁻³).

**Relative density** is the ratio of the density of a substance to the density of water (no unit):

$$\text{RD} = \dfrac{\text{density of substance}}{\text{density of water}} = \dfrac{\text{mass of substance}}{\text{mass of equal volume of water}}$$
:::

Water has density 1000 kg m⁻³ (RD = 1). Mercury has RD 13.6, so its density is 13 600 kg m⁻³.

## Pressure

:::callout{type=definition}
**Pressure** is the force acting **normally (perpendicularly) per unit area**:

$$p = \dfrac{F}{A}$$

Its SI unit is the **pascal (Pa)**: 1 Pa = 1 N m⁻².
:::

The same force produces a **larger pressure on a smaller area**:

- a sharp knife or a needle has a tiny area, so it cuts or pierces easily
- tractors and lorries have wide tyres; camels have broad feet: large areas give low pressure, so they do not sink into soft ground
- a woman in stiletto heels exerts more pressure on a floor than an elephant standing on its broad feet

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A block of weight 120 N measures 0.2 m × 0.3 m × 0.5 m. Find the greatest and least pressures it can exert on the floor.**

- Greatest pressure: smallest face, $0.2 \times 0.3 = 0.06$ m². $p = \dfrac{120}{0.06} = 2000$ Pa.
- Least pressure: largest face, $0.3 \times 0.5 = 0.15$ m². $p = \dfrac{120}{0.15} = 800$ Pa.
:::

## Pressure in liquids

![A tall can of water with three holes at different depths; water spurts out furthest from the lowest hole, showing that pressure increases with depth](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/pressure-depth.svg)

*Figure 1. Pressure in a liquid increases with depth. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

The pressure at depth $h$ in a liquid of density $\rho$ is

$$p = h\rho g$$

(plus atmospheric pressure acting on the surface, if the total pressure is wanted).

### Properties of pressure in a liquid

1. Pressure **increases with depth**.
2. At a given depth, pressure acts **equally in all directions**.
3. Pressure depends on **depth and density** only — **not** on the shape or cross-section of the container.
4. A liquid **finds its own level**: the surface is at the same height in connected vessels.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Find the pressure due to water at the bottom of a well 12 m deep. If atmospheric pressure is $1.0 \times 10^{5}$ Pa, find the total pressure there. ($\rho = 1000$ kg m⁻³, $g = 10$ m s⁻²)**

$$p = h\rho g = 12 \times 1000 \times 10 = 1.2 \times 10^{5}\ \text{Pa}$$

Total $= 1.2 \times 10^{5} + 1.0 \times 10^{5} = 2.2 \times 10^{5}$ Pa.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Dams such as Bumbuna are built **much thicker at the base** because the water pressure on the wall increases with depth. Divers in the Atlantic off the Freetown peninsula feel the pressure on their ears increase rapidly as they go down: every 10 m of sea water adds about one atmosphere.
:::

## Atmospheric pressure

The air above us has weight, so it exerts a pressure: **atmospheric pressure**, about **$1.01 \times 10^{5}$ Pa** at sea level (often called 1 atmosphere, or 760 mmHg). It decreases with altitude.

Evidence: the **collapsing-can experiment** (a can with a little water is boiled, sealed and cooled — it is crushed by atmospheric pressure as the steam condenses); drinking through a straw; a rubber sucker sticking to a wall.

### The mercury barometer

![A long glass tube of mercury inverted in a dish of mercury; the column stands about 760 millimetres high with a vacuum above it, supported by air pressure on the dish](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/mercury-barometer.svg)

*Figure 2. A simple mercury barometer. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

Atmospheric pressure on the mercury in the dish supports the column. At sea level the column is about 760 mm high:

$$p_{atm} = h\rho g = 0.76 \times 13\,600 \times 9.8 \approx 1.01 \times 10^{5}\ \text{Pa}$$

Tilting the tube does not change the **vertical** height of the column. **Water** is not used because the column would need to be about 10.3 m tall.

### The manometer

A **U-tube manometer** measures the pressure of a gas supply. One arm is connected to the gas; the other is open to the air. The difference in liquid levels $h$ gives the **excess pressure** of the gas above atmospheric: $\Delta p = h\rho g$.

## Pascal's principle and hydraulic machines

:::callout{type=definition}
**Pascal's principle:** pressure applied to an **enclosed fluid** is **transmitted equally** to every part of the fluid and to the walls of the container.
:::

In a hydraulic press, a small force on a small piston produces a large force on a large piston: $\dfrac{F_1}{A_1} = \dfrac{F_2}{A_2}$. Applications: hydraulic jacks, hydraulic car brakes, barber's chairs, hydraulic lifts in garages.

## Key points

- Density $\rho = m/V$; relative density compares with water (no unit).
- Pressure $p = F/A$ (Pa); smaller area → greater pressure.
- Liquid pressure $p = h\rho g$: increases with depth, same in all directions, independent of the container's shape.
- Atmospheric pressure ≈ $1.01 \times 10^{5}$ Pa ≈ 760 mmHg; measured with a barometer.
- Pascal's principle underlies hydraulic machines.

## Quick check

1. Why do tractors have wide tyres?
2. Find the pressure at a depth of 5 m in water ($\rho = 1000$ kg m⁻³, $g = 10$ m s⁻²).
3. State one reason why mercury is used in barometers.

**Answers:** 1. To spread the weight over a large area, reducing the pressure so they do not sink into soft soil. 2. $5 \times 1000 \times 10 = 5.0 \times 10^{4}$ Pa. 3. For example: its high density gives a short, convenient column (about 0.76 m instead of about 10 m for water).
