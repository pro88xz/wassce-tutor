The **factors affecting agriculture** are **physical** (climate, soil, relief, water and pests) and **human** (labour, capital, land tenure, markets, transport, technology and government policy). In West Africa, agriculture employs more people than any other activity, but it faces serious **problems**: poor soils, unreliable rainfall, simple tools, poor roads, pests and diseases, lack of credit and low prices. This lesson explains each factor and problem and the ways of improving agriculture, with Sierra Leone examples.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the physical factors that influence agriculture
- explain the human and economic factors that influence agriculture
- explain the importance of agriculture to West African economies
- describe the problems of agriculture in West Africa and Sierra Leone
- suggest ways of improving agriculture

## Physical factors

| Factor | Influence |
|---|---|
| **Climate** | rainfall amount and reliability, temperature and length of the growing season decide which crops grow: cocoa needs heavy rain and shade; millet and sorghum tolerate drought |
| **Soil** | fertile, deep, well-drained loams give high yields; leached lateritic soils need fallow or fertiliser; waterlogged swamp soils suit rice |
| **Relief** | flat land is easy to farm and mechanise; steep slopes suffer erosion and need terracing |
| **Water** | rivers, lakes and groundwater allow irrigation and livestock |
| **Pests and diseases** | locusts, birds, stem borers; **tsetse flies** spread sleeping sickness in cattle, limiting livestock in humid areas |
| **Natural vegetation** | thick forest is costly to clear; grassland suits grazing |

## Human and economic factors

| Factor | Influence |
|---|---|
| **Labour** | availability and skill; migration of young people to towns reduces farm labour |
| **Capital** | money for tools, seeds, fertiliser, machines and irrigation |
| **Land tenure** | how land is owned and inherited; insecure or fragmented holdings discourage investment |
| **Markets** | demand and prices for produce, at home and abroad |
| **Transport** | roads to carry produce to market before it spoils |
| **Technology** | improved seeds, fertilisers, machines and storage raise yields |
| **Government policy** | subsidies, credit, extension services, research, prices and land policies |
| **Culture and tradition** | food preferences, attitudes to change, farming customs |

## Importance of agriculture

1. **Food** for the population.
2. **Employment** for most of the workforce, especially in rural areas.
3. **Raw materials** for industry: cocoa, palm oil, cotton, rubber, sugar cane.
4. **Export earnings** and foreign exchange.
5. **Government revenue** from taxes and duties.
6. **Market** for industrial goods such as tools and fertiliser.

## Problems of agriculture in West Africa

1. **Poor soils:** leached tropical soils and erosion.
2. **Unreliable rainfall** and drought in the north; floods in the south.
3. **Simple tools and methods**, giving low yields.
4. **Small, fragmented farms** and insecure land tenure.
5. **Pests and diseases** of crops and animals.
6. **Poor roads and transport**, so produce rots before it reaches markets.
7. **Lack of capital and credit** for farmers.
8. **Poor storage and processing**, leading to large post-harvest losses.
9. **Low and unstable prices** for cash crops on world markets.
10. **Shortage of labour** as young people migrate to towns and mining areas.
11. **Limited extension services** and research.
12. **Conflicts** between farmers and herders.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Although rice is Sierra Leone's staple food and the country has huge areas of swamps suitable for it, Sierra Leone still **imports a large share of its rice**. The main reasons are low yields from upland bush fallowing, under-use of the **inland valley swamps** and **bolilands**, lack of irrigation, labour shortages, poor feeder roads, and post-harvest losses. Improving swamp rice production is a major goal of agricultural policy.
:::

## Improving agriculture

1. **Improved seeds** and **fertilisers** to raise yields.
2. **Mechanisation** with tractors and power tillers where land is suitable.
3. **Irrigation** and development of swamps and floodplains.
4. **Credit and loans** for farmers, and farmers' cooperatives.
5. **Extension services** to teach better methods.
6. **Better feeder roads**, storage and processing to reduce losses.
7. **Guaranteed prices** and better marketing.
8. **Land reform** to give farmers secure tenure.
9. **Research** into crops, pests and soils.
10. **Soil conservation** and agroforestry.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not list only physical problems. Examiners expect both **physical** (soil, rainfall, pests) and **human** (capital, tenure, roads, markets, labour) factors and problems.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Physical factors**: the natural conditions that affect farming: climate, soil, relief, water, pests and diseases, and vegetation.
- **Human factors**: the human conditions that affect farming: labour, capital, land tenure, markets, transport and government policy.
- **Tsetse fly**: a fly that spreads sleeping sickness to cattle, limiting livestock in humid areas.
:::

## Key points

- Physical factors: **climate, soil, relief, water, pests and diseases, vegetation**.
- Human factors: **labour, capital, land tenure, markets, transport, technology, government, culture**.
- Agriculture provides **food, jobs, raw materials, exports and revenue**.
- Problems: **poor soils, unreliable rain, simple tools, small farms, pests, poor roads, lack of credit, storage losses, low prices, labour shortage**.
- Solutions: **seeds, fertiliser, machines, irrigation, credit, extension, roads, storage, pricing, land reform, research**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **explain the factors that influence agriculture**, to **describe the problems facing agriculture in West Africa**, and to **suggest solutions**. Linking each solution to a specific problem, and using Sierra Leone examples such as swamp rice, scores well.
:::

## Quick check

1. Give two physical factors that affect agriculture.
2. How does land tenure affect farming?
3. State three problems of agriculture in West Africa.
4. Why does Sierra Leone import rice?
5. Suggest two ways of improving agriculture.

**Answers:**

1. Any two: climate; soil; relief; water; pests and diseases; vegetation.
2. Insecure or fragmented ownership discourages farmers from investing in their land.
3. Any three: poor soils; unreliable rainfall; simple tools; small farms; pests and diseases; poor roads; lack of credit; storage losses; low prices; labour shortage.
4. Low yields from upland farming, under-used swamps, little irrigation, labour shortage, poor roads and post-harvest losses.
5. Any two: improved seeds; fertiliser; mechanisation; irrigation; credit; extension services; better roads and storage; guaranteed prices; land reform; research.
