:::callout{type=definition}
**Source documents** are the original business papers that **prove a transaction took place** and give the details needed to record it: the **invoice**, **credit note**, **debit note**, **receipt**, **cheque and its counterfoil**, **bank paying-in slip**, **petty cash voucher** and **bank statement**.
:::

No entry should be made in the books without a source document, because it is the **evidence** the auditor and the tax authority will ask for. This lesson describes each document, who issues it, and where it is recorded.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the meaning and importance of source documents
- describe the purpose and contents of an invoice, credit note, debit note and receipt
- describe the cheque and counterfoil, paying-in slip, petty cash voucher and bank statement
- state which book of original entry each document is recorded in
- calculate invoice totals with trade discount

## Why source documents matter

1. They are **evidence** that a transaction happened.
2. They give the **details** to record: date, parties, goods, prices, amounts.
3. They allow **checking and auditing** of the records.
4. They help to **settle disputes** with customers and suppliers.
5. They support **tax returns** to the National Revenue Authority.
6. They help to **prevent and detect fraud**.

## The main source documents

| Document | Issued by → to | Purpose | Recorded in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Invoice** | seller → buyer | bill for goods sold **on credit**: quantities, prices, trade discount, total due | seller: **sales day book**; buyer: **purchases day book** |
| **Credit note** | seller → buyer | reduces the amount owed because goods were **returned**, damaged or **overcharged** | seller: **returns inwards book**; buyer: **returns outwards book** |
| **Debit note** | buyer → seller | asks the seller to issue a credit note, or tells the seller of an undercharge | supports the returns outwards entry |
| **Receipt** | receiver of money → payer | proof that **money was paid** | **cash book** |
| **Cheque counterfoil (stub)** | kept by the drawer | record of a **cheque paid** | **cash book** (bank column, credit side) |
| **Paying-in slip** | business → bank | record of cash or cheques **deposited** | **cash book** (bank column, debit side) |
| **Petty cash voucher** | petty cashier | proof of a **small cash payment** | **petty cash book** |
| **Bank statement** | bank → customer | copy of the customer's account **in the bank's books** | used for the **bank reconciliation** |
| **Statement of account** | seller → buyer | summary of invoices, credit notes and payments for the month, showing the **balance owed** | used to check the supplier's account |

## The invoice and trade discount

:::callout{type=definition}
**Trade discount** is a reduction from the list price given by a wholesaler or manufacturer to a **trader** who buys for resale.
:::

It is shown on the invoice and **is not recorded in the books**: only the net amount is entered.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Sesay Wholesalers sell 20 bags of rice at NLe 600 a bag and 10 cartons of oil at NLe 450 a carton to Amie's Store on credit, less 10% trade discount. Find the invoice total.**

| Item | NLe |
|---|---|
| 20 bags of rice × 600 | 12,000 |
| 10 cartons of oil × 450 | 4,500 |
| **Gross (list) price** | **16,500** |
| Less trade discount 10% | (1,650) |
| **Net amount due** | **14,850** |

Sesay records a **credit sale of NLe 14,850** in its sales day book; Amie's Store records a **credit purchase of NLe 14,850** in its purchases day book. The NLe 1,650 trade discount is **not** recorded in either ledger.
:::

## Trade discount and cash discount

| Trade discount | Cash discount |
|---|---|
| given for buying **in bulk or for resale** | given for **paying promptly**, within the agreed time |
| deducted **on the invoice** | deducted **when payment is made** |
| **not recorded** in the ledger | **recorded**: discount allowed (expense) or discount received (income) |
| encourages large orders | encourages early payment |

Cash discount is recorded in the **discount columns of the cash book**, explained in the cash book lesson.

## Other terms on invoices

- **E&OE** (errors and omissions excepted): the seller may correct mistakes on the invoice later.
- **Terms 2/10, net 30**: 2% cash discount if paid within 10 days; full amount due within 30 days.
- **Carriage paid / carriage forward:** whether the seller or the buyer pays for delivery.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not record **trade discount** in the discount columns of the cash book. Trade discount is deducted on the invoice and never appears in the ledger. Only **cash discount** (discount allowed and received) is recorded.
:::

## Key points

- Source documents are **evidence** of transactions and the basis of every entry.
- **Invoice** (credit sale or purchase), **credit note** (returns or overcharges), **debit note**, **receipt**, **cheque counterfoil**, **paying-in slip**, **petty cash voucher**, **bank statement**, **statement of account**.
- **Trade discount** is deducted on the invoice and **not recorded**; **cash discount** is for prompt payment and **is recorded**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **name and describe source documents**, to **state the book of original entry** for each, to **distinguish trade from cash discount**, and to **calculate an invoice total after trade discount**. Matching each document with its book is a common objective question.
:::

## Quick check

1. What is a source document?
2. Which document is sent by a seller when goods are returned?
3. Where does a seller record a sales invoice?
4. Goods listed at NLe 8,000 are sold less 15% trade discount. What amount is recorded?
5. Give two differences between trade discount and cash discount.

**Answers:**

1. An original business document that proves a transaction took place and gives the details needed to record it.
2. A credit note.
3. In the sales day book.
4. $8\,000 - 1\,200 = 6\,800$: NLe 6,800.
5. Any two: trade discount is for bulk buying or resale, cash discount for prompt payment; trade discount is deducted on the invoice, cash discount at payment; trade discount is not recorded, cash discount is recorded.
