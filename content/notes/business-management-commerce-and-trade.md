A farmer in Pujehun harvests more palm oil than her family can use. A family in Freetown needs palm oil but grows none. Between them stand traders who buy, carry, store and sell, and banks, transporters and mobile networks that make the buying and selling possible. **Trade** is the buying and selling itself; **commerce** is trade together with everything that helps it happen.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define commerce and trade and explain how they differ
- describe the branches of trade: home trade and foreign trade
- explain the roles of retailers and wholesalers
- explain why commerce is important to business

## Commerce and trade

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Commerce**: all the activities involved in the distribution and exchange of goods and services, made up of **trade** and the **aids to trade**.
- **Trade**: the buying and selling of goods and services.
:::

So trade is **part** of commerce. The aids to trade (transport, communication, banking, insurance, warehousing and advertising) are the other part; they are covered in the next lesson.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Commerce and trade are **not** the same thing. Trade is only buying and selling; commerce is trade **plus** the aids to trade. Every act of trade is commerce, but not every part of commerce is trade (insurance, for example, is commerce but not trade).
:::

## Branches of trade

### Home (domestic) trade

:::callout{type=definition}
**Home trade** is buying and selling within the boundaries of one country.
:::

It has two parts:

| Branch | Meaning | Examples in Sierra Leone |
|---|---|---|
| **Wholesale trade** | Buying goods in large quantities from producers or importers and selling in smaller quantities to retailers | Wholesale shops on Kissy Road and in Bo selling cartons and bags |
| **Retail trade** | Buying from wholesalers or producers and selling in small quantities to final consumers | Market women, provision shops, supermarkets, street hawkers |

### Foreign (international) trade

:::callout{type=definition}
**Foreign trade** is buying and selling between different countries.
:::

| Branch | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Import trade** | Buying goods from other countries | Rice, fuel, vehicles, medicines |
| **Export trade** | Selling goods to other countries | Iron ore, diamonds, rutile, cocoa, coffee |
| **Entrepôt (re-export) trade** | Importing goods and re-exporting them to another country | Goods brought in through a port and sent on to a neighbouring country |

## The chain of distribution

Goods usually pass through a chain from producer to consumer:

**Producer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer**

The **wholesaler** buys in bulk, stores the goods and breaks bulk for retailers. The **retailer** sells in the small quantities consumers want, close to where they live. Sometimes a stage is skipped: a farmer may sell directly to consumers at a weekly market.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Tracing trade and commerce in one sale.** A wholesaler in Freetown imports 1,000 bags of rice and sells 40 bags to a retailer in Makeni, who sells it by the cup.

- Importing the rice: **foreign trade (import)**.
- Selling to the Makeni retailer: **home trade (wholesale)**.
- Selling by the cup to households: **home trade (retail)**.
- The ship, the lorry, the bank transfer, the insurance on the cargo and the store in Freetown: **aids to trade**.

Trade and the aids to trade together make up **commerce**.
:::

## Importance of commerce to business

1. It links **producers and consumers**, so goods reach the people who need them.
2. It allows **specialisation**: farmers, miners and manufacturers can concentrate on producing while traders sell.
3. It widens the **market**, letting businesses sell across the country and abroad.
4. It raises **living standards** by making a wider range of goods available.
5. It earns **foreign exchange** through exports and brings in goods the country cannot produce.
6. It creates **employment** for traders, transporters, bankers and others.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Weekly *lumo* markets in towns across the provinces show home trade at work: farmers, wholesalers and retailers meet to buy and sell, and transporters and mobile money agents help them do it.
:::

## Key points

- **Trade** is buying and selling; **commerce** is trade plus the aids to trade.
- **Home trade**: wholesale and retail. **Foreign trade**: import, export and entrepôt.
- The usual **chain of distribution** is producer → wholesaler → retailer → consumer.
- Commerce links producers and consumers, widens markets, allows specialisation and creates jobs.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
WASSCE often asks you to **distinguish commerce from trade**, to **draw or describe the branches of trade**, and to **explain the importance of commerce**. A tree diagram of commerce (trade and aids to trade, with the branches of each) is a quick way to earn marks.
:::

## Quick check

1. What is the difference between commerce and trade?
2. Name the two branches of home trade.
3. What is entrepôt trade?
4. Give two ways commerce is important to a business.

**Answers:** 1. Trade is buying and selling; commerce is trade together with the aids to trade. 2. Wholesale trade and retail trade. 3. Importing goods and re-exporting them to another country. 4. Any two of linking producers and consumers, widening the market, allowing specialisation, creating jobs, earning foreign exchange.
