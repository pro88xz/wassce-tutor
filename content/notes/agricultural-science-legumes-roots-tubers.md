After rice, the foods that fill Sierra Leonean pots are **cassava** and **sweet potato** (roots and their leaves), **groundnuts** (for soup and snacks), **cowpeas** and **yams**. Legumes supply protein and enrich the soil; roots and tubers supply cheap energy and grow on poorer soils, and cassava can stay in the ground until it is needed, making it a vital food security crop. This lesson describes the legumes and the root and tuber crops, and the cultivation of groundnut, cassava, sweet potato and yam.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name the main grain legumes and root and tuber crops and their uses
- explain the importance of legumes in farming
- describe the cultivation of groundnut and cowpea
- describe the cultivation of cassava, sweet potato and yam
- name their main pests and diseases and methods of control
- describe the processing of cassava

## Legumes

| Crop | Scientific name | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **groundnut** | *Arachis hypogaea* | groundnut soup, oil, paste, snacks; haulms (vines) fed to animals |
| **cowpea** (black-eyed bean) | *Vigna unguiculata* | beans, leaves as vegetable; fodder |
| **soya bean** | *Glycine max* | oil, flour, animal feed (high protein) |
| **pigeon pea** | *Cajanus cajan* | grain, fodder, hedges; deep roots |
| **bambara groundnut** | *Vigna subterranea* | grain; very drought tolerant |

### Why legumes matter

- Their seeds are rich in **protein** (about 20–40%), cheaper than meat or fish.
- *Rhizobium* bacteria in their **root nodules** fix nitrogen, **improving the soil** for the next crop.
- They make good **cover crops** and **fodder**.

## Cultivation of groundnut

1. **Soil:** light, well-drained **sandy loam**, so that the pegs can push into the soil and pods can be lifted cleanly.
2. **Planting:** at the start of the rains, shelled seed, **50 cm × 20 cm**, 1–2 seeds per hole, about 5 cm deep. Do not shell too long before planting.
3. **Care:** weed early; **earth up** around the plants when the **pegs** (flower stalks) begin to grow down into the soil, because pods form only underground.
4. **Fertiliser:** little nitrogen is needed; phosphorus and calcium (e.g. single superphosphate, gypsum) improve pod filling.
5. **Pests and diseases:** groundnut rosette virus (spread by aphids), leaf spots (fungi), termites, rodents.
6. **Harvesting:** at about 90–120 days, when leaves turn yellow and the inside of the shell darkens; lift with a hoe or by hand.
7. **Drying:** dry quickly on clean mats or tarpaulins, off the ground, to prevent **aflatoxin** mould.

**Cowpea** is grown in a similar way, often intercropped with maize or cassava. It is attacked by many insects (aphids, pod borers, pod-sucking bugs) at flowering and podding, and stored grain by **bruchid beetles**, which can be controlled by storing in airtight bags.

## Roots and tubers

| Crop | Planting material | Maturity | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **cassava** (*Manihot esculenta*) | stem cuttings | 9–18 months | roots: gari, fufu, starch, boiled or roasted; **leaves: cassava leaf sauce** |
| **sweet potato** (*Ipomoea batatas*) | vine cuttings | 3–5 months | roots boiled, fried; **leaves: potato leaf sauce**; vines for animals |
| **yam** (*Dioscorea* spp.) | setts or minisetts | 7–10 months | pounded, boiled, fried |
| **cocoyam** (*Xanthosoma*, *Colocasia*) | corms, cormels | 9–12 months | corms boiled; leaves as vegetable |

## Cultivation of cassava

1. **Land:** grows on poorer, acidic soils where other crops fail, but yields best on loamy soils. Plant on **ridges or mounds** on heavier soils.
2. **Planting material:** healthy cuttings **20–25 cm long with 5–7 nodes**, from mature stems (8–18 months old) of **disease-free, improved varieties** (e.g. the SLICASS varieties released in Sierra Leone).
3. **Planting:** at the start of the rains, at **1 m × 1 m** (10 000 plants per hectare), cuttings planted at a slant or upright with about two-thirds buried, **right way up** (buds pointing upwards).
4. **Care:** weed at about 4, 8 and 12 weeks, until the canopy closes; often intercropped with maize, groundnut or vegetables in the early months.
5. **Pests:** cassava mealybug and green mite (controlled biologically by natural enemies), grasshoppers, rodents (cane rats).
6. **Diseases:** **cassava mosaic disease** (virus, spread by whiteflies and infected cuttings), cassava bacterial blight, root rots.
7. **Harvesting:** from about 9 months; roots can be left in the ground and harvested as needed (**piecemeal harvesting**), but they become woody if left too long.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Taking cuttings from diseased plants.** Cassava mosaic virus is carried **inside** the stem, so cuttings from an infected plant produce infected plants. Choose cuttings only from healthy, vigorous plants with clean, unmottled leaves, or from certified multiplication sites.
:::

### Processing cassava

Fresh cassava roots **spoil within two or three days** of harvest and contain compounds that release poisonous **cyanide**. Processing solves both problems:

- **Gari:** peel, wash, grate, ferment in sacks for 2–3 days, press out the liquid, sieve, then roast in a wide pan.
- **Fufu:** peel, soak in water for several days to ferment and soften, sieve, and press into a paste.
- **Dried chips and flour:** peel, slice, sun-dry, then mill.
- **Starch:** grate, wash out the starch with water, settle and dry.

## Sweet potato and yam

**Sweet potato:** plant vine cuttings about 30 cm long, with 2–3 nodes buried, on **ridges or mounds** about 1 m apart. Harvest at 3–5 months. Pests: **sweet potato weevil** (tunnels in roots and vines). Its leaves are a popular vegetable ("potato leaves"), so many farmers harvest both leaves and roots.

**Yam:** plant **setts** (pieces of tuber) or **minisetts** (small pieces of about 25–50 g, dusted with ash or fungicide) in mounds or ridges. **Stake** the vines so leaves get more light. Harvest when leaves turn yellow and dry. Store in well-ventilated **barns**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Cassava leaves and potato leaves.** In Sierra Leone, cassava and sweet potato are grown as much for their **leaves** as for their roots: cassava leaf and potato leaf sauces, cooked with palm oil and fish, are among the most popular dishes. Young leaves are rich in **protein, vitamins and iron**. Farmers who pick some leaves for sale every week get a steady income long before the roots are ready.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Give three reasons why legumes are important in farming."}
Any three: protein-rich food; fix nitrogen through *Rhizobium*, improving soil; cover crops that control erosion and weeds; fodder for animals; oil (groundnut, soya).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Why is earthing up important in groundnut?"}
Groundnut pods form **underground**: the pegs must push into loose soil. Earthing up helps the pegs enter the soil and covers the developing pods.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Describe a good cassava cutting and how to plant it."}
20–25 cm long, 5–7 nodes, from a healthy mature stem of a disease-free variety; planted at the start of the rains at 1 m × 1 m, at a slant or upright with two-thirds buried, buds pointing upwards.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. How is cassava mosaic disease spread, and how is it controlled?"}
Spread by **whiteflies** and by **infected cuttings**. Control: resistant varieties, clean cuttings from healthy plants, uprooting and destroying infected plants.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Give two reasons why cassava is processed."}
Fresh roots spoil within two or three days; processing removes poisonous cyanide compounds; it gives products that store and travel well (gari, chips, flour) and adds value.
:::

## Key points

- Legumes (**groundnut, cowpea, soya bean, pigeon pea**) give **protein** and **fix nitrogen**.
- Groundnut: sandy loam, 50 × 20 cm, **earth up**, dry quickly to avoid **aflatoxin**.
- Roots and tubers (**cassava, sweet potato, yam, cocoyam**) give cheap energy.
- Cassava: **clean cuttings 20–25 cm**, 1 m × 1 m, mature 9–18 months; **mosaic disease** spread by whiteflies and cuttings.
- Cassava must be **processed** (gari, fufu, chips) to store it and remove cyanide.
