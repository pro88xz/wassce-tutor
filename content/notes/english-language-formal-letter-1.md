A formal letter is the most predictable question in the essay section of Paper 2, and one of the easiest to score well on, because half the work is **format** and format can be learnt perfectly. Before an examiner reads your first sentence, they check your addresses, your date, your salutation, your heading and your close. Get every one right and you have protected marks that careless candidates throw away. This lesson takes you through a formal letter part by part, shows you a complete model, and gives you practice to make the layout automatic.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- decide when a letter must be formal
- set out all seven parts of a formal letter in the right place and order
- write addresses, dates, salutations and closes correctly, with the right punctuation
- write a heading that tells the reader exactly what the letter is about
- spot and correct the format errors examiners see most often

## When is a letter formal?

Ask one question: **am I writing to this person because of the job or office they hold?** If yes, the letter is formal, even if you know them.

| Formal: write to the office | Not formal |
|---|---|
| the Principal, about a school problem | your uncle, who happens to be a principal, about a family visit |
| the Chairman of Kenema City Council | a friend who lives in Kenema |
| the Editor of a newspaper | a classmate who writes for the school magazine |
| the Manager of a bank, applying for a job | your cousin who works at the bank |
| the Minister of Health, about a clinic | a nurse you know, to thank her personally |

:::callout{type=tip}
Read the question for **who** the reader is and **why** you are writing. *"Write a letter to the Chairman of your District Council"* is formal. *"Write a letter to your friend who has just moved to Makeni"* is informal. *"Write to your uncle, thanking him for paying your fees"* is semi-formal (next to informal, with more respect).
:::

## The seven parts

:::callout{type=definition}
A formal letter has seven parts, always in this order:

1. the **writer's address**
2. the **date**
3. the **recipient's title and address**
4. the **salutation**
5. the **heading** (title)
6. the **body**
7. the **complimentary close**, **signature** and **full name**
:::

Here is a complete model, with each part numbered. Read it once for the layout, then study each part below.

::::model{title="To the Chairman of Bo City Council" label="Model formal letter"}
:::right
:mark[Government Secondary School,\
P.M.B. 25,\
Bo.]{n=1}

:mark[14th March, 2026.]{n=2}
:::

:mark[The Chairman,\
Bo City Council,\
Bo.]{n=3}

:mark[Dear Sir,]{n=4}

:mark[**REQUEST FOR STREETLIGHTS ON THE ROAD TO GOVERNMENT SECONDARY SCHOOL, BO**]{n=5}

I am writing, on behalf of the twelve hundred students of Government Secondary School, Bo, to request streetlights on the stretch of road between Bo Main Motor Park and our school gate. The two lights that once stood there were knocked down by a lorry over two years ago and have never been replaced, and the road has since become dangerous for students who attend evening classes.

First, the darkness puts our safety at risk. Students who leave the library and the science laboratory after seven o'clock walk almost a kilometre in complete darkness, sharing the road with lorries, cars and okadas. Last term, two girls had their phones and bags snatched near the motor park, and a Form Five student was knocked down by an okada rider who did not see him until it was too late. He spent three weeks in hospital and missed his mock examinations. Since then, many students walk home in large groups or run the whole way, which is no way to end a day of study.

Secondly, the lack of light is affecting our studies. Many parents now refuse to let their children stay for evening lessons, even in the months before the WASSCE, because they fear for their safety on the way home. Our evening study programme, which helped many students pass last year, now has fewer than half its usual numbers, and the students who miss it are often those who need it most, because they have no electricity to study by at home.

Finally, streetlights would benefit the whole community, not only students. Traders at the motor park would be able to work later and more safely, travellers arriving on night buses would not have to walk into darkness, and the road would be safer for everyone who uses it after sunset. Solar-powered lights, which several other councils have installed, would also spare the Council the cost of electricity.

We are aware that the Council has many demands on its budget, and we do not ask for the whole town to be lit at once. Six or seven lights along this one kilometre would be enough to make it safe, and the road is used every day by students from at least three schools, so few projects could protect so many young people at so little cost.

We therefore appeal to the Council to install streetlights along this road before the end of the rainy season, when the evenings are darkest. The students' union is willing to help by keeping the area around the lights clean, reporting any damage at once, and organising a fund-raising day towards the cost if that would speed up the work.

Thank you for your kind consideration of this request.

:::right
:mark[Yours faithfully,]{n=7}

*(signature)*

**Aminata Sesay**\
(Senior Prefect)
:::
::::

This is a complete examination answer: about 460 words in the body, comfortably above the minimum length, with three developed points, an answer to the obvious objection (cost) and a clear request. Now each part of the format, in detail.

### 1. The writer's address

Top **right**. Write it on three or four short lines, with a **comma** at the end of each line and a **full stop** after the last:

- school or house name / street, then
- P.O. Box or P.M.B. if there is one, then
- town.

In the examination, use the address the question suggests, or a sensible school address. **Never write your own name in the address.** Your name comes at the end of the letter.

### 2. The date

Directly **below** your address, also on the right. Write it in full:

| Good | Avoid |
|---|---|
| **14th March, 2026.** | 14/3/26 (it could mean 3rd February in some countries) |
| **March 14, 2026.** | Thursday (no date at all) |
| | March 2026 (no day) |

### 3. The recipient's title and address

On the **left**, a line or two **below** the date. Begin with the **title** of the office, not a person's name:

- **The Principal,** Government Secondary School, Bo.
- **The Editor,** Awoko Newspaper, Freetown.
- **The Manager,** Rokel Commercial Bank, Kenema.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Writing *"Mr Joseph Kamara, Chairman"* when the question just says *"the Chairman"*. Use the title. You are writing to the office, and the question did not give you a name.
:::

### 4. The salutation

On the left, below the recipient's address:

- **Dear Sir,** to a man, or when the question's context points to one
- **Dear Madam,** to a woman
- **Dear Sir/Madam,** when you cannot know

Start with a capital **D**, and end with a **comma**.

### 5. The heading

Directly below the salutation. It tells the reader the subject of the letter **before** they read it, so make it **specific**:

| Weak heading | Strong heading |
|---|---|
| LETTER | REQUEST FOR STREETLIGHTS ON THE ROAD TO OUR SCHOOL |
| COMPLAINT | COMPLAINT ABOUT THE IRREGULAR WATER SUPPLY IN KISSY |
| APPLICATION | APPLICATION FOR THE POST OF SALES ASSISTANT |

Write it in **capital letters** *or* underline it in small letters. Not both.

### 6. The body

Several paragraphs: an **introduction** saying why you are writing, **one paragraph for each point**, and a short **conclusion** saying what you want to happen. (Lesson 2 covers how to write them.)

### 7. The close, signature and full name

- **Yours faithfully,** with a capital **Y**, small **f**, and a **comma**. No apostrophe in *Yours*.
- Your **signature** (in the exam, write *(signature)* or simply sign).
- Your **full name**, and your **position** in brackets if it matters (*Senior Prefect*, *Class Representative*, *Secretary, Youth Club*).

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The five closing errors examiners see every year:

| Wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| Your's faithfully | no apostrophe in *Yours* |
| Yours Faithfully | small *f* |
| Yours faithfully. | a comma, not a full stop |
| Yours sincerely (after *Dear Sir*) | *Dear Sir/Madam* takes *Yours faithfully* |
| Yours faithfully, Aminata | a formal letter needs your **full name** |
:::

## Formal, semi-formal and informal side by side

| | Formal | Semi-formal | Informal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | an office holder | an older person you know (teacher, uncle, family friend) | a friend, brother, sister, cousin |
| Recipient's address | **yes** | no | no |
| Salutation | Dear Sir, / Dear Madam, | Dear Mr Kamara, / Dear Uncle Sorie, | Dear Kadie, |
| Heading | **yes** | usually no | no |
| Close | **Yours faithfully,** | Yours sincerely, | Yours sincerely, / Your loving friend, |
| Name | **full name** (and position) | full name | first name only |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Formal letters are a real part of life in Sierra Leone: a letter to the **District Education Office** asking for a transfer, to **EDSA** about a faulty meter, to the **Guma Valley Water Company** about broken pipes, to a **Paramount Chief** inviting him to open a school project. In every case, the same seven parts apply.
:::

## Practise the layout

:::reveal{title="Try it 1: put these parts in the right order"}
You were given: *Yours faithfully, / The Principal, St Edward's Secondary School, Freetown. / REQUEST FOR A SCHOOL DEBATING CLUB / Dear Sir, / 22 Pademba Road, Freetown. 3rd May, 2026. / (body) / Mohamed Bangura (Class Representative)*

**Correct order:**

1. 22 Pademba Road, Freetown. *(top right)*
2. 3rd May, 2026. *(below it, right)*
3. The Principal, St Edward's Secondary School, Freetown. *(left)*
4. Dear Sir,
5. REQUEST FOR A SCHOOL DEBATING CLUB
6. (body)
7. Yours faithfully, (signature) Mohamed Bangura (Class Representative)
:::

:::reveal{title="Try it 2: find the five errors"}
*Dear Mr Chairman. / complaint about refuse / … / Your's sincerely, / Fatu*

1. *Dear Mr Chairman.* should be **Dear Sir,** (and a comma, not a full stop).
2. The heading needs capitals (or underlining) and should be specific: **COMPLAINT ABOUT UNCOLLECTED REFUSE ON BAI BUREH ROAD**.
3. *Your's* has no apostrophe: **Yours**.
4. After *Dear Sir* the close is **faithfully**, not *sincerely*.
5. A formal letter ends with the **full name**: **Fatu Kamara**.
:::

:::reveal{title="Try it 3: write the opening block"}
You are the secretary of the Youth Club in Lunsar, writing to the Manager of a mining company to ask for sports equipment. Write everything from the writer's address down to the heading.

**Model:**

Lunsar Youth Club,\
12 Station Road,\
Lunsar.\
10th June, 2026.

The Manager,\
Marampa Mines Limited,\
Lunsar.

Dear Sir,

**REQUEST FOR SPORTS EQUIPMENT FOR THE LUNSAR YOUTH CLUB**
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Salutation**: the greeting of a letter, such as "Dear Sir," or "Dear Madam,".
- **Heading**: the line in a formal letter, below the salutation, that states its subject.
- **Complimentary close**: the closing words of a letter; "Yours faithfully," in a formal letter that begins "Dear Sir/Madam,".
- **Recipient's address**: the title and address of the person written to, on the left of a formal letter.
:::

## Key points

- A letter is **formal** when you write to someone **because of the office they hold**.
- **Seven parts in order**: writer's address and date (**right**), recipient's title and address (**left**), salutation, heading, body, close, signature and full name.
- **Dear Sir/Madam** goes with **Yours faithfully,**.
- Write the date **in full**; make the heading **specific**; end with your **full name** and position.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Format is marked under **Organisation (10 marks)**. Examiners check the two addresses, the date, the salutation, the heading and the matching close. Each missing or wrong part costs marks, and a mismatch such as *Dear Sir … Yours sincerely* is one of the commonest faults in the whole paper. Learn the layout until you can write it without thinking; then all your exam time goes into the content.
:::

## Quick check

1. Your mother's friend is a bank manager. You write to ask her for advice on careers in banking. Formal or semi-formal?
2. Write today's date in the correct formal style.
3. What is the complimentary close for a letter beginning *"Dear Madam,"*?
4. Give a strong heading for a letter to the Editor about the dangers of okada riders who carry three passengers.

**Answers:** 1. **Semi-formal**: you are writing to her as someone you know, not to the bank. 2. For example, **5th November, 2026.** 3. **Yours faithfully,** 4. For example: **THE DANGER OF OVERLOADED OKADAS ON OUR ROADS**.
