**Departmental accounts** are accounts prepared to show the **trading results of each department** of a business separately, such as the clothing, electronics and grocery departments of a supermarket. They show which departments are **profitable** and which are not, so that management can decide where to expand, cut back or change prices. Costs that belong to one department are charged to it **directly**; costs shared by all departments are **apportioned** on a fair basis such as floor area, sales or number of staff. This lesson explains the purposes of departmental accounts and how to apportion shared expenses.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the meaning and purposes of departmental accounts
- distinguish direct (allocated) from shared (apportioned) expenses
- choose a fair basis for apportioning each shared expense
- apportion shared expenses between departments
- explain the limitations of departmental accounts

## Purposes of departmental accounts

1. To find the **gross profit and net profit of each department**.
2. To **compare** the performance of departments.
3. To decide whether to **expand, close or change** a department.
4. To help **control costs** and set prices.
5. To reward departmental managers on **results**.

## Allocation and apportionment

| Allocation (direct expenses) | Apportionment (shared expenses) |
|---|---|
| charged **wholly** to the department that caused it | **shared** between departments on a fair basis |
| e.g. wages of a department's own sales staff, its purchases | e.g. rent of the whole building, advertising, electricity |

### Common bases of apportionment

| Expense | Fair basis |
|---|---|
| rent, rates, building insurance | **floor area** |
| electricity, lighting | floor area or metered use |
| advertising, sales commission | **sales** (turnover) |
| canteen and staff welfare | **number of employees** |
| depreciation and insurance of equipment | **value of equipment** in each department |
| carriage inwards | purchases |
| discount allowed | sales |
| discount received | purchases |

## Worked example

*Figure 1. Apportioning rent by floor area. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Rent of NLe 36,000 is to be apportioned between departments A, B and C, which occupy 300, 200 and 100 square metres.**

Total floor area $= 300 + 200 + 100 = 600$

- A: $\frac{300}{600} \times 36\,000 = 18\,000$
- B: $\frac{200}{600} \times 36\,000 = 12\,000$
- C: $\frac{100}{600} \times 36\,000 = 6\,000$

Check: $18\,000 + 12\,000 + 6\,000 = 36\,000$ ✓
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Advertising of NLe 10,000 is apportioned on sales. Department A sold NLe 120,000 and department B NLe 180,000.**

Ratio $= 120\,000 : 180\,000 = 2 : 3$

- A: $\frac{2}{5} \times 10\,000 = 4\,000$
- B: $\frac{3}{5} \times 10\,000 = 6\,000$
:::

## Limitations

1. Apportionment involves **judgement**: a different basis gives a different departmental profit.
2. A department that shows a **loss** may still help the business, for example by attracting customers who buy in other departments.
3. Shared costs such as rent may **not fall** if a department is closed.

:::callout{type=local-example}
A supermarket in Freetown with a groceries section, a household goods section and a small bakery can use departmental accounts to see that the bakery has the highest margin but uses little floor space, while groceries bring in most customers. The figures help the owner decide where to give more space.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not apportion **every** expense on the same basis. Choose the basis that best reflects **why** the cost is incurred: floor area for rent, sales for advertising, staff numbers for the canteen.
:::

## Key points

- **Departmental accounts** show the profit of each department.
- **Direct expenses** are **allocated**; **shared expenses** are **apportioned**.
- Bases: **floor area** (rent, rates), **sales** (advertising), **staff numbers** (canteen), **asset value** (depreciation, insurance).
- Departmental profit depends on the bases chosen.

## Quick check

1. Give two purposes of departmental accounts.
2. What is the difference between allocation and apportionment?
3. Suggest a basis for apportioning building insurance.
4. Electricity of NLe 9,000 is shared by floor area 2 : 1. How much does each department bear?
5. Why might a loss-making department be kept open?

**Answers:**

1. Any two: find each department's profit; compare departments; decide whether to expand or close; control costs; reward managers.
2. Allocation charges an expense wholly to the department that caused it; apportionment shares an expense between departments on a fair basis.
3. Floor area (or value of the part of the building used).
4. NLe 6,000 and NLe 3,000.
5. Because it may attract customers who also buy in other departments, and its share of fixed costs would continue anyway.
