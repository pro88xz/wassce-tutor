On polling day, a voter in Sierra Leone shows a voter ID card, receives ballot papers, marks them in a private booth and drops them into transparent boxes. That night, the votes are counted in front of party agents and observers. Each step is designed to make the result trustworthy. This lesson explains what elections are, the types of election, the franchise, how elections are conducted and what makes them free and fair.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define an election and state its functions
- identify the types of election
- explain the franchise and universal adult suffrage
- describe the stages in the conduct of an election
- state the conditions for free and fair elections and identify electoral malpractices

## What is an election?

:::callout{type=definition}
An **election** is the process by which qualified citizens **choose representatives** to hold public office, such as President, Members of Parliament and councillors, by casting votes.
:::

**Functions of elections:**

- Give **legitimacy** to government through the consent of the people.
- Allow a **peaceful change** of government.
- Make leaders **accountable**: voters can remove them.
- Enable **political participation** and political education.
- **Recruit leaders** into public office.

## Types of election

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **General election** | Elections held throughout the country for the presidency, Parliament or councils at the end of their term |
| **By-election** | An election to fill a vacancy caused by the death, resignation or removal of a member |
| **Primary election** | An election within a party to choose its candidates |
| **Run-off election** | A second round between the leading candidates when no one wins the required share |
| **Local government election** | Elections for councillors, mayors and council chairpersons |
| **Referendum** | A direct vote by the electorate on a single question |
| **Direct and indirect elections** | Voters choose the officeholder themselves (direct) or choose people who then elect the officeholder (indirect) |

## The franchise

:::callout{type=definition}
The **franchise** (suffrage) is the **right to vote**. **Universal adult suffrage** means that all adult citizens can vote, regardless of sex, wealth, education, religion or ethnic group, subject only to reasonable conditions such as age and sanity.
:::

In the past, many countries limited the franchise by **property**, **income**, **literacy** or **sex**. In Sierra Leone today, every citizen aged **18 or above** who is of sound mind and registered may vote.

**Arguments for universal adult suffrage:** equality of all citizens; government by consent; political education; legitimacy. **Arguments against (historically):** illiterate voters may be misled; votes can be bought; the uninformed may choose poorly.

## Conduct of elections

![Eight stages: delimitation, voter registration, nomination, campaign, polling, counting, declaration and petitions](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/election-process.svg)

*Figure 1. How an election is run. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

1. **Delimitation** of constituencies and wards.
2. **Registration of voters**, display of the provisional register for corrections, and issue of voter ID cards.
3. **Nomination** of candidates, who file papers and pay fees.
4. **Campaigning** by parties and candidates within set dates.
5. **Polling** by **secret ballot**, supervised by electoral officials, with party agents and observers present.
6. **Counting and collation** of votes, often at polling stations, then at district and national tally centres.
7. **Declaration** of results by the electoral commission.
8. **Election petitions**: disputes are settled in court.

## Conditions for free and fair elections

- An **independent and impartial electoral commission**
- An **accurate voters' register**
- **Secret ballot**
- **Universal adult suffrage**
- **Freedom to campaign** and equal access to the media
- **Neutral security forces**
- **Independent observers** (local and international)
- **Transparent counting** and quick announcement of results
- **Independent courts** to hear petitions
- **Political education** of voters

## Electoral malpractices

- **Rigging**: ballot stuffing, falsifying results
- **Multiple voting** and voting by under-age or unregistered people
- **Vote buying** and bribery of officials
- **Intimidation and violence** against voters and candidates
- **Snatching of ballot boxes**
- **Misuse of state resources** by the ruling party
- **Manipulation of the register** or of constituency boundaries (gerrymandering)

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's elections are run by the **Electoral Commission for Sierra Leone (ECSL)**, known until 2022 as the National Electoral Commission. The **Public Elections Act 2022** sets the rules. General elections, for the President, Parliament and local councils, are held together every five years; the most recent were on **24 June 2023**. Observers from **ECOWAS**, the **African Union**, the **European Union** and local groups such as **National Election Watch** monitor the polls.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A **by-election** and a **run-off** are different. A by-election fills a **vacant seat** between general elections; a run-off is a **second round** held because no candidate won enough votes in the first round.
:::

## Key points

- An **election** is the choosing of representatives by vote; it gives **legitimacy, accountability and peaceful change**.
- Types: **general, by-election, primary, run-off, local, referendum; direct or indirect**.
- **Universal adult suffrage**: all adult citizens may vote (18 and above in Sierra Leone).
- Stages: **delimitation → registration → nomination → campaign → polling → counting → declaration → petitions**.
- Free and fair elections need an **independent commission, accurate register, secret ballot, neutral security and observers**.

3. State three conditions for free and fair elections.
