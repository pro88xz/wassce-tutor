A woman lifting a 50 kg bag of rice onto a lorry, a mechanic raising a car with a jack, a farmer moving earth in a wheelbarrow: each uses a machine to make a hard job possible. A machine never gives you energy for free, but it lets a small force overcome a large one.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a machine, mechanical advantage, velocity ratio and efficiency
- explain why the efficiency of a real machine is always less than 100%
- classify levers and calculate their mechanical advantage
- analyse pulley systems, including the block and tackle

## What is a machine?

:::callout{type=definition}
A **machine** is a device by which a **force applied at one point** (the **effort**) is used to **overcome a force at another point** (the **load**).
:::

Machines make work easier by letting a small effort overcome a large load, changing the direction of a force, or moving a load faster or further.

## The three key quantities

:::callout{type=definition}
**Mechanical advantage (MA)** is the ratio of the load to the effort:

$$\text{MA} = \dfrac{\text{load}}{\text{effort}} = \dfrac{L}{E}$$

**Velocity ratio (VR)** is the ratio of the distance moved by the effort to the distance moved by the load (in the same time):

$$\text{VR} = \dfrac{\text{distance moved by effort}}{\text{distance moved by load}} = \dfrac{d_E}{d_L}$$

**Efficiency** is the ratio of the useful work output to the work input, as a percentage:

$$\eta = \dfrac{\text{work output}}{\text{work input}} \times 100\% = \dfrac{L \times d_L}{E \times d_E} \times 100\% = \dfrac{\text{MA}}{\text{VR}} \times 100\%$$
:::

MA and VR are ratios, so they have **no units**.

### Why efficiency is less than 100%

Some of the work input is wasted:

1. overcoming **friction** at moving parts (turned to heat)
2. lifting **moving parts of the machine itself** (such as the lower pulley block)
3. stretching or bending parts of the machine

So the **MA is always less than the VR** in a real machine. VR depends only on the machine's geometry; MA depends also on friction and load. Efficiency can be improved by **lubrication** and by using **lighter moving parts**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A machine of velocity ratio 5 lifts a load of 400 N with an effort of 100 N. Find its MA and efficiency.**

$$\text{MA} = \dfrac{400}{100} = 4 \qquad \eta = \dfrac{4}{5} \times 100\% = 80\%$$
:::

## Levers

:::callout{type=definition}
A **lever** is a rigid bar that turns about a fixed point, the **fulcrum** (pivot).
:::

By the principle of moments,

$$\text{effort} \times \text{effort arm} = \text{load} \times \text{load arm}$$

so for an ideal lever $\text{MA} = \text{VR} = \dfrac{\text{effort arm}}{\text{load arm}}$.

![The three classes of lever: class 1 with the fulcrum between effort and load, class 2 with the load in the middle, class 3 with the effort in the middle](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/lever-classes.svg)

*Figure 1. The three classes of lever. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Class | Arrangement | MA | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | fulcrum **between** effort and load | can be more or less than 1 | crowbar, see-saw, pliers, scissors, claw hammer |
| Second | load **between** fulcrum and effort | always **more than 1** | wheelbarrow, bottle opener, nutcracker |
| Third | effort **between** fulcrum and load | always **less than 1** | human forearm, tweezers, fishing rod, broom |

Third-class levers make the effort larger than the load, but they move the load further and faster: your biceps moves your hand quickly through a large distance.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A wheelbarrow carrying 600 N of sand has the load 0.4 m from the wheel axle (fulcrum); the handles are 1.2 m from the axle. Find the effort needed.**

$$E \times 1.2 = 600 \times 0.4 \Rightarrow E = 200\ \text{N} \qquad \text{MA} = 3$$
:::

## Pulleys

**Single fixed pulley:** changes only the direction of the force. VR = 1, so MA ≤ 1. Used to raise a flag or draw water from a well.

**Single movable pulley:** the load hangs on the pulley, supported by two strands. VR = 2.

**Block and tackle:** two blocks, each with one or more pulleys. The upper block is fixed; the lower block carries the load.

![A block and tackle with two pulleys in the upper fixed block and two in the lower block carrying the load; four rope strands support the lower block and the effort pulls on the free end](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/pulley-block-and-tackle.svg)

*Figure 2. Count the strands that support the lower block to find the VR. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=definition}
For a pulley system, the **velocity ratio = the number of rope strands supporting the movable (lower) block**, which equals the total number of pulleys in both blocks for a block and tackle.
:::

If the load rises 1 m, each of the 4 supporting strands must shorten by 1 m, so the effort end must be pulled 4 m: VR = 4.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A block and tackle has 3 pulleys in each block. An effort of 250 N lifts a load of 1200 N. Find (a) the VR, (b) the MA, (c) the efficiency, (d) the work wasted when the load is raised 2 m.**

(a) VR = 3 + 3 = 6. (b) MA = $\dfrac{1200}{250} = 4.8$. (c) $\eta = \dfrac{4.8}{6} \times 100\% = 80\%$.

(d) Work output $= 1200 \times 2 = 2400$ J. Effort moves $6 \times 2 = 12$ m, so work input $= 250 \times 12 = 3000$ J. Wasted: $3000 - 2400 = 600$ J.
:::

## Key points

- MA = load ÷ effort; VR = effort distance ÷ load distance; efficiency = MA ÷ VR × 100%.
- Real machines: MA < VR and efficiency < 100%, because of friction and the weight of moving parts.
- Levers: class 1 (fulcrum in the middle), class 2 (load in the middle, MA > 1), class 3 (effort in the middle, MA < 1).
- Pulley system: VR = number of strands supporting the movable block.

## Quick check

1. Which class of lever is a pair of tweezers?
2. A machine has VR 4 and efficiency 75%. What effort lifts a 600 N load?
3. Why is the MA of a pulley system less than its VR?

**Answers:** 1. Third class. 2. MA $= 0.75 \times 4 = 3$, so $E = 600 \div 3 = 200$ N. 3. Some effort is used to overcome friction and to lift the movable pulley block.
