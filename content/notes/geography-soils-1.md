:::callout{type=definition}
**Soil** is the thin layer of loose material on the Earth's surface in which plants grow.
:::

It is made of **mineral particles** from weathered rock, **humus** (decayed plant and animal matter), **water**, **air** and **living organisms**. A **soil profile** is a vertical section through the soil from the surface down to the bedrock, showing layers called **horizons**: the organic layer (O), topsoil (A), subsoil (B), weathered parent rock (C) and bedrock (R). This lesson explains how soil forms and what its profile and properties tell us.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define soil and state its components
- explain the factors of soil formation
- describe a soil profile and its horizons
- explain the processes of leaching, laterisation and humification
- describe the main properties of soil

## The components of soil

A typical fertile soil contains about:

| Component | Approximate share | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Mineral matter** | 45% | particles of sand, silt and clay from weathered rock; supplies plant nutrients |
| **Water** | 25% | dissolves nutrients so roots can take them in |
| **Air** | 25% | oxygen for roots and soil organisms |
| **Organic matter (humus)** | about 5% | holds water and nutrients; gives dark colour; binds particles |

**Living organisms**, such as earthworms, termites, fungi and bacteria, mix the soil, break down dead matter and release nutrients.

## Factors of soil formation

1. **Parent material:** the rock from which the soil forms determines its texture and minerals; granite gives sandy soils, shale gives clay soils.
2. **Climate:** temperature and rainfall control the speed of weathering and the processes in the soil. Hot, wet climates form **deep**, heavily **leached** soils.
3. **Relief:** steep slopes have thin soils because material is washed away; valley bottoms collect deep and often waterlogged soils.
4. **Organisms:** plants supply humus; animals and microbes mix and enrich the soil; people farm, fertilise or degrade it.
5. **Time:** soil forms very slowly, often taking hundreds of years for a few centimetres. Older soils are deeper and have clearer horizons.

## The soil profile

![A soil profile: O horizon of leaf litter and humus, A horizon of dark topsoil, B horizon of subsoil where minerals collect, C horizon of weathered parent rock, R horizon of bedrock, with leaching carrying minerals downwards](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/soil-profile.svg)

*Figure 1. A soil profile and its horizons. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Horizon | Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **O** | organic layer | fresh leaf litter and partly decayed humus on the surface |
| **A** | topsoil | dark, rich in humus and roots, full of organisms; minerals are washed out of it (**eluviation**) |
| **B** | subsoil | lighter; receives minerals such as clay and iron washed down from above (**illuviation**); fewer roots |
| **C** | weathered parent rock | broken, partly weathered rock fragments |
| **R** | bedrock | solid, unweathered rock |

## Soil-forming processes

- **Humification:** the breakdown of dead plants and animals into humus by microorganisms. It is rapid in the hot, wet tropics.
- **Leaching:** rainwater percolating down dissolves soluble minerals and carries them out of the topsoil, making it less fertile.
- **Laterisation:** in hot, wet tropical climates, intense leaching removes silica and most nutrients, leaving iron and aluminium oxides behind. The result is a red or yellow **lateritic** soil; when exposed and dried, it may harden into a rock-like crust (**laterite** or ironstone).
- **Podzolisation:** in cool, wet climates under coniferous forest, acidic leaching leaves a pale, ash-like layer.
- **Calcification:** in dry climates, calcium carbonate accumulates in the soil.
- **Gleying:** in waterlogged soils, lack of oxygen gives grey and blue mottled colours.

## Properties of soil

| Property | Meaning | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Texture** | proportions of sand, silt and clay | controls drainage, air and water-holding |
| **Structure** | how particles cling together in crumbs, blocks or plates | crumb structure is best for roots and drainage |
| **Colour** | dark, red, yellow, grey | dark often means humus; red, iron oxides; grey, waterlogging |
| **Porosity and permeability** | amount of pore space; how easily water passes through | sandy soils drain fast; clay soils hold water |
| **Acidity (pH)** | acid or alkaline | most crops prefer slightly acid to neutral soil (pH 6–7) |
| **Fertility** | ability to supply nutrients | humus, minerals and good structure make soil fertile |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Most upland soils in Sierra Leone are **red or yellow lateritic soils**, formed by long, intense leaching and laterisation in the hot, wet climate. They are deep but **low in nutrients and often acidic**, which is why farmers traditionally leave land in **bush fallow** for several years to recover. Where the topsoil has been stripped away by erosion, hard **laterite** crusts appear at the surface and are dug as gravel for roads.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A deep tropical soil is not necessarily a fertile one. Heavy leaching removes nutrients, so most of the fertility in a rainforest soil lies in the **thin humus layer** at the top. Once the forest is cleared, that fertility is quickly lost.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Soil profile**: a vertical section through the soil from the surface to the bedrock, showing its layers.
- **Horizon**: a layer of the soil profile.
- **Humus**: the dark organic matter in the soil, formed from decayed plants and animals.
- **Parent material**: the rock from which a soil forms, which decides its texture and minerals.
- **Soil texture**: the proportions of sand, silt and clay in a soil.
- **Soil structure**: the way soil particles cling together in crumbs, blocks or plates.
:::

## Key points

- Soil: **minerals (45%), water (25%), air (25%), humus (~5%)** and living organisms.
- Formation factors: **parent material, climate, relief, organisms, time**.
- Profile: **O** (litter), **A** (topsoil), **B** (subsoil), **C** (weathered rock), **R** (bedrock).
- Processes: **humification, leaching, laterisation** (tropics), podzolisation, calcification, gleying.
- Properties: **texture, structure, colour, porosity, pH, fertility**.

## Quick check

1. Name the four main components of soil.
2. State three factors of soil formation.
3. Which horizon is the topsoil, and what is it rich in?
4. What is leaching?
5. Why are many tropical soils of low fertility?

**Answers:**

1. Mineral matter, water, air and organic matter (humus).
2. Any three: parent material; climate; relief; organisms; time.
3. The A horizon; it is rich in humus, roots and organisms.
4. The washing of soluble minerals down out of the topsoil by percolating rainwater.
5. Heavy rain causes intense leaching (and laterisation), removing nutrients from the soil.
