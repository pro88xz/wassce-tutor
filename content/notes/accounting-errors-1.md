**Errors in accounting** are mistakes made when recording or posting transactions. They fall into two families: **errors that do not affect the agreement of the trial balance**, because they affect debits and credits equally (**omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal** and **compensating** errors), and **errors that do affect the agreement**, because the debit and credit entries are unequal (such as a **one-sided entry**, a **casting error** or a **wrong balance**). This lesson explains each type of error with an example, and shows why some errors are invisible to the trial balance.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain why errors occur in accounting records
- classify errors into those that do and do not affect the trial balance
- define and give an example of each of the six errors not revealed by the trial balance
- describe errors that do affect the trial balance
- explain how errors are discovered

## Errors that do not affect the trial balance

| Error | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Omission** | a transaction is **completely left out** of the books | a credit sale of NLe 3,000 to Kamara is not recorded at all |
| **Commission** | the right amount is posted to the **wrong account of the same class**, usually the wrong person | NLe 1,200 received from Bah is credited to **Bai's** account |
| **Principle** | an item is posted to the **wrong class** of account | a computer bought for NLe 8,000 is debited to **purchases** (an expense) instead of office equipment (an asset) |
| **Original entry** | the **wrong amount** is recorded in the book of original entry, so **both** entries are wrong | a sale of NLe 2,450 is entered as **2,540** in the sales day book |
| **Complete reversal** | the correct accounts are used but the **debit and credit are swapped** | cash paid to Sesay, NLe 1,500: cash **debited** and Sesay **credited** |
| **Compensating** | two or more errors **cancel each other out** | the sales account is overcast by NLe 500 and the wages account also overcast by NLe 500 |

In every case **debits still equal credits**, so the trial balance agrees and the errors stay hidden until they are found by other checks.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Name the error in each case.**

1. A payment of NLe 4,000 for repairs to the van was debited to the motor van account. **Principle** (a revenue expense treated as a capital asset).
2. Goods sold on credit to M. Koroma for NLe 2,000 were posted to the account of M. Kamara. **Commission.**
3. A purchase invoice for NLe 6,700 was entered in the purchases day book as NLe 7,600. **Original entry.**
4. Rent received of NLe 1,800 was debited to rent received and credited to cash. **Complete reversal.**
:::

## Errors that affect the trial balance

| Error | Example |
|---|---|
| **Single entry** (one side only) | wages of NLe 900 entered in the cash book but not posted to the wages account |
| **Unequal amounts** | NLe 3,450 debited to a customer but NLe 3,540 credited to sales |
| **Two debits or two credits** | a payment debited to both the expense and the cash book |
| **Casting (addition) error** | the sales day book total is **undercast** by NLe 200 |
| **Wrong extraction of a balance** | a balance of NLe 5,600 is listed in the trial balance as NLe 6,500 |

These make the totals **disagree**. The difference is put in a **suspense account** until the errors are found and corrected (see the topic on the suspense account).

## How errors are discovered

1. **Trial balance** totals disagree.
2. **Statements of account** from suppliers do not agree with their ledger accounts.
3. **Customers query** their invoices or statements.
4. **Bank reconciliation** reveals errors in the cash book.
5. **Control accounts** do not agree with the ledgers.
6. **Audits** and checks by managers.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
An error of **commission** and an error of **principle** are often confused. **Commission:** wrong account **of the same type** (one customer for another). **Principle:** wrong **type** of account (an asset recorded as an expense, or capital expenditure as revenue expenditure).
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Error of omission**: a transaction completely left out of the books.
- **Error of commission**: the right amount posted to the wrong account of the same class, usually the wrong person.
- **Error of principle**: an item posted to the wrong class of account, such as a non-current asset debited to purchases.
- **Error of original entry**: the wrong amount recorded in the book of original entry, so both entries are wrong.
- **Complete reversal of entries**: the correct accounts used but the debit and credit swapped.
- **Compensating errors**: two or more errors that cancel each other out.
- **Casting error**: a mistake in adding up a book or account, such as an undercast day book total.
:::

## Key points

- Errors **not revealed** by the trial balance: **omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal, compensating**.
- Errors **revealed**: **single entry, unequal amounts, two debits or two credits, casting errors, wrong balances**.
- Revealed errors create a **difference** placed in a **suspense account**.
- Errors are found through the **trial balance, statements, bank reconciliation, control accounts and audits**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **define and give examples of errors not revealed by the trial balance**, and to **identify the type of error** in a given situation. This is one of the most common theory questions in Financial Accounting: learn all six with an example each.
:::

## Quick check

1. Name four errors that do not affect the trial balance.
2. What is an error of principle?
3. A sale of NLe 3,800 was recorded as NLe 3,080 in the sales day book. What error is this?
4. Why does an error of omission not affect the trial balance?
5. Give two errors that would make the trial balance disagree.

**Answers:**

1. Any four: omission; commission; principle; original entry; complete reversal; compensating.
2. An item posted to the wrong class of account, such as an asset recorded as an expense.
3. An error of original entry.
4. Because neither the debit nor the credit has been recorded, so both totals are equally affected.
5. Any two: single entry; unequal amounts; two debits or two credits; casting error; balance extracted wrongly.
