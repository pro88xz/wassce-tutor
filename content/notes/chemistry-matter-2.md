Salt from seawater, clean water from a muddy river, diamonds from gravel, petrol from crude oil: all are separations of mixtures. Each method works because the parts of the mixture differ in some physical property. This lesson explains the standard separation techniques and how chemists check that a substance is pure.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- choose a separation method from the properties of the substances in a mixture
- describe filtration, evaporation, crystallisation, distillation, fractional distillation, the separating funnel, chromatography and sublimation
- calculate Rf values from a chromatogram
- plan the separation of a mixture of three substances
- use melting and boiling points and chromatography to test purity

## Choosing a method

| Mixture | Property difference | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Insoluble solid + liquid (sand + water) | Particle size; one dissolves, one does not | **Filtration** (or decantation) |
| Dissolved solid + liquid, want the solid (salt solution) | Liquid evaporates, solid does not | **Evaporation** or **crystallisation** |
| Dissolved solid + liquid, want the liquid (seawater) | Different boiling points | **Simple distillation** |
| Miscible liquids (ethanol + water; crude oil) | Boiling points close together | **Fractional distillation** |
| Immiscible liquids (oil + water) | Different densities; do not mix | **Separating funnel** |
| Mixture of coloured dyes | Different solubility and attraction to paper | **Chromatography** |
| Solid that sublimes + one that does not (ammonium chloride + salt; iodine + sand) | One changes directly from solid to gas | **Sublimation** |
| Magnetic + non-magnetic solids (iron + sulphur) | Magnetism | **Magnetic separation** |
| Solids of different particle sizes (rice + stones) | Size | **Sieving** |
| Light and heavy solids (rice + chaff) | Density in wind | **Winnowing** |
| Solid suspended in liquid (blood cells) | Density, under rapid spinning | **Centrifugation** |

## Filtration, evaporation and the separating funnel

![Filtration leaves a residue on the filter paper and collects the filtrate; evaporation drives off water to leave a solid; a separating funnel lets the denser liquid be run off first](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/separation-methods.svg)

*Figure 1. Three everyday separation techniques. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

- **Filtration:** the solid left on the paper is the **residue**; the liquid that passes through is the **filtrate**.
- **Evaporation:** heating drives off the solvent completely. Use it for solids that do not decompose on heating, such as common salt.
- **Crystallisation:** heat the solution only until it is **saturated** (a drop on a cold rod forms crystals), then let it cool slowly. Crystals form; filter, wash with a little cold water and dry. Crystallisation gives **purer** crystals than evaporation to dryness and suits hydrated salts such as copper(II) sulphate.
- **Separating funnel:** the **denser** liquid forms the lower layer and is run off first through the tap.

## Distillation

![Simple distillation: salt solution boils in a flask, the vapour passes the thermometer at the side arm into a water-cooled Liebig condenser and drips into a beaker as pure water](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/distillation.svg)

*Figure 2. Simple distillation of salt solution. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

Key points of the set-up:

- The **thermometer bulb** is level with the **side arm**, to measure the temperature of the vapour that distils.
- Cold water enters the condenser at the **bottom** and leaves at the **top**, so the condenser stays full and cooling is efficient.
- **Anti-bumping granules** in the flask give smooth boiling.

**Fractional distillation** adds a **fractionating column** (packed with glass beads) between the flask and condenser. Vapour condenses and re-boils many times up the column, so the liquid with the **lower boiling point** reaches the top first. It is used to separate ethanol (78 °C) from water (100 °C), crude oil into fractions, and liquid air into nitrogen and oxygen.

## Chromatography

![Paper chromatography: a pure dye A and an ink are spotted on a pencil base line; the solvent separates the ink into two dyes, one matching dye A. Rf values are worked out from distances moved](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/chromatography.svg)

*Figure 3. Paper chromatography and Rf values. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

$$R_f = \dfrac{\text{distance moved by spot}}{\text{distance moved by solvent}}$$

- The base line is drawn in **pencil**, because ink would itself separate.
- The base line must be **above** the solvent level, or the spots dissolve into the solvent.
- An Rf value is always **less than 1**. A substance always gives the **same Rf** with the same solvent and paper, so Rf values identify substances.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Rf values.** The solvent front moves 10 cm. A blue spot moves 8 cm and a red spot moves 5.5 cm.

- Rf (blue) $= 8 \div 10 = 0.80$
- Rf (red) $= 5.5 \div 10 = 0.55$

A pure dye A, run on the same paper, also has Rf 0.80, so the ink contains dye A.
:::

## Separating a mixture of three substances

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Separate a mixture of sand, common salt and iron filings.**

1. Pass a **magnet** over the dry mixture: the **iron filings** stick to it.
2. Add **water** to the rest and stir: the salt dissolves; the sand does not.
3. **Filter:** the **sand** stays on the paper as the residue. Wash and dry it.
4. **Evaporate** (or crystallise) the filtrate: **salt** is left.
:::

## Testing for purity

| Test | Pure substance | Impure substance |
|---|---|---|
| **Melting point** | Melts sharply at a fixed temperature | Melts **lower** and over a **range** |
| **Boiling point** | Boils at a fixed temperature (water: 100 °C at sea level) | Boils **higher** (salt water boils above 100 °C) |
| **Chromatography** | Gives **one** spot | Gives **two or more** spots |

Purity matters most in **medicines** (impurities can be harmful), **food** and **chemicals** for industry and research.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Separations are part of everyday life in Sierra Leone:

- **Salt pans:** salt makers along the coast let the sun **evaporate** seawater.
- **Gravel washing:** in Kono, diamond diggers **wash and sieve** gravel, using the high density of diamonds to separate them.
- **Rice:** women **winnow** rice to remove chaff.
- **Palm oil:** the oil is **skimmed (decanted)** from the top of boiled palm fruit.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
In distillation, **cold water enters the condenser at the bottom**, not the top. Water entering at the top would run straight out and leave the condenser half empty.
:::

## Key points

- Choose a method from a **property difference**: solubility, boiling point, density, magnetism, particle size, sublimation.
- **Filtration** separates insoluble solids; **evaporation/crystallisation** recovers dissolved solids; **distillation** recovers the solvent.
- **Fractional distillation** separates miscible liquids with close boiling points; a **separating funnel** separates immiscible liquids.
- **Chromatography** separates dyes; **Rf = spot distance ÷ solvent distance**.
- A **pure** substance has a **sharp, fixed melting point** and gives **one spot** on a chromatogram.

## Quick check

1. Name the method for separating (a) kerosene and water, (b) ethanol and water.
2. Why is the chromatography base line drawn in pencil?
3. A spot moves 3.6 cm while the solvent front moves 9.0 cm. Find its Rf.
4. How does an impurity affect the melting point of a solid?

**Answers:**

1. (a) Separating funnel; (b) fractional distillation.
2. Pencil (graphite) does not dissolve in the solvent; ink would separate and spoil the result.
3. $3.6 \div 9.0 = 0.40$.
4. It lowers the melting point and makes it melt over a range of temperatures.
