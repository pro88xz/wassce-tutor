**Double-entry bookkeeping** is the system in which **every transaction is recorded twice**: once on the **debit** side of one account and once on the **credit** side of another, for the **same amount**. It rests on the dual aspect concept: every transaction gives value and receives value. The **rules of debit and credit** tell you which account to debit and which to credit: **debit** increases in **assets, expenses and drawings**; **credit** increases in **liabilities, capital and income**. This lesson explains the rules, the T-account, and how to record common transactions with worked examples.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the principle of double entry
- describe the layout of a T-account
- state the rules of debit and credit for each type of account
- record common transactions with the correct debit and credit
- explain why double entry provides an arithmetical check

## The principle

:::callout{type=definition}
In **double entry**, every transaction has **two aspects**, giving and receiving, and is therefore entered in **two accounts**: one account is **debited** and another is **credited** with the **same amount**. As a result, **total debits always equal total credits**.
:::

**The T-account:**

| Dr (debit) | NLe | Cr (credit) | NLe |
|---|---|---|---|
| left side | | right side | |

"Dr" and "Cr" come from the Latin *debere* (to owe) and *credere* (to trust). Debit and credit simply mean **left** and **right**; they do not mean "good" and "bad" or "money in" and "money out".

## The rules of debit and credit

![A T-account showing on the debit side increases in assets, expenses and drawings and decreases in liabilities, capital and income, and on the credit side increases in liabilities, capital and income and decreases in assets, with the memory aid DEAD CLIC](https://wasscetutor.com/img/accounting/debit-credit-rules.svg)

*Figure 1. The rules of debit and credit. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Type of account | Increase | Decrease |
|---|---|---|
| **Assets** (cash, bank, stock, van) | **Debit** | Credit |
| **Expenses** (wages, rent, purchases) | **Debit** | Credit |
| **Drawings** | **Debit** | Credit |
| **Liabilities** (loans, trade payables) | Credit | **Debit** |
| **Capital** | Credit | **Debit** |
| **Income** (sales, commission received) | Credit | **Debit** |

A simple way to remember: **DEAD CLIC**, which stands for **D**ebit **E**xpenses, **A**ssets, **D**rawings; **C**redit **L**iabilities, **I**ncome, **C**apital.

## Recording common transactions

| Transaction | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Owner starts business with cash | Cash | Capital |
| Buys goods for resale by cash | Purchases | Cash |
| Buys goods on credit from Bangura | Purchases | Bangura |
| Sells goods for cash | Cash | Sales |
| Sells goods on credit to Kamara | Kamara | Sales |
| Kamara pays by cheque | Bank | Kamara |
| Pays Bangura by cheque | Bangura | Bank |
| Pays wages in cash | Wages | Cash |
| Buys a motor van by cheque | Motor van | Bank |
| Takes a bank loan | Bank | Loan |
| Owner takes cash for private use | Drawings | Cash |
| Kamara returns goods | Returns inwards | Kamara |
| Business returns goods to Bangura | Bangura | Returns outwards |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Record these transactions of Fatmata's business in ledger accounts (NLe).**

- 1 June: started business with NLe 30,000 cash.
- 3 June: bought goods for NLe 8,000 cash.
- 6 June: sold goods for NLe 5,000 cash.
- 9 June: paid wages NLe 1,200 cash.

**Cash account**

| Dr | NLe | Cr | NLe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital | 30,000 | Pur­chases | 8,000 |
| Sales | 5,000 | Wages | 1,200 |

**Capital account:** Cr Cash 30,000. **Purchases account:** Dr Cash 8,000. **Sales account:** Cr Cash 5,000. **Wages account:** Dr Cash 1,200.

Each transaction appears once on a debit side and once on a credit side:

- debits: cash (from capital) 30,000 + cash (from sales) 5,000 + purchases 8,000 + wages 1,200 $= 44\,200$
- credits: capital 30,000 + sales 5,000 + cash (for purchases) 8,000 + cash (for wages) 1,200 $= 44\,200$ ✓
:::

In each ledger entry, the **details** column names the **other account** in the double entry. In the cash account, "Capital" on the debit side means the other entry is in the capital account.

## Why double entry is used

1. It gives a **complete record** of both aspects of every transaction.
2. It provides an **arithmetical check**: the trial balance should balance.
3. It allows **final accounts** to be prepared from the ledger.
4. It shows **amounts owed to and by** the business in personal accounts.
5. It helps to **detect fraud and errors**.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not debit the bank account when a business **pays** by cheque. Money leaving the bank **reduces an asset**, so the **bank is credited**; the account receiving the value (the supplier, or the expense) is debited.
:::

## Key points

- Every transaction is recorded **twice**: a **debit** in one account and an equal **credit** in another.
- **Debit:** increase in assets, expenses, drawings. **Credit:** increase in liabilities, capital, income. (**DEAD CLIC**)
- The **details** column names the other account in the entry.
- **Total debits = total credits**, which the trial balance checks.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **state the rules of double entry**, to **name the accounts to be debited and credited** for given transactions (a very common objective question), and to **post transactions to ledger accounts**. Read each transaction carefully: "on credit" means a personal account, "by cheque" means the bank account.
:::

## Quick check

1. What is double-entry bookkeeping?
2. Which side records an increase in an asset?
3. Give the double entry for goods sold on credit to Kamara.
4. Give the double entry for wages paid by cheque.
5. Give the double entry for the owner taking goods for private use.

**Answers:**

1. A system in which every transaction is recorded twice, as a debit in one account and an equal credit in another.
2. The debit side.
3. Debit Kamara's account; credit sales account.
4. Debit wages account; credit bank account.
5. Debit drawings account; credit purchases account.
