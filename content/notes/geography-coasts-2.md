**Wind erosion** shapes hot deserts such as the Sahara, creating landforms like **mushroom rocks, zeugens, yardangs and deflation hollows**, while wind deposition builds **sand dunes** such as **barchans and seif dunes**. **Glacial erosion** by moving ice shapes high mountains, creating **corries, arêtes, pyramidal peaks, U-shaped valleys and hanging valleys**, and glaciers deposit **moraines and drumlins**. This lesson explains how wind and ice erode, transport and deposit, and the landforms each produces.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain why wind is an effective agent of erosion in hot deserts
- describe the processes of wind erosion and transportation
- explain the formation of desert landforms of erosion and deposition
- describe how glaciers erode and the landforms of glacial erosion
- describe the landforms of glacial deposition
- give African examples of desert and glacial landforms

## Wind as an agent of erosion

Wind does most of its work in **hot deserts**, because there:

- there is little **vegetation** to bind the surface
- the ground is **dry and loose** after physical weathering
- strong winds blow over **open, flat land**

### Processes

| Process | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Deflation** | wind lifts and blows away loose, fine particles, lowering the surface |
| **Abrasion (sand-blasting)** | sand carried by the wind wears away rock surfaces, most strongly within about 1 m of the ground |
| **Attrition** | wind-blown grains collide and become small, round and smooth |

The wind moves sand by **saltation** (bouncing), **surface creep** (rolling) and **suspension** (fine dust carried high, sometimes thousands of kilometres).

## Desert landforms

**Landforms of wind erosion:**

| Landform | Formation |
|---|---|
| **Deflation hollow** | a basin formed where deflation removes loose material, sometimes down to the water table to form an **oasis** (the Qattara Depression in Egypt is a huge example) |
| **Mushroom rock (rock pedestal)** | abrasion is strongest near the ground, so the base of a rock is worn away faster than the top |
| **Zeugen** | a ridge capped by hard rock over softer rock, where horizontal layers have been eroded along joints |
| **Yardang** | a long ridge of hard rock with softer rock removed on either side, where the layers are vertical and parallel to the wind |
| **Inselberg** | an isolated, steep-sided hill of hard rock rising above a plain |

**Landforms of wind deposition:**

| Landform | Description |
|---|---|
| **Barchan** | a crescent-shaped dune; gentle slope facing the wind, steep slip face behind, and **horns pointing downwind**; it moves slowly forward |
| **Seif dune** | a long, sharp ridge of sand lying **parallel** to the prevailing wind, up to hundreds of kilometres long |
| **Erg** | a sandy desert (sand sea) |
| **Loess** | thick deposits of fine wind-blown dust, which form fertile soils (northern China) |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Every year from about December to February, the **Harmattan**, a dry north-east trade wind from the Sahara, carries fine dust in **suspension** all the way to Sierra Leone, turning the sky hazy, drying the air and coating everything in dust. It shows how far the wind can transport material.
:::

## Glaciers and glacial erosion

A **glacier** is a large mass of ice moving slowly downhill under its own weight. Glaciers form where more snow falls in winter than melts in summer, and the snow is compressed into ice. **Valley glaciers** flow down mountain valleys; **ice sheets** cover huge areas, as in Antarctica and Greenland.

**Processes of glacial erosion:**

- **Plucking:** meltwater freezes onto rock, and as the ice moves it pulls away blocks of rock.
- **Abrasion:** rocks frozen into the base of the ice scrape the valley floor like sandpaper, leaving scratches (striations).
- **Freeze–thaw** weathering on the valley sides supplies more rock.

### Landforms of glacial erosion

| Landform | Formation |
|---|---|
| **Corrie (cirque)** | an armchair-shaped hollow on a mountainside, where snow collected and ice eroded by plucking and abrasion; may hold a small lake (**tarn**) |
| **Arête** | a knife-edged ridge between two corries |
| **Pyramidal peak** | a sharp peak where three or more corries are eroded back to back (the Matterhorn, Switzerland) |
| **U-shaped valley (glacial trough)** | a river valley widened and deepened by a glacier into a U with a flat floor and steep sides |
| **Hanging valley** | a tributary valley left "hanging" above the main U-shaped valley, often with a waterfall |
| **Fjord** | a U-shaped valley drowned by the sea after the ice melted (Norway) |

### Landforms of glacial deposition

When the ice melts, it drops its load, called **till** or **boulder clay**:

- **Moraines:** ridges of till. **Lateral** moraine along the sides, **medial** in the middle where two glaciers meet, **terminal** at the furthest point the ice reached.
- **Drumlins:** egg-shaped hills of till, streamlined in the direction of ice flow.
- **Erratics:** large boulders carried far from their source.
- **Outwash plain:** sand and gravel spread by meltwater beyond the ice.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Africa has small glaciers only on its highest mountains near the Equator: **Kilimanjaro** (Tanzania), **Mount Kenya** and the **Ruwenzori** range. All of them are shrinking rapidly as the climate warms, and scientists expect some to disappear within a few decades.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A **barchan** has its horns pointing **downwind**, not towards the wind, because the horns move faster than the thicker centre of the dune.
:::

## Key points

- Wind works best in **dry, bare, flat** deserts: **deflation, abrasion, attrition**.
- Wind erosion landforms: **deflation hollows, mushroom rocks, zeugens, yardangs, inselbergs**.
- Wind deposition: **barchans** (crescent, horns downwind), **seif dunes** (parallel to the wind), **ergs, loess**.
- Glaciers erode by **plucking and abrasion**: **corries, arêtes, pyramidal peaks, U-shaped valleys, hanging valleys, fjords**.
- Glacial deposition: **moraines, drumlins, erratics, outwash plains**.
- The **Harmattan** carries Saharan dust to Sierra Leone.

## Quick check

1. Give two reasons why wind erosion is effective in hot deserts.
2. Why is a mushroom rock narrow at its base?
3. Distinguish a barchan from a seif dune.
4. Name two processes of glacial erosion.
5. What is a fjord?

**Answers:**

1. Any two: little vegetation; dry, loose surface material; strong winds over open, flat land.
2. Wind abrasion by blown sand is strongest near the ground, so the base is worn faster than the top.
3. A barchan is a crescent-shaped dune with horns pointing downwind; a seif is a long ridge parallel to the wind.
4. Plucking and abrasion.
5. A U-shaped glacial valley drowned by the sea.
