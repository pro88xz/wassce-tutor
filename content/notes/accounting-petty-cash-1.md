The **petty cash book** records the business's **small, everyday cash payments**, such as postage, transport fares, cleaning and refreshments, so that the main cashier does not have to handle them. Most businesses keep it on the **imprest system**: the petty cashier is given a **fixed float** (the **imprest**) at the start of each period, pays small expenses against **vouchers**, and is then **reimbursed exactly the amount spent**, which restores the float to its original amount. This lesson explains petty cash, the imprest system, petty cash vouchers and the advantages of the system.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the meaning and purpose of the petty cash book
- explain the imprest system and how the float is restored
- describe the petty cash voucher
- state the advantages of the imprest system
- calculate the amount of reimbursement

## The petty cash book

:::callout{type=definition}
The **petty cash book** is a book of original entry (and, like the cash book, a ledger account) used to record **small cash payments** for minor expenses. It is kept by a **petty cashier**, a junior member of staff, under the supervision of the main cashier.
:::

**Why keep a petty cash book?**

1. It relieves the **main cashier** of many small payments.
2. It allows a **junior clerk** to be trained and given responsibility.
3. It keeps **small items** out of the main cash book.
4. **Analysis columns** group expenses, so only **totals** are posted to the ledger.
5. It gives **control** over small spending.

## The imprest system

![The imprest system in three steps: a float of NLe 5,000 is given, petty expenses of NLe 4,300 are paid on vouchers, and NLe 4,300 is reimbursed, bringing cash in hand back to NLe 5,000](https://wasscetutor.com/img/accounting/imprest-system.svg)

*Figure 1. How the imprest system works. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=definition}
Under the **imprest system**, the main cashier gives the petty cashier a fixed sum of money, the **imprest** or **float**, at the start of a period. At the end of the period the petty cashier presents the **vouchers** for all payments made and is **reimbursed exactly the amount spent**, so that the cash in hand is restored to the imprest.
:::

$$\text{Reimbursement} = \text{Imprest} - \text{Cash in hand at the end}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A business keeps a petty cash imprest of NLe 5,000. During March the petty cashier paid out NLe 4,300 and has NLe 700 left. How much is reimbursed?**

Reimbursement $= 5\,000 - 700 = 4\,300$, which equals the amount spent.

After reimbursement, cash in hand $= 700 + 4\,300 = 5\,000$, the full imprest.
:::

If the imprest is **raised or lowered**, the reimbursement is adjusted. For example, if the imprest is raised to NLe 6,000, the cashier receives $6\,000 - 700 = 5\,300$.

## The petty cash voucher

Every payment must be supported by a **petty cash voucher**, signed by the person receiving the money and **authorised** by a responsible officer. It shows:

- the **voucher number** (vouchers are numbered in order),
- the **date**,
- the **purpose** of the payment,
- the **amount**,
- the **signatures** of the receiver and the authorising officer,
- any **receipt** attached (for example, a taxi or shop receipt).

## Advantages of the imprest system

1. **Control:** the cash in hand plus vouchers must always equal the imprest, so shortages are noticed at once.
2. The **main cashier's time is saved**.
3. **Fraud and errors are limited**, because only a small sum is at risk.
4. **Spending is checked** regularly when vouchers are presented.
5. **Junior staff are trained** in handling money and records.
6. Small expenses are **analysed** and posted in totals.

## Typical petty cash expenses

| Expense | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Postage and stationery** | stamps, envelopes, pens, printer paper |
| **Travelling** | bus, okada and taxi fares on business errands |
| **Cleaning** | cleaner's wages, cleaning materials |
| **Refreshments** | tea, sugar, water for visitors |
| **Sundries** | small repairs, newspapers |
| **Ledger payments** | small amounts paid to creditors, posted to their personal accounts |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The petty cashier is **not** reimbursed a fixed amount every period. The reimbursement equals **what was actually spent** (imprest minus cash left), so the float always returns to the same total.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Imprest system**: a petty cash system with a fixed float: payments are made on vouchers and the amount spent is reimbursed, restoring the float.
- **Imprest**: the fixed amount of the petty cash float.
- **Petty cash voucher**: the document that supports each petty cash payment.
- **Reimbursement**: the amount paid to the petty cashier to restore the float: imprest minus cash in hand.
:::

## Key points

- The **petty cash book** records **small cash payments**, kept by a **petty cashier**.
- **Imprest system:** fixed float; payments on **vouchers**; reimbursement **equals amount spent**; float restored.
- **Reimbursement = imprest − cash in hand.**
- Advantages: **control, time saved, limited fraud, regular checking, training, analysis**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
WAEC regularly asks candidates to **explain the imprest system and the petty cash book**, and to **state the advantages of the imprest system**. Define the imprest as the fixed float and state clearly that the reimbursement equals the amount spent.
:::

## Quick check

1. What is the petty cash book used for?
2. Explain the imprest system.
3. An imprest is NLe 4,000 and NLe 650 is left at the month end. Find the reimbursement.
4. State three items shown on a petty cash voucher.
5. Give three advantages of the imprest system.

**Answers:**

1. To record small cash payments for minor expenses.
2. The petty cashier is given a fixed float (the imprest), pays small expenses against vouchers, and is reimbursed exactly the amount spent, restoring the float.
3. $4\,000 - 650 = 3\,350$: NLe 3,350.
4. Any three: voucher number; date; purpose; amount; signature of receiver; signature of authorising officer.
5. Any three: control of small spending; main cashier's time saved; limited fraud and errors; regular checking; training of junior staff; analysis of expenses.
