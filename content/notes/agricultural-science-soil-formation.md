Every crop a farmer grows depends on **soil**: it holds the plant up and supplies water, air and nutrients to its roots. Yet soil forms extremely slowly: it can take hundreds of years to make a few centimetres from solid rock, and a single heavy storm can wash that away from bare land. Understanding how soil forms and what it is made of explains why tropical soils in Sierra Leone are often poor and acidic, why the topsoil matters so much, and why it must be protected. This lesson covers the factors and processes of soil formation, the soil profile and the components of soil.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define soil
- explain the five factors of soil formation
- describe physical, chemical and biological weathering
- describe a soil profile and the features of each horizon
- state the components of soil and the importance of each

## What is soil?

:::callout{type=definition}
**Soil** is the loose, weathered material on the surface of the Earth, made of **mineral particles, organic matter, water, air and living organisms**, in which plants grow.
:::

## Factors of soil formation

Five factors decide what kind of soil forms in a place:

| Factor | How it affects the soil |
|---|---|
| **parent material** (rock) | sets the minerals and texture: granite gives sandy soils; basalt and shale give clayey soils |
| **climate** (rainfall and temperature) | heavy rain and high temperatures speed up weathering and **leaching**; Sierra Leone's high rainfall makes soils deep, weathered and often acidic |
| **living organisms** | plants add organic matter; roots break rock; earthworms, termites and microbes mix and decompose |
| **topography** (relief) | steep slopes have thin soils because soil washes downhill; valley bottoms collect deep, wet soils |
| **time** | older soils are deeper and more weathered |

:::callout{type=tip}
Remember the five factors as **"Parent material, Climate, Organisms, Relief, Time"**: the first letters give **P-C-O-R-T**.
:::

## Weathering: how rock becomes soil

![Parent rock is broken down by physical, chemical and biological weathering, and with organic matter added it becomes soil](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/weathering.svg)

**Weathering** is the breakdown of rock in place.

### Physical (mechanical) weathering

Rock breaks into smaller pieces **without changing its minerals**.

- **Temperature changes**: hot days and cooler nights make rock surfaces expand and contract; outer layers peel off (**exfoliation**).
- **Water**: running water and rain wear rock and carry particles that grind other rocks.
- **Wind**: blows sand that wears rock surfaces, mainly in dry areas.

### Chemical weathering

The minerals in the rock are **changed** into new substances.

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Hydrolysis**: water reacts with minerals such as feldspar to form clay.
- **Oxidation**: iron in the rock reacts with oxygen, giving the **red and yellow colours** of many tropical soils and forming **laterite** (ironstone).
- **Carbonation**: rainwater with dissolved carbon dioxide (a weak acid) dissolves limestone.
- **Solution**: some minerals simply dissolve in water.
- **Hydration**: minerals take up water and swell, weakening the rock.
:::

Chemical weathering is fast in hot, wet climates like Sierra Leone's.

### Biological weathering

- **Roots** grow into cracks and widen them.
- **Burrowing animals** (earthworms, termites, rodents) mix and break up material.
- **Lichens, bacteria and fungi** release acids that dissolve rock and later add **humus**.

## The soil profile

![A soil profile with horizons O (leaf litter and humus), A (dark topsoil), B (lighter subsoil where leached minerals collect), C (weathered parent rock) and R (bedrock)](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/soil-profile.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
A **soil profile** is a vertical section through the soil from the surface down to the parent rock, showing its layers, called **horizons**.

| Horizon | Name | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **O** | organic layer | fresh and decaying leaves and humus |
| **A** | **topsoil** | dark (humus), crumbly, most roots and soil organisms; most fertile; **leaching** removes nutrients from it |
| **B** | **subsoil** | lighter, more compact; **leached minerals and clay accumulate** here; fewer roots |
| **C** | weathered parent material | broken, partly weathered rock |
| **R** | bedrock | solid parent rock |
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Thinking all soil is equally valuable.** Nearly all of a soil's fertility is in the **thin topsoil** (A horizon), often only 15–30 cm deep. When erosion removes the topsoil, the infertile subsoil left behind grows poor crops for many years.
:::

## Components of soil

![A good loam soil by volume: about 45% mineral matter, 25% water, 25% air and 5% organic matter](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/soil-composition.svg)

| Component | Importance |
|---|---|
| **mineral matter** (sand, silt, clay, gravel) | the skeleton of the soil; supplies mineral nutrients; decides texture |
| **organic matter** (humus) | holds water and nutrients; improves structure; releases nutrients as it decays; feeds soil organisms; darkens the soil |
| **soil water** | dissolves nutrients so roots can absorb them; needed for germination and all plant processes |
| **soil air** | supplies oxygen for root respiration and soil organisms; nitrogen for nitrogen-fixing bacteria |
| **living organisms** (earthworms, termites, bacteria, fungi) | decompose organic matter, recycle nutrients, fix nitrogen, mix and aerate the soil |

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Laterite and red soils.** Drive along the roads of Sierra Leone and you see red and orange cuttings and hard, reddish ironstone (laterite) gravel used for road building. This is the result of intense **chemical weathering and leaching** under high rainfall: the bases are washed out, iron and aluminium oxides remain, and the soils become **acidic and low in nutrients**. That is why adding organic matter, liming and careful fertiliser use matter so much on upland farms.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Define soil."}
The loose weathered layer of the Earth's surface, made of mineral particles, organic matter, water, air and living organisms, in which plants grow.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. State the five factors of soil formation."}
Parent material, climate, living organisms, topography (relief), and time.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Distinguish physical from chemical weathering, with one example of each."}
**Physical** weathering breaks rock into smaller pieces without changing its minerals (e.g. exfoliation by temperature changes). **Chemical** weathering changes the minerals (e.g. oxidation of iron, hydrolysis of feldspar to clay).
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Why is the A horizon the most important for crops?"}
It contains most of the **humus, nutrients, roots and soil organisms**, with good structure and aeration, so it supplies most of what crops need.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. State two functions of soil air."}
Supplies **oxygen** for root respiration and soil organisms; supplies **nitrogen** for nitrogen-fixing bacteria.
:::

## Key points

- Soil = **mineral matter + organic matter + water + air + organisms**.
- Five factors: **parent material, climate, organisms, relief, time**.
- Weathering: **physical** (temperature, water, wind), **chemical** (hydrolysis, oxidation, carbonation, solution, hydration), **biological** (roots, animals, microbes).
- Profile: **O, A (topsoil), B (subsoil), C, R**; the topsoil holds most of the fertility.
- Heavy rainfall makes many Sierra Leone soils **leached, acidic and low in nutrients**.
