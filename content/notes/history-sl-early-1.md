The **early inhabitants of Sierra Leone** included peoples such as the **Limba**, **Bullom (Sherbro)**, **Kissi**, **Gola** and **Krim**, who were living in the land before the fifteenth century, together with the ancestors of the **Temne**. Archaeology at **Yengema** in Kono and **Kamabai** in the north shows that people lived here thousands of years ago, and the stone **nomoli** figures and **Sapi ivories** show their skill. From the sixteenth century new groups arrived: the **Mane** invaders from the south-east (from about **1545**), and later the **Mende**, **Susu**, **Yalunka**, **Koranko**, **Fula** and **Mandinka**. This lesson explains **who the early inhabitants of Sierra Leone were** and **how the main ethnic groups came to live in the country**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the evidence for early human settlement in Sierra Leone
- name the earliest known peoples of Sierra Leone
- explain the Mane invasions and their effects
- describe the later migrations of Mande-speaking and northern peoples

- locate the main ethnic groups on a map

## Evidence of early settlement

| Evidence | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Yengema cave** (Kono) | stone tools showing that people lived in eastern Sierra Leone thousands of years ago |
| **Kamabai rock shelter** and other sites in the north | **stone tools and pottery** several thousand years old, and later **iron working** |
| **Nomoli** | small **soapstone figures** of people, found by farmers in Mende, Kissi and Kono country; made centuries ago by the ancestors of local peoples |
| **Sapi ivories** | finely carved **ivory salt cellars, spoons and horns** made by coastal craftsmen for Portuguese buyers in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries |
| **Oral traditions** | stories of migration and the founding of chiefdoms, passed down by elders and griots |
| **Portuguese written accounts** | descriptions of coastal peoples, kings and trade from the fifteenth century onwards |

:::callout{type=definition}
The **Sapi** was the Portuguese name for the coastal peoples of Sierra Leone and Guinea, including the ancestors of the **Bullom, Temne, Baga and Landuma**. Their carvers produced the **Sapi-Portuguese ivories**, now treasured in museums around the world.
:::

## The earliest peoples

1. **Limba**: regarded as among the **oldest inhabitants**, living in the hills of the north (the Wara Wara area). Their language has no close relatives in the region.
2. **Bullom and Sherbro**: lived along the **coast**, from the Bullom shore north of Freetown to Sherbro Island and beyond. The **Sherbro** are a southern branch of the Bullom.
3. **Kissi, Gola and Krim**: lived in the **east and south-east**, near the present borders with Guinea and Liberia.
4. **Temne**: their traditions say they came from the **Futa Jallon** area to the north. They were already established in the north-west when the Portuguese arrived in the fifteenth century.

## The Mane invasions

From about **1545** to the **1560s**, Sierra Leone was invaded by the **Mane**, Mande-speaking warriors who came from the **south-east**, through present-day Liberia. Portuguese writers described them as fierce fighters; tradition says they were led at first by a woman, **Macarico**.

**Effects of the Mane invasions:**

1. The Mane **conquered the coastal Bullom and Temne** and set up new **ruling families** over them.
2. They brought **Mande language and customs**, and many coastal peoples adopted Mande titles and practices.
3. They introduced better **iron weapons** and military organisation.
4. Some of their followers settled and mixed with local people; traditions link the **Loko**, **Kono** and **Vai** with these movements.
5. War and disorder caused **loss of life** and supplied captives for the early Portuguese slave trade.

## Later migrations

| People | Where they came from | When and why |
|---|---|---|
| **Mende** | the **south-east**, from Mande-speaking lands | moved in during the **seventeenth and eighteenth centuries**; became the largest group in the south and east |
| **Susu** and **Yalunka** | **Futa Jallon** (Guinea) | from the **eighteenth century**, partly pushed by the **Fula jihad**; settled in the **north-west** (Kambia) and **north-east** (Falaba) |
| **Koranko** | the **upper Niger** region of Guinea | settled in the **north-east** (Koinadugu) |
| **Fula** | Futa Jallon | came as **traders, cattle herders and Muslim teachers** from the eighteenth century |
| **Mandinka** | the Western Sudan | came as **traders (Dyula)** and **Muslim clerics**, spreading Islam |
| **Krio** | descendants of settlers and Liberated Africans | formed in **Freetown** from **1787** to the nineteenth century |

## The ethnic groups today

Sierra Leone is usually said to have about **sixteen ethnic groups**. They belong to two main language families, plus Krio:

| Mande languages | Atlantic languages | Creole |
|---|---|---|
| **Mende**, **Kono**, **Vai**, **Loko**, **Koranko**, **Susu**, **Yalunka**, **Mandinka** | **Temne**, **Limba**, **Bullom/Sherbro**, **Kissi**, **Gola**, **Krim**, **Fula** | **Krio**, an English-based creole that is now the **common language (lingua franca)** of Sierra Leone |

The **Mende** and **Temne** are the two largest groups, each making up roughly a third of the population.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many Mende farmers who found **nomoli** figures in their fields kept them as protectors of the farm and called them "**rice gods**", sometimes "punishing" them if the harvest was poor. This shows how later peoples gave new meaning to the objects left by the land's earlier inhabitants.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not write that all of Sierra Leone's peoples arrived at the same time or came from one place. The country was settled by **many waves of people** over hundreds of years, from the early Limba and Bullom to the Mane, the Mende and the northern Mande and Fula peoples.
:::

## Key points

- Early settlement is shown by **Yengema**, **Kamabai**, **nomoli**, **Sapi ivories**, oral traditions and Portuguese accounts.
- Earliest peoples: **Limba, Bullom/Sherbro, Kissi, Gola, Krim**, and the **Temne** from Futa Jallon.
- **Mane invasions** (from about **1545**): new ruling families, Mande influence, iron weapons, warfare.
- Later arrivals: **Mende** (south-east), **Susu, Yalunka, Fula** (Futa Jallon), **Koranko**, **Mandinka**; **Krio** formed in Freetown.
- About **sixteen ethnic groups** in **Mande** and **Atlantic** language families; **Krio** is the lingua franca.
