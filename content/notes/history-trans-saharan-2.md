**Islam** spread into West Africa mainly **peacefully**, carried by **North African and Berber traders** along the trans-Saharan routes from about the **8th century**, then by **West African merchants** such as the **Wangara (Dyula)**, by **clerics and scholars**, and by the **conversion of rulers**, who valued literate Muslim advisers and links with the Islamic world. Later, **jihads** spread it by force in some areas. Islam brought **literacy in Arabic**, **Islamic law and learning**, new **architecture**, and closer ties with North Africa and the Middle East. This lesson explains how Islam spread in West Africa and its effects, including in Sierra Leone.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the agents and methods by which Islam spread in West Africa
- explain why West African rulers accepted Islam
- describe the effects of Islam on West African societies
- explain how Islam reached Sierra Leone
- describe the role of Timbuktu as a centre of learning

## How Islam spread

| Agent | How it spread Islam |
|---|---|
| **Traders** | North African and Berber merchants, and later West African traders such as the **Wangara (Dyula)**, settled in trading towns and converted people they dealt with |
| **Clerics and scholars (marabouts)** | taught the Quran, made amulets, advised rulers, opened Quranic schools |
| **Rulers** | when a king converted, many courtiers and townspeople followed |
| **Pilgrimage** | pilgrims returning from Mecca brought new learning and zeal |
| **Intermarriage** | Muslim traders married local women; children were raised as Muslims |
| **Jihads** | holy wars, such as the **Almoravid** movement in the 11th century and the **19th-century jihads**, spread Islam by force |

## Why rulers accepted Islam

1. Muslim **scribes** could keep records and write letters in Arabic.
2. Islam linked rulers to the **trans-Saharan trade** and the wider Muslim world.
3. Islamic **law** and organisation helped to govern large empires.
4. **Prestige**: pilgrimage to Mecca brought international recognition (Mansa Musa, Askia Muhammad).
5. Many rulers combined Islam with **traditional religion**, keeping the loyalty of non-Muslim subjects.

## Effects of Islam

| Area | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Learning** | **literacy in Arabic**; Quranic schools; centres of learning at **Timbuktu** (Sankore), Djenné and Gao; written chronicles such as the **Tarikh al-Sudan** |
| **Government** | literate officials; **Islamic law (Sharia)** used in courts; ideas of a Muslim state |
| **Trade** | shared religion and law built **trust and credit** among merchants |
| **Architecture** | **Sudano-Sahelian mud-brick mosques**, such as the **Djinguereber Mosque** in Timbuktu and the **Great Mosque of Djenné** |
| **Society** | new festivals, dress, names, diet; Islamic marriage and inheritance customs |
| **Foreign relations** | diplomatic and trade ties with North Africa and Egypt |
| **Conflict** | tension between Muslims and followers of traditional religions; later, jihads |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Timbuktu as a centre of learning**

By the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, **Timbuktu** had many Quranic schools and scholars at mosques such as **Sankore**. Scholars studied the Quran, law, grammar, astronomy and medicine, and copied thousands of **manuscripts**, many of which survive today. Its fame drew students from across West Africa, showing how the trans-Saharan trade spread ideas as well as goods.
:::

## Islam in Sierra Leone

:::callout{type=local-example}
Islam reached Sierra Leone mainly through **Fula (Fulani)** and **Mandingo (Mandinka)** traders and clerics from the north, especially from the **Fouta Djallon** in present-day Guinea, from the **seventeenth and eighteenth centuries**. Muslim traders settled in northern towns, married into local families and served as advisers to chiefs. The **Susu**, **Fula**, **Mandingo** and many **Temne** became Muslim, and Islam later spread widely. Today Muslims form the **majority** of Sierra Leone's population, living peacefully alongside a large Christian community.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not say Islam spread in West Africa mainly by war. For most of its history it spread **peacefully** through **trade, teaching and the conversion of rulers**; jihads came later and affected particular regions.
:::

## Key points

- Agents: **traders (Berbers, Wangara), clerics, rulers, pilgrims, intermarriage, jihads**.
- Rulers accepted Islam for **literate officials, trade links, law and prestige**.
- Effects: **Arabic literacy, Timbuktu's scholarship, Sharia, trade trust, mud-brick mosques, social change**.
- Islam reached **Sierra Leone** through **Fula and Mandingo** traders and clerics from the **Fouta Djallon**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **explain how Islam spread in West Africa** and to **discuss its effects** on West African states and societies. Questions sometimes focus on **Timbuktu** or on the **role of traders and clerics**.
:::

## Quick check

1. Name three agents who spread Islam in West Africa.
2. Give two reasons why rulers accepted Islam.
3. Give three effects of Islam on West Africa.
4. Which peoples brought Islam to Sierra Leone?
5. Name one famous mosque in Timbuktu.

**Answers:**

1. Any three: traders; clerics and scholars; rulers; pilgrims; intermarriage; jihad leaders.
2. Any two: literate scribes; trade links; Islamic law for government; prestige and international recognition.
3. Any three: Arabic literacy; centres of learning; Islamic law; trust among traders; new architecture; social changes; ties with North Africa.
4. The Fula (Fulani) and Mandingo (Mandinka), especially from the Fouta Djallon.
5. The Djinguereber Mosque (or the Sankore Mosque).
