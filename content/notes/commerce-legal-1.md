Buying bread, hiring a taxi, ordering goods from a wholesaler and renting a shop are all **contracts**. Most are made without a written word, yet the law will enforce them if something goes wrong. Business depends on contracts, so every trader needs to know when an agreement is legally binding and what happens when it is broken.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a contract and explain its essential elements
- distinguish an offer from an invitation to treat
- explain how an offer ends
- describe the types of contract: valid, void, voidable, unenforceable and illegal
- explain how contracts are discharged and the remedies for breach

## What is a contract?

:::callout{type=definition}
A **contract** is a **legally binding agreement** between two or more parties, which the courts will enforce.
:::

All contracts are agreements, but not all agreements are contracts. An agreement to meet a friend is not a contract, because there is no intention to create legal relations.

## Essential elements of a valid contract

![The elements of a valid contract: offer and acceptance, consideration, intention to create legal relations, capacity, legality and genuine consent](https://wasscetutor.com/img/commerce/contract-elements.svg)

*Figure 1. The elements of a valid contract. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Element | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Offer** | A definite promise by one party (the **offeror**) to be bound on stated terms | "I will sell you my generator for NLe 3,000" |
| **Acceptance** | **Unconditional** agreement to all the terms of the offer, **communicated** to the offeror | "I accept" |
| **Consideration** | Something of value given by each side: price, goods, services or a promise | The NLe 3,000 and the generator |
| **Intention to create legal relations** | Both parties mean the agreement to be legally binding; presumed in business deals | Business agreements, unlike most family or social arrangements |
| **Capacity** | Both parties must be legally able to contract | Minors, people of unsound mind and drunk people have limited capacity |
| **Legality** | The purpose must be **lawful** | A contract to sell stolen goods or drugs is illegal |
| **Genuine consent** | Agreement must be freely given, without **misrepresentation, mistake, duress or undue influence** | A contract signed under threat is not genuine |
| **Certainty and possibility** | The terms must be clear, and performance possible | "Some goods at a fair price" is too vague |

### Offer and invitation to treat

An **invitation to treat** is an invitation to others to **make an offer**. It is **not** itself an offer.

| Invitation to treat (not an offer) | Offer |
|---|---|
| Goods displayed in a shop window or on shelves with price tags | The customer offering to buy at the till |
| Advertisements, catalogues and price lists | A specific promise to a specific person |
| An auctioneer asking for bids | The **bid** is the offer; the fall of the hammer is acceptance |

So a shopkeeper who has mistakenly priced goods too low **need not sell** at that price: the price tag is only an invitation to treat.

### How an offer ends

1. **Acceptance**: it becomes a contract.
2. **Rejection** by the offeree.
3. **Counter-offer**: a new offer on different terms rejects the original.
4. **Revocation (withdrawal)** by the offeror **before** acceptance, communicated to the offeree.
5. **Lapse of time**: a stated time, or a reasonable time, has passed.
6. **Death** of either party before acceptance.
7. **Failure of a condition** on which the offer depended.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Is there a contract?**

1. Fatmata offers to sell her phone to Ibrahim for NLe 1,200. Ibrahim replies, "I'll give you NLe 1,000." Fatmata refuses. Ibrahim then says, "All right, I accept NLe 1,200." **No contract yet.** Ibrahim's counter-offer ended the first offer, so his "acceptance" is now a **new offer**, which Fatmata may accept or refuse.
2. A shop displays a radio priced NLe 50 by mistake (it should be NLe 500). A customer insists on buying at NLe 50. **No contract.** The display is an **invitation to treat**; the customer's offer to pay NLe 50 can be refused.
3. A 15-year-old buys a necessary school uniform on credit. **Binding.** Minors are bound by contracts for **necessaries** at a reasonable price.
:::

## Types of contract

### By form

- **Simple contracts**: oral, written or implied by conduct. Most everyday contracts are simple.
- **Specialty contracts (deeds)**: written, signed and **sealed**. Examples are the sale of land and long leases.
- **Express** contracts state their terms in words; **implied** contracts arise from conduct, as when a passenger boards a taxi.

### By validity

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Valid** | Has all the essential elements; fully enforceable | A normal sale of goods |
| **Void** | Has **no legal effect** at all from the start | A contract with a person of unsound mind; a contract about goods that no longer existed |
| **Voidable** | Valid until the injured party chooses to **cancel** it | A contract induced by misrepresentation or duress |
| **Unenforceable** | Valid but cannot be enforced in court because of a technical defect | A contract that needed to be in writing but was not |
| **Illegal** | Its purpose is against the law; void, and the parties may be punished | Smuggling, selling stolen goods |

## Discharge of contract

A contract ends (is **discharged**) by:

1. **Performance**: both sides do what they promised. This is the most common way.
2. **Agreement**: both sides agree to end it.
3. **Frustration**: an event beyond either party's control makes performance **impossible**, such as destruction of the subject matter, death or a change in the law.
4. **Breach**: one party fails to perform, or refuses to.
5. **Operation of law**: bankruptcy, or the expiry of the time limit for claims.

## Remedies for breach of contract

| Remedy | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Damages** | Money compensation for the loss caused; the usual remedy |
| **Specific performance** | A court order to **carry out** the contract, used where money is not enough (e.g. sale of a particular piece of land) |
| **Injunction** | A court order **stopping** a party from doing something in breach of contract |
| **Rescission** | Cancelling the contract and restoring both parties to their original positions |
| **Quantum meruit** | Payment for work already done ("as much as it is worth") |
| **Refusal to perform** | The injured party may refuse to carry out its own side |

:::callout{type=local-example}
A contractor agrees to build a shop in Makeni by December for NLe 200,000 but stops halfway. The owner can claim **damages** for extra costs and lost rent, and the contractor may claim **quantum meruit** for the work already done. If the land were flooded and the site washed away, the contract might be **frustrated**, and neither side would be liable.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A **void** contract never had legal effect; a **voidable** contract is valid **until** the injured party cancels it. Do not use the words interchangeably.
:::

## Key points

- A **contract** is a legally binding agreement.
- Elements: **offer and acceptance, consideration, intention, capacity, legality, genuine consent, certainty**.
- Shop displays, adverts and catalogues are **invitations to treat**, not offers.
- Contracts may be **valid, void, voidable, unenforceable or illegal**.
- Discharge: **performance, agreement, frustration, breach, operation of law**. Remedies: **damages, specific performance, injunction, rescission, quantum meruit**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **explain the essential elements of a valid contract**, to **distinguish an offer from an invitation to treat**, to **distinguish void from voidable contracts**, and to **state ways a contract may be discharged** and **remedies for breach**. Short scenarios ("Is there a contract?") are common: name the rule and apply it.
:::

## Quick check

1. Give four essential elements of a valid contract.
2. Is a price tag in a shop window an offer? Explain.
3. What is frustration of contract?
4. Name two remedies for breach of contract.

**Answers:** 1. Any four: offer, acceptance, consideration, intention to create legal relations, capacity, legality, genuine consent, certainty. 2. No. It is an invitation to treat; the customer makes the offer, which the seller may accept or refuse. 3. The ending of a contract because an event beyond the parties' control makes performance impossible. 4. Any two: damages, specific performance, injunction, rescission, quantum meruit.
