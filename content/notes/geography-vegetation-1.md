**Natural vegetation** is the plant cover that grows in an area without human interference. The main **types of natural vegetation** are **forests** (such as the tropical rainforest), **grasslands** (such as the savanna), **desert vegetation**, **mangrove swamps** and **mountain (montane) vegetation**. Which type grows where depends mainly on climate, especially rainfall and temperature, and also on soil, relief and human activity. This lesson describes each type, its features and how plants adapt to their environment.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define natural vegetation and explain the factors that influence it
- describe the characteristics of the tropical rainforest, including its layers
- describe savanna vegetation and its adaptations to the dry season
- describe desert, mangrove and montane vegetation
- compare the main vegetation types

## Factors influencing vegetation

1. **Climate:** rainfall and temperature are the most important; heavy rain all year gives forest, seasonal rain gives grassland, little rain gives desert scrub.
2. **Soil:** fertile, deep, well-drained soils support dense vegetation; waterlogged or salty soils support special plants such as mangroves.
3. **Relief and altitude:** vegetation changes with height on mountains as temperature falls.
4. **Drainage:** swamps and floodplains have their own vegetation.
5. **Human activities:** farming, logging, bush burning and grazing replace natural vegetation with secondary growth or grassland.
6. **Animals:** grazing and browsing shape grasslands.

## Tropical rainforest

![Layers of the tropical rainforest: scattered emergent trees over 40 m, a continuous canopy at about 25 to 35 m, an under-canopy, a shrub layer and a dark forest floor of leaf litter, with lianas](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/rainforest-layers.svg)

*Figure 1. The layers (stratification) of the tropical rainforest. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

**Location:** equatorial and tropical regions with heavy rain most of the year: the Congo Basin, coastal West Africa, the Amazon, South-East Asia.

**Characteristics:**

1. **Evergreen:** trees shed leaves at different times, so the forest is always green.
2. **Layered (stratified):** emergents, canopy, under-canopy, shrub layer and forest floor.
3. **Tall trees** (30–50 m) with straight trunks and **buttress roots** for support in shallow soils.
4. **Great variety of species**: many different trees in a small area, rather than pure stands.
5. **Hardwoods** such as **mahogany, iroko, obeche, sapele and ebony**.
6. **Lianas** (woody climbers) and **epiphytes** (plants growing on trees).
7. **Little undergrowth**, because the dense canopy blocks sunlight from the floor.
8. **Thick leaf litter** that decays quickly in the heat and humidity.

## Savanna

**Location:** tropical areas with a distinct wet and dry season, between the rainforest and the deserts: much of West, East and Southern Africa.

**Characteristics:**

1. **Grass** is the main vegetation: tall (up to 3 m) in the wetter Guinea savanna, shorter in the drier Sudan and Sahel savanna.
2. **Scattered trees** such as the **baobab, shea, locust bean, acacia** and **palms**.
3. Grass is **green in the rainy season** and **dries up in the dry season**.

**Adaptations of savanna trees to drought:**

- **long, deep roots** to reach groundwater
- **thick, fire-resistant bark** to survive bush fires
- **shedding leaves** in the dry season to reduce water loss (deciduous)
- **small or thorny leaves**, and **umbrella-shaped crowns**
- **water storage** in swollen trunks (the baobab)

## Desert vegetation

Plants in hot deserts are **xerophytes**, adapted to extreme drought: long roots, thick waxy skins, spines instead of leaves (cacti), water-storing stems, and seeds that lie dormant for years until rain falls. **Date palms** grow at **oases**, where groundwater reaches the surface.

## Mangrove swamps

**Mangroves** grow on **muddy, tidal coasts, estuaries and creeks** in the tropics.

- **Stilt (prop) roots** anchor the trees in soft mud and tidal water.
- **Breathing roots (pneumatophores)** stick up out of the mud to take in air.
- They **tolerate salt water**.
- They protect coasts from erosion and are **breeding grounds for fish, crabs and oysters**.

## Montane (mountain) vegetation

On high mountains, vegetation changes with altitude, as temperature falls: forest on the lower slopes, then grassland, then low shrubs and mosses near the summit. On Mount Kilimanjaro and Mount Cameroon, distinct belts of vegetation can be seen from base to top.

## The main types compared

| | Rainforest | Savanna | Desert | Mangrove |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rainfall** | over 1,500–2,000 mm, most months | 500–1,500 mm, seasonal | under 250 mm | coastal; tidal water |
| **Main plants** | tall evergreen hardwoods | grass with scattered trees | xerophytes, scrub | salt-tolerant trees |
| **Key features** | layers, buttress roots, lianas | deep roots, thick bark, deciduous trees | spines, water storage | stilt and breathing roots |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone has examples of nearly all of West Africa's lowland vegetation types: **mangroves** along the coast and estuaries (the Sierra Leone River, Sherbro and Scarcies); **rainforest** in the south and east, best preserved in the **Gola Rainforest National Park**; **secondary forest and farm bush** across much of the country where forest has been cleared for farming; and **savanna** in the north, including the grasslands around **Outamba-Kilimi National Park**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Rainforest trees are tall and the forest is dense, but its **floor has little undergrowth**, because so little light gets through the canopy. Dense, tangled undergrowth is typical of **secondary forest** that has grown back after clearing.
:::

## Key points

- Vegetation depends on **climate**, soil, relief, drainage, human activity and animals.
- **Rainforest:** evergreen, layered, tall hardwoods, buttress roots, lianas, little undergrowth.
- **Savanna:** grass with scattered trees; deep roots, thick bark, deciduous trees adapted to the dry season.
- **Desert:** xerophytes and oasis palms. **Mangrove:** stilt and breathing roots on tidal coasts.
- **Montane** vegetation changes with altitude.

## Quick check

1. Define natural vegetation.
2. Give three characteristics of the tropical rainforest.
3. How are savanna trees adapted to the dry season?
4. What are pneumatophores?
5. Name a national park in Sierra Leone that protects rainforest.

**Answers:**

1. The plant cover that grows in an area without human interference.
2. Any three: evergreen; layered; tall trees with buttress roots; many species; hardwoods; lianas and epiphytes; little undergrowth.
3. Deep roots, thick bark, shedding leaves, small or thorny leaves, umbrella crowns, water storage (baobab).
4. Breathing roots of mangroves that stick up out of the mud to take in air.
5. Gola Rainforest National Park.
