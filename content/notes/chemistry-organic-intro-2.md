Butane and 2-methylpropane share one formula, C₄H₁₀, yet boil at different temperatures. To talk about organic compounds clearly, chemists need exact **names**, and they need to recognise when the same formula hides different structures (**isomers**). This lesson covers IUPAC naming, isomerism, and how organic compounds are purified and analysed.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name straight-chain and branched alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, alkanols and acids by IUPAC rules
- draw structures from IUPAC names
- define isomerism and give examples of chain, position and functional group isomers
- describe geometric (cis–trans) isomerism in alkenes
- describe methods of purifying organic compounds and tests for their purity
- describe how carbon and hydrogen are detected and how formulas are found

## IUPAC naming

**1. The stem** gives the number of carbons in the **longest chain**:

| Carbons | Stem | Carbons | Stem |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **meth-** | 6 | **hex-** |
| 2 | **eth-** | 7 | **hept-** |
| 3 | **prop-** | 8 | **oct-** |
| 4 | **but-** | 9 | **non-** |
| 5 | **pent-** | 10 | **dec-** |

**2. The suffix** gives the functional group:

| Series | Suffix | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Alkane | **-ane** | propane, CH₃CH₂CH₃ |
| Alkene | **-ene** | propene, CH₃CH=CH₂ |
| Alkyne | **-yne** | propyne, CH₃C≡CH |
| Alkanol | **-ol** | propan-1-ol, CH₃CH₂CH₂OH |
| Alkanoic acid | **-oic acid** | propanoic acid, CH₃CH₂COOH |
| Ester | **-yl … -oate** | ethyl ethanoate, CH₃COOC₂H₅ |

**3. Prefixes** name the side chains and substituents: **methyl** (–CH₃), **ethyl** (–C₂H₅), **chloro** (–Cl), **bromo** (–Br). Use **di-, tri-, tetra-** for two, three or four of the same group.

**4. Numbering:** number the longest chain from the end that gives the **functional group** (or the first branch) the **lowest number**. Put locants before the part they describe, separated by hyphens; separate numbers with commas.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Naming compounds.**

| Structure | Name | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃ | **2-methylpropane** | Longest chain 3 C (propane); methyl on C2 |
| CH₃CH=CHCH₃ | **but-2-ene** | 4 C; double bond starts at C2 |
| CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ | **but-1-ene** | Number from the end nearer the double bond |
| CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ | **butan-2-ol** | –OH on C2 (counting from the nearer end) |
| CH₃CHClCH₃ | **2-chloropropane** | Cl on C2 |
| CH₃C(CH₃)₂CH₃ | **2,2-dimethylpropane** | Two methyl groups, both on C2 |
:::

## Isomerism

:::callout{type=definition}
**Isomers** are compounds with the **same molecular formula** but **different structures**. Isomers have different physical properties and sometimes different chemical properties.
:::

![Chain isomers butane and 2-methylpropane; position isomers but-1-ene and but-2-ene; functional group isomers ethanol and methoxymethane](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/isomers.svg)

*Figure 1. Three kinds of structural isomerism. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Type | Difference | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Chain isomerism** | Different carbon skeleton (straight or branched) | Butane and 2-methylpropane (C₄H₁₀) |
| **Position isomerism** | Same skeleton, functional group in a different position | But-1-ene and but-2-ene; propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol |
| **Functional group isomerism** | Different functional groups | Ethanol (an alkanol) and methoxymethane (an ether), C₂H₆O |

The number of isomers grows quickly with carbon number: C₄H₁₀ has **2**, C₅H₁₂ has **3** (pentane, 2-methylbutane and 2,2-dimethylpropane), and C₁₀H₂₂ has 75.

**Geometric (cis–trans) isomerism.** Rotation about a C=C double bond is **not possible**. So but-2-ene exists as two forms: **cis** (both CH₃ groups on the same side) and **trans** (on opposite sides). They have different boiling points.

## Purifying organic compounds

| Method | Use |
|---|---|
| **Distillation** | Separating a liquid from non-volatile impurities |
| **Fractional distillation** | Separating liquids with close boiling points (crude oil; ethanol from water) |
| **Crystallisation** | Purifying solids (e.g. benzoic acid from hot water) |
| **Sublimation** | Purifying solids that sublime (naphthalene) |
| **Solvent extraction** | Using a separating funnel and an immiscible solvent |
| **Chromatography** | Separating mixtures of dyes, sugars or amino acids |

**Tests for purity:** a pure organic solid has a **sharp melting point**; a pure liquid has a **constant boiling point**; a pure substance gives **one spot** on a chromatogram.

## Analysing organic compounds

- **Carbon and hydrogen:** heat the compound with dry **copper(II) oxide**. Carbon is oxidised to CO₂ (turns lime water milky) and hydrogen to water (turns anhydrous CuSO₄ blue).
- **Empirical formula:** from the masses of CO₂ and water produced (see the Empirical and Molecular Formulas topic).
- **Molecular formula:** from the empirical formula and the relative molecular mass (from vapour density or a mass spectrometer).

:::callout{type=local-example}
Cooking gas sold in cylinders in Sierra Leone is liquefied **propane and butane**, which come from crude-oil refining and natural gas. The "butane" in the mixture is itself a mixture of **two isomers**, butane and 2-methylpropane. Each burns to give the same products, but they have slightly different boiling points.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Number the chain from the end that gives the functional group the **lowest** number. CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ is **but-1-ene**, not but-3-ene.
:::

## Key points

- IUPAC name = **prefix (substituents) + stem (chain length) + suffix (functional group)**, with locants as low as possible.
- **Isomers** have the same molecular formula but different structures: **chain, position and functional group** isomers.
- **Cis–trans isomerism** arises because a C=C bond cannot rotate.
- Organic compounds are purified by **distillation, fractional distillation, crystallisation, sublimation, solvent extraction and chromatography**.
- **C and H** are detected by heating with CuO (CO₂ and H₂O); formulas come from combustion data and Mᵣ.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **name compounds from their structures** and **draw structures from names**, to **define isomerism** and **draw isomers** of C₄H₁₀, C₅H₁₂ or C₄H₈, and to **describe purification methods**. Isomer questions often ask "how many isomers" and want each one drawn and named.
:::

## Quick check

1. Name CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH.
2. Draw (in condensed form) and name the isomers of C₅H₁₂.
3. What type of isomerism do propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol show?
4. How would you show that an organic compound contains carbon and hydrogen?

**Answers:**

1. Butan-1-ol.
2. CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ (pentane); CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ (2-methylbutane); CH₃C(CH₃)₂CH₃ (2,2-dimethylpropane).
3. Position isomerism.
4. Heat it with dry copper(II) oxide: CO₂ turns lime water milky (carbon) and water turns anhydrous CuSO₄ blue (hydrogen).
