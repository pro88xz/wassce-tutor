# Equations of motion: constant acceleration in a straight line

## Learning objectives
You should be able to define displacement, velocity and acceleration; distinguish speed from velocity; select equations for uniformly accelerated motion; and solve numerical problems with consistent units.

## Describing motion
Distance is the total length of the path travelled and is a scalar. Displacement is the change in position in a specified direction and is a vector. Speed is distance travelled per unit time; velocity is displacement per unit time. Average speed and average velocity are therefore not always equal.

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity:

(a=\frac{v-u}{t})

where (u) is initial velocity, (v) is final velocity and (t) is elapsed time. Acceleration is a vector. If an object slows while moving in the positive direction, its acceleration is negative relative to that chosen direction.

## Equations for uniform acceleration
For motion in a straight line with constant acceleration, the following equations apply:

(v=u+at)

(s=ut+\frac{1}{2}at^2)

(v^2=u^2+2as)

(s=\frac{(u+v)}{2}t)

Here, (s) is displacement. These equations should not be used without checking that acceleration is constant. They are derived from the definitions of acceleration and average velocity under uniform acceleration.

## Worked example
A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at (2.0\,m,s^{-2}) for 6.0 s. Find its final velocity and displacement.

Given: (u=0\,m,s^{-1}), (a=2.0\,m,s^{-2}), (t=6.0\,s).

Final velocity:

(v=u+at=0+(2.0)(6.0)=12\,m\,s^{-1}).

Displacement:

(s=ut+\frac12at^2=0+(0.5)(2.0)(6.0^2)=36\,m).

The result is reasonable: the average velocity is 6 m/s, and (6\times6=36) m.

## Graphical interpretation
On a displacement–time graph, the gradient represents velocity. On a velocity–time graph, the gradient represents acceleration and the signed area under the graph represents displacement. A horizontal velocity–time line means constant velocity and zero acceleration. A straight sloping velocity–time line indicates constant acceleration.

## Problem-solving method
Choose a positive direction and keep signs consistent. List known quantities with units, identify the unknown, select an equation containing those quantities, substitute carefully and check the unit of the answer. Convert kilometres per hour to metres per second by multiplying by (5/18), and metres per second to kilometres per hour by multiplying by (18/5).

## WASSCE examination focus
State the equation used and show substitution. Do not confuse distance with displacement or speed with velocity. For a graph question, identify the physical meaning of gradient and area before calculating.

## Check your understanding
1. Define acceleration.
2. A cyclist changes velocity from 4 m/s to 16 m/s in 3 s. Find the acceleration.
3. A body starts from rest and accelerates at 3 m/s² for 4 s. Find its final velocity.
4. For the same body, find its displacement.
5. What does the area under a velocity–time graph represent?

## Answers for self-assessment
1. Rate of change of velocity with time.
2. ((16-4)/3=4\,m\,s^{-2}).
3. (v=0+3(4)=12\,m\,s^{-1}).
4. (s=0+\frac12(3)(4^2)=24\,m).
5. Displacement.
