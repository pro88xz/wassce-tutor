On Saturday a nurse works an extra shift at a private clinic and is paid for it. On Sunday she cooks for her family, helps clean the church and teaches her younger brother to read. She is busy all weekend, but only Saturday's work is an **economic activity**. The difference between economic and non-economic activities is the starting point for studying business.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define economic and non-economic activities
- give examples of each from everyday life in Sierra Leone
- state the differences between them
- classify economic activities as business activities or professions and employment

## Economic activities

:::callout{type=definition}
**Economic activities** are activities carried out to earn money or produce goods and services for sale, in order to make a living.
:::

They include:

- **Business**: producing or trading goods and services for profit, such as farming for the market, trading, transport and manufacturing.
- **Employment**: working for someone else in return for a **wage** or **salary**, such as a factory worker, a bank clerk or a teacher.
- **Profession**: providing a specialised service that needs long training and is controlled by a professional body, in return for a **fee**, such as a doctor, lawyer or accountant in private practice.

## Non-economic activities

:::callout{type=definition}
**Non-economic activities** are activities done for love, duty, religion, social reasons or personal satisfaction, without the aim of earning money.
:::

Examples:

- a mother cooking for her family
- helping neighbours clear a farm in a communal work group without pay
- worship and church or mosque work
- volunteering in a community clean-up
- playing football for fun

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The **same task** can be economic or non-economic. Cooking for your family is non-economic; cooking at a restaurant for wages, or selling cooked food, is economic. Look at the **purpose**: is the person earning a living?
:::

## Differences between economic and non-economic activities

| Basis | Economic activities | Non-economic activities |
|---|---|---|
| **Aim** | To earn money or make a profit | Love, duty, religion or personal satisfaction |
| **Reward** | Profit, wages, salary or fees | No money reward; satisfaction or goodwill |
| **Measurement** | Can be measured in money and counted in national income | Not measured in money; not in national income |
| **Legal ties** | Often involve contracts and taxes | Usually no contract or tax |
| **Examples** | Trading, farming for sale, teaching for a salary | Cooking for family, volunteering, worship |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Classify each activity.**

1. A carpenter makes chairs and sells them at Bo market. **Economic (business)**
2. A girl helps her grandmother fetch water. **Non-economic**
3. A lawyer charges a client a fee for drafting a will. **Economic (profession)**
4. A driver is paid a monthly salary by a mining company. **Economic (employment)**
5. A youth group cleans the drains in its community free of charge. **Non-economic**
:::

## Why the difference matters

Only economic activities are counted when a country measures its **national income** and output. A great deal of valuable work in Sierra Leone, such as farming for home consumption and caring for children, is non-economic and is therefore left out of the official figures. Knowing the difference also helps us understand where business fits: business is one kind of economic activity, alongside employment and the professions.

:::callout{type=local-example}
In many Sierra Leonean villages, families farm partly to feed themselves and partly to sell. The rice eaten at home is not sold, so it is non-economic in the business sense; the bags taken to the weekly market and sold are economic activity.
:::

## Key points

- **Economic activities** aim to earn a living: business (profit), employment (wages and salaries) and professions (fees).
- **Non-economic activities** are done for love, duty, religion or satisfaction, with no money reward.
- The same task can be either; the **purpose** decides.
- Economic activities are measured in money and counted in national income; non-economic ones are not.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
WASSCE asks you to **distinguish between economic and non-economic activities** and to **give examples**. A comparison table with three or four clear bases (aim, reward, measurement, examples) earns marks quickly. You may also be asked to classify economic activities into business, employment and professions.
:::
