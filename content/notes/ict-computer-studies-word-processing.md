:::callout{type=definition}
**Word processing** is the use of a computer program, called a **word processor**, to create, edit, format, save and print text documents such as letters, reports, CVs and notices. Microsoft Word, Google Docs and LibreOffice Writer are word processors.
:::

Almost every typed document you see, a school report, a letter of application, a church programme, was made with a word processor. This lesson describes the **parts of a word processor window**, the **features of word processing software**, how to edit and format text, paragraphs and pages, and useful tools such as spelling check, tables, find and replace and **mail merge**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define word processing and name examples of word processors
- state the advantages of a word processor over a typewriter
- identify the parts of a word processor window
- describe editing tasks: inserting, deleting, copying, moving, finding and replacing text
- format characters, paragraphs and pages
- explain features such as tables, headers and footers, spelling check and mail merge

## Word processors and their uses

| Word processor | Made by |
|---|---|
| **Microsoft Word** | Microsoft (part of Microsoft Office) |
| **Google Docs** | Google (works in a web browser) |
| **LibreOffice Writer** | open-source, free |
| **WPS Writer** | Kingsoft (common on Android phones) |
| **WordPad**, **Notepad** | simple editors that come with Windows |

**Uses:** letters, reports, CVs, assignments, minutes of meetings, notices, question papers, newsletters and books.

### Advantages of a word processor over a typewriter

- mistakes are **corrected on screen** before printing, with no correction fluid
- text can be **edited, moved and copied** without retyping
- documents can be **saved** and used again, and **many copies printed**
- **spelling and grammar** can be checked automatically
- text can be **formatted** with fonts, sizes, colours, tables and pictures
- the same letter can be sent to many people with **mail merge**

## The word processor window

:::callout{type=definition}
**Insertion point (cursor):** the flashing vertical line that shows where the next character typed will appear.

**Word wrap:** the automatic moving of a word that does not fit at the end of a line onto the next line, so the user does not press Enter at the end of each line.

**Ribbon (toolbar):** the band of command buttons, grouped under tabs, across the top of the window.
:::

## Editing text

- **Insert mode** puts new text in at the cursor, pushing existing text along; **overtype mode** types over existing text. The **Insert** key switches between them.
- **Backspace** deletes to the left of the cursor; **Delete** deletes to the right.
- **Select** text by dragging over it, double-clicking a word or pressing **Ctrl + A** for all.
- **Copy and paste** (Ctrl + C, Ctrl + V) repeats text; **cut and paste** (Ctrl + X, Ctrl + V) moves it.
- **Undo** (Ctrl + Z) reverses the last action; **Redo** (Ctrl + Y) puts it back.
- **Find** (Ctrl + F) locates a word; **Find and Replace** (Ctrl + H) changes every "Freetown" to "Bo", say, in one step.

## Formatting

:::callout{type=definition}
**Formatting:** changing the appearance of text and of the page, such as the font, size, style, alignment, spacing and margins, without changing the words themselves.

**Font:** a set of characters of one design, such as Arial, Times New Roman or Calibri.
:::

### Character formatting

- **Font** (typeface) and **font size**, measured in **points** (12 pt is normal body text).
- **Bold** (Ctrl + B), *italic* (Ctrl + I) and underline (Ctrl + U).
- **Font colour**, highlight, subscript (H₂O) and superscript (x²).
- **Change case**: UPPERCASE, lowercase, Sentence case, Capitalise Each Word.

### Paragraph formatting

- **Alignment:**

| Alignment | Shortcut | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Left** | Ctrl + L | lines start evenly at the left margin; the right edge is ragged |
| **Centre** | Ctrl + E | lines are centred, as in a title |
| **Right** | Ctrl + R | lines end evenly at the right margin, as for an address or date |
| **Justify** | Ctrl + J | lines are even at **both** margins, as in a newspaper |

- **Line spacing:** single (1.0), 1.5 or double (2.0).
- **Indentation:** moving a paragraph in from the margin; a **first-line indent** moves only the first line.
- **Bullets** (•) and **numbering** (1, 2, 3) for lists.
- **Borders and shading** around paragraphs.

### Page formatting (page layout)

- **Margins:** the blank space around the edge of the page.
- **Orientation:** **portrait** (taller than wide) or **landscape** (wider than tall).
- **Paper size:** usually **A4**.
- **Header and footer:** text repeated at the **top** (header) or **bottom** (footer) of every page, such as the school name or **page numbers**.
- **Page break** (Ctrl + Enter): starts a new page at once.
- **Columns:** text in two or more columns, as in a newsletter.

## Other features

- **Tables:** rows and columns for timetables and lists (Insert → Table).
- **Pictures, shapes, WordArt, charts** and **symbols** (Insert tab).
- **Spelling and grammar check** (F7): spelling mistakes have a red wavy underline; grammar problems a blue or green one.
- **Thesaurus** (Shift + F7): finds words with a similar meaning.
- **Word count:** shown on the status bar.
- **Templates:** ready-made layouts for letters, CVs and certificates.
- **Print preview** (File → Print): shows how the pages will look before printing, so paper is not wasted.

### Mail merge

:::callout{type=definition}
**Mail merge:** a word-processing feature that combines a **main document** (such as a standard letter) with a **data source** (a list of names and addresses) to produce a personalised copy for each person on the list.
:::

The **main document** contains **merge fields** such as «FirstName» and «Class». For each record in the **data source**, the word processor makes a copy with that person's details filled in.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Mail merge in a school.** The principal writes one letter inviting parents to the PTA meeting, with «ParentName» and «StudentName» fields. Mail merge with the list of 400 students produces 400 letters, each with the right names, without typing each one.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Pressing Enter at the end of every line.** Word wrap moves text to the next line by itself. Press **Enter** only to **end a paragraph**; extra Enters spoil the layout when text is edited.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Define word processing and name two word processors."}
The use of a computer program to **create, edit, format, save and print** text documents. Examples: **Microsoft Word, Google Docs, LibreOffice Writer, WPS Writer** (any two).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. State three advantages of a word processor over a typewriter."}
Any three: errors are **corrected easily** on screen; text can be **edited and moved** without retyping; documents can be **saved** and reused; **many copies** can be printed; **spelling check**; rich **formatting**; **mail merge**.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Explain the four types of text alignment."}
**Left**: even at the left margin; **centre**: in the middle of the line; **right**: even at the right margin; **justify**: even at **both** margins.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. What is the difference between a header and a footer?"}
A **header** appears at the **top** of every page; a **footer** appears at the **bottom** of every page. Both are used for items such as page numbers, the title or the date.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Name the two documents used in mail merge."}
The **main document** (the standard letter with merge fields) and the **data source** (the list of names and details).
:::

## Key points

- A **word processor** creates, edits, formats, saves and prints documents: **Word, Google Docs, LibreOffice Writer**.
- Window parts: **title bar, menu tabs, ribbon, ruler, document area, cursor, scroll bar, status bar**.
- Editing: **insert, delete, select, copy, cut, paste, undo, find and replace**.
- Formatting: **character** (font, size, bold, italic, underline), **paragraph** (alignment, spacing, indents, bullets), **page** (margins, orientation, headers and footers, page numbers).
- Tools: **tables, pictures, spelling and grammar check, thesaurus, word count, templates, print preview, mail merge**.
