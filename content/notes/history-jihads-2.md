The **Masina and Tukulor jihads** were the two great Islamic reform wars that followed the Sokoto jihad in the **Western Sudan**. **Seku Ahmadu** (Ahmad Lobbo) led a jihad from **1818** that created the strict Islamic state of **Masina**, with its capital at **Hamdullahi**. **Al-Hajj Umar Tall**, a Tukulor scholar of the **Tijaniyya** brotherhood, began a jihad in **1852** that built the **Tukulor empire**, conquering **Segou (1861)** and **Masina (1862)**, before it fell to the **French** in the 1890s. This lesson explains these jihads and the **effects of the nineteenth-century jihads on West Africa**.

## The Masina jihad: Seku Ahmadu

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figure"}
**Seku Ahmadu (Ahmad Lobbo, about 1776–1845)** was a Fulani scholar from **Masina**, the fertile land of the **inland Niger delta** in present-day Mali.
:::

He was influenced by Usman dan Fodio's success.

**Causes:**

1. **Religious:** the local Fulani rulers (**Ardos**) and the **Bambara** of Segou practised traditional religion or a mixed form of Islam.
2. **Political:** Masina was under the **Bambara kingdom of Segou**, and the Ardos ruled as its vassals.
3. **Social and economic:** the people resented **heavy taxes** and the oppression of their rulers.
4. **Example of Sokoto:** Usman's jihad showed that reform was possible.

**Course:** In **1818** Seku Ahmadu defeated the Ardos and the Bambara army. He founded a new capital, **Hamdullahi** ("praise be to God"), about **1820**, and brought **Djenne** and **Timbuktu** under his control.

**Government of Masina (the "Dina"):**

| Feature | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Grand Council** | a council of about **forty scholars** advised the ruler and checked his power |
| **Strict Sharia** | alcohol, tobacco, music and dancing were banned; women's dress and conduct were regulated |
| **Provinces** | ruled by governors appointed by the council |
| **Education** | Qur'anic schools were set up in every village |
| **Settlement of herders** | Fulani herders were organised with fixed grazing routes and seasons |

Seku Ahmadu died in **1845** and was followed by his son **Ahmadu II** and grandson **Ahmadu III**. Masina was conquered by **al-Hajj Umar in 1862**.

## The Tukulor jihad: al-Hajj Umar Tall

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figure"}
**Al-Hajj Umar Tall (about 1796–1864)** was a scholar from **Futa Toro** in the Senegal valley, of the **Tukulor** (Toucouleur) people.
:::

He made the **pilgrimage to Mecca**, where he was made **khalifa (leader) of the Tijaniyya brotherhood** for West Africa. On his way home he stayed for years at **Sokoto** with Muhammad Bello, whose daughter he married. He then settled at **Dinguiraye**, in the Futa Jallon area, where he trained and armed a large following.

**Causes:**

1. **Religious:** he wanted to spread the **Tijaniyya** and convert the "pagan" **Bambara** states of **Kaarta** and **Segou**.
2. **Political ambition:** to build a large Islamic state under his leadership.
3. **Rivalry with the Qadiriyya**, the older brotherhood of the existing Muslim leaders.
4. **The French advance** up the Senegal valley, which threatened Muslim lands.
5. **Example of earlier jihads**, especially Sokoto, which he had seen at first hand.

**Course:**

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **1852** | jihad declared from Dinguiraye |
| **1854–55** | conquest of the Bambara kingdom of **Kaarta** |
| **1857** | failed siege of the French fort at **Medina**, on the Senegal; Umar turned east, away from the French |
| **1861** | capture of **Segou**, the Bambara capital |
| **1862** | conquest of **Masina** and Hamdullahi |
| **1864** | Umar killed during a revolt in Masina, near **Bandiagara** |

**Decline of the Tukulor empire:** Umar's son **Ahmadu Seku** ruled from Segou, but the empire was weakened by:

1. **Revolts** by the conquered Bambara and the people of Masina, who resented Tukulor rule.
2. **Quarrels among Umar's sons and relatives** over power.
3. Its **huge size** and poor communications.
4. **Rivalry with Samori Toure** and other neighbours.
5. **French conquest**: the French under **Archinard** took **Segou (1890)** and **Nioro (1891)**, and Ahmadu Seku fled east; the empire was finished by **1893**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Comparing Usman dan Fodio and al-Hajj Umar**

Both were Fulani-speaking scholars who used jihad to reform Islam and build large states. But Usman's jihad began as a **revolt of the oppressed** within Hausaland, whereas Umar's was a **campaign of conquest** against neighbouring states. Usman's caliphate lasted about a century; Umar's empire was weakened by revolts within a generation and then **fell to the French**.
:::

## Effects of the nineteenth-century jihads

| Area | Effects |
|---|---|
| **Political** | creation of **large new states** (Sokoto, Masina, Tukulor); destruction of old ones (Hausa kingdoms, Bambara kingdoms, Old Oyo); a new **Fulani and Tukulor ruling class** |
| **Religious** | **spread and revival of Islam** from the towns to rural areas; growth of the **Qadiriyya** and **Tijaniyya** brotherhoods |
| **Educational** | **Qur'anic schools** and Arabic literacy spread; scholars wrote histories, poetry and law books |
| **Legal and administrative** | **Sharia** law, Islamic courts and organised taxation (zakat) |
| **Economic** | greater unity and security helped **trade** in places such as Kano; but wars destroyed farms and **slave raiding** grew |
| **Social** | loss of life and **displacement** of people; refugees fled to hills and forests; enslaved people increased |
| **On European conquest** | strong Islamic states **resisted the French and British**; but their **internal revolts and divisions** made conquest easier |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not confuse **Masina** (Seku Ahmadu, capital **Hamdullahi**) with the **Tukulor empire** (al-Hajj Umar, capital **Segou** after 1861). Masina was an independent jihad state for over forty years before Umar conquered it in 1862.
:::

## Key points

- **Masina:** jihad of **Seku Ahmadu** from **1818**; capital **Hamdullahi**; ruled by a **council of forty scholars** under strict Sharia.
- **Tukulor empire:** jihad of **al-Hajj Umar Tall** (Tijaniyya) from **1852**; took **Kaarta, Segou (1861)** and **Masina (1862)**; Umar died **1864**.
- The Tukulor empire declined through **revolts, family quarrels, size** and **French conquest (1890–93)**.
- The jihads **created large states, spread Islam and learning**, introduced **Sharia**, but also caused **wars and slave raiding**.
