The President of Sierra Leone is head of state, head of government and commander-in-chief of the armed forces, all at once. That is a great deal of power in one office. Yet the 1991 Constitution also builds fences around it: term limits, parliamentary approval, the courts and the ballot box. This lesson looks at how the executive in Sierra Leone is made up, what its members do, and how its power is controlled.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the composition of the executive in Sierra Leone
- state the qualifications, powers and tenure of the President
- explain the roles of the Vice-President, ministers, the cabinet and the Attorney-General
- explain the ways in which executive power is limited

### The President

Under **Chapter V** of the 1991 Constitution, the President is **Head of State**, the holder of **supreme executive authority** and **Commander-in-Chief** of the armed forces.

- **Qualifications:** a citizen of Sierra Leone, at least **40 years** old, a member of a political party, and qualified to be elected as a Member of Parliament.
- **Election:** directly by the voters. A candidate needs at least **55% of the valid votes** to win in the first round; otherwise the two leading candidates face a **run-off**.
- **Tenure:** a term of **five years**, and no one may hold the office for more than **two terms**.
- **Removal:** by Parliament for **gross misconduct or violation of the Constitution**, after investigation by a tribunal (section 51), or on grounds of **mental or physical incapacity** (section 50).

**Powers and functions of the President:**

1. Appoints ministers, ambassadors, judges and heads of agencies, many **subject to Parliament's approval**.
2. Presides over the **Cabinet** and directs government policy.
3. **Assents** to bills passed by Parliament, or returns them with reasons.
4. Commands the **armed forces** and can declare a **state of public emergency**, subject to Parliament.
5. Exercises the **prerogative of mercy** (pardons).
6. Represents Sierra Leone abroad and signs agreements.
7. Addresses Parliament on the state of the nation at the opening of each session.

### The Vice-President

The Vice-President is elected on the **same ticket** as the President and must have the same qualifications. The Vice-President is the President's **principal assistant**, performs duties assigned by the President, and **takes over** if the office of President becomes vacant.

### Ministers and the Cabinet

**Ministers** are appointed by the President **with the approval of Parliament**. Each heads a ministry, such as Finance, Health or Education. The **Cabinet**, made up of the President, the Vice-President and senior ministers, is the main policy-making body. A Member of Parliament who is appointed a minister must give up the seat in Parliament, which keeps the executive and the legislature apart.

### The Attorney-General and Minister of Justice

In Sierra Leone one person holds both offices. The Attorney-General is the **principal legal adviser** to the government, responsible for **public prosecutions** and for drafting government bills.

### The civil service

The ministries are staffed by career civil servants led by **permanent secretaries**. They advise ministers, carry out policy and provide continuity when governments change.

## Limits on executive power

| Control | How it works in Sierra Leone |
|---|---|
| **The Constitution** | Sets the President's powers, term and qualifications; anything outside it is void |
| **Term limits** | No more than two five-year terms |
| **Parliament** | Approves ministers and other appointments, the budget and loans; questions ministers; can remove the President |
| **The courts** | Can declare executive actions unconstitutional or unlawful (judicial review) |
| **Elections** | Voters can reject the ruling party at the next general election |
| **Independent bodies** | Audit Service (Auditor-General), Anti-Corruption Commission, Ombudsman, Human Rights Commission |
| **The press and public opinion** | Expose abuses and criticise policies |
| **Pressure groups and civil society** | Lobby, protest and monitor government |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone has seen power pass peacefully between parties through the ballot box: the APC's **Ernest Bai Koroma** defeated the ruling SLPP in **2007**, and the SLPP's **Julius Maada Bio** won in **2018** after Koroma had served his two terms. The two-term limit and competitive elections are among the strongest checks on the executive.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**"State five ways in which the power of the executive can be checked."** A strong answer names the control and says what it does:

- **Parliament** approves appointments and the budget and can remove the President.
- **The courts** can declare unconstitutional actions void.
- **Elections** let voters remove a government.
- **The press** exposes abuse of power.
- **Term limits** prevent one person staying in office indefinitely.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The President of Sierra Leone is **not** removed by a vote of no confidence. That is a feature of parliamentary systems. Under the 1991 Constitution, removal is only for **misconduct** (after a tribunal) or **incapacity**, through a special procedure in Parliament.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Term limit**: the rule that a President may serve no more than two five-year terms.
- **Judicial review**: the power of the courts to declare executive actions unconstitutional or unlawful.
- **Ombudsman**: an independent official who investigates complaints against public bodies and officials.
:::

## Key points

- The President is **head of state, head of government and commander-in-chief** (Chapter V, 1991 Constitution).
- Qualifications include being a citizen aged at least **40**; election needs **55%** in round one; tenure is **five years, two terms**.
- Ministers are appointed **with Parliament's approval**; the **Attorney-General and Minister of Justice** is the chief legal adviser.
- Executive power is limited by the **Constitution, Parliament, courts, elections, independent commissions and the press**.

## Quick check

1. What is the minimum age to be President of Sierra Leone?
2. What share of valid votes is needed to win the presidential election in the first round?
3. Who must approve the appointment of ministers?
4. Name two independent bodies that check the executive in Sierra Leone.

**Answers:**

1. 40 years.
2. At least 55%.
3. Parliament.
4. Any two: the Audit Service (Auditor-General); the Anti-Corruption Commission; the Ombudsman; the Human Rights Commission of Sierra Leone; the Electoral Commission.
