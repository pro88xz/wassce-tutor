A bag labelled "1 kg" that holds 900 g, a cough syrup past its expiry date, a phone charger that catches fire, an advert that promises what the product cannot do: consumers meet these every day. One buyer is weak against a large seller who knows more about the product. **Consumer protection** is the set of measures that guard buyers against such exploitation.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a consumer, consumerism and consumer protection
- explain why consumers need protection
- describe the ways in which consumers are exploited
- state and explain the rights of consumers
- calculate the loss to a consumer from short weights and measures

## Meaning

:::callout{type=definition}
- A **consumer** is a person who **buys or uses goods and services** for personal use, not for resale.
- **Consumer protection** means the laws, organisations and actions that **safeguard consumers** against unfair practices, harmful goods and exploitation by sellers.
- **Consumerism** is the **movement** that promotes the rights and interests of consumers.
:::

## Why consumers need protection

1. **Ignorance**: consumers often know little about the quality, contents or safety of what they buy, especially technical goods and medicines.
2. **Weak bargaining power**: one buyer cannot negotiate with a large company or a monopoly.
3. **Profit motive**: some sellers cheat to raise profits.
4. **Persuasive and misleading advertising**.
5. **Complex products and contracts**: electronics, insurance and credit agreements.
6. **Monopolies** can raise prices and lower quality when there is no competition.
7. **Fake and substandard goods** look like genuine ones.
8. **Health and safety**: unsafe food, drugs and appliances can kill.

## How consumers are exploited

| Practice | Example |
|---|---|
| **Short weights and measures** | Selling 900 g as 1 kg; using a dented or false-bottomed measuring cup or pan for rice, gari or palm oil |
| **Adulteration** | Mixing palm oil with dye, diluting fuel, adding stones or sand to grain |
| **Expired goods** | Selling food or drugs past their expiry date, sometimes with relabelled dates |
| **Fake (counterfeit) goods** | Fake drugs, spare parts, phones and cosmetics that copy famous brands |
| **Misleading advertising and labelling** | False claims about what a product does or contains |
| **Overpricing and hoarding** | Holding goods back to create scarcity, then raising prices |
| **Dangerous goods** | Unsafe electrical appliances, harmful chemicals in cosmetics (for example skin bleaching creams) |
| **Unfair contract terms** | Hidden charges, harsh credit terms, "goods once sold cannot be returned" on faulty goods |
| **Poor after-sales service** | Refusing to honour guarantees or repair faulty goods |
| **Price discrimination and cartels** | Sellers agreeing to fix high prices |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**What does a short measure cost the consumer?** A trader sells rice by a measure claimed to hold 2 kg, at NLe 40 a measure. The measure actually holds 1.6 kg.

1. Price the consumer believes they pay per kg $= 40 \div 2 = $ NLe 20
2. Price actually paid per kg $= 40 \div 1.6 = $ NLe 25
3. Overcharge per kg $= 25 - 20 = $ NLe 5, a hidden price rise of $\dfrac{5}{20} \times 100 = 25\%$
4. A family that buys 10 measures a month loses $10 \times 0.4 = 4$ kg of rice, worth $4 \times 20 = $ **NLe 80** a month.

Short measures look small on each sale, but they add up to a serious loss.
:::

## Consumer rights

These rights are recognised in the **United Nations Guidelines for Consumer Protection** and by consumer movements worldwide:

| Right | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Right to safety** | Protection from goods and services that are dangerous to health or life |
| **Right to be informed** | Accurate information on contents, price, weight, quality, expiry date and use, so consumers can choose wisely |
| **Right to choose** | Access to a variety of goods at competitive prices |
| **Right to be heard** | Consumers' views considered in making policies and laws |
| **Right to redress** | Fair settlement of complaints: refund, repair, replacement or compensation |
| **Right to consumer education** | Knowledge and skills to be an informed consumer |
| **Right to a healthy environment** | Living and working in an environment free from pollution |
| **Right to basic needs** | Access to essential goods and services: food, water, shelter, health care, education |

## Effects of consumer exploitation

- **Health hazards** and even death: fake drugs, contaminated food.
- **Loss of money** to short measures and overpricing.
- **Loss of confidence** in markets and local products.
- **Honest businesses lose** customers to cheats.
- **Government loses revenue**: fake goods are often smuggled.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Market buyers in Sierra Leone often buy rice, gari and palm oil by the **cup, pan or bucket**, so a dented or false-bottomed measure cheats them on every sale. Fake and expired medicines sold by unlicensed street sellers are a serious danger. The authorities regularly seize and destroy such drugs, and campaigns warn people to buy from **licensed pharmacies**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
"Consumer protection" is not only about the **government**. It also includes **consumer associations, producers' self-regulation, the media and consumers themselves**, all covered in the next lesson.
:::

## Key points

- **Consumer protection** safeguards buyers from unfair practices and harmful goods; **consumerism** is the movement for consumer rights.
- Consumers need protection because of **ignorance, weak bargaining power, the profit motive, misleading adverts, monopolies and fake goods**.
- Exploitation includes **short measures, adulteration, expired and fake goods, misleading adverts, hoarding and unsafe products**.
- Consumer rights: **safety, information, choice, to be heard, redress, education, a healthy environment and basic needs**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **define consumer protection**, to **explain why consumers need protection**, to **describe ways consumers are exploited**, and to **state the rights of consumers**. Use local examples, such as fake drugs or short measures, to show understanding.
:::

## Quick check

1. Define consumer protection.
2. Give three ways in which consumers are exploited.
3. What is the right to redress?
4. A "5-litre" container of palm oil holds 4.5 litres and sells for NLe 90. What is the true price per litre?

**Answers:** 1. The laws, organisations and actions that safeguard consumers against unfair practices, harmful goods and exploitation by sellers. 2. Any three: short weights and measures, adulteration, expired goods, fake goods, misleading advertising, overpricing and hoarding, dangerous goods, unfair contract terms, poor after-sales service. 3. The right to a fair settlement of genuine complaints, by refund, repair, replacement or compensation. 4. $90 \div 4.5 = $ NLe 20 per litre (not NLe 18 as it appears).
