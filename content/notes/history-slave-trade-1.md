:::callout{type=definition}
The **Atlantic slave trade** was the forced transport of **millions of Africans** across the **Atlantic Ocean** to work as enslaved labourers on **plantations and mines in the Americas**, from the sixteenth to the nineteenth century.
:::

It was driven by European demand for **cheap labour** to grow **sugar, tobacco, cotton and coffee**. Historians estimate that about **12.5 million** Africans were put on ships, and about **10.7 million** survived the crossing, known as the **Middle Passage**. This lesson explains the causes of the Atlantic slave trade and how it was organised.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the causes of the Atlantic slave trade
- describe how enslaved people were obtained in West Africa
- describe the triangular trade
- describe the conditions of the Middle Passage
- explain the roles of European companies and African middlemen

## Causes of the Atlantic slave trade

1. **Demand for labour in the Americas:** Europeans set up **plantations** of sugar, tobacco, cotton and coffee, and **mines**, which needed huge amounts of labour.
2. **Decline of the Native American population**, which was devastated by European diseases, war and forced labour.
3. **Failure of European labour**: indentured European servants were too few and often died of tropical diseases.
4. **Africans' resistance to tropical diseases** such as malaria, and their farming and mining skills.
5. **Profits**: the trade made great fortunes for merchants, shipowners and planters.
6. **Support from European governments**, which granted charters and the **Asiento** (licence to supply slaves to Spanish colonies).
7. **Participation of some African rulers and merchants**, who sold captives for guns, cloth and other goods.

## How enslaved people were obtained

1. **Prisoners of war**, often from wars fought partly to obtain captives.
2. **Raids and kidnapping** of villages.
3. **Criminals** and people condemned by courts.
4. **Debtors** and people pawned for debt.
5. **Tribute** paid by weaker states to stronger ones.

Captives were marched to the coast, held in **forts, barracoons and slave pens**, and sold to European traders.

## The triangular trade

| Leg | From → to | Cargo |

## The Middle Passage

The voyage across the Atlantic took **several weeks to months**. Enslaved people were:

- **chained** and packed tightly below deck, with little room to move;
- given poor food and water;
- exposed to **disease** (dysentery, smallpox), brutal punishment and abuse.

Many died on the voyage; others resisted through **revolts**, hunger strikes and suicide.

## Organisation

| Participants | Role |
|---|---|
| **European chartered companies and private traders** | financed voyages, owned ships and forts |
| **African rulers and middlemen** | supplied captives, collected duties, controlled access |
| **Ship captains and crews** | transported captives across the Atlantic |
| **Planters in the Americas** | bought enslaved people at auctions |

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Bunce Island**, in the Sierra Leone River near Freetown, was one of the most important British slave-trading forts on the Upper Guinea coast in the eighteenth century. Many captives shipped from there were sent to **South Carolina and Georgia**, where planters valued their knowledge of **rice farming**. Bunce Island's ruins are now a historic site visited by people from the African diaspora.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not describe the Atlantic slave trade as "just another trade". It was a system of **forced migration and human suffering** on a vast scale, and essays should explain its human cost as well as its organisation.
:::

## Key points

- Cause: **demand for plantation and mine labour in the Americas**, plus profits, government support and African participation.
- Captives came from **wars, raids, courts, debt and tribute**.
- **Triangular trade:** Europe → Africa → Americas → Europe; the **Middle Passage** was the crossing to the Americas.
- About **12.5 million** were embarked and about **10.7 million** landed.
- **Bunce Island** was a major slave-trading fort in Sierra Leone.

## Quick check

1. Why did European planters want African labour?
2. Give three ways in which captives were obtained.
3. Name the three legs of the triangular trade.
4. What was the Middle Passage?
5. Name a slave-trading fort in Sierra Leone.

**Answers:**

1. Native American populations had collapsed and European labour was insufficient, while plantations and mines needed large numbers of workers.
2. Any three: prisoners of war; raids and kidnapping; criminals; debtors; tribute.
3. Europe to West Africa (manufactured goods); West Africa to the Americas (enslaved Africans); the Americas to Europe (plantation produce).
4. The voyage of enslaved Africans across the Atlantic to the Americas.
5. Bunce Island.
