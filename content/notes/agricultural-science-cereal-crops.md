**Cereals** are grasses grown for their grain: rice, maize, sorghum, millet and fonio. In Sierra Leone, **rice** is far more than a crop: it is the national staple, eaten at nearly every main meal, and people often say they have not eaten until they have eaten rice. Yet the country still imports a large share of its rice. Growing more rice, and more maize for food and animal feed, is central to food security. This lesson describes the cereals, the rice-growing ecologies of Sierra Leone, and the cultivation of rice and maize from land preparation to storage.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name the main cereal crops and their uses
- describe the rice-growing ecologies of Sierra Leone
- describe the cultivation of swamp rice and maize step by step
- name the main pests and diseases of rice and maize and their control
- describe harvesting, processing and storage of cereals

## The cereal crops

| Crop | Scientific name | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **rice** | *Oryza sativa* (Asian); *Oryza glaberrima* (African) | staple food; straw for animal feed, mulch and roofing; bran for animal feed |
| **maize** | *Zea mays* | roasted or boiled cobs; flour; **animal feed** (especially poultry); starch, oil |
| **sorghum** (guinea corn) | *Sorghum bicolor* | flour, porridge, local beer; stalks for fencing and fuel |
| **millet** | *Pennisetum glaucum* | porridge, flour; very drought tolerant |
| **fonio** | *Digitaria exilis* | small grain grown in the north; quick to mature, eaten as porridge or like rice |

Cereals provide mainly **carbohydrate** (energy), with some protein. Sorghum, millet and fonio tolerate drier conditions and are grown mainly in the **north**.

## Rice ecologies of Sierra Leone

![A cross-section of the landscape from hill to coast: upland rice on rain-fed slopes, inland valley swamps in valley bottoms, boliland and riverine grassland on flooded plains, and mangrove swamp on the coast](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/rice-ecologies.svg)

| Ecology | Features | Yields |
|---|---|---|
| **upland** | rain-fed slopes; bush fallow; most common traditional system | low; land must rest after one or two crops |
| **inland valley swamp (IVS)** | valley bottoms, wet most of the year; can be developed with bunds and channels | higher; can be cropped every year, sometimes twice |
| **boliland** | seasonally flooded grassland in the north; heavy soils | moderate; suits mechanization |
| **riverine grassland** | flood plains of rivers in the south | moderate |
| **mangrove swamp** | tidal swamps of the Scarcies and other estuaries; rains wash out the salt | good |

Improved varieties released in Sierra Leone include the **ROK** series bred at the Rokupr Agricultural Research Centre and **NERICA** ("New Rice for Africa") varieties bred by crossing African and Asian rice.

## Cultivation of swamp rice

1. **Land preparation:** clear vegetation; build **bunds** (low walls) to hold water and **channels** to let water in and out; plough or hoe and **puddle** the wet soil (churn it into soft mud), then level.
2. **Nursery:** sow pre-soaked seed in a well-prepared bed; seedlings ready in about **21 days**.
3. **Transplanting:** 2–3 seedlings per hill at about **20 cm × 20 cm**, in straight rows.
4. **Fertiliser:** NPK at or soon after transplanting; **urea top dressing** at tillering and at panicle initiation.
5. **Water management:** keep about 5 cm of water once seedlings are established; drain about two weeks before harvest.
6. **Weeding:** by hand or with a rotary weeder in the first 4–6 weeks; flooding also suppresses many weeds.
7. **Pest and disease control** (see below), including **bird scaring** as the grain ripens.
8. **Harvesting:** when about **80–85% of the grains are straw-coloured**, roughly 30–35 days after flowering; cut with a sickle.
9. **Post-harvest:** thresh, winnow, dry to about **14% moisture**, then store or parboil and mill.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Harvesting too late or drying on the road.** Over-ripe rice **shatters** (grains fall off) and birds take more. Paddy dried on tarmac roads picks up stones and dirt, and cracks if dried too fast in hot sun, giving many broken grains at milling. Dry on clean tarpaulins, turning regularly.
:::

## Cultivation of maize

1. **Land preparation:** clear, plough and harrow, or make ridges on heavier soils.
2. **Planting:** at the start of the rains, **75 cm × 25 cm**, one seed per hole (or 75 cm × 50 cm, two seeds), about 3–5 cm deep. Improved varieties mature in about 90–120 days.
3. **Fertiliser:** NPK at planting; urea top dressing at about 4–6 weeks (knee height).
4. **Weeding:** at about 2–3 and 5–6 weeks; earth up the base of the plants.
5. **Harvesting:** green cobs for roasting at about 75–90 days; dry grain when the husks are brown and dry and the grains hard.
6. **Drying and shelling:** dry cobs, then shell (by hand or machine) and dry the grain further before storage.

## Pests and diseases

| Crop | Pests | Diseases |
|---|---|---|
| **rice** | **stem borers** (dead hearts, white heads), **African rice gall midge** (onion-like tubes instead of panicles), rice bugs (empty grains), **birds** (weaver birds), **rats** | **rice blast** (fungus: diamond-shaped leaf spots, neck rot), **rice yellow mottle virus**, brown spot (fungus) |
| **maize** | **stem borers**, **fall armyworm** (ragged leaves, droppings in the whorl), grasshoppers; **weevils** in store | **maize streak virus** (yellow streaks; spread by leafhoppers), rust, leaf blight |

**Control:** resistant varieties; clean seed; timely planting; field sanitation (destroy stubble that harbours stem borers); balanced fertiliser; bird scaring; rat control; approved pesticides as a last resort.

## Processing and storage

- **Threshing** separates grain from straw; **winnowing** removes chaff.
- **Drying** to safe moisture (about 12–14%) prevents moulds and storage insects.
- **Parboiling** rice (soaking, steaming, then drying before milling) hardens the grain, reduces breakage, keeps more nutrients and improves storage. Much rice in Sierra Leone is parboiled.
- **Milling** removes the husk (and bran for white rice).
- **Storage:** clean, dry stores; airtight bags or containers; protection from rats; regular inspection.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Developing the swamps.** An inland valley swamp with good bunds and water-control channels lets a farmer keep the right depth of water, control weeds by flooding, and grow rice every year, and sometimes a second crop or dry-season vegetables. Developed swamps commonly yield two to three times as much rice per hectare as traditional upland farms, which is why swamp development is central to efforts to feed the country from its own fields.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Cereal**: a grass grown for its grain, such as rice, maize, sorghum and millet.
- **Upland rice**: rice grown on rain-fed slopes, usually under bush fallow; the most common traditional system in Sierra Leone.
- **Inland valley swamp** (IVS): a valley bottom that stays wet most of the year and can be developed for rice with bunds and channels.
- **Boliland**: seasonally flooded grassland in the north with heavy soils, used for rice.
- **Mangrove swamp rice**: rice grown in tidal swamps, such as those of the Scarcies, where the rains wash out the salt.
- **Stem borer**: an insect larva that tunnels inside the stems of rice and maize, causing dead hearts and white heads.
- **Fall armyworm**: a caterpillar pest of maize that leaves ragged leaves and droppings in the whorl.
:::

## Key points

- Cereals: **rice, maize, sorghum, millet, fonio**: mainly energy foods.
- Rice ecologies: **upland, inland valley swamp, boliland, riverine grassland, mangrove swamp**.
- Swamp rice: bunds and puddling, nursery, transplant at about 21 days at 20 × 20 cm, top dress with urea, manage water, harvest at 80–85% straw colour.
- Maize: 75 × 25 cm, NPK then urea, weed early, harvest green or dry.
- Pests: **stem borers, gall midge, fall armyworm, birds, rats**; diseases: **blast, RYMV, maize streak**.
- Post-harvest: **thresh, winnow, dry to about 14%, parboil, mill, store safely**.
