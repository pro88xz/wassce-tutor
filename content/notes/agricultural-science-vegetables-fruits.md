Walk through any market in Sierra Leone and you see the work of vegetable and fruit growers: piles of pepper, okra, garden eggs and tomatoes, bundles of potato leaves and krain-krain, heaps of mangoes, oranges, pineapples and plantains. Vegetables supply the **vitamins and minerals** that a rice-based diet lacks, and both vegetables and fruits earn quick cash, especially for women and young farmers. This lesson classifies vegetables and fruits, explains how to grow vegetables (including in the dry season), and describes the establishment and care of fruit trees.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- classify vegetables and name local examples
- describe the cultivation of a fruit vegetable (tomato or pepper) and a leafy vegetable
- explain dry-season vegetable production in inland valley swamps
- describe the propagation and care of fruit trees such as mango, citrus, pineapple and plantain
- explain why vegetables and fruits are perishable and how losses are reduced

## Classifying vegetables

| Group | Part eaten | Local examples |
|---|---|---|
| **leafy vegetables** | leaves | potato leaves, cassava leaves, **krain-krain** (jute mallow), **greens** (amaranth), lettuce, cabbage |
| **fruit vegetables** | fruits | **pepper**, **tomato**, **okra**, **garden egg** (African eggplant), cucumber, pumpkin |
| **bulb vegetables** | bulbs | onion, shallot |
| **root vegetables** | roots | carrot |
| **legume vegetables** | pods | green beans, cowpea pods |

**Importance:** vitamins (A, C), minerals (iron, calcium), fibre; quick income because many mature in 1–3 months; can be grown on small plots and in the dry season.

## Growing vegetables

### Site and beds

- **Near water**: most vegetables need regular watering.
- **Fertile, well-drained** soil rich in organic matter.
- **Raised beds** (about 1 m wide, any length) drain well in the rains and are easy to water and weed.
- Plenty of **compost or well-rotted manure** dug in before planting.

### Direct sowing and transplanting

- **Direct sowing** in the bed: okra, krain-krain, greens (amaranth), cucumber, beans.
- **Nursery and transplanting**: tomato, pepper, garden egg, cabbage, lettuce, onion, which are raised in a nursery and transplanted at about 4–6 weeks.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Outline the cultivation of tomato.**

1. **Nursery:** sow seed thinly in a shaded seed bed or tray; water lightly.
2. **Transplant** at 4–6 weeks (4–6 true leaves), in the evening, at about **60 cm × 45 cm**.
3. **Staking:** tie plants to stakes so fruits stay off the ground and do not rot.
4. **Feeding:** compost or manure, plus NPK; top dress as fruits set.
5. **Watering:** regular and even (uneven watering causes cracked fruit and blossom-end rot).
6. **Pests and diseases:** whiteflies and aphids (spread viruses), fruit worms, nematodes; **bacterial wilt**, early and late blight, damping off in the nursery. Rotate with non-related crops.
7. **Harvest** at about 2–3 months, when fruits turn from green to pink or red; pick regularly.
:::

### Leafy vegetables

Krain-krain and greens are sown directly, thinly, in rows. They can be harvested by **cutting** the shoots, which then sprout again, or by uprooting the whole plant at about 4–6 weeks. Potato leaves are harvested from established sweet potato beds every few weeks. Leafy vegetables need plenty of **nitrogen** (compost, poultry manure, urea in small doses) and regular water.

### Dry-season vegetables in the swamps

After the rice harvest, many **inland valley swamps** still have moisture and a high water table. Farmers grow **pepper, tomato, okra, garden egg, onion, cucumber and leafy vegetables** on beds or mounds in the drained swamp, watering from shallow wells or channels. Prices are high in the dry season because vegetables are scarce, so this second crop can earn more than the rice.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Growing the same vegetable family in the same bed every season.** Tomato, pepper, garden egg and Irish potato belong to the **same family** and share pests and diseases such as **bacterial wilt** and nematodes. Rotate them with leafy vegetables, legumes or onions.
:::

## Fruit crops

| Fruit | Propagation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **mango** | **grafting** (seedlings take long to bear and vary) | grafted trees bear in 3–5 years; spacing about 10 m |
| **citrus** (orange, lime, grapefruit) | **budding** onto a vigorous rootstock | bears in 3–5 years; spacing about 6–8 m |
| **pineapple** | **suckers, slips or crowns** | first fruit at 15–20 months; flowering can be induced |
| **plantain and banana** | **suckers** (or tissue-culture plantlets) | fruit at 9–12 months; remove extra suckers (desuckering) |
| **pawpaw** | seeds | trees are male, female or hermaphrodite; fruit in about a year |
| **avocado** | grafting | |
| **cashew** | seeds or grafting | nuts for export; apples for juice |

### Establishing an orchard

1. **Plan** the layout and spacing; choose well-drained land.
2. **Dig planting holes** (about 60 cm × 60 cm × 60 cm), refill with topsoil mixed with compost or manure.
3. **Plant** healthy budded or grafted seedlings with the graft union **above** the soil; stake and water.
4. **Care:** ring weeding, mulching, fertilising, pruning to shape and remove dead wood, controlling pests (fruit flies, scale insects) and diseases.
5. **Intercrop** with short crops (groundnut, vegetables) while the trees are young.

### Plantain care

Plantain is planted from **suckers**: choose healthy **sword suckers** (narrow leaves, from vigorous mats). Keep only 2–3 suckers per plant (**desuckering**) to avoid competition; **prop** plants carrying heavy bunches; mulch heavily. Main problems: **black sigatoka** (leaf spot fungus), banana weevil and nematodes.

## Perishability and losses

Vegetables and fruits contain much water and keep respiring after harvest, so they **spoil quickly**. Losses come from bruising, heat, rotting and poor transport.

**Reducing losses:** harvest in the cool morning; handle gently; pack in crates, not sacks; keep in the shade; transport quickly; sell or process soon. **Processing**: drying (pepper, okra), juice and jam (mango, citrus), and pickles.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Garden eggs and the Freetown market.** Farmers in the Western Rural District and around Waterloo supply Freetown's markets with vegetables. Those who pick in the early morning, pack in plastic crates and get to market fast lose far less produce than those who pile vegetables in sacks on top of a hot poda-poda. Better handling is often the cheapest way to earn more.
:::

Leafy: krain-krain, potato leaves. Fruit: okra, pepper, garden egg.
:::

To keep the plants upright and the **fruits off the ground**, which reduces rotting and disease and makes spraying and harvesting easier.
:::

(a) grafting (b) budding (c) suckers, slips or crowns (d) suckers.
:::

The swamp still has moisture and water close by, and vegetables are **scarce** in the dry season, so **prices are high**.
:::

Any three: harvest in the cool morning; handle gently; pack in crates; keep in the shade; transport quickly; process (dry, juice) surplus.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Leafy vegetables**: vegetables grown for their leaves, such as krain-krain, amaranth, cabbage and lettuce.
- **Fruit vegetables**: vegetables grown for their fruits, such as tomato, pepper, okra and garden egg.
- **Raised bed**: a bed of soil about 1 m wide, raised above the ground so that it drains well and is easy to water and weed.
- **Transplanting**: moving seedlings raised in a nursery to the field, usually at 4–6 weeks.
- **Grafting**: joining a shoot of a good variety onto a rootstock, used for mango and avocado.
- **Budding**: inserting a bud of a good variety into a vigorous rootstock, used for citrus.
- **Sucker**: a shoot growing from the base of a plant, used to propagate plantain, banana and pineapple.
:::

## Key points

- Vegetables: **leafy** (potato leaves, krain-krain), **fruit** (pepper, tomato, okra, garden egg), **bulb** (onion); rich in vitamins and minerals.
- Use **raised beds**, compost and regular water; transplant tomato, pepper, garden egg, cabbage; sow okra and leafy vegetables directly.
- **Dry-season vegetables** in swamps earn high prices.
- Fruits: **mango (grafting), citrus (budding), pineapple and plantain (suckers)**; plant in well-prepared holes and care for young trees.
- Produce is **perishable**: handle gently, keep cool, sell or process quickly.
