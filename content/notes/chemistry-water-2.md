Clean water is the most important medicine in the world. Cholera, typhoid and other waterborne diseases spread wherever drinking water is polluted, as Sierra Leone learned in the 2012 cholera outbreak. This lesson explains how a town's water is made safe, how families can treat water at home, and what pollutes rivers, wells and the sea.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name the sources of water and the impurities found in natural water
- describe the stages of municipal water treatment and the purpose of each
- describe methods of treating water at home
- state the main water pollutants, their sources and their effects
- explain eutrophication and suggest ways of controlling water pollution

## Sources and impurities

| Source | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Rainwater** | Fairly pure, but dissolves gases and dust; collected from roofs in many homes |
| **Rivers, streams and dams** | Contain mud, plant matter, germs and dissolved salts |
| **Wells and boreholes** | Filtered through soil; often clearer, but can be polluted by nearby latrines |
| **Springs** | Groundwater emerging at the surface |
| **Sea water** | About 3.5% dissolved salts; needs desalination to drink |

**Impurities in natural water:**

- suspended solids (mud, clay, sand)
- dissolved salts
- dissolved gases (oxygen, carbon(IV) oxide)
- **micro-organisms** (bacteria, viruses, parasites)
- organic matter, colour and smell
- in polluted water: chemicals such as oil, fertilisers, mercury and lead

## Municipal water treatment

![Water treatment stages: intake, screening, coagulation with alum, sedimentation, sand filtration, chlorination, storage and supply to homes](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/water-treatment.svg)

*Figure 1. The stages of treating water for a town supply. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Stage | What happens | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Screening** | Water passes through wire screens | Removes large objects: leaves, sticks, fish, plastic |
| **Coagulation (flocculation)** | **Alum** (aluminium sulphate) is added, sometimes with lime | Fine clay particles clump together into larger **flocs** |
| **Sedimentation** | Water stands in large tanks | Flocs and heavy particles settle to the bottom |
| **Filtration** | Water flows through beds of **sand and gravel** | Removes remaining small particles and some bacteria |
| **Chlorination** | **Chlorine** (or sodium hypochlorite) is added | **Kills germs** (bacteria) |
| **pH adjustment** | Lime may be added | Reduces acidity so pipes do not corrode |
| **Storage and distribution** | Treated water is stored in covered reservoirs and piped | Keeps it clean until use |

Some countries also add **fluoride** to reduce tooth decay.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Freetown's piped water comes mainly from the **Guma Dam** in the Western Area peninsula. It is treated by the **Guma Valley Water Company** through coagulation, filtration and chlorination before being piped to the city. In the dry season, supply often falls short, and many households rely on wells, springs and water vendors, so home treatment is important.
:::

## Treating water at home

| Method | How it helps |
|---|---|
| **Boiling** (at least 1 minute at a rolling boil) | Kills germs; also removes temporary hardness |
| **Chlorine tablets or liquid bleach** (correct dose) | Kills germs; protects stored water |
| **Filtering** through cloth, sand or ceramic filters | Removes particles and some germs |
| **Settling and alum** | Clears muddy water before boiling or chlorinating |
| **Solar disinfection (SODIS)** | Clear plastic bottles left in strong sunlight for about 6 hours; UV kills many germs |
| **Covered, clean storage** | Prevents re-contamination; use a clean cup or a tap |

## Water pollution

| Pollutant | Main sources | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| **Sewage and human waste** | Open defecation, overflowing latrines and septic tanks | **Cholera, typhoid, diarrhoea**; oxygen depletion |
| **Refuse and plastics** | Dumping in drains, streams and the sea | Blocked drains and **flooding**; harm to marine life |
| **Fertilisers** (nitrates, phosphates) | Run-off from farms | **Eutrophication** |
| **Industrial waste** | Factories, tanneries, breweries | Toxic chemicals; heated water lowers oxygen |
| **Heavy metals** (mercury, lead) | Artisanal gold mining, batteries, paint | Poisoning; mercury builds up in fish and in people who eat them |
| **Oil** | Spills, generator and garage waste | Coats birds and fish; stops oxygen entering water |
| **Silt** | Mining, sand mining, deforestation | Muddy water; destroys fish breeding grounds |
| **Pesticides** | Farming | Kill fish and other wildlife |

### Eutrophication

When **fertilisers** wash into a lake or slow river:

1. Nitrates and phosphates make **algae grow rapidly** (an algal bloom).
2. The algae block light, and plants below die.
3. **Bacteria** decompose the dead plants and algae, using up the **dissolved oxygen**.
4. **Fish and other animals die** from lack of oxygen.

### Controlling water pollution

- Build and maintain **proper toilets and sewage treatment**.
- Collect and dispose of **refuse properly**; recycle plastics; never dump in drains.
- Use fertilisers carefully; keep farming away from riverbanks.
- Treat industrial waste before discharge; enforce environmental laws.
- Ban the use of **mercury** in gold mining.
- Protect water sources: fence wells and keep latrines well away.
- Educate communities on hygiene and safe water.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Clear water is not necessarily safe water. Filtration and alum remove **particles**, but **germs** are killed only by **chlorination, boiling** or similar disinfection.
:::

## Key points

- Natural water contains **suspended solids, dissolved substances and germs**.
- Treatment: **screening → coagulation (alum) → sedimentation → filtration (sand) → chlorination → storage**.
- **Alum coagulates** fine particles; **chlorine kills germs**.
- At home: **boil, chlorinate, filter, settle, SODIS**, and store covered.
- Pollutants include **sewage, refuse, fertilisers, heavy metals, oil and silt**; fertilisers cause **eutrophication**.

## Quick check

1. What is the purpose of adding alum during water treatment?
2. Which stage of treatment kills bacteria?
3. Explain how fertilisers can kill fish in a lake.
4. Give two ways a family can make well water safe to drink.

**Answers:**

1. To coagulate fine suspended particles into flocs that settle out.
2. Chlorination.
3. Fertilisers cause rapid algal growth; when the algae and plants die, bacteria decomposing them use up the dissolved oxygen, and fish die (eutrophication).
4. Any two: boiling, adding chlorine tablets or bleach, filtering, solar disinfection, covered storage.
