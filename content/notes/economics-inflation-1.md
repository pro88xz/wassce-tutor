In 2022 and 2023 Sierra Leone's shoppers watched the prices of rice, fuel and transport climb month after month, as the leone fell and world food and fuel prices rose. A general, continuous rise in prices is inflation. This lesson explains how it is measured and what causes it.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define inflation and describe its types by speed
- construct a simple consumer price index and calculate the rate of inflation
- explain demand-pull, cost-push, imported and monetary causes of inflation
- show demand-pull and cost-push inflation on a diagram

## What is inflation?

:::callout{type=definition}
**Inflation** is a **persistent and general rise in the price level** over a period of time, which causes a **fall in the value (purchasing power) of money**.
:::

A rise in the price of one good (say, tomatoes in the dry season) is **not** inflation. Inflation means prices **in general** keep rising.

**Types by speed:**

| Type | Rate | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Creeping (mild)** | Low, a few per cent a year | Can encourage production; people hardly notice |
| **Walking / trotting** | Moderate, rising | Begins to erode savings and wages |
| **Galloping** | High, double or triple figures | Confidence in money falls; people buy goods or foreign currency |
| **Hyperinflation** | Extremely rapid, prices rising daily | Money becomes almost worthless; people return to barter |

## Measuring inflation: the consumer price index

A **price index** shows the average change in prices of a "basket" of goods and services compared with a **base year** (index = 100). Each item gets a **weight** showing its importance in household spending.

$$\text{price relative} = \dfrac{\text{current price}}{\text{base-year price}} \times 100$$

$$\text{price index} = \dfrac{\sum (\text{price relative} \times \text{weight})}{\sum \text{weights}}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A simple consumer price index.** Base year 2024 = 100.

| Item | Weight | Price 2024 (NLe) | Price 2025 (NLe) | Price relative | Relative × weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rice (cup) | 5 | 10 | 12 | 120 | 600 |
| Fuel (litre) | 3 | 25 | 30 | 120 | 360 |
| Transport (fare) | 2 | 5 | 7 | 140 | 280 |
| **Total** | **10** | | | | **1,240** |

$$\text{price index} = \dfrac{1240}{10} = 124$$

Prices rose by **24%** between 2024 and 2025.
:::

$$\text{rate of inflation} = \dfrac{\text{index this year} - \text{index last year}}{\text{index last year}} \times 100$$

If the index rises from 124 to 136.4, inflation $= \dfrac{12.4}{124} \times 100 = 10\%$.

In Sierra Leone the consumer price index is compiled by **Statistics Sierra Leone (Stats SL)**.

**Uses of price indices:** measuring inflation; adjusting wages and pensions; finding real income and real GDP; guiding monetary policy.

**Limitations:** the basket may not match every household's spending; weights go out of date; quality changes are hard to measure; and price collection in markets can be inaccurate.

## Causes of inflation

### 1. Demand-pull inflation

:::callout{type=definition}
**Demand-pull inflation** occurs when **total (aggregate) demand rises faster than total supply**: too much money chasing too few goods.
:::

**Causes:** increased government spending (especially financed by borrowing from the central bank); rapid growth of bank credit; rising incomes and wages; a big increase in exports or remittances; a rapid rise in population.

### 2. Cost-push inflation

:::callout{type=definition}
**Cost-push inflation** occurs when **costs of production rise** and firms pass them on in higher prices.
:::

**Causes:** wage increases above productivity (often won by trade unions); higher fuel and transport costs; dearer imported raw materials; higher indirect taxes; higher interest rates on business loans.

### 3. Imported inflation

When a country imports much of its food, fuel and manufactured goods, a **rise in world prices** or a **fall in its own currency** raises prices at home. Sierra Leone imports most of its fuel and much of its rice, so a weaker leone quickly pushes up local prices.

### 4. Monetary causes

An **excessive increase in the money supply**, for example when the government finances its deficit by borrowing from the central bank, raises spending and prices (see $MV = PT$ in *Money and Banking*).

### 5. Structural causes

In developing countries, **bottlenecks** such as poor roads, low farm output, power shortages and hoarding by traders mean supply cannot keep up with demand.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not explain inflation only as "prices going up". Say **why**: too much demand, higher costs, imported prices, more money or supply bottlenecks. Examiners award marks for the cause, not the description.
:::

## Key points

- **Inflation** is a persistent rise in the **general** price level and a fall in the value of money.
- Types by speed: **creeping, walking, galloping, hyperinflation**.
- A **price index** uses a weighted basket and a base year; inflation is the percentage change in the index.
- Causes: **demand-pull, cost-push, imported, monetary and structural**.

## Quick check

1. Is a rise in the price of tomatoes in the dry season inflation? Why?
2. A price index rises from 150 to 165. What is the rate of inflation?
3. Distinguish demand-pull from cost-push inflation.
4. Why does a fall in the value of the leone raise prices in Sierra Leone?

**Answers:** 1. No. Inflation is a general, persistent rise in prices, not a seasonal rise in one good. 2. $\dfrac{15}{150} \times 100 = 10\%$. 3. Demand-pull: total demand exceeds supply. Cost-push: rising costs are passed on in higher prices. 4. Imports such as fuel and rice cost more leones, and firms pass these higher costs on to consumers (imported inflation).
