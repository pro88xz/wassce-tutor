When Sierra Leone imports more than it exports, how does it pay the difference? Every payment between Sierra Leone and the rest of the world, from a cargo of fuel to a remittance from a relative in London, is recorded in one set of accounts: the balance of payments. WASSCE expects you to read it, calculate its balances and explain how a deficit is corrected.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define the balance of trade and the balance of payments
- describe the parts of the balance of payments
- calculate the balance of trade and the current account balance
- explain the causes of a balance of payments deficit
- explain the measures used to correct a deficit

## Balance of trade and balance of payments

:::callout{type=definition}
- The **balance of trade (visible balance)** is the difference between the value of a country's **exports and imports of goods**.
- The **balance of payments (BOP)** is a systematic record of **all economic transactions** between the residents of a country and the rest of the world over a period, usually a year.
:::

The balance of trade is only **one part** of the balance of payments.

## The structure of the balance of payments

![Three boxes: the current account, covering goods, services, primary income and secondary income; the capital and financial account, covering direct investment, portfolio investment and loans; and official reserves of foreign currency and gold held by the central bank](https://wasscetutor.com/img/economics/bop-structure.svg)

*Figure 1. The three main parts of the balance of payments. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Account | What it records | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Current account** | Trade in goods and services, income and transfers | |
| – Visible trade (goods) | Exports and imports of goods | Iron ore exports; rice and fuel imports |
| – Invisible trade (services) | Exports and imports of services | Shipping, insurance, tourism, banking |
| – Primary income | Wages, interest and profits paid to or from abroad | Profits sent home by foreign mining firms |
| – Secondary income (transfers) | One-way transfers with nothing in return | Remittances from Sierra Leoneans abroad; aid grants |
| **Capital and financial account** | Flows of investment and loans | Foreign direct investment in a mine; loans from abroad; portfolio investment |
| **Official reserves** | Changes in the central bank's foreign currency and gold | Reserves fall to pay for a deficit |

Because every transaction is recorded twice (as a credit and a debit), the balance of payments **always balances in an accounting sense**. A "deficit" or "surplus" refers to the balance of **particular accounts**, usually the current account, before official financing.

## Calculating the balances

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A country's transactions in a year (US\$ million):**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Exports of goods | 900 |
| Imports of goods | 1,300 |
| Exports of services | 150 |
| Imports of services | 350 |
| Primary income received | 40 |
| Primary income paid (profits sent abroad) | 160 |
| Secondary income (remittances and grants) received, net | 420 |

- **Balance of trade (visible balance)** $= 900 - 1300 = -400$: a **deficit** of US\$400 million.
- **Balance on services** $= 150 - 350 = -200$.
- **Net primary income** $= 40 - 160 = -120$.
- **Current account balance** $= -400 - 200 - 120 + 420 = -300$: a **current account deficit** of US\$300 million.

This deficit must be financed by inflows on the capital and financial account (investment, loans) or by drawing on official reserves.
:::

## Causes of a balance of payments deficit

1. **Heavy dependence on imports** of food, fuel and manufactured goods.
2. **Low and unstable export earnings** from a few primary products.
3. **Worsening terms of trade**.
4. **High domestic inflation**, which makes exports dear and imports cheap.
5. **Large payments of profits, interest and debt service** abroad.
6. **An overvalued exchange rate**.
7. **Large government spending abroad** (embassies, imports for projects).
8. **Capital flight**: residents moving money out of the country.
9. **Natural disasters, epidemics or conflict** that cut exports.

## Correcting a deficit

| Measure | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Devaluation or depreciation** | Makes exports cheaper abroad and imports dearer at home |
| **Tariffs, quotas and import controls** | Directly reduce imports |
| **Exchange control** | Rations foreign currency for essential imports |
| **Deflationary policies** (higher interest rates, less government spending, higher taxes) | Reduce total demand, and so imports, and slow inflation |
| **Export promotion** | Subsidies, credit, better quality and marketing to raise exports |
| **Import substitution** | Produce at home goods that were imported |
| **Attracting foreign investment and remittances** | Brings in foreign exchange |
| **Borrowing** (including from the IMF) | Finances the deficit in the short run |

Measures that **cut spending** (deflation) are called **expenditure-reducing**; measures that shift spending from imports to home goods (devaluation, tariffs) are called **expenditure-switching**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone usually has a **trade deficit**: imports of fuel, rice and machinery exceed exports, except in years of very high iron ore output. **Remittances** from Sierra Leoneans abroad and **grants** from development partners are an important source of foreign exchange that helps to finance the current account.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not use "balance of trade" and "balance of payments" as if they mean the same thing. The balance of trade covers **goods only**; the balance of payments covers **all** transactions, including services, income, transfers and capital flows.
:::

## Key points

- The **balance of trade** is exports minus imports of **goods**.
- The **balance of payments** records **all** transactions with the rest of the world: **current account**, **capital and financial account**, and **official reserves**.
- **Current account balance** = visible + invisible balances + net primary income + net transfers.
- Deficits arise from **heavy imports, weak exports, inflation and profit outflows**.
- Corrections are **expenditure-reducing** (deflation) or **expenditure-switching** (devaluation, tariffs).

## Quick check

1. Exports of goods are \$500 million and imports of goods \$650 million. What is the balance of trade?
2. In which account are remittances from relatives abroad recorded?
3. Give two causes of a balance of payments deficit in a West African country.
4. What is an expenditure-switching policy?

**Answers:** 1. $500 - 650 = -150$: a deficit of \$150 million. 2. The current account (secondary income / transfers). 3. Any two: dependence on imports, low or unstable export earnings, worsening terms of trade, inflation, profit and debt payments abroad, overvalued currency, capital flight. 4. A policy that shifts spending from imports to home-produced goods, such as devaluation or tariffs.
