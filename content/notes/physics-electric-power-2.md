An electric kettle, an iron and a bulb all turn electrical energy into heat. Joule's law of heating explains why, and it also explains why power is sent across the country at very high voltages.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state Joule's law of electrical heating and use $H = I^2Rt$
- explain the choice of materials for heating elements and fuses
- solve heating problems combining electrical energy and specific heat capacity
- explain why electrical power is transmitted at high voltage

## Joule's law of heating

:::callout{type=definition}
**Joule's law:** the heat $H$ produced in a conductor is proportional to the **square of the current**, the **resistance** and the **time**:

$$H = I^2Rt$$
:::

Doubling the current produces **four times** as much heat.

## Heating elements and fuses

**Heating elements** (in kettles, irons, cookers, water heaters) are made of **nichrome** because it has:

- a **high resistivity** (so a reasonable length gives the needed resistance)
- a **high melting point**
- resistance to **oxidation** when red hot

**Filament lamps** use **tungsten**, which has a very high melting point (about 3400 °C); the bulb is filled with an inert gas so the filament does not burn.

**A fuse** is a short, thin wire (of tin–lead alloy or a similar low-melting-point metal) in series with the live wire. If the current becomes too large, the fuse **melts** ("blows") and breaks the circuit before the wiring overheats.

## Heating water electrically

If all the electrical energy goes into the water:

$$VIt = mc\Delta\theta$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**An immersion heater of resistance 24 Ω is connected to 240 V. How long does it take to heat 5 kg of water from 25 °C to 85 °C? (c = 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹; no heat losses)**

Power: $P = \dfrac{V^2}{R} = \dfrac{240^2}{24} = 2400$ W.

Heat needed: $Q = mc\Delta\theta = 5 \times 4200 \times 60 = 1\,260\,000$ J.

$$t = \dfrac{1\,260\,000}{2400} = 525\ \text{s} \approx 8.75\ \text{minutes}$$
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A kettle rated 2 kW is 80% efficient. How long does it take to boil away 0.2 kg of water already at 100 °C? (latent heat of vaporisation = 2.26 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹)**

Useful power $= 0.8 \times 2000 = 1600$ W. Heat needed $= 0.2 \times 2.26 \times 10^{6} = 452\,000$ J.

$$t = \dfrac{452\,000}{1600} = 282.5\ \text{s}$$
:::

## Transmitting electrical power

Power stations generate electricity far from towns. The power lost as heat in the transmission cables is

$$P_{loss} = I^2R$$

For a given power $P = VI$, a **higher voltage** means a **smaller current**. Since the loss depends on $I^2$, transmitting at very high voltage cuts the losses enormously.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A power station sends 100 kW along cables of total resistance 5 Ω. Compare the power lost when it is transmitted at (a) 1000 V, (b) 20 000 V.**

(a) $I = \dfrac{100\,000}{1000} = 100$ A; loss $= 100^2 \times 5 = 50\,000$ W — half the power is wasted!

(b) $I = \dfrac{100\,000}{20\,000} = 5$ A; loss $= 5^2 \times 5 = 125$ W — only 0.125%.
:::

So power is **stepped up** by transformers to very high voltages (for example 161 kV) for transmission, then **stepped down** in stages near towns (to 11 kV, then 230 V) for safe use in homes.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Power from the Bumbuna hydroelectric dam travels to Freetown along high-voltage transmission lines on tall steel pylons. Substations near the city step the voltage down, and pole-mounted transformers in each neighbourhood step it down again for homes.
:::

## Advantages of electrical energy

- easily transmitted over long distances
- easily converted into other forms (heat, light, motion, sound)
- clean at the point of use
- easily controlled and switched

## Key points

- Joule's law: $H = I^2Rt$; heat ∝ current².
- Heating elements use nichrome (high resistivity and melting point); lamp filaments use tungsten; fuses melt when the current is too large.
- Electrical heating of water: $VIt = mc\Delta\theta$ (or $mL$ for boiling).
- Transmission at high voltage gives a small current and small $I^2R$ losses.

## Quick check

1. By what factor does the heat produced change if the current through a resistor is tripled?
2. Why is nichrome used in heating elements?
3. Why is electrical power transmitted at high voltage?

**Answers:** 1. Nine times ($H \propto I^2$). 2. It has high resistivity and a high melting point and does not oxidise easily. 3. At high voltage the current is small, so the $I^2R$ power losses in the cables are small.
