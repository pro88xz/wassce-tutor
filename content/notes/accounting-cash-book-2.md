The **three-column cash book** has three money columns on each side, **discount, cash and bank**, so that **cash discounts** can be recorded at the same time as the payment. **Discount allowed** (given to customers who pay promptly) goes in the discount column on the **debit** side; **discount received** (from suppliers we pay promptly) goes on the **credit** side. Unlike the cash and bank columns, the discount columns are **totalled, not balanced**, and the totals are posted to the **discount allowed** and **discount received** accounts. This lesson works through a full three-column cash book with discounts, contra entries and balances.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the layout of the three-column cash book
- distinguish discount allowed from discount received
- record payments and receipts with cash discount
- record contra entries and balance the cash book
- post the discount totals and personal accounts to the ledger

## Discount allowed and discount received

| Discount allowed | Discount received |
|---|---|
| given **by** the business **to customers** who pay promptly | received **by** the business **from suppliers** it pays promptly |
| an **expense** (loss of income) | an **income** (gain) |
| recorded on the **debit** side of the cash book, beside the receipt | recorded on the **credit** side, beside the payment |
| total **debited** to discount allowed account | total **credited** to discount received account |
| the customer's account is credited with cash **plus** discount | the supplier's account is debited with cash **plus** discount |

## Worked example

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Record these February 2026 transactions in a three-column cash book (NLe).**

- 1 Feb: balances: cash 2,000; bank 15,000.
- 3 Feb: Kamara paid his debt of 5,000 by cheque, less 5% cash discount.
- 5 Feb: paid Sesay's account of 10,000 by cheque, less 3% cash discount.
- 8 Feb: cash sales 6,000.
- 12 Feb: paid wages in cash 1,500.
- 15 Feb: paid cash into the bank 4,000.
- 20 Feb: withdrew 2,000 from the bank for office use.
- 26 Feb: paid rent by cheque 3,500.

**Calculations:**

- Kamara: discount $= 5\% \times 5\,000 = 250$; cheque $= 5\,000 - 250 = 4\,750$
- Sesay: discount $= 3\% \times 10\,000 = 300$; cheque $= 10\,000 - 300 = 9\,700$
:::

**Balancing:**

- Cash: debit $2\,000 + 6\,000 + 2\,000 = 10\,000$; credit $1\,500 + 4\,000 = 5\,500$; balance c/d $= 4\,500$
- Bank: debit $15\,000 + 4\,750 + 4\,000 = 23\,750$; credit $9\,700 + 2\,000 + 3\,500 = 15\,200$; balance c/d $= 8\,550$
- Discount columns: **totalled only**: allowed 250, received 300.

## Posting to the ledger

| Account | Entry |
|---|---|
| **Kamara** | credit **4,750** (bank) and credit **250** (discount allowed): his whole debt of 5,000 is cleared |
| **Sesay** | debit **9,700** (bank) and debit **300** (discount received): the whole 10,000 owed is cleared |
| **Discount allowed** | debit **250** (total of the debit discount column) |
| **Discount received** | credit **300** (total of the credit discount column) |
| **Sales** | credit 6,000 |
| **Wages** | debit 1,500 |
| **Rent** | debit 3,500 |
| **Contra entries** | nothing posted: both sides are in the cash book |

The discount columns are **memorandum columns**: they are not part of the cash book's double entry, so their totals are posted to the discount accounts, with the other side already made in the personal accounts.

## Discounts in the final accounts

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Discount allowed** is an **expense** in the income statement (profit and loss section).
- **Discount received** is **income** in the income statement.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Wholesalers at Freetown's big markets often give regular retail customers a small cash discount for paying within a week, because getting cash quickly helps them restock. In the wholesaler's cash book this is **discount allowed**; in the retailer's cash book the same amount is **discount received**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not **balance** the discount columns or carry a discount balance down. They are **totalled** and the totals posted: discount allowed to the debit of its account, discount received to the credit of its account.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Discount column**: a memorandum column of the cash book whose totals are posted to the discount allowed and discount received accounts.
:::

## Key points

- Three-column cash book: **discount, cash and bank** on each side.
- **Discount allowed** (to customers): debit side, an **expense**. **Discount received** (from suppliers): credit side, an **income**.
- Personal accounts are cleared with **cash plus discount**.
- Discount columns are **totalled, not balanced**; contra entries are marked "c" and not posted.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
The three-column cash book is one of the most frequently set Paper 2 questions. Expect to **calculate discounts, enter receipts, payments and contras, balance the cash and bank columns, and total the discount columns**. Examiners often report candidates who balance the discount columns or put discount allowed on the wrong side.
:::

## Quick check

1. Where is discount allowed recorded in the cash book?
2. A customer owing NLe 8,000 pays by cheque less 2.5% cash discount. How much is the cheque?
3. Why are the discount columns not balanced?
4. To which side of the discount received account is its total posted?
5. Is discount allowed an expense or an income?

**Answers:**

1. In the discount column on the debit side, beside the receipt from the customer.
2. Discount $= 200$, so the cheque is $8\,000 - 200 = 7\,800$: NLe 7,800.
3. Because they are memorandum columns, not accounts; their totals are posted to the discount accounts.
4. The credit side.
5. An expense.
