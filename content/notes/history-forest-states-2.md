The **Asante** and **Dahomey** kingdoms were powerful states of the **Akan forest** and the **coast** that grew rich in the era of the **Atlantic trade**. **Asante**, in present-day **Ghana**, was united around **1701** by **Osei Tutu** and the priest **Okomfo Anokye**, who gave it the **Golden Stool** as the symbol of its unity, and fought several wars against the British in the nineteenth century. **Dahomey**, in present-day **Benin Republic**, was a highly centralised military kingdom of the **Fon**, with a standing army that included **women soldiers**, and a capital at **Abomey**. This lesson explains the rise, organisation and fall of both states.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the rise of Asante under Osei Tutu and Okomfo Anokye
- explain the importance of the Golden Stool
- describe the government and economy of Asante
- outline the Anglo-Asante wars and the fall of Asante
- describe the rise, government and economy of Dahomey and its conquest by France

## The Asante Kingdom

**Rise:** in the late seventeenth century, several Akan states around **Kumasi** were dominated by the kingdom of **Denkyira**. **Osei Tutu**, chief of Kumasi, and the priest **Okomfo Anokye** united them into the **Asante Union**. In **1701** Asante defeated Denkyira at the **Battle of Feyiase**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**The Golden Stool**

According to tradition, Okomfo Anokye **called down a Golden Stool from the sky**, which landed on Osei Tutu's knees. It was declared to contain the **soul (sunsum) of the Asante nation**. Every chief swore loyalty to it, and the **Odwira festival** brought chiefs to Kumasi each year to renew their allegiance. The stool was the symbol of **unity**: it gave the union a shared identity above the individual states.
:::

**Factors in the rise of Asante:**

1. Leadership of **Osei Tutu** and **Okomfo Anokye**, and later of **Opoku Ware**.
2. The **Golden Stool** and the **Odwira festival**, which united the states.
3. A strong, well-organised **army**, armed with guns obtained through trade.
4. Wealth from **gold**, **kola** (traded north) and the **Atlantic trade** through the coast.
5. Conquest of neighbouring states, which paid **tribute**.

**Government:** the **Asantehene** (king) ruled with the **Asantemanhyiamu** (council of chiefs). Kumasi was the centre; inner states were ruled by their own chiefs loyal to the Golden Stool; conquered outer provinces paid tribute and were watched by officials. A **bureaucracy** handled finance, roads and messages.

**Anglo-Asante wars and the fall of Asante:**

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **1824** | Asante defeated a British force at Nsamankow; **Sir Charles MacCarthy**, Governor of Sierra Leone and the British forts, was killed |
| **1874** | a British army under **Sir Garnet Wolseley** captured and burned **Kumasi** |
| **1896** | the British occupied Kumasi; **Asantehene Prempeh I** was arrested and **exiled**, first to **Sierra Leone** and later to the Seychelles |
| **1900** | the **War of the Golden Stool**, led by **Yaa Asantewaa**, after the British governor demanded to sit on the stool; Asante was then annexed |

## The Kingdom of Dahomey

**Rise:** Dahomey, a kingdom of the **Fon** centred on **Abomey**, expanded under **King Agaja**, who conquered the coastal states of **Allada (1724)** and **Whydah (Ouidah) (1727)**, giving Dahomey direct access to European traders on the coast.

**Government and army:**

1. A **very centralised monarchy**: the king controlled appointments and the economy.
2. Officials were paired with **female counterparts** in the palace who reported to the king, to check their loyalty.
3. A **standing army**, including a famous corps of **women soldiers** (often called the "Amazons" by Europeans).
4. An **annual census** and taxation, and royal control of trade through officials at **Ouidah**.

**Economy:** Dahomey took part heavily in the **Atlantic slave trade** through Ouidah. After the British ban, **King Gezo** (ruled 1818–1858) also developed the **palm oil** trade.

**Fall:** France conquered Dahomey in **1892–1894**, defeating **King Behanzin**, and made it a French colony.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not confuse **Asante** and **Dahomey**. **Asante** was an Akan union in present-day **Ghana**, held together by the **Golden Stool**; **Dahomey** was a Fon kingdom in present-day **Benin Republic**, known for its centralised rule and women soldiers.
:::

## Key points

- **Asante:** united by **Osei Tutu** and **Okomfo Anokye**; victory over **Denkyira at Feyiase (1701)**; the **Golden Stool** and **Odwira** festival as symbols of unity.
- Asante's wealth came from **gold, kola and trade**; the British exiled **Prempeh I (1896)**; **Yaa Asantewaa** led the **1900** war.
- **Dahomey:** Fon kingdom at **Abomey**; **Agaja** took Allada and Ouidah; centralised rule; **women soldiers**; slave trade then **palm oil** under **Gezo**; conquered by **France (1892–94)**.

## Quick check

1. Who founded the Asante Union?
2. What did the Golden Stool symbolise?
3. Where was Prempeh I first sent in exile?
4. Which coastal state did Agaja of Dahomey conquer in 1727?
5. Give two features of Dahomey's government.

**Answers:**

1. Osei Tutu, with the priest Okomfo Anokye.
2. The soul and unity of the Asante nation.
3. To Sierra Leone.
4. Whydah (Ouidah).
5. Any two: a very centralised monarchy; officials checked by female palace counterparts; a standing army with women soldiers; census and taxation; royal control of trade.
