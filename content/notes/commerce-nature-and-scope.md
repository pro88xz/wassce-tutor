# Nature and scope of commerce

## Learning objectives
Define commerce, distinguish it from trade, identify its branches and explain its role in connecting production with consumption.

## Meaning of commerce
Commerce is the system of activities concerned with the exchange and distribution of goods and services, together with the services that make exchange possible. It connects producers with people who need their output. Trade is the buying and selling of goods and services; commerce is wider because it includes trade and aids to trade.

A farmer may grow rice, a miller may process it, a wholesaler may purchase large quantities, and retailers may sell smaller quantities to households. Commerce covers the exchange and supporting activities that help the rice reach buyers safely, at the right time and in a useful condition.

## Branches and scope
Commerce has two broad branches: **trade** and **aids to trade**. Trade may be divided into home trade, which takes place within one country, and foreign trade, which involves parties in different countries. Home trade includes wholesale and retail trade. Foreign trade includes imports and exports.

Aids to trade include transport, warehousing, banking, insurance, communication and advertising. Transport overcomes distance. Warehousing stores goods until they are needed. Banking facilitates payments and finance. Insurance provides cover for specified risks. Communication carries orders and market information. Advertising informs potential customers about products.

## Importance of commerce
Commerce creates markets for producers, gives consumers access to goods from different locations, supports employment and encourages specialisation. Drivers, shopkeepers, warehouse workers, bankers, insurers and marketers all contribute to commercial activity. It also links rural production areas with urban markets.

For example, a farmer in Koinadugu may sell produce to a trader who transports it to Freetown. The transaction depends on transport, communication, storage and payment arrangements. If roads are disrupted or goods are stored badly, distribution costs rise and produce may be lost.

## WASSCE examination focus
When defining commerce, mention both trade and the services that facilitate trade. Do not define commerce as buying and selling alone; that is the narrower meaning of trade. Explain that trade is one branch of commerce.

## Quick check
1. Define commerce.
2. State two differences between commerce and trade.
3. Name four aids to trade and explain their functions.
4. Explain how commerce connects a rural farmer with an urban consumer.

**Answers:** 1. Commerce is trade and the supporting activities that facilitate the exchange and distribution of goods and services. 2. Trade is buying and selling; commerce includes trade and aids to trade. Trade is one branch of commerce. 3. Transport moves goods; warehousing stores goods; banking facilitates payment and finance; insurance covers specified risks; communication exchanges information; advertising informs customers. 4. Traders purchase produce, transporters move it, storage protects it, and retailers make it available to consumers.
