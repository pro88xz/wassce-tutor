**Siaka Stevens** ruled Sierra Leone for **seventeen years (1968–1985)**, first as **Prime Minister** and, from **1971**, as **executive President** of the new **Republic of Sierra Leone**. A former trade unionist who founded the **All People's Congress (APC)** in **1960**, he survived **coup attempts**, made Sierra Leone a **republic on 19 April 1971**, and in **1978** turned it into a **one-party state** under the APC. His rule brought a period of **political stability** but also **violence against opponents, centralised power, corruption** and **economic decline**. He retired in **1985**, handing over to **Major-General Joseph Saidu Momoh**. This lesson explains **the political history of the Siaka Stevens era**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the background and rise of Siaka Stevens
- explain why Sierra Leone became a republic in 1971
- describe the political violence of the 1970s

- describe the role of Sierra Leone in the 1980 OAU summit
- evaluate the political legacy of Siaka Stevens

## Siaka Stevens: background

**Siaka Probyn Stevens (1905–1988)** was born in **Moyamba District**. He worked as a **policeman**, a **railway worker** and a **mine worker**, and became a leading **trade unionist**, organising the **mine workers** at Marampa. He was a founding member of the **Sierra Leone Organisation Society** and the **SLPP**, and a minister in the 1950s. He left the SLPP to form the **PNP (1958)** with Albert Margai, refused to sign the **Lancaster House agreement (1960)**, and founded the **APC (1960)**. He became **Mayor of Freetown (1964)** and won the **1967 election**.

## The republic of 1971

**Events:** in **March 1971** army officers attempted a **coup**; it failed, and Stevens invited troops from **Guinea** under a defence agreement with President **Sékou Touré**. On **19 April 1971**, Parliament made Sierra Leone a **republic**; Stevens was sworn in as **executive President** on **21 April 1971**.

**Why Sierra Leone became a republic:**

1. To end the role of the **British Queen** as head of state and complete **full independence**.
2. To create a **strong executive president** who controlled the government and **the army**, after the coups of **1967, 1968 and 1971**.
3. To remove the **Governor-General**, whose role had been at the centre of the **1967 crisis**.
4. To follow other African states, such as **Ghana** and **Nigeria**, which had already become republics.

## Political violence and opposition in the 1970s

1. **Coup plots and trials:** alleged plotters were tried and some were **executed**, including the former army commander **Brigadier John Bangura (1971)**.
2. **The 1973 election:** amid **violence and intimidation**, the **SLPP withdrew**, leaving the APC almost unopposed.
3. **Use of party youths ("thugs")** to intimidate opponents.
4. **1977 student demonstrations:** students at **Fourah Bay College** protested against the government during a convocation, leading to nationwide demonstrations; the **1977 election** that followed was violent.

## The one-party state (1978)

In **1978** a new **one-party constitution**, approved in a **referendum**, made the **APC** the **only legal party**. Members of the SLPP were invited to join the APC.

**Reasons given by the government:**

1. To **end political violence** between parties, as seen in 1973 and 1977.
2. To promote **national unity** and end **ethnic and regional** party rivalry.
3. To concentrate on **development** instead of party conflict.
4. Many other African states had one-party systems.

**Criticisms:**

1. It **removed choice** from voters and silenced opposition.
2. It concentrated **power in the President** and the APC.
3. It allowed **corruption** to grow without challenge.
4. The **referendum** was widely seen as **unfair**.

## The 1980 OAU summit

In **July 1980** Freetown hosted the **summit of the Organisation of African Unity**, and **Stevens became OAU chairman**. New buildings, including the **Bintumani Hotel** and conference villas at **Hill Station**, were built. The summit raised Sierra Leone's **international profile**, but its huge **cost** and **financial scandals** worsened the economic crisis.

## Stevens's retirement (1985)

In **1985**, aged eighty, Stevens retired and chose **Major-General Joseph Saidu Momoh**, the **army commander**, as his successor. Momoh was elected as the **single candidate** under the one-party system and became President in **November 1985**. Stevens died in **1988**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Evaluating Siaka Stevens's political legacy**

**Positive:** he brought the **APC to power** through elections in 1967, **ended military rule**, gave Sierra Leone **seventeen years without a successful coup**, completed full sovereignty with the **republic**, and raised the country's profile through the **OAU summit**.

**Negative:** he used **violence and detention** against opponents, introduced the **one-party state**, **centralised power**, tolerated **corruption**, and left an **economy in decline** and institutions too weak to resist the **civil war** that began in 1991.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Stevens was popularly known as "**Pa Shaki**" and was famous for his political skill in outmanoeuvring rivals. The national stadium in Freetown, built with Chinese help and opened around **1980**, was originally named the **Siaka Stevens Stadium** before it was renamed the **National Stadium**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Sierra Leone became a **republic** in **1971** but a **one-party state** only in **1978**. These are two separate changes: the first replaced the Queen as head of state; the second banned all parties except the APC.
:::

## Key figures and terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figures and terms"}
- **Siaka Stevens** (1905–1988): the trade unionist who founded the APC, became Prime Minister in 1968 and was President from 1971 to 1985.
- **Republic**: a state whose head is a president, not a monarch; Sierra Leone became one on 19 April 1971.
- **One-party state**: a state in which only one political party is allowed; Sierra Leone became one under the APC in 1978.
:::

## Key points

- **Siaka Stevens (1905–1988)**: trade unionist; founded the **APC (1960)**; PM **1968**; President **1971–1985**.
- **Republic, 19 April 1971**: end of the Queen's role; **strong executive President** after the coups of 1967–1971.
- 1970s: **coup trials and executions**, **SLPP withdrawal (1973)**, **student protests and violent election (1977)**.
- **One-party state (1978)**: APC the only party; justified by unity and peace; criticised as undemocratic.
- **OAU summit (1980)**: prestige but huge costs; Stevens retired in **1985**, succeeded by **Momoh**.
