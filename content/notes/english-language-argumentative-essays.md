An argumentative essay takes a position on a question and defends it with reasons and evidence: *"Should students be allowed to use phones in school?"*, *"Day schools are better than boarding schools. Do you agree?"*, *"Farming, not mining, is the key to Sierra Leone's future."* Examiners reward essays that take a clear side, build a logical case, deal fairly with the other side, and persuade. This lesson shows you how, with a complete model essay.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state a clear position in your introduction
- build each paragraph around one reason, supported by evidence and explanation
- acknowledge and answer the opposing view (rebuttal)
- use persuasive language without becoming emotional or rude
- write a conclusion that restates your position with force

## Argumentative or expository?

| Argumentative | Expository |
|---|---|
| takes a **side** and tries to **persuade** | **explains** without taking a side |
| *"Should…?"*, *"Do you agree…?"*, *"…is better than…"* | *"Explain the causes of…"*, *"How does…?"* |
| uses *I believe*, *clearly*, *therefore* | uses neutral, factual language |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Sitting on the fence.** *"Phones have advantages and disadvantages, so it depends"* does not answer *"Should students be allowed phones in school?"*. You may admit the other side has a point, but your essay must come down **clearly** on one side.
:::

## The structure

:::callout{type=definition}
| Paragraph | Content |
|---|---|
| **Introduction** | hook the reader; give a little background; state your **position** clearly (the thesis) |
| **Reason 1** | your strongest argument, explained and supported |
| **Reason 2** | a second argument |
| **Reason 3** | a third argument |
| **Counter-argument and rebuttal** | the strongest point on the other side, fairly stated, and your answer to it |
| **Conclusion** | restate your position in fresh words; end with a call or a striking final thought |
:::

## Building a reason paragraph: PEEL

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| **P**oint | *The strongest argument for boarding schools is that they give students more time to study.* |
| **E**vidence | *A boarder has supervised evening prep every night, while many day students spend two hours a day in traffic or doing chores at home.* |
| **E**xplanation | *Those extra hours add up to hundreds over a school year, and they come at the time when the mind is free to concentrate.* |
| **L**ink | *Time, however, is not the only advantage.* |

### Kinds of evidence you can use

- **Examples** from your experience or community
- **Facts** that are widely known (avoid inventing precise statistics)
- **Reasoning**: *if… then…*, cause and effect
- **Comparison**: *in schools where…, students…*

:::callout{type=tip}
You do not need exact statistics to argue well, and invented numbers can make an essay less believable. *"Many students in our school spend over an hour a day travelling"* is honest and persuasive. *"87.3% of students spend 2.4 hours travelling"* sounds made up, because it is.
:::

## The rebuttal: answering the other side

Stating and answering the strongest opposing argument shows the examiner you have **thought**, not just repeated one side.

> *Some people argue that boarding schools separate children from their families at an important age. This is a fair concern. However, most boarding students go home every holiday and at mid-term, and many say they value their families more because of the time apart. Boarding does not end family life; it gives it a new shape.*

The pattern: **state it fairly → admit what is true → show why your side still wins**.

## Persuasive language

| Technique | Example |
|---|---|
| Confident statements | *Clearly…*, *There is no doubt that…*, *It is undeniable that…* |
| Signposts | *Firstly…*, *Furthermore…*, *More importantly…*, *Finally…* |
| Concession | *Admittedly…*, *It is true that… However…* |
| Rhetorical questions | *Can a hungry nation ever be truly independent?* |
| Inclusive language | *We…*, *our country…*, *our children…* |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Insulting the other side.** *"Anyone who supports mining is foolish and greedy"* weakens your essay. Strong arguments attack **ideas**, not **people**.
:::

## A complete model essay

**Question:** *"Agriculture, not mining, is the key to Sierra Leone's future." Write an essay supporting or opposing this statement.*

::::model{title="Agriculture is the key to our future" label="Model argumentative essay"}
**AGRICULTURE, NOT MINING, IS THE KEY TO SIERRA LEONE'S FUTURE**

Sierra Leone is famous around the world for its diamonds, and in recent years iron ore, rutile and gold have brought in much of our export earnings. Yet every day, many Sierra Leoneans go to bed hungry, and the country spends large sums importing the rice we eat. I firmly believe that our future lies not under the ground but on top of it: agriculture, not mining, is the key to Sierra Leone's future.

Firstly, agriculture feeds the nation. No country can be secure while it depends on others for its staple food. Every bag of imported rice is money that leaves the country and a job that is created on someone else's farm. With our fertile soils, reliable rains and wide inland valley swamps, we could grow far more of our own rice, cassava and vegetables, and a nation that feeds itself is protected from sudden rises in world food prices.

Secondly, agriculture employs far more people than mining. A mine needs heavy machinery and a relatively small number of skilled workers, many of them brought from elsewhere. Farming, processing and transporting food, on the other hand, can give work to millions: farmers, millers, traders, drivers, mechanics and market women. If young people could earn a decent living on the land, fewer of them would crowd into Freetown in search of work that does not exist.

Furthermore, farming lasts and mining does not. A diamond or an iron ore deposit, once dug out, is gone for ever, often leaving behind holes, polluted rivers and ruined farmland, as many communities in Kono can testify. A farm that is well managed can feed a family for generations. Investing in agriculture is investing in something that renews itself every season.

It is true that mining brings in large amounts of foreign exchange quickly, and that the government needs that money for roads, schools and hospitals. Admittedly, the country cannot simply abandon its minerals. However, mining wealth has too often flowed to a few companies and individuals rather than to the people, and its earnings rise and fall with world prices that we cannot control. The wise course is to use mining revenue to build the roads, irrigation and storage that our farmers need, so that the wealth under the ground helps to grow the wealth above it.

Finally, agriculture can build industries. Cocoa, coffee, palm oil and cashew can be processed here into chocolate, soap, oil and packaged nuts, creating factory jobs and earning more than raw exports ever could. Every successful farming nation has followed this path.

Our minerals will one day run out; our land will not. If we give our farmers the tools, the roads, the credit and the respect they deserve, Sierra Leone can feed itself, employ its young people and build a future that does not depend on what lies buried in the ground. The key to that future is already in our hands. We only have to plant it.
::::

### Why it scores

| Feature | Where |
|---|---|
| **Clear thesis** | end of the introduction: *"I firmly believe… agriculture, not mining…"* |
| **Three developed reasons** | food security, employment, sustainability (plus industry) |
| **PEEL paragraphs** with local evidence | inland valley swamps, Kono, young people moving to Freetown |
| **Fair rebuttal** | paragraph 5: admits mining's value, then shows why agriculture still wins |
| **Persuasive language** | *firmly believe*, *furthermore*, *admittedly… however*, *the wise course* |
| **A strong ending** | *"The key to that future is already in our hands. We only have to plant it."* |

It is about 500 words, with no invented statistics: every claim is reasoned or widely known.

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Write a clear thesis for: 'Should students be allowed to use phones in school?'"}
*Although phones can be useful for learning, I strongly believe that students should not be allowed to use them during school hours, because they distract from lessons, encourage cheating and widen the gap between rich and poor students.*
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Write a rebuttal paragraph for the phones essay."}
*Supporters of phones in school argue that students need them for research. It is true that the internet holds a great deal of useful information. However, a school already has teachers, textbooks and a library, and research can be done at home or in supervised computer lessons. Allowing phones in every classroom to serve this purpose is like opening every door in the house to let in one breeze.*
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Improve this weak sentence: 'Mining is bad and the people who do it are wicked.'"}
*Mining, when it is poorly regulated, can destroy farmland and pollute rivers, leaving communities poorer than before.* (It attacks the **problem**, not people, and explains **why**.)
:::

## Key points

- Take a **clear side** and state it in the introduction.
- Build each paragraph with **PEEL**: point, evidence, explanation, link.
- Include a **fair rebuttal**: state the other side, admit what is true, show why your side wins.
- Use **confident, persuasive** language; attack **ideas**, never people.
- End by **restating** your position in fresh, memorable words.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Argumentative essays are rewarded for a **clear position**, **logical, developed reasons**, an awareness of the **other side**, and **persuasive expression**. Examiners mark down essays that do not take a side, that list reasons without explaining them, or that use rude or emotional language instead of argument.
:::
