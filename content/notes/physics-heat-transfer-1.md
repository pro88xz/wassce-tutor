The handle of a metal spoon in hot soup gets hot. Warm air rises above a fire. The Sun warms the Earth across 150 million kilometres of empty space. These are the three ways heat travels: conduction, convection and radiation.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe and explain conduction, convection and radiation
- compare good and bad conductors and explain their uses
- describe experiments that demonstrate each method of heat transfer
- state the factors that affect the emission and absorption of radiation

## Conduction

:::callout{type=definition}
**Conduction** is the transfer of heat through a material **from particle to particle**, **without** the material as a whole moving. It is the main way heat travels through **solids**.
:::

**How it works:** particles at the hot end vibrate more vigorously and pass energy to their neighbours by collisions. In **metals**, **free electrons** also carry energy quickly through the material, which is why metals are the best conductors.

| Good conductors | Bad conductors (insulators) |
|---|---|
| metals: copper, aluminium, iron, silver | wood, plastic, rubber, cloth, wool |
| | still air, glass, water, cork, polystyrene |

**Uses of good conductors:** cooking pots and kettles (aluminium, steel), soldering-iron bits (copper), heat sinks in electronics.

**Uses of bad conductors:** wooden or plastic pot handles, oven gloves, lagging round hot-water pipes and tanks, woollen clothes and blankets (trapped air), polystyrene cool boxes, thatched roofs.

:::callout{type=local-example}
A metal bench and a wooden bench in the same shade feel different to sit on: the metal feels colder, although both are at the same temperature. Metal conducts heat away from your skin much faster than wood.
:::

**Experiment — comparing conductors:** rods of copper, aluminium, iron and glass of equal length and thickness are coated with wax and fixed through holes in the side of a trough of hot water. The wax melts furthest along the **best conductor** (copper) in a given time.

**Water is a poor conductor:** ice wrapped in gauze and held at the bottom of a tube of water does not melt while the water at the top is boiled.

## Convection

:::callout{type=definition}
**Convection** is the transfer of heat in a **fluid** (liquid or gas) by the **movement of the fluid itself**. Warmer, less dense fluid rises and cooler, denser fluid sinks to take its place, forming **convection currents**.
:::

Convection **cannot** happen in solids (the particles cannot flow) or in a vacuum.

**Experiment:** drop a crystal of potassium permanganate into a beaker of water near one side and heat gently beneath it. Purple streaks rise above the crystal, move across the top and sink on the other side, showing the convection current.

**Examples:**

- a pot of soup heats throughout because heating at the bottom sets up convection currents
- the heating element of an electric kettle is at the **bottom**; the freezer compartment of a fridge is at the **top**
- smoke rising from a chimney or a cooking fire
- **sea and land breezes** (next lesson)
- ventilation: warm, stale air leaves through high windows, cool air enters lower down

## Radiation

:::callout{type=definition}
**Radiation** is the transfer of heat by **electromagnetic waves** (infrared radiation). It **needs no medium** and can travel through a **vacuum** at the speed of light.
:::

This is how the Sun's heat reaches the Earth. All bodies emit radiation; hotter bodies emit more.

### Good and bad emitters and absorbers

| Surface | Emitting radiation | Absorbing radiation |
|---|---|---|
| Dull black | **best** emitter | **best** absorber |
| Shiny white or silvered | **worst** emitter | **worst** absorber (best reflector) |

**Leslie's cube experiment:** a metal cube with four different faces (dull black, shiny silver, white, grey) is filled with boiling water. A thermopile (or a hand) held at the same distance from each face shows that the **dull black** face radiates most and the **shiny** face least.

**Absorption experiment:** two metal plates, one dull black and one shiny, each have a coin stuck to the back with wax and are placed equal distances from a heater. The wax behind the **black** plate melts first, and its coin falls first.

**Applications:**

- people wear **white or light-coloured clothes** in the hot sun to reflect radiation
- the inside of a vacuum flask is **silvered** to reduce radiation
- **solar water heaters** and the collectors of solar cookers are painted **dull black**
- fuel storage tanks are painted **white or silver**
- cooling fins at the back of a fridge are painted **black** to radiate heat well

## Comparing the three methods

| | Conduction | Convection | Radiation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medium needed? | yes | yes (a fluid) | no |
| Main in | solids | liquids and gases | through space and air |
| Mechanism | particle-to-particle collisions (and free electrons) | movement of the fluid itself | electromagnetic (infrared) waves |
| Speed | slow | fairly slow | very fast (speed of light) |

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Good conductor**: a material that lets heat pass through it easily; metals are the best.
- **Bad conductor** (insulator): a material that conducts heat poorly, such as wood, plastic or still air.
- **Convection current**: the flow of a fluid in which hot fluid rises and cold fluid sinks.
:::

## Key points

- **Conduction**: particle to particle, mainly in solids; metals are the best conductors.
- **Convection**: fluid currents; hot fluid rises, cold fluid sinks.
- **Radiation**: infrared waves, no medium needed; dull black surfaces are the best emitters and absorbers, shiny ones the worst.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
**Explain each method of heat transfer**, **describe an experiment** for each (comparing conductors, potassium permanganate convection, Leslie's cube or the two-plate absorption test), and **apply** them: why cooking pots have wooden handles, why kettle elements are at the bottom, why white clothes are worn in the tropics. Remember: only radiation crosses a vacuum.
:::

## Quick check

1. Which method of heat transfer can take place through a vacuum?
2. Why is the heating element of a kettle placed at the bottom?
3. Which surface is the best absorber of radiation?

**Answers:** 1. Radiation. 2. Heated water at the bottom rises and cold water sinks to be heated, so convection currents heat all the water. 3. A dull black surface.
