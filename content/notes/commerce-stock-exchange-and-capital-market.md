# The stock exchange and capital market: securities and investment

## Learning objectives
Explain the capital market and stock exchange, distinguish shares from debentures and describe the roles of investors and intermediaries.

## Capital market and stock exchange
The capital market is the part of the financial system through which longer-term funds are raised and invested. It connects organisations seeking finance with investors willing to provide funds. A stock exchange is an organised market where eligible securities can be listed and traded under established rules. Not every capital-market transaction takes place on a stock exchange.

The **primary market** is where securities are issued to investors for the first time and the issuer raises funds. The **secondary market** is where existing securities are bought and sold between investors. Secondary trading can provide liquidity and price information, but the company does not normally receive proceeds from each later trade.

## Main securities
**Shares** represent ownership in a company. Ordinary shareholders may receive dividends if declared and may have voting rights under the company's rules. Dividends are not guaranteed, and share prices can rise or fall.

**Debentures and bonds** are debt securities. The issuer borrows money and promises to pay interest or other returns under the terms, and to repay principal according to the agreement. Debt investors are creditors, not owners, merely because they hold the security.

## Participants and functions
An issuer raises capital by offering securities. Investors provide funds in the hope of income or capital growth. Stockbrokers execute buy and sell orders for clients under applicable rules. Regulators oversee market conduct, disclosure and investor protection. Registrars maintain ownership records, while custodians may safeguard financial assets.

Capital markets can mobilise savings, finance business expansion and infrastructure, support investment opportunities and provide a market for securities. They also involve risks: prices may fall, issuers may fail to pay, markets may be illiquid and investors may lose some or all of their capital.

## Responsible investment
Investors should understand the issuer, financial statements, fees, liquidity, risk, time horizon and diversification. They should use properly authorised intermediaries and avoid promises of guaranteed high returns. A listed security is not automatically safe, and past performance does not guarantee future results.

## WASSCE examination focus
A shareholder owns part of a company; a debenture holder is a creditor. Distinguish the primary market, where new securities raise funds for the issuer, from the secondary market, where existing securities change hands.

## Quick check
1. What is a stock exchange?
2. Distinguish primary and secondary markets.
3. State two differences between shares and debentures.
4. Give three risks of investing in securities.

**Answers:** 1. An organised market for listing and trading eligible securities under established rules. 2. Primary markets issue new securities and raise funds for issuers; secondary markets trade existing securities between investors. 3. Shares represent ownership and dividends are not guaranteed; debentures are debt with contractual repayment terms. 4. Market-price loss, issuer default, low liquidity and fraud.
