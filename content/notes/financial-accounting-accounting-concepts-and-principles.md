# Accounting concepts and principles: foundations of reliable records

## Learning objectives
You should be able to explain the purpose of accounting concepts; describe key concepts used in preparing financial statements; apply them to simple situations; and distinguish concepts from accounting procedures.

## Purpose of accounting concepts
Accounting concepts are fundamental assumptions and conventions that guide the recording and reporting of financial transactions. They promote consistency, comparability and faithful communication. They do not replace the need for evidence, correct calculations or applicable accounting standards.

## Important concepts
The business entity concept treats the business as separate from its owner for accounting purposes. If an owner takes cash for personal use, it is recorded as drawings rather than as a business expense. This separation helps measure business performance and financial position.

The going concern concept assumes that the business will continue operating for the foreseeable future unless evidence suggests otherwise. This assumption supports the use of normal asset values and depreciation over useful lives rather than assuming immediate liquidation.

The money measurement concept records transactions that can be measured reliably in monetary terms. Staff skill and customer loyalty may be valuable, but they are not normally recorded as assets simply because they are important if they cannot be measured and recognised under the relevant rules.

The historical cost concept records an asset initially at the amount paid or the value of consideration given, subject to applicable standards and later measurement requirements. The dual aspect concept states that every transaction has two-sided effects and underpins the accounting equation:

Assets = Capital + Liabilities

The accrual or matching concept recognises income when earned and expenses when incurred in the relevant period, rather than simply when cash changes hands. For example, electricity used in March but paid for in April is an expense of the period in which it was consumed. The prudence concept requires caution under uncertainty so that assets and income are not overstated and liabilities and expenses are not understated. Prudence does not justify deliberate understatement.

The consistency concept supports applying accounting policies consistently from period to period so that performance can be compared. A change may be justified when required or when it produces more reliable and relevant information, with appropriate disclosure. The materiality concept focuses attention on information that could influence users' decisions; insignificant items may be treated in a practical way where standards permit.

## Applying concepts
Suppose the owner pays a personal school fee from the business bank account. The entity concept requires this to be treated as drawings, not a business operating expense. If goods are sold on credit in June and the customer pays in July, accrual accounting recognises the sale in June when the performance obligation is met under the applicable rules, not merely when cash is received.

## WASSCE examination focus
Learn each concept with a clear definition and a short practical example. Do not confuse the business entity concept with legal incorporation; it is an accounting separation. When asked to explain a transaction, identify the concept and show how it affects the records.

## Check your understanding
1. State the accounting equation.
2. Explain the business entity concept.
3. Which concept supports recording credit sales in the period earned?
4. Why is consistency useful?
5. Explain prudence without suggesting intentional understatement.
