**Sierra Leone's economy** depends on **agriculture**, which employs most people, and **mining**, which earns most of its exports, with smaller **manufacturing**, **energy**, **tourism** and **service** sectors. The country is rich in natural resources, but **economic development** has been held back by the **civil war (1991–2002)**, the **Ebola outbreak (2014–2016)**, poor **infrastructure**, unreliable **power**, dependence on a few **mineral exports** and **unemployment**. This lesson brings the sectors together, explains the problems of development and the prospects for the future.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the main sectors of Sierra Leone's economy
- describe the importance of mining and the main mineral exports
- describe manufacturing, energy, transport and tourism in Sierra Leone
- explain the problems of economic development in Sierra Leone
- suggest ways of promoting development

## The sectors of the economy

| Sector | Main activities | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | farming, fishing, forestry, **mining** | employs most people; mining earns most exports |
| **Secondary** | brewing, soft drinks, soap, paint, plastics, cement, flour, rice and palm-oil milling | small; mainly at **Wellington, Freetown** |
| **Tertiary** | trade, transport, banking, government, education, health, telecommunications, **tourism** | growing, mostly in towns; large **informal sector** of petty traders |

## Mining

Mining is Sierra Leone's main source of **export earnings**:

- **Iron ore** at **Tonkolili** and **Marampa**, by railway to **Pepel**.
- **Diamonds** in **Kono** and around **Tongo** (Kenema District), mostly alluvial.
- **Rutile and ilmenite** in **Moyamba and Bonthe** districts.
- **Bauxite** at **Mokanji**, Moyamba District.
- **Gold** at **Baomahun** and in many artisanal sites.

See the lessons on mining for methods and effects.

## Energy, transport and tourism

- **Energy:** electricity from **Bumbuna** (50 MW) and other hydro stations, thermal plants (including a power ship off Freetown), imports through the **CLSG** regional line, and solar mini-grids; most households cook with **firewood and charcoal**.
- **Transport:** roads carry most traffic; the **Queen Elizabeth II Quay** in Freetown is the main port; **Lungi International Airport** (new terminal in 2023) handles international flights.
- **Tourism:** beaches, islands, **Bunce Island**, Tacugama, Tiwai, Gola and the Loma Mountains offer great potential, but visitor numbers are still small.

## Importance of resources to development

1. **Foreign exchange** from mineral and agricultural exports.
2. **Government revenue** from royalties, taxes and licences.
3. **Employment** in farming, mining and services.
4. **Food** from farms and fisheries.
5. **Raw materials** for local industries (palm oil for soap, rice for milling).
6. **Infrastructure** built for mines and power.

## Problems of economic development

| Problem | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Effects of the civil war** | destroyed roads, schools, hospitals, farms and power lines; killed and displaced many people |
| **Ebola (2014–2016)** | killed nearly 4,000 people in Sierra Leone, closed schools and markets and frightened away investors |
| **Dependence on a few mineral exports** | earnings rise and fall with **world prices** |
| **Poor infrastructure** | bad roads, unreliable electricity and water |
| **Low agricultural productivity** | the country still imports much of its rice |
| **Unemployment** | especially among young people |
| **Shortage of skilled labour** | limited technical training |
| **Corruption and smuggling** | reduce government revenue |
| **Natural disasters** | floods, landslides (Regent, 2017) |
| **High inflation and a weak currency** | raise prices and costs; the leone was redenominated (three zeros removed) in **2022** |

## Promoting development

1. **Diversify the economy** away from minerals into agriculture, fisheries, tourism and manufacturing.
2. **Improve agriculture**, especially swamp rice, under programmes such as **Feed Salone** (2023).
3. **Process minerals and crops locally** to add value.
4. **Invest in power, roads and ports.**
5. **Expand education and skills training**; the **Free Quality School Education** programme began in **2018**.
6. **Fight corruption and smuggling**, and manage mineral revenues openly.
7. **Attract investment** through stability and good laws.
8. **Develop tourism** and protect the environment.
9. **Regional cooperation** through ECOWAS and the Mano River Union.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone is a good example of the **"resource paradox"**: a country rich in diamonds, iron ore, rutile, gold, fertile swamps and fish, yet with many people living in poverty. Turning resources into development depends on infrastructure, skills, good governance and adding value at home rather than exporting raw materials.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not say "mining employs most people". **Agriculture** employs the most people; **mining** earns the most **export revenue**. Keep the two ideas separate.
:::

## Key points

- **Agriculture** employs most people; **mining** earns most exports; manufacturing is small and centred on **Freetown**.
- Minerals: **iron ore, diamonds, rutile, ilmenite, bauxite, gold**.
- Problems: **war damage, Ebola, mineral dependence, poor infrastructure, low farm output, unemployment, corruption, disasters, inflation**.
- Solutions: **diversify, improve agriculture, add value, infrastructure, education, good governance, tourism, regional cooperation**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **describe the economic resources of Sierra Leone**, to **explain their importance**, and to **discuss the problems of economic development and suggest solutions**. A sketch map of resources and a balanced essay on problems and prospects score well.
:::

## Quick check

1. Which sector employs most people in Sierra Leone?
2. Which sector earns most of Sierra Leone's exports?
3. Give two effects of the civil war on the economy.
4. Why is dependence on mineral exports a problem?
5. Suggest three ways of promoting economic development in Sierra Leone.

**Answers:**

1. Agriculture.
2. Mining.
3. Any two: destroyed infrastructure; damaged farms; killed and displaced people; discouraged investment.
4. Earnings rise and fall with world prices, and minerals run out.
5. Any three: diversify the economy; improve agriculture; process raw materials locally; invest in power and roads; education and training; fight corruption and smuggling; attract investment; develop tourism; regional cooperation.
