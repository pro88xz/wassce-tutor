The **factors affecting the location of industries** are the conditions that decide where a factory or industry is set up: **raw materials**, **power**, **labour**, **market**, **transport**, **capital**, **water supply**, **land**, **government policy** and **early start**. An industry is usually located where the total cost of bringing in raw materials and sending out finished goods is lowest. This lesson explains each factor, the difference between raw-material-oriented and market-oriented industries, and how industries are classified.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define industry and manufacturing
- classify industries as primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary, and as light, heavy or cottage
- explain the factors that influence the location of industries
- distinguish raw-material-oriented, market-oriented and footloose industries
- explain industrial inertia and agglomeration
- give West African examples of each factor

## What is industry?

:::callout{type=definition}
An **industry** is any economic activity that produces goods or services. **Manufacturing** is the secondary activity of turning raw materials into finished or semi-finished goods in factories or workshops.
:::

### Types of industry

![Primary industries extract raw materials, secondary industries manufacture goods, and tertiary industries provide services, with examples such as cocoa farming, a chocolate factory and shops selling chocolate](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/industry-sectors.svg)

*Figure 1. Primary, secondary and tertiary industries, with chains of examples. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Type | What it does | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | extracts raw materials from nature | farming, fishing, mining, lumbering |
| **Secondary** | processes raw materials into goods | brewing, cement making, oil refining, furniture |
| **Tertiary** | provides services | transport, trade, banking, teaching, tourism |
| **Quaternary** | provides information and research | ICT, research, consultancy |

| By size and weight | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Heavy industry**: bulky raw materials and products, large plants | iron and steel, oil refining, cement, aluminium smelting |
| **Light industry**: light materials, small products | textiles, food processing, plastics, electronics assembly |
| **Cottage (craft) industry**: done at home with simple tools | gara tie-dye, country cloth weaving, blacksmithing, pottery, basket making |

## Factors influencing the location of industries

![Factors influencing industrial location arranged around the word industry: raw materials, power, labour, market, transport, capital, government policy, and water and land](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/industry-location.svg)

*Figure 2. Factors influencing the location of industries. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Factor | Explanation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Raw materials** | industries using heavy, bulky or perishable raw materials locate near them to cut transport costs | sawmills near forests; cement works near limestone (Ewekoro, Obajana in Nigeria) |
| **Power** | factories need a reliable, cheap supply of electricity or fuel | aluminium smelting at Tema using power from the Akosombo dam |
| **Labour** | skilled or cheap labour in enough numbers | textiles in Kano and Lagos |
| **Market** | industries making bulky, fragile or perishable products locate near customers | bakeries, breweries and soft-drink plants in large towns |
| **Transport** | good roads, railways and ports to bring in materials and send out goods | industries around the ports of Lagos, Tema and Abidjan |
| **Capital** | money to build and run factories; banks and investors are found in large cities | most factories are in capital cities |
| **Water supply** | for cooling, washing, processing and as an ingredient | breweries, textile mills, paper mills |
| **Land** | large, flat, cheap and well-drained sites | industrial estates on the edges of towns |
| **Government policy** | tax holidays, industrial estates, free zones and decisions to spread industry | export processing zones; industrial estates |
| **Early start (industrial inertia)** | once established, an industry tends to stay because of its buildings, workers and links, even if the original advantage is lost | older industries in established towns |
| **Agglomeration** | firms cluster together to share services, suppliers and skilled workers | the Ikeja industrial area in Lagos |
| **Security and political stability** | investors avoid unstable areas | |

### Raw-material, market and footloose industries

- **Raw-material-oriented (weight-losing) industries** lose a lot of weight in processing, so they locate **near the raw material**: sugar refining, cement, ore smelting, palm-oil mills, sawmills.
- **Market-oriented (weight-gaining) industries** gain weight or bulk, or make perishable or fragile goods, so they locate **near the market**: brewing and soft drinks (water added), bread, furniture assembly.
- **Footloose industries** are not tied to raw materials or markets because their inputs and products are light and valuable: electronics, software, clothing. They can locate almost anywhere with good transport and labour.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Why are breweries in Sierra Leone located in Freetown rather than in the countryside?**

A brewery is **market-oriented**: beer is mostly water, which is available almost everywhere, so the finished product is much heavier than the raw materials that must be brought in. Bottles are fragile and bulky. Locating near the largest market, Freetown, with its port, labour and power supply, keeps transport costs lowest. The **Sierra Leone Brewery** is at **Wellington**, on the eastern edge of Freetown.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Most of Sierra Leone's factories are in and around **Freetown**, especially the **Wellington Industrial Estate**, because Freetown has the main **port**, the largest **market**, the most reliable **electricity**, **banks** and a large **labour force**. Smaller processing industries are found near raw materials: rice and palm-oil mills in the provinces, and mineral processing at the mines.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not just list factors. Explain **why** each one attracts industry, and name an **example**. "Power: aluminium smelting needs huge amounts of electricity, so VALCO at Tema uses hydroelectric power from Akosombo" scores far more than "power".
:::

## Key points

- Industries are **primary, secondary, tertiary, quaternary**; **heavy, light, cottage**.
- Location factors: **raw materials, power, labour, market, transport, capital, water, land, government, early start, agglomeration, stability**.
- **Weight-losing** industries locate near raw materials; **weight-gaining** and perishable ones near markets; **footloose** ones almost anywhere.
- In Sierra Leone, most industry is in **Freetown** (Wellington Industrial Estate).

Expect to **explain the factors that influence the location of industries**, using a named industry or industrial area in West Africa, and to **classify industries**. Distinguishing raw-material-oriented from market-oriented industries, with examples, is often asked.
:::

## Quick check

1. Define manufacturing.
2. Give one example each of a primary, a secondary and a tertiary activity.
3. Why are cement works often located near limestone deposits?
4. What is a footloose industry?
5. Give three reasons why most industries in Sierra Leone are in Freetown.

**Answers:**

1. The processing of raw materials into finished or semi-finished goods.
2. Primary: farming, fishing, mining or lumbering. Secondary: brewing, cement making or furniture. Tertiary: transport, trade, banking or teaching.
3. Limestone is heavy and bulky and loses weight in processing, so it is cheaper to make cement near the raw material.
4. An industry that is not tied to raw materials or markets and can locate almost anywhere.
5. Any three: the main port; the largest market; more reliable power; banks and capital; a large labour force; government offices.
