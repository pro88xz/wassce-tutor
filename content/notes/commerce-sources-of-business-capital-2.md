# Cost of capital, working capital and financial planning

## Learning objectives
Distinguish fixed and working capital, compare sources of finance and calculate basic working-capital requirements.

## Fixed and working capital
**Fixed capital** finances long-term assets used repeatedly, such as buildings, vehicles, machinery and equipment. **Working capital** finances day-to-day operations: inventory, wages, utilities, transport and amounts owed by customers.

A business can be profitable on paper yet fail because it cannot pay bills when they fall due. This is a liquidity problem.

**Net working capital = Current assets − Current liabilities.**

If current assets are NLe 180,000 and current liabilities are NLe 125,000, net working capital is NLe 55,000.

## Matching finance to need
Long-term assets are generally financed with longer-term funds such as owner capital, retained profits, long-term loans or share capital. Seasonal inventory may be financed through retained cash, supplier credit or short-term bank facilities. Using a short-term loan to buy an asset that takes many years to generate cash can create repayment pressure.

## Comparing finance sources
| Source | Benefit | Cost or risk |
|---|---|---|
| Retained profit | No interest payment; owners retain control | Limited by past profitability |
| Bank loan | Predictable finance for planned investment | Interest, security and repayment obligations |
| Trade credit | Allows stock to be received before payment | Late payment may damage supplier relations or incur charges |
| Leasing | Use of equipment without full purchase price upfront | Regular payments and contractual restrictions |
| New shares | Can raise permanent risk capital | Ownership and control may be diluted |
| Owner's savings | Simple and independent | Personal funds are exposed |

## Cash-flow planning
A cash-flow forecast estimates cash receipts and payments over a period. It helps identify months when a business may face a shortage. The owner can respond by negotiating supplier terms, collecting debts earlier, reducing unnecessary stock, arranging suitable finance or postponing non-essential spending.

Profit and cash are not the same: a credit sale may create revenue before cash is collected.

## WASSCE examination focus
Clearly distinguish capital expenditure from working expenditure, and profit from cash flow. Show formula, substitution and units when calculating working capital.

## Quick check
1. Distinguish fixed capital from working capital.
2. Calculate net working capital if current assets are NLe 96,000 and current liabilities are NLe 61,000.
3. State two disadvantages of borrowing.
4. Why can a profitable firm experience a cash shortage?

**Answers:** 1. Fixed capital buys long-term assets; working capital supports daily operations. 2. NLe 35,000. 3. Interest and compulsory repayments; security may be required. 4. Customers may not yet have paid for credit sales while wages and suppliers must be paid now.
