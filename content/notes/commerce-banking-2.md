A supplier in Freetown can be paid by cash, cheque, bank transfer, card, standing order or mobile money. Each method has its own rules, risks and costs, and the cheque has the most rules of all. This lesson explains how cheques work, why they may be dishonoured, and the other means of payment businesses use today.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a cheque and name its parties
- distinguish open, crossed, bearer and order cheques
- explain endorsement
- explain why cheques are dishonoured and define stale and post-dated cheques
- describe other means of payment: bank draft, standing order, direct debit, transfers, cards, POS and mobile money

## The cheque

:::callout{type=definition}
A **cheque** is an **unconditional order in writing**, addressed by a customer to a bank, **signed** by the customer, ordering the bank to **pay on demand** a stated sum of money to a named person or to the bearer.
:::

![A cheque drawn on ABC Bank, Freetown, by F. Conteh, paying Kamara Hardware Ltd two thousand leones, with the drawer, drawee and payee labelled](https://wasscetutor.com/img/commerce/cheque-parties.svg)

*Figure 1. The three parties to a cheque. The bank name is fictional. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Party | Who | In Figure 1 |
|---|---|---|
| **Drawer** | The account holder who writes and **signs** the cheque | F. Conteh |
| **Drawee** | The **bank** ordered to pay | ABC Bank |
| **Payee** | The person **to whom** the money is paid | Kamara Hardware Ltd |

**A valid cheque shows:** the date; the payee's name; the amount in **words** and **figures** (which must agree); and the drawer's **signature**, matching the bank's specimen.

## Types of cheque

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Open (uncrossed) cheque** | Can be **cashed over the counter** at the drawer's bank |
| **Crossed cheque** | Has **two parallel lines** across it; must be **paid into a bank account**, not cashed |
| **Bearer cheque** | Payable to whoever **holds** (bears) it; risky if lost |
| **Order cheque** | Payable only to the **named payee**, or to someone the payee endorses it to |
| **Certified cheque** | The bank confirms there are funds and sets them aside |

### Crossings

![Four cheques: an open cheque that can be cashed; a general crossing with two parallel lines and '& Co'; a special crossing naming a bank; and an 'account payee only' crossing](https://wasscetutor.com/img/commerce/cheque-crossings.svg)

*Figure 2. Open and crossed cheques. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Crossing | Effect |
|---|---|
| **General crossing**: two parallel lines, with or without "& Co" | Must be paid into **a** bank account |
| **Special crossing**: the name of a bank between the lines | Must be paid through **that named bank** |
| **"Account payee only"** | Must be paid only into the **payee's own** account |
| **"Not negotiable"** | Can be transferred, but the new holder gets **no better title** than the person who gave it (protects against theft) |

**Why cross a cheque?** If it is lost or stolen, a thief cannot cash it over the counter, and the money can be **traced** through the account it was paid into.

### Endorsement

:::callout{type=definition}
**Endorsement** is the payee's **signature on the back** of an order cheque to transfer it to someone else.
:::

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Blank endorsement**: just the signature, which makes the cheque payable to the bearer.
- **Special endorsement**: names the new payee ("Pay A. Sesay, signed B. Kamara").
- **Restrictive endorsement**: stops further transfer ("Pay A. Sesay only").
:::

## Dishonoured cheques

A cheque is **dishonoured** when the bank **refuses to pay** it. Common reasons:

1. **Insufficient funds**: the bank marks it **"R/D" (refer to drawer)**.
2. **Stale cheque**: presented more than **six months** after its date.
3. **Post-dated cheque**: dated in the **future**; it cannot be paid before that date.
4. **Words and figures differ**.
5. The **signature** differs from the specimen, or is missing.
6. **Payment stopped** (countermanded) by the drawer.
7. **Alterations** not signed by the drawer.
8. The drawer has **died**, become **bankrupt** or insane, or the account is **closed or frozen** by court order.
9. The cheque is **mutilated** or torn.
10. An order cheque has been **wrongly endorsed**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Will the bank pay?** Today is 12 November 2026.

| Cheque | Decision |
|---|---|
| Dated 12 March 2026, NLe 2,000, signed | **Stale**: more than 6 months old; returned |
| Dated 30 December 2026 | **Post-dated**: cannot be paid until 30 December |
| "Two thousand leones" in words, NLe 2,500 in figures | **Returned**: words and figures differ |
| Dated 1 November 2026, drawer has NLe 500 in the account, cheque for NLe 2,000 and no overdraft | **Dishonoured**: R/D, insufficient funds |
| Dated 1 November 2026, correct and funded, crossed "A/C payee only", presented at the counter for cash | **Not cashed**: must be paid into the payee's account |
:::

**Advantages of cheques:** safer than carrying cash; a convenient way to pay large or exact sums; crossed cheques are traceable; the counterfoil and bank statement give a **record**.

**Disadvantages:** they can be dishonoured; they are not legal tender, so sellers may refuse them; clearing takes time; there is a risk of fraud and forgery.

**Cheque clearing:** the payee's bank sends the cheque to the **clearing house** (run by the central bank). There, banks settle what they owe each other, and the money is moved from the drawer's account to the payee's.

## Other means of payment

| Method | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Bank draft** | A cheque drawn **by a bank on itself**, bought by the customer; almost certain to be paid | Large payments to people who will not accept a personal cheque (e.g. buying a vehicle, paying fees) |
| **Standing order** | Customer instructs the bank to pay a **fixed sum** to a named payee at **regular intervals** | Rent, loan repayments, subscriptions |
| **Direct debit** | Customer authorises the **payee** to take varying sums from the account when due | Bills that change in amount |
| **Credit transfer (bank giro)** | One payment slip pays several people at once | Salaries, paying many suppliers |
| **Electronic funds transfer** | Money moved between accounts online or by the bank | Fast payments between banks |
| **Debit / ATM card** | Withdraw cash at an ATM or pay at a **POS** terminal; money leaves the account at once | Shopping, cash at any time |
| **Credit card** | Buy now, pay the card company later; interest if not paid in full | Travel, online purchases |
| **Mobile money** | Send, receive and pay by phone (Orange Money, Afrimoney) | Small and medium payments everywhere, including rural areas |
| **Internet and mobile banking** | Customers make transfers and pay bills by computer or app | Business payments without visiting the bank |
| **Postal and money orders** | Bought at a post office and sent by post | Small sums by post |
| **Letter of credit** | Bank guarantees payment to a foreign exporter | Foreign trade |

| Standing order | Direct debit |
|---|---|
| A **fixed** amount | The amount can **vary** |
| The **customer** controls it and tells the bank | The **payee** claims the money, with the customer's authority |
| Example: monthly rent of NLe 1,500 | Example: an electricity or insurance bill |

:::callout{type=local-example}
In Sierra Leone, **mobile money** has become the most common way to send money across the country: traders pay suppliers, parents send school fees and customers pay at shops. Banks link accounts to mobile wallets, and **POS terminals** are now common in Freetown supermarkets and filling stations. Cheques are still used for large business and government payments.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A **stale** cheque is **too old** (more than six months); a **post-dated** cheque is dated in the **future**. Students often reverse them.
:::

## Key points

- A **cheque** has three parties: **drawer** (signs), **drawee** (bank) and **payee** (receives).
- **Crossed cheques** must go into a bank account; **"A/C payee only"** restricts it to the payee's account.
- Cheques are **dishonoured** for insufficient funds (R/D), staleness, post-dating, differing words and figures, a wrong signature, stopped payment and so on.
- Other means of payment: **bank draft, standing order, direct debit, credit transfer, cards, POS, mobile money and internet banking**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **name the parties to a cheque**, to **explain types of crossing**, to **give reasons why a bank may dishonour a cheque**, to **define stale and post-dated cheques**, and to **distinguish a standing order from a direct debit** or explain a bank draft. Draw a crossed cheque if asked.
:::

## Quick check

1. Who is the drawee of a cheque?
2. What is the effect of a general crossing?
3. Give three reasons why a cheque may be dishonoured.
4. Distinguish a standing order from a direct debit.

**Answers:** 1. The bank on which the cheque is drawn. 2. The cheque must be paid into a bank account; it cannot be cashed over the counter. 3. Any three: insufficient funds, stale, post-dated, words and figures differ, signature differs, payment stopped, unsigned alterations, drawer dead or bankrupt, mutilated, wrongly endorsed. 4. A standing order pays a fixed sum at regular intervals on the customer's instruction; a direct debit lets the payee collect varying amounts with the customer's authority.
