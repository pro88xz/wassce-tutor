# Bank accounts, credit facilities and safe business payments

## Learning objectives
Compare common bank accounts and credit facilities, explain the role of banks in business and identify safe payment practices.

## Common accounts
A **current account** supports frequent deposits and withdrawals and is commonly used by businesses to make and receive payments. A **savings account** encourages saving and may pay interest subject to the bank's terms. A **fixed or term deposit** places funds with a bank for an agreed period, often with restrictions on early withdrawal.

Businesses should compare fees, minimum balances, access, interest, transaction limits, digital services and deposit-protection arrangements where applicable.

## Credit facilities
An **overdraft** allows a customer to withdraw beyond the available account balance up to an agreed limit. It is flexible for short-term cash-flow needs but may be repayable on demand and can carry fees and interest.

A **term loan** provides a sum that is repaid over an agreed schedule. It may suit a planned asset purchase. Trade finance products can support imports and exports, subject to documentary and credit conditions.

Borrowing should be assessed against repayment capacity, total interest and fees, collateral, currency risk and the purpose of the facility.

## Payment systems
Businesses may use bank transfers, cards, cheques and regulated digital or mobile-money services. They should verify beneficiary details, reconcile statements, restrict payment authority, use strong authentication and keep transaction evidence. Staff should never share passwords or one-time verification codes.

## Bank reconciliation
A bank reconciliation compares the cash book with the bank statement and explains timing differences or errors, such as unpresented cheques, deposits not yet credited, bank charges and direct debits. It helps detect mistakes and unauthorised transactions.

## WASSCE examination focus
An overdraft is generally a flexible short-term facility; a term loan has an agreed repayment schedule. In reconciliation questions, identify whether an item appears first in the cash book or bank statement.

## Quick check
1. Distinguish a current account from a savings account.
2. Give one suitable use of an overdraft.
3. State three safe payment controls.
4. What is the purpose of bank reconciliation?

**Answers:** 1. Current accounts support frequent transactions; savings accounts are designed to hold savings and may earn interest. 2. Managing a temporary cash-flow shortage. 3. Verify beneficiaries, restrict authority, use strong authentication and reconcile transactions. 4. To explain differences, detect errors and identify unauthorised transactions.
