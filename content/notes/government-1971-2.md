The 1971 Republican Constitution made one office the centre of Sierra Leonean politics: the **executive presidency**. The President became head of state and head of government at once, with wide powers of appointment and command. This lesson explains the features of the 1971 Constitution, compares it with the 1961 Constitution, and looks at its effects, which led within seven years to a one-party state.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the main features of the 1971 Republican Constitution
- compare the 1961 and 1971 constitutions
- explain the merits and demerits of the 1971 Constitution
- explain how political developments after 1971 led to the one-party state

## Features of the 1971 Constitution

1. **Republic**: the British monarch ceased to be head of state.
2. **Executive President**: **head of state, head of government and commander-in-chief**.
3. **Vice-President** to assist the President.
4. A **Prime Minister** was retained, appointed by the President, as leader of government business in Parliament.
5. **Ministers** appointed by the President.
6. **Unicameral Parliament** of elected members and **Paramount Chief members**.
7. **Supreme Court** of Sierra Leone as the **final court of appeal**: appeals to the Privy Council ended.
8. **Multi-party system** remained in law.
9. **Fundamental human rights** provisions were retained.
10. Sierra Leone remained in the **Commonwealth** as a republic.

## The 1961 and 1971 constitutions compared

| Feature | 1961 | 1971 |
|---|---|---|
| **Form of state** | Constitutional monarchy | Republic |
| **Head of state** | British monarch (through Governor-General) | Executive President |
| **Head of government** | Prime Minister | Executive President (with a Prime Minister as assistant) |
| **System** | Parliamentary | Presidential features with some parliamentary elements |
| **Executive** | Dual | Effectively single |
| **Final court of appeal** | Privy Council, London | Supreme Court of Sierra Leone |
| **Removal of head of government** | Vote of no confidence | Special procedure for the President |

## Merits and demerits

| Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|
| Completed sovereignty: a Sierra Leonean head of state | **Concentrated power** in one person |
| Final justice decided at home | Weakened **checks and balances** |
| Clear lines of authority and quicker decisions | Parliament dominated by the President's party |
| Symbol of national pride | Encouraged **personal rule** and patronage |
| | Opened the way to the **one-party state** |

## Developments after 1971

- **1973** general election: amid violence and intimidation, the **SLPP withdrew**, leaving the APC almost unopposed.
- **1977**: student demonstrations in Freetown against the government, followed by a violent general election.
- **1978**: the government introduced a **one-party constitution**, making the APC the only lawful party.

These developments showed how a powerful presidency, combined with a dominant party and weak opposition, could undermine multi-party democracy.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Under the 1971 Constitution, **Sorie Ibrahim (S. I.) Koroma** served as Vice-President and Prime Minister, and **Christian Alusine Kamara-Taylor** later became Prime Minister. Both were senior APC figures. Their offices show that the constitution kept some parliamentary features, but real power had moved to the **executive President, Siaka Stevens**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The 1971 Constitution did **not** abolish multi-party politics. Opposition parties remained legal until the **1978** Constitution. What changed in 1971 was the **head of state** and the concentration of executive power in the **President**.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Executive President**: a president who is head of state, head of government and commander-in-chief, as Siaka Stevens became in 1971.
- **Constitutional monarchy**: the form of state before 1971, in which the British monarch was head of state through a Governor-General.
- **Vote of no confidence**: a vote by Parliament that removes the head of government, as under the 1961 Constitution.
- **Supreme Court**: the court that became Sierra Leone's final court of appeal in 1971, when appeals to the Privy Council ended.
:::

## Key points

- The **1971 Constitution** created an **executive President** (head of state and government), with a **Vice-President** and a **Prime Minister**.
- The **Supreme Court** became the final court; **Privy Council appeals ended**.
- It **concentrated power** and weakened checks and balances.
- **1973** SLPP withdrawal, **1977** protests and violence, and **1978** one-party state followed.

## Quick check

1. Who was head of state under the 1971 Constitution?
2. What happened to appeals to the Privy Council in 1971?
3. State two differences between the 1961 and 1971 constitutions.
4. Give two demerits of the 1971 Constitution.

**Answers:**

1. The executive President.
2. They ended; the Supreme Court of Sierra Leone became the final court of appeal.
3. Any two: monarchy versus republic; British monarch versus executive President as head of state; dual versus effectively single executive; Privy Council versus Supreme Court as final court; vote of no confidence versus special removal procedure.
4. Any two: concentrated power in one person; weakened checks and balances; Parliament dominated by the President's party; encouraged personal rule; opened the way to the one-party state.
