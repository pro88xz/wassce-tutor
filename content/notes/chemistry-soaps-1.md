Long before soap came in wrapped bars, people in West Africa made it by boiling palm oil with lye from plant ashes. The chemistry has not changed: a **fat or oil** is boiled with an **alkali**, and the ester splits into **glycerol** and **soap**. This lesson covers where fats and oils come from, how they are used, and how soap is made.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name sources of fats and oils and describe how oils are extracted
- state the uses of fats and oils
- describe saponification with a word equation and a general equation
- describe the industrial and local manufacture of soap, including salting out
- explain the difference between sodium and potassium soaps
- calculate masses in saponification

## Sources and extraction of fats and oils

Fats and oils are **triglycerides**: esters of **glycerol** with three **long-chain fatty acids** (see the Esters topic).

| Source | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Plant seeds and fruits** | Palm oil, palm kernel oil, coconut oil, groundnut oil, sesame (benniseed) oil, shea butter, cottonseed oil |
| **Animals** | Butter, lard (pig fat), tallow (beef fat), fish oil, cod-liver oil |

**Extraction methods:**

1. **Boiling (rendering) and skimming:** fruits or animal fat are boiled in water; the oil floats and is skimmed off. This is the traditional method for palm oil.
2. **Pressing (expression):** seeds or fruit pulp are crushed in a press (groundnuts, palm kernels).
3. **Solvent extraction:** crushed seeds are soaked in a volatile organic solvent such as hexane, which dissolves the oil; the solvent is then distilled off and reused. This gives the highest yield.

## Uses of fats and oils

- **Food:** cooking oils, margarine, butter; a concentrated energy source (about 37 kJ/g)
- **Soap** and **detergent** making
- **Cosmetics:** creams, lotions, hair oils (shea butter, coconut oil)
- **Paints and varnishes:** highly unsaturated "drying oils" such as linseed oil harden in air
- **Fuel:** **biodiesel** made from vegetable oils; oil lamps
- **Lubricants** and candles

## Saponification

:::callout{type=definition}
**Saponification** is the **alkaline hydrolysis** of a fat or oil. Boiling a fat or oil with **sodium hydroxide** (or potassium hydroxide) solution gives **glycerol** and **soap**, the sodium (or potassium) salt of a fatty acid.
:::

$$\text{fat or oil} + \text{sodium hydroxide} \rightarrow \text{glycerol} + \text{soap}$$

![A triglyceride boiled with three NaOH gives glycerol and three molecules of soap; below, the steps of boiling, salting out, skimming and finishing](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/saponification.svg)

*Figure 1. Saponification and the steps of soap making. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

For glyceryl tristearate (a fat made from stearic acid):

$$(\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COO})_3\text{C}_3\text{H}_5 + 3\text{NaOH} \rightarrow 3\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COONa} + \text{C}_3\text{H}_5(\text{OH})_3$$

The products are **sodium stearate** (soap) and **glycerol** (propane-1,2,3-triol).

## Manufacture of soap

1. **Boiling:** the fat or oil is boiled with **concentrated sodium hydroxide** solution, stirring, until saponification is complete.
2. **Salting out:** **concentrated sodium chloride solution (brine)** or solid salt is added. Soap is much less soluble in salt water, so it separates as a **curd** that floats on top.
3. **Separation:** the soap curd is skimmed off. The liquid below contains **glycerol**, salt and excess alkali; glycerol is recovered from it.
4. **Finishing:** the soap is washed, then **perfume, colouring, antiseptics or fillers** are added, and it is moulded and cut into bars.

**By-product:** **glycerol** is valuable. It is used in cosmetics, medicines, sweets and to make explosives (nitroglycerine).

| Soap type | Made with | Properties |
|---|---|---|
| **Sodium soap** | Sodium hydroxide (caustic soda) | **Hard** bar soap |
| **Potassium soap** | Potassium hydroxide (caustic potash) | **Soft** or liquid soap; shaving soap, liquid hand soap |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Soap from a fat.** What mass of sodium stearate is formed when 89 g of glyceryl tristearate is saponified completely? (Mᵣ: glyceryl tristearate = 890; sodium stearate = 306)

- Moles of fat $= 89 \div 890 = 0.10$ mol
- 1 mol of fat gives 3 mol of soap: soap $= 0.30$ mol
- Mass $= 0.30 \times 306 = 91.8$ g
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Traditional **"black soap"** (country soap) is still made in villages across Sierra Leone and West Africa. Ash from burnt cocoa pods, palm-bunch stalks or plantain peels is soaked in water; the liquid that drips through is a solution of **potassium carbonate and hydroxide** (potash lye). Boiled with palm oil or palm kernel oil, it saponifies the oil to a soft, dark **potassium soap**. Industrial soap factories do the same reaction with pure sodium hydroxide and salt out the soap with brine.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Salting out does **not** react with the soap. It simply makes the soap **less soluble**, so it separates from the glycerol solution. The salt is NaCl, not NaOH.
:::

## Key points

- Fats and oils come from **plants** (palm, groundnut, coconut, shea) and **animals** (butter, lard, tallow); oils are extracted by **boiling and skimming, pressing or solvent extraction**.
- Uses: **food, soap, margarine, cosmetics, paints, biodiesel**.
- **Saponification:** fat or oil + NaOH → **glycerol + soap** (sodium salt of a fatty acid).
- Soap making: **boil** with NaOH → **salt out** with brine → skim the curd → add perfume and colour; **glycerol** is a by-product.
- **Sodium** soaps are hard; **potassium** soaps are soft.

## Quick check

1. What is saponification?
2. Why is sodium chloride added during soap making?
3. Name the by-product of soap manufacture and give one use.
4. Why is liquid soap usually made with potassium hydroxide?

**Answers:**

1. The alkaline hydrolysis of a fat or oil to form glycerol and soap.
2. To salt out the soap: soap is less soluble in brine, so it separates as a curd that can be skimmed off.
3. Glycerol (propane-1,2,3-triol); used in cosmetics, medicines, sweets or explosives.
4. Potassium soaps are softer and more soluble than sodium soaps.
