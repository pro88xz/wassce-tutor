**Reconciling a control account with the ledger** means finding out why the control account balance and the **total of the individual balances** in the ledger disagree, and correcting both until they agree. Some errors affect **only the control account** (such as a day book total cast wrongly), some affect **only the list of ledger balances** (such as one customer's account posted wrongly), and some affect **both**. This lesson shows how to sort the errors, correct each side, and record **set-offs (contra entries)** between the sales and purchases ledgers.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain set-offs (contra entries) and record them
- decide whether an error affects the control account, the list of balances, or both
- prepare a corrected control account and a corrected list of balances
- reconcile the two totals

## Set-offs (contra entries)

A **set-off** happens when a business both **buys from** and **sells to** the same firm. Instead of paying each other in full, the two balances are **set off** and only the difference is paid.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Kamara Ltd owes the business NLe 2,000 (sales ledger) and the business owes Kamara Ltd NLe 5,000 (purchases ledger). Record the set-off.**

The smaller amount, NLe 2,000, is set off:

- **Personal accounts:** debit Kamara (purchases ledger) 2,000; credit Kamara (sales ledger) 2,000.
- **Control accounts:** **debit** purchases ledger control 2,000; **credit** sales ledger control 2,000.

Afterwards the business owes Kamara Ltd only NLe 3,000, and Kamara owes the business nothing.
:::

## Which side does an error affect?

| Error | Affects |
|---|---|
| a day book, cash book or returns book **total** cast wrongly | **control account only** (it uses totals) |
| a transaction **posted wrongly to one customer's** account | **list of balances only** |
| a customer's balance **listed wrongly** or **left out** of the list | **list of balances only** |
| a transaction **left out of the books completely** | **both** |
| a bad debt written off in the customer's account but **not** in the journal | **control account only** |

## Worked example

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**At 31 January 2026, Mansaray Traders' sales ledger control account showed a balance of NLe 59,000, but the list of customers' balances totalled NLe 57,650. The following errors were found:**

1. A credit sale of 900 entered in the sales day book was not posted to the customer's account.
2. The sales day book was overcast by 600.
3. A customer's debit balance of 850 was listed as 1,000.

**Prepare (a) the corrected control account balance and (b) the corrected list total.**
:::

**(a) Corrected sales ledger control account**

| | NLe |
|---|---|
| Balance as given | 59,000 |
| Less: sales day book overcast (2) | (600) |
| **Corrected balance** | **58,400** |

Error 1 does **not** affect the control account: the sale was included in the day book total. Error 3 affects only the list.

**(b) Corrected list of balances**

| | NLe |
|---|---|
| Total as given | 57,650 |
| Add: sale not posted to the customer (1) | 900 |
| | 58,550 |
| Less: balance overstated, 1,000 − 850 (3) | (150) |
| **Corrected total** | **58,400** |

Both now show **NLe 58,400**: the sales ledger is reconciled ✓.

## Why the method works

The control account is built from **totals** in the books of original entry; the list is built from the **individual accounts**. An error in a total upsets only the control account; an error in one personal account upsets only the list. Sorting each error before correcting it avoids double counting.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not correct the control account for an error made only in an individual customer's account, or the list for an error in a day book total. Decide first **where** the error was made, then correct only that side.
:::

## Key points

- A **set-off**: debit purchases ledger control, credit sales ledger control, with the smaller balance.
- **Total errors** (day books, cash book) affect the **control account**; **individual posting and listing errors** affect the **list of balances**.
- Correct each side separately until the **two totals agree**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **prepare a corrected control account and a corrected list of balances** from a list of errors, and to **record set-offs**. Set out the control account adjustments and the list adjustments in two separate statements, and show that the final totals agree.
:::

## Quick check

1. What is a set-off?
2. Give the control account entries for a set-off of NLe 1,500.
3. The returns inwards book was undercast by NLe 300. Which side is affected, and how?
4. A customer's balance of NLe 600 was left out of the list. What is corrected?
5. Why might the control account and the list of balances disagree?

**Answers:**

1. The cancelling of a customer's balance against the same firm's supplier balance, so only the difference is paid.
2. Debit purchases ledger control 1,500; credit sales ledger control 1,500.
3. The control account: returns inwards (a credit) should be 300 more, so the control balance is reduced by NLe 300.
4. The list of balances: add NLe 600.
5. Because of errors in the day book or cash book totals (affecting the control account) or in individual accounts or the listing (affecting the list).
