A land dispute in a chiefdom may start in a Local Court, go up to a Magistrate Court or the High Court, then the Court of Appeal, and finally reach the Supreme Court in Freetown. Each level has its own powers, and each can correct the mistakes of the court below. This lesson describes the courts of Sierra Leone, what each one does and how appeals move up the system.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the hierarchy of courts in Sierra Leone
- explain the composition and jurisdiction of the Supreme Court, Court of Appeal and High Court
- explain the work of Magistrate Courts and Local Courts
- distinguish original and appellate jurisdiction
- describe special courts, including the Special Court for Sierra Leone

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition}
**Jurisdiction** is the authority of a court to hear and decide particular kinds of cases. **Original jurisdiction** is the power to hear a case for the first time; **appellate jurisdiction** is the power to hear appeals from lower courts.
:::

## The hierarchy of courts

![A ladder of courts from top to bottom: Supreme Court, Court of Appeal, High Court (the superior courts), then Magistrate Courts and Local Courts (the lower courts). Appeals move upward](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/sl-court-hierarchy.svg)

*Figure 1. The courts of Sierra Leone. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

The **Superior Court of Judicature** consists of the **Supreme Court**, the **Court of Appeal** and the **High Court**. Below these are the **subordinate courts**: Magistrate Courts and Local Courts.

### The Supreme Court

- **Highest court** in Sierra Leone and the **final court of appeal**.
- Headed by the **Chief Justice**, who sits with other Justices of the Supreme Court.
- **Original jurisdiction:** interpreting and enforcing the **Constitution**, and deciding whether laws or actions are constitutional.
- **Appellate jurisdiction:** hears appeals from the Court of Appeal.
- Its decisions **bind all other courts**, though it may depart from its own earlier decisions.

### The Court of Appeal

- Hears **appeals from the High Court** in civil and criminal cases.
- Made up of the Chief Justice and the **Justices of Appeal**.

### The High Court

- Has **original jurisdiction** in serious criminal cases (such as murder and robbery with violence) and major civil cases.
- Hears **appeals** from Magistrate Courts and Local Courts.
- Hears **petitions challenging the election of Members of Parliament** and applications to **enforce fundamental human rights**.
- Has specialised divisions, such as the **Commercial Court** for business disputes.

### Magistrate Courts

- Found in Freetown and the district headquarters.
- Try **less serious criminal offences** and **civil claims below a set value**.
- Hold **preliminary investigations** in serious criminal cases before they go to the High Court.

### Local Courts

- Sit in the **chiefdoms** and apply **customary law**, for example in land, family, inheritance and minor disputes.
- Under the **Local Courts Act 2011**, they are part of the national judiciary rather than being run by chiefs.
- Appeals go first to a **District Appeal Court** presided over by a magistrate, and then to the High Court.

## Other courts and tribunals

| Court | Work |
|---|---|
| **Juvenile courts** | Hear cases involving children, in private and with an emphasis on rehabilitation |
| **Court martial** | Tries members of the armed forces under military law |
| **Sexual Offences Model Court** | A specialised court set up to speed up sexual-violence cases |
| **Special Court for Sierra Leone** | An international court (2002–2013) that tried those most responsible for crimes during the civil war |

:::callout{type=local-example}
The **Special Court for Sierra Leone** was created in **2002** by agreement between the Government of Sierra Leone and the **United Nations**. It tried leaders of the RUF, the AFRC and the CDF for war crimes and crimes against humanity committed during the civil war. In **2012** it convicted former Liberian President **Charles Taylor**, the first former head of state convicted by an international court since the Nuremberg trials. His trial was held in The Hague for security reasons.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Following an appeal.** A man is convicted of robbery with violence in the **High Court** in Kenema. He believes the judge misdirected the jury. His appeal goes to the **Court of Appeal**. If it upholds the conviction, he may appeal finally to the **Supreme Court**, whose decision is final. At each step, the higher court can **confirm, reverse or vary** the decision below.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The **Court of Appeal** is not the highest court in Sierra Leone. The **Supreme Court** is the final court of appeal. Also, **Local Courts** apply customary law, not English common law; the superior courts apply the Constitution, statutes, common law and customary law.
:::

## Key points

- Superior courts: **Supreme Court → Court of Appeal → High Court**; lower courts: **Magistrate Courts → Local Courts**.
- The **Supreme Court**, headed by the **Chief Justice**, is the **final court of appeal** and interprets the **Constitution**.
- The **High Court** tries serious cases, hears appeals from lower courts and handles **parliamentary election petitions**.
- **Local Courts** apply **customary law** in the chiefdoms (**Local Courts Act 2011**).
- The **Special Court for Sierra Leone (2002–2013)** convicted **Charles Taylor** in **2012**.

## Quick check

1. Which court is the final court of appeal in Sierra Leone?
2. Distinguish original and appellate jurisdiction.
3. What kind of law do Local Courts apply?
4. Which former head of state was convicted by the Special Court for Sierra Leone?

**Answers:**

1. The Supreme Court.
2. Original jurisdiction is the power to hear a case for the first time; appellate jurisdiction is the power to hear appeals from lower courts.
3. Customary law.
4. Charles Taylor, former President of Liberia (convicted in 2012).
