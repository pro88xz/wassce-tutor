**Joseph Saidu Momoh** was President of Sierra Leone from **November 1985** to **April 1992**. An army officer chosen by Siaka Stevens as his successor, he promised a "**New Order**" of discipline and accountability, but inherited a **bankrupt economy** and a corrupt one-party system. His years saw **shortages, inflation and unpaid salaries**, a **state of economic emergency (1987)**, an alleged **coup plot** that led to the execution of former Vice-President **Francis Minah**, and growing demands for **multi-party democracy**, which produced the **1991 Constitution**. In **March 1991** the **Revolutionary United Front (RUF)** invaded from Liberia, starting the **civil war**, and in **April 1992** young officers of the **NPRC** overthrew him. This lesson explains **the Momoh administration and why it failed**.

## How Momoh became President

**Major-General Joseph Saidu Momoh (1937–2003)**, from **Binkolo** in Bombali District, was **Force Commander** of the army and a nominated member of Parliament. Siaka Stevens chose him as successor because the army's loyalty was important. Under the **one-party constitution**, Momoh was the **single candidate** in **1985**, and became President in **November 1985**. His popularity was high at first: many hoped a **soldier** would bring discipline and clean government.

## The "New Order"

Momoh promised a **New Order** of:

1. **discipline and accountability** in public life;
2. a fight against **corruption** and **smuggling**;
3. **economic recovery**, including agreements with the **IMF** and **World Bank**;
4. **national unity** under the APC.

## Economic problems

| Problem | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Inflation and currency collapse** | the **leone** lost value rapidly; prices of rice and fuel soared |
| **Shortages** | of **fuel, rice and electricity**; Freetown often had no power |
| **Unpaid salaries** | teachers and civil servants were unpaid for months; schools and hospitals declined further |
| **Smuggling and corruption** | continued on a large scale, especially in diamonds; a small group of insiders controlled trade |
| **Debt and IMF conditions** | the government struggled to repay foreign debts; structural adjustment measures raised prices |
| **State of economic emergency (November 1987)** | Momoh declared an emergency to stop hoarding and smuggling, but it had little effect |

As hardship grew and corruption continued, many Sierra Leoneans lost confidence in the government and the one-party system.

## Political events, 1985–1991

1. **1986:** one-party **parliamentary elections** were held, with only APC candidates.
2. **1987:** an alleged **coup plot** was uncovered. Former First Vice-President **Francis Minah** and others were tried and **executed in 1989**.
3. **Pressure for democracy:** the end of the **Cold War (1989–1990)**, the example of other African countries, and pressure from **students, lawyers, the press, churches and donors** led to calls for multi-party politics.
4. **1990:** Momoh set up a **Constitutional Review Commission** under **Dr Peter Tucker**.
5. **1991:** a new constitution restoring **multi-party democracy** was approved in a **referendum** and came into force on **1 October 1991**. New parties began to form.

## The start of the civil war

On **23 March 1991**, the **Revolutionary United Front (RUF)**, led by **Foday Sankoh** and backed by **Charles Taylor's** NPFL in Liberia, attacked **Bomaru** in **Kailahun District**. The poorly paid, poorly equipped army struggled to stop the RUF, which spread through the **east and south**. The war is studied in the next lesson.

## Why Momoh was overthrown

On **29 April 1992**, a group of **young army officers** from the war front came to Freetown to protest about **unpaid salaries and lack of supplies**. Their protest turned into a **coup**: they set up the **National Provisional Ruling Council (NPRC)** under **Captain Valentine Strasser**, and Momoh **fled to Guinea**.

**Reasons for the coup:**

1. **Neglect of soldiers** fighting the RUF: no pay, food, equipment or medical care.
2. **Economic collapse** and widespread hardship.
3. **Corruption** and loss of public confidence in the APC government.
4. **Failure to end the war**.
5. **Weak leadership**: Momoh seemed unable to control his ministers or the economy.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Assessing Momoh's government**

Momoh deserves credit for allowing the **return of multi-party democracy** through the **1991 Constitution**, which is still in force. But his government failed to stop **economic collapse, corruption and smuggling**, neglected the **army**, and could not respond to the RUF invasion. Many historians see the Momoh years as the time when the **Sierra Leonean state collapsed** under the weight of problems inherited from the Stevens era.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Older Sierra Leoneans remember the late 1980s as the time of "**no light, no water, no salary**", when long fuel queues and power cuts were part of daily life in Freetown. These memories help to explain the joyful welcome many young people gave to the NPRC coup in 1992.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The **1991 Constitution** was introduced under **Momoh**, not by the NPRC or Kabbah. The NPRC actually **suspended** it in 1992; it was restored after the **1996** elections.
:::

## Key figures and terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figures and terms"}
- **Joseph Saidu Momoh**: the army commander chosen by Stevens who became President in November 1985 and promised a New Order.
- **Constitution of 1991**: the constitution of 1 October 1991 that restored multi-party politics in Sierra Leone.
:::

## Key points

- **Momoh** (army commander) became President in **November 1985** as Stevens's chosen successor; promised a **New Order**.
- Economic crisis: **inflation, shortages, unpaid salaries, smuggling, debt**; **economic emergency (1987)**.
- Politics: **1987 coup plot**, **Francis Minah executed (1989)**; **Tucker Commission (1990)**; **multi-party constitution (1 October 1991)**.
- **Civil war began 23 March 1991** (RUF attack on **Bomaru**).
- **NPRC coup, 29 April 1992**: unpaid soldiers, economic collapse, corruption, failure to end the war.
