**Sierra Leone's climate** is **tropical**, with high temperatures all year and two seasons: a **rainy season** from about **May to October**, brought by the **south-west monsoon** winds, and a **dry season** from about **November to April**, when the dry **harmattan** blows from the Sahara. Rainfall is heaviest on the coast, where **Freetown receives over 3,000 mm a year**, and decreases towards the north-east. The natural **vegetation** ranges from **mangrove swamps** on the coast and **rainforest** in the south and east to **savanna** in the north, and the main **soils** are leached **lateritic soils** on the uplands and fertile **alluvial and swamp soils** in the valleys. This lesson explains each, and how they affect people.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the climate of Sierra Leone: temperature, rainfall and seasons
- explain the causes of the rainy and dry seasons
- describe the natural vegetation zones of Sierra Leone
- describe the main soil types and their uses
- explain how climate, vegetation and soils affect human activities

### Temperature

- High all year: monthly averages of about **25–28 °C**.
- Small **annual range** of temperature, because the sun is always high in the sky.
- Slightly cooler in the rainy season (cloud cover) and on high ground; the coast is moderated by the sea.
- In the harmattan months, nights can be cool and days hazy, especially in the north.

### Rainfall and seasons

| Season | Months (approximately) | Cause and features |
|---|---|---|
| **Rainy season** | **May to October**; heaviest in **July and August** | the **ITCZ** moves north and the moist **south-west monsoon** winds blow in from the Atlantic; heavy convectional and relief rain, thunderstorms at the start and end |
| **Dry season** | **November to April** | the ITCZ moves south; the dry **north-east trade wind (harmattan)** blows from the Sahara, bringing dust and haze, especially from **December to February** |

- **Coastal areas** receive the most rain: Freetown receives **over 3,000 mm** a year, helped by the **Peninsula mountains**, which force moist air to rise (relief rain).
- Rainfall **decreases north-eastwards** inland, and the dry season is longer in the north.
- **Humidity** is high, especially on the coast in the rainy season.

## Natural vegetation

| Zone | Location | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Mangrove swamp forest** | estuaries and creeks along the coast (Sierra Leone River, Sherbro, Scarcies) | trees with stilt roots in salty, muddy water; used for firewood, fish smoking, poles |
| **Tropical rainforest** | south and east (Gola, Kambui Hills) and the Peninsula | tall evergreen trees in layers; much cleared for farms, now **secondary forest and farm bush** |
| **Guinea savanna (wooded savanna)** | north and centre | tall grasses with scattered trees such as the oil palm, shea and locust bean |
| **Montane grassland** | high slopes of the Loma Mountains | grasses and shrubs |
| **Swamp grassland (bolilands)** | seasonally flooded areas in the north | grasses; used for rice |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Much of Sierra Leone's original forest has been replaced by **farm bush**, the low secondary growth that appears on land left fallow after farming. The largest remaining block of lowland rainforest is in **Gola Rainforest National Park**, on the border with Liberia, which protects forest elephants, chimpanzees and pygmy hippopotamuses.
:::

## Soils

| Soil | Where | Features and use |
|---|---|---|
| **Lateritic (ferralitic) soils** | uplands across most of the country | red or brown, **heavily leached** by heavy rain; low fertility; form hard crusts (laterite) when exposed; used for upland rice, cassava, tree crops |
| **Alluvial soils** | river valleys and floodplains | deposited by rivers; **fertile**; good for rice and vegetables |
| **Swamp soils** | inland valley swamps, bolilands, mangrove swamps | waterlogged and rich in organic matter; good for **swamp rice**; mangrove soils can be salty and acid |
| **Sandy soils** | beaches and coastal ridges | infertile and dry; coconuts and cassava |

## Effects on human activities

1. **Farming follows the seasons:** land is cleared in the dry season and planted with the first rains.
2. **Heavy rain** leaches soils, damages roads and causes **floods and landslides**, especially in Freetown.
3. **Harmattan** dust reduces visibility, affecting air travel, and dries crops.
4. **Swamps and floodplains** are ideal for **rice**.
5. The **forest** zone suits **cocoa, coffee and oil palm**; the **savanna** suits **groundnuts, millet and cattle**.
6. The high rainfall feeds rivers used for **hydroelectric power**.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The harmattan is **dry**, not wet. It is the north-east trade wind from the Sahara, and it brings **dust, haze, low humidity and cool nights** in the dry season.
:::

## Key points

- Climate: **tropical**, **25–28 °C**, small range; **rainy season May–October** (south-west monsoon); **dry season November–April** (harmattan).
- Rainfall is heaviest on the **coast** (Freetown over **3,000 mm**) and decreases north-eastwards.
- Vegetation: **mangroves** (coast), **rainforest** (south and east, mostly farm bush now), **savanna** (north), **montane grassland** (Loma).
- Soils: **lateritic** (uplands, leached), **alluvial** (valleys, fertile), **swamp** (rice), **sandy** (coast).

## Quick check

1. Name the months of the rainy season in Sierra Leone.
2. Which wind brings rain to Sierra Leone?
3. Why is Freetown wetter than places further inland?
4. Name two vegetation zones of Sierra Leone.
5. Why are lateritic soils not very fertile?

**Answers:**

1. About May to October.
2. The south-west monsoon (moist winds from the Atlantic).
3. It is on the coast, where moist air arrives first, and the Peninsula mountains force the air to rise and give heavy relief rain.
4. Any two: mangrove swamp forest; tropical rainforest; Guinea savanna; montane grassland; swamp grassland.
5. Heavy rain has leached the plant nutrients out of them.
