## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define supply and distinguish it from stock
- draw a supply schedule and a supply curve
- state the law of supply and explain why the supply curve slopes upwards

## What is supply?

:::callout{type=definition}
**Supply** is the quantity of a good or service that producers are **willing and able to offer for sale** at a given price, over a given period of time.
:::

### Supply is not the same as stock

:::callout{type=definition}
**Stock** is the **total quantity** of a good that a producer or trader has at a given time. **Supply** is the part of that stock that is actually **offered for sale** at a particular price.
:::

A rice trader in Bo may have 200 bags in her store (her **stock**). At NLe 400 a bag she may be willing to sell only 80 bags (her **supply** at that price), keeping the rest in the hope that the price will rise later.

## The supply schedule

:::callout{type=definition}
A **supply schedule** is a table showing the quantities of a good that producers will offer for sale at different prices.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Weekly supply of bunches of plantain to a market in Kenema**

| Price per bunch (NLe) | Quantity supplied (bunches) |
|---|---|
| 5 | 20 |
| 10 | 40 |
| 15 | 60 |
| 20 | 80 |
| 25 | 100 |
| 30 | 120 |

As the price rises, farmers bring more bunches to market.
:::

The **market supply schedule** adds up the quantities offered by all producers at each price.

## The supply curve

With **price on the vertical axis** and **quantity supplied on the horizontal axis**, plot the schedule and join the points. The result is the **supply curve**, labelled **S**. It slopes **upwards from left to right**.

## The law of supply

:::callout{type=definition}
**The law of supply** states that, **other things being equal**, the **higher the price** of a good, the **greater the quantity supplied**; and the **lower the price**, the **smaller the quantity supplied**.
:::

Price and quantity supplied move in the **same direction**: a **direct (positive)** relationship.

### Why the supply curve slopes upwards

1. **Higher profit.** A higher price makes the good more profitable, so producers want to sell more of it.
2. **Covering higher costs.** Producing more often costs more per unit (diminishing returns), so producers will supply extra only if the price is higher.
3. **New producers.** A higher price attracts new firms or farmers into the market.
4. **Switching production.** Producers move resources from less profitable goods into the one whose price has risen.
5. **Releasing stock.** Traders holding stock sell more of it when the price rises.

:::callout{type=local-example}
When the price of palm oil rises, farmers harvest more of their palm trees, women who process oil work longer hours, and traders release the drums they have been keeping in store. The quantity of palm oil supplied rises.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not write that "supply increases when price rises". A rise in the good's own price increases the **quantity supplied**: a movement **along** the supply curve. "Supply increases" means the whole curve shifts to the right, which needs another cause (see the next lesson).
:::

## Key points

- **Supply** = the quantity producers are **willing and able to offer for sale** at a given price, over a period.
- **Stock** is everything held; **supply** is what is offered at a price.
- A **supply curve** slopes **upwards**.
- **The law of supply**: price up, quantity supplied up (other things being equal).

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **define supply**, **distinguish supply from stock**, **state the law of supply** and **draw a supply curve from a schedule**. Give each answer the same care as for demand: labelled axes, a labelled curve **S**, and *other things being equal* in the statement of the law.
:::

## Quick check

1. A fisherman has 50 fish but offers only 30 for sale at today's price. Which figure is his supply, and which his stock?
2. From the schedule above, how many bunches are supplied at NLe 20?
3. Give two reasons why the supply curve slopes upwards.

**Answers:** 1. Supply: 30 fish; stock: 50 fish. 2. 80 bunches. 3. For example: a higher price means higher profit; new producers are attracted; extra output costs more to produce.
