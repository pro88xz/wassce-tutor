# Specialisation, division of labour and productivity

## Learning objectives
Explain specialisation and division of labour, identify their benefits and limitations, and relate them to productivity.

## Meaning
**Specialisation** occurs when a person, firm, region or country concentrates on a particular task or product. **Division of labour** is the separation of a production process into smaller tasks performed by different workers. It is one way specialisation is organised within production.

A rice-processing business may assign separate workers to cleaning, drying, milling, weighing, packaging and quality inspection. Repetition allows workers to develop skill and reduces time lost switching between tasks.

## Benefits
- **Higher output:** workers become faster at familiar tasks.
- **Improved skill:** repeated practice increases competence.
- **Time saving:** workers do not repeatedly change tools or workstations.
- **Use of machinery:** specialised tasks make it easier to introduce suitable equipment.
- **Lower unit cost:** greater output can spread fixed costs over more units.
- **Interdependence and trade:** specialists exchange the goods and services they produce.

## Limitations
Repetitive work can cause boredom and fatigue. A worker may lose the ability to perform the whole production process. If one stage breaks down, the entire chain may stop. Specialisation can also create dependence on suppliers, markets or imported inputs.

A small rural enterprise may therefore choose partial specialisation: workers have main duties but can cover other tasks when demand changes.

## Productivity
Productivity measures output relative to input. Labour productivity can be expressed as:

**Labour productivity = Total output ÷ Number of workers**

If 8 workers package 1,200 bags in a day, average output is 150 bags per worker. If improved equipment raises daily output to 1,440 with the same workforce, productivity becomes 180 bags per worker.

Higher productivity does not automatically mean higher profit. The business must also consider equipment costs, quality, demand, wages and waste.

## WASSCE examination focus
Distinguish specialisation (concentration on a task or product) from division of labour (breaking a process into tasks). In evaluation questions, give both advantages and disadvantages.

## Quick check
1. Define division of labour.
2. State four advantages.
3. Explain two disadvantages.
4. Calculate labour productivity if 6 workers produce 900 units.

**Answers:** 1. Breaking production into separate tasks performed by different workers. 2. Skill, speed, time saving, machinery use and lower unit cost. 3. Monotony and dependence on each production stage are examples. 4. 900 ÷ 6 = 150 units per worker.
