:::callout{type=definition}
A **locus** (plural **loci**) is the set of all points that obey a rule.
:::

"All the points 3 cm from O" is a locus: it is a circle. "All the points the same distance from two villages" is a locus: it is a straight line. Loci turn a description in words into a drawing, and they answer real questions such as *where should a well be dug so that it is the same distance from two compounds?* WASSCE sets loci with constructions: you draw two or more loci and find where they meet. This lesson teaches the four basic loci and how to combine them.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe and construct the four basic loci
- describe a locus in words from a diagram, and the reverse
- find points that satisfy two conditions by intersecting loci
- shade regions defined by loci (inside, outside, nearer to)

## The four basic loci

![Four panels: points a fixed distance from a point form a circle; points equidistant from A and B form the perpendicular bisector of AB; points equidistant from two lines form the angle bisector; points a fixed distance from a line form two parallel lines](https://wasscetutor.com/img/mathematics/loci-panels.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
| The points that are… | form… | construct with… |
|---|---|---|
| a **fixed distance $r$ from a point** O | a **circle**, centre O, radius $r$ | compasses |
| **equidistant from two points** A and B | the **perpendicular bisector** of AB | equal arcs from A and B |
| **equidistant from two intersecting lines** | the **bisector of the angle** between them | arcs from the vertex, then from the arms |
| a **fixed distance $d$ from a straight line** | **two lines parallel** to it, one on each side, distance $d$ away | perpendiculars of length $d$, then join |
:::

:::callout{type=tip}
The two "equidistant" loci are easy to confuse. Ask: **equidistant from what?** From two **points** → perpendicular bisector. From two **lines** → angle bisector.
:::

### A fixed distance from a line segment

If the line has **ends** (a segment AB, such as a wall), the points a fixed distance from it form a **"running track" shape**: two parallel lines joined by **semicircles** round each end.

## Loci in words

WASSCE often asks you to **describe** a locus. Name the shape **and** its key measurements.

| Description | Locus |
|---|---|
| A goat tied to a peg by a 4 m rope can reach… | the region **inside** a circle, centre the peg, radius 4 m |
| The tip of a clock's minute hand moves along… | a **circle**, centre the middle of the clock face |
| A point P moves so that $PA = PB$ | the **perpendicular bisector** of AB |
| A point moves so that it is always 2 cm from line $l$ | **two lines parallel** to $l$, 2 cm away on each side |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Drawing only one parallel line.** "2 cm from the line" means 2 cm on **either side**, so there are **two** parallel lines, unless the question restricts the point to one side.
:::

## Intersecting loci

When a point must satisfy **two** conditions, construct **both** loci. The answer is where they **cross**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A and B are two villages 8 km apart. A borehole is to be drilled so that it is equidistant from A and B, and 5 km from A. Using a scale of 1 cm to 1 km, find the possible positions.**

1. Draw $AB = 8$ cm.
2. **Equidistant from A and B**: construct the perpendicular bisector of AB.
3. **5 km from A**: draw a circle, centre A, radius 5 cm.
4. The circle crosses the bisector at **two points**, $P_1$ and $P_2$, one on each side of AB.

**Check by Pythagoras:** the bisector meets AB at its midpoint, 4 cm from A. Each position is $\sqrt{5^2 - 4^2} = 3$ cm from AB, so the borehole is **3 km from the line joining the villages**, on either side.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Triangle PQR has $PQ = 9$ cm, $QR = 7$ cm and $PR = 6$ cm. Find the point inside the triangle that is equidistant from PQ and PR, and also equidistant from P and Q.**

1. Construct triangle PQR (three sides: arcs of 7 cm from Q and 6 cm from P).
2. **Equidistant from the lines PQ and PR**: bisect angle QPR.
3. **Equidistant from the points P and Q**: construct the perpendicular bisector of PQ.
4. The two lines cross at the required point, X. Measure as asked: here $PX \approx 5.0$ cm.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Stopping at one point when there are two.** A circle and a straight line usually cross **twice**. Check both sides of the line before writing your answer.
:::

## Regions

Loci can mark the edge of a **region**. Shade, or describe, the side that obeys the rule.

| Condition | Region |
|---|---|
| **less than** 3 cm from O | **inside** the circle |
| **more than** 3 cm from O | **outside** the circle |
| **nearer to A than to B** | the side of the perpendicular bisector that contains **A** |
| **nearer to line $l_1$ than to $l_2$** | the side of the angle bisector next to $l_1$ |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A rectangular school garden ABCD measures 10 m by 6 m. A tap at A sprays water up to 4 m. Seedlings must be planted nearer to AB than to AD and outside the spray. Describe the region.**

- **Nearer to AB than to AD**: on the AB side of the bisector of angle DAB.
- **Outside the spray**: outside the circle, centre A, radius 4 m.

The region is inside the garden, **between the angle bisector and AB, and outside the quarter circle** round A.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Siting a market in Kono.** Two communities, Koidu and a village 6 km away, agree that a new weekly market should be the same distance from both and within 4 km of the main road, which runs straight past both. The planners draw the perpendicular bisector of the line joining the communities and the strip within 4 km of the road. The possible sites are the part of the bisector that lies inside the strip: a straight segment of road-side land that is fair to both communities.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Describe the locus of a point 5 cm from a fixed point O."}
A **circle**, centre O, radius 5 cm.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Describe the locus of the points equidistant from two straight lines that cross at X."}
The **bisectors of the angles** at X. (There are two, at right angles to each other, because two crossing lines make two pairs of angles.)
:::

:::reveal{title="3. A goat is tied to the corner of a square shed, 3 m by 3 m, with a 5 m rope. Describe the region it can graze."}
Three-quarters of a circle of radius 5 m round the corner (the shed blocks the other quarter), **plus** two quarter-circles of radius $5 - 3 = 2$ m round the two neighbouring corners, where the rope bends round the shed.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. A and B are 10 cm apart. How many points are both 6 cm from A and 6 cm from B? How far is each from AB?"}
**Two** points (the two circles cross twice, once on each side of AB). Both lie on the perpendicular bisector of AB. The midpoint of AB is 5 cm from A, so each point is $\sqrt{6^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{11} \approx 3.3$ cm from AB.
:::

## Key points

- A **locus** is the set of points that obey a rule.
- **Distance from a point** → circle. **Equidistant from two points** → perpendicular bisector.
- **Equidistant from two lines** → angle bisector. **Distance from a line** → two parallel lines.
- For **two conditions**, construct both loci; the answers are where they **cross**, often at two points.
- **Regions**: inside/outside a circle, the side of a bisector nearer to a point or line.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Loci appear in objective questions (name the locus described) and in Paper 2 construction questions, where you construct a triangle or quadrilateral and then two loci inside it, mark the point where they meet, and measure a distance. Marks are given for each locus correctly constructed with arcs shown, and for the measurement.
:::
