The **climate of West Africa** is controlled by the yearly movement of the **Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)**, where two air masses meet: the moist **tropical maritime** air of the **south-west monsoon** from the Atlantic, and the dry **tropical continental** air of the **north-east trades (harmattan)** from the Sahara. As a result, **rainfall decreases from the coast northwards**, the dry season gets longer, and the **vegetation** changes in belts from **mangrove and rainforest** in the south through **Guinea, Sudan and Sahel savanna** to the **Sahara desert**. Each belt supports different **economic activities**. This lesson explains the climate, vegetation and economy of West Africa.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain how the movement of the ITCZ causes the seasons of West Africa
- describe the main climatic regions of West Africa
- describe the vegetation belts and relate them to rainfall
- describe the main economic activities in each belt
- describe the distribution of population in West Africa

## The ITCZ and the seasons

![Map of West Africa showing the ITCZ near the coast in January and far north in July, with the south-west monsoon blowing in from the Atlantic and the dry north-east trade wind blowing from the Sahara](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/itcz-west-africa.svg)

*Figure 1. Positions of the ITCZ over West Africa in January and July. Outlines from Natural Earth; WASSCE Tutor map.*

| | Around July (northern summer) | Around January (northern winter) |
|---|---|---|
| **Position of ITCZ** | far north, about **18–20°N** | south, near the coast, about **5–8°N** |
| **Dominant wind** | moist **south-west monsoon** over most of West Africa | dry **north-east trades (harmattan)** over most of West Africa |
| **Weather** | **rainy season**; heavy rain on the coast, lighter in the north | **dry season**; dust, haze, low humidity, cool nights |

The ITCZ follows the **overhead sun** with a lag of a few weeks. Places in the south are under moist air for longer, so they have **more rain and a shorter dry season**. Along part of the Gulf of Guinea coast (from Côte d'Ivoire to Nigeria), the ITCZ passes over twice, giving **two rainfall peaks** with a short drier spell in between.

## Climatic regions

| Region | Where | Rainfall | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tropical wet (equatorial-type)** | coasts of Sierra Leone, Liberia, south-east Nigeria | over 2,000 mm | rain most of the year; high humidity; small temperature range |
| **Tropical wet-and-dry (savanna)** | most of the interior | 750–1,500 mm | distinct rainy and dry seasons |
| **Semi-arid (Sahel)** | Senegal, Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, northern Nigeria | 250–750 mm | short, unreliable rainy season; droughts |
| **Hot desert** | Sahara: Mauritania, northern Mali and Niger | under 250 mm | very hot days, cold nights, almost no rain |

## Vegetation belts

![Vegetation belts of West Africa from the coast northwards: mangrove swamp, tropical rainforest, Guinea savanna, Sudan savanna, Sahel savanna and the Sahara desert, with rainfall falling northwards](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/vegetation-belts-wa.svg)

*Figure 2. Vegetation belts of West Africa. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Belt | Vegetation | Main economic activities |
|---|---|---|
| **Mangrove swamp** | salt-tolerant trees on tidal coasts | fishing, salt making, firewood, swamp rice |
| **Tropical rainforest** | tall evergreen trees in layers | **cocoa** (Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana), **oil palm**, **rubber** (Liberia), coffee, **timber**, food crops (cassava, yams, plantains) |
| **Guinea savanna** | tall grass with scattered trees | yams, maize, rice, groundnuts, shea; some cattle |
| **Sudan savanna** | shorter grass, fewer trees | **groundnuts** (Senegal, northern Nigeria), **cotton**, millet, sorghum, **cattle** |
| **Sahel savanna** | short grass, thorn scrub | **pastoral nomadism** (cattle, camels, goats), millet, irrigated rice along rivers |
| **Desert** | almost no vegetation, except at oases | dates at oases, salt, camel herding, **uranium** mining (Niger) |

## Other economic activities

- **Mining:** oil and gas (Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal), gold (Ghana, Mali, Burkina Faso), bauxite (Guinea), iron ore (Liberia, Mauritania, Sierra Leone), diamonds (Sierra Leone), uranium (Niger), phosphate (Togo, Senegal).
- **Fishing:** along the Atlantic coast, in lagoons, rivers, Lake Volta and Lake Chad.
- **Manufacturing:** Lagos, Tema, Abidjan, Dakar, Kano.
- **Trade and services** in the large coastal cities.

## Population distribution

| Densely populated | Sparsely populated |
|---|---|
| **southern Nigeria** (Lagos, Ibadan, the south-east) | the **Sahara** (northern Mauritania, Mali, Niger) |
| the **Kano** region of northern Nigeria | the dry **Sahel** |
| southern **Ghana** and the **coastal corridor** from Abidjan to Lagos | areas affected by **tsetse** or **river blindness** |
| the **Dakar** area of Senegal | thick forest and swamps |
| the **Mossi** plateau around Ouagadougou | |

People cluster where there is **reliable rainfall**, **fertile soil**, **jobs**, **ports and cities**, and good **transport**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Sierra Leone** lies in the wettest part of West Africa: its coast receives some of the highest rainfall in the region because the moist monsoon arrives straight from the Atlantic and is forced up over the Freetown Peninsula. This is why it has rainforest, mangroves and large areas of swamp suitable for rice.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The harmattan does not blow "from the sea". It is the **north-east trade wind** from the **Sahara**, and it brings dry, dusty air. The **south-west monsoon** is the wind from the sea that brings rain.
:::

## Key points

- The **ITCZ** moves north in the northern summer (about **18–20°N** in July) and south in the winter (about **5–8°N** in January).
- **South-west monsoon:** moist, brings rain. **North-east trades (harmattan):** dry, dusty.
- Rainfall and the length of the rainy season **decrease northwards**.
- Belts: **mangrove → rainforest → Guinea savanna → Sudan savanna → Sahel → desert**, each with its own activities.
- Population is densest in **southern Nigeria**, **Kano**, the **Abidjan–Lagos coast** and **Dakar**.

## Quick check

1. What is the ITCZ?
2. Where is the ITCZ in July?
3. Name the two winds that affect West Africa and state the weather each brings.
4. Name two economic activities in the Sudan savanna.
5. Give two reasons why southern Nigeria is densely populated.

**Answers:**

1. The Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone, the belt where the moist south-west monsoon and the dry north-east trades meet.
2. Far north, at about 18–20°N.
3. The south-west monsoon brings moist air and rain; the north-east trades (harmattan) bring dry, dusty air.
4. Any two: groundnut growing; cotton growing; millet and sorghum farming; cattle rearing.
5. Any two: reliable rainfall; fertile soils; large cities and ports; jobs; oil industry; good transport.
