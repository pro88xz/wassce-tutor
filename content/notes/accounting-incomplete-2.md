**Converting incomplete records into final accounts** means rebuilding the missing figures, especially **sales** and **purchases**, from cash records and lists of debts, so that a full **income statement** and **statement of financial position** can be prepared. The key tools are the **total receivables (debtors) account**, which finds credit sales, the **total payables (creditors) account**, which finds credit purchases, and **mark-up and margin**, which find missing figures such as cost of sales or goods lost in a fire or theft. This lesson works through each tool.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- find credit sales with a total receivables account
- find credit purchases with a total payables account
- convert between mark-up and margin
- use mark-up or margin to find cost of sales, sales or closing inventory
- calculate inventory lost by fire or theft

## Finding credit sales

A **total receivables account** works like a sales ledger control account; the **missing figure** is credit sales.

$$\text{Credit sales} = \text{Cash received} + \text{Discount allowed} + \text{Closing receivables} - \text{Opening receivables}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Receivables were NLe 5,000 on 1 January and NLe 7,000 on 31 December. Customers paid NLe 60,000 and were allowed NLe 1,000 discount. Cash sales were NLe 20,000. Find total sales.**

Credit sales $= 60\,000 + 1\,000 + 7\,000 - 5\,000 = 63\,000$

Total sales $= 63\,000 + 20\,000 = 83\,000$
:::

## Finding credit purchases

$$\text{Credit purchases} = \text{Cash paid} + \text{Discount received} + \text{Closing payables} - \text{Opening payables}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Payables were NLe 4,000 on 1 January and NLe 6,000 on 31 December. Suppliers were paid NLe 50,000 with NLe 800 discount received. Find credit purchases.**

Credit purchases $= 50\,000 + 800 + 6\,000 - 4\,000 = 52\,800$
:::

The same reasoning finds **expenses for the year** from cash paid adjusted for opening and closing accruals and prepayments.

## Mark-up and margin

- **Mark-up** is gross profit as a percentage of **cost of sales**.
- **Margin** is gross profit as a percentage of **sales**.

| Mark-up | Margin |
|---|---|
| 25% (cost 100, sales 125) | $\frac{25}{125} = 20\%$ |
| $33\frac{1}{3}\%$ (cost 3, sales 4) | 25% |
| 50% (cost 100, sales 150) | $\frac{50}{150} = 33\frac{1}{3}\%$ |
| 100% (cost 100, sales 200) | 50% |

To convert: **margin = mark-up ÷ (100 + mark-up)**, and **mark-up = margin ÷ (100 − margin)**, as fractions of 100.

## Inventory lost by fire or theft

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A fire destroyed most of Sesay's inventory. Opening inventory was NLe 15,000, purchases NLe 70,000 and sales NLe 90,000, and Sesay's mark-up is 25%. Goods worth NLe 4,000 at cost were saved. Find the inventory lost.**

- Cost of sales $= 90\,000 \times \frac{100}{125} = 72\,000$
- Inventory that should remain $= 15\,000 + 70\,000 - 72\,000 = 13\,000$
- Inventory lost $= 13\,000 - 4\,000 = 9\,000$

The loss of NLe 9,000 at cost is the amount to claim from the insurance company (or to write off if uninsured).
:::

## Putting it together

With sales, purchases and expenses rebuilt, prepare the final accounts in the usual way:

1. **Statement of affairs** at the start to find **opening capital**.
2. **Total receivables and payables accounts** to find **credit sales and purchases**.
3. **Adjust expenses** for accruals and prepayments.
4. Prepare the **income statement** and **statement of financial position**.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not use the **mark-up** percentage as if it were the **margin**. A mark-up of 25% means gross profit is 25% of **cost**, which is only 20% of **sales**. Always check which base the question gives.
:::

## Key points

- **Credit sales = cash received + discount allowed + closing receivables − opening receivables.**
- **Credit purchases = cash paid + discount received + closing payables − opening payables.**
- **Mark-up** is on cost; **margin** is on sales; **margin = mark-up ÷ (100 + mark-up)**.
- Mark-up or margin finds **cost of sales** and **inventory lost**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect a full incomplete records question: **find opening capital, credit sales and purchases, adjusted expenses**, then **prepare the final accounts**. Questions on **goods lost by fire or theft** using mark-up or margin are also common.
:::

## Quick check

1. Opening receivables NLe 3,000, closing NLe 4,500, cash received NLe 40,000. Find credit sales.
2. A mark-up of 50% equals what margin?
3. Sales are NLe 60,000 at a margin of 20%. Find the cost of sales.
4. Opening payables NLe 2,000, closing NLe 3,000, cash paid NLe 30,000. Find credit purchases.
5. Why is a total receivables account useful with incomplete records?

**Answers:**

1. $40\,000 + 4\,500 - 3\,000 = 41\,500$: NLe 41,500.
2. $\frac{50}{150} = 33\frac{1}{3}\%$.
3. $60\,000 \times 80\% = 48\,000$: NLe 48,000.
4. $30\,000 + 3\,000 - 2\,000 = 31\,000$: NLe 31,000.
5. Because the missing credit sales figure can be found as the balancing figure.
