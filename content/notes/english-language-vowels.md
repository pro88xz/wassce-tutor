English is written with 5 vowel **letters** (a, e, i, o, u) but spoken with **20 vowel sounds**. That gap is why Oral English questions catch so many students: *"seat"* and *"sit"*, *"cart"* and *"cut"*, *"bird"* and *"bed"* may sound the same in everyday Sierra Leonean speech, but the examination treats them as different sounds. This lesson teaches all 20, with the symbols WAEC uses, example words for each, the pairs that are easiest to confuse, and the kind of questions you will face.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name and recognise the 12 pure vowels and 8 diphthongs of English
- read the phonetic symbol for each vowel sound
- tell apart the pairs of vowels most often confused in West African English
- answer "same vowel sound" and rhyme questions confidently

## Letters are not sounds

:::callout{type=definition}
A **vowel sound** is made with the air flowing freely through the mouth, without being blocked by the tongue, teeth or lips. English has:

- **12 pure vowels** (monophthongs): the tongue stays in one position
- **8 diphthongs**: the tongue glides from one vowel position to another

Phonetic symbols are written between slashes: /iː/ is the vowel sound in *seat*.
:::

Spelling is an unreliable guide. The letters **ea** spell three different vowel sounds in *s**ea**t*, *br**ea**d* and *gr**ea**t*; the sound /iː/ is spelt in at least six ways: *s**ee**, s**ea**, f**ie**ld, rec**ei**ve, k**ey**, mach**i**ne*. In Oral English, **listen to the sound, not the spelling**.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Matching by spelling.** *"Blood"* and *"food"* are both spelt with **oo**, but *blood* has /ʌ/ (like *cut*) and *food* has /uː/. *"Women"* is spelt with **o** but its first vowel is /ɪ/ (like *sit*). Always say the word in your head.
:::

## How the questions work

### Same vowel sound

> *Choose the word that has the same vowel sound as the one in **bought**: A. boat B. bout C. port D. pot*

**Answer: C. port** (/ɔː/). *Boat* has /əʊ/, *bout* has /aʊ/, *pot* has /ɒ/.

**Listen:** [bought /bɔːt/](https://wasscetutor.com/audio/oral/bought.mp3?v=b081fd49a7) [boat /bəʊt/](https://wasscetutor.com/audio/oral/boat.mp3?v=996143f877) [bout /baʊt/](https://wasscetutor.com/audio/oral/bout.mp3?v=c0b448b0a6) [port /pɔːt/](https://wasscetutor.com/audio/oral/port.mp3?v=ab38c85609) [pot /pɒt/](https://wasscetutor.com/audio/oral/pot.mp3?v=c0550461fc)

### Rhyme

> *Choose the word that rhymes with **said**: A. paid B. bed C. raid D. seed*

**Answer: B. bed.** *Said* is pronounced /sed/, not with the /eɪ/ of *paid*. Rhyme depends on **sound**, not spelling.

**Listen:** [said /sed/](https://wasscetutor.com/audio/oral/said.mp3?v=cb0195b465) [paid /peɪd/](https://wasscetutor.com/audio/oral/paid.mp3?v=7da142282b) [bed /bed/](https://wasscetutor.com/audio/oral/bed.mp3?v=3341d5579d) [raid /reɪd/](https://wasscetutor.com/audio/oral/raid.mp3?v=14d2db3f56) [seed /siːd/](https://wasscetutor.com/audio/oral/seed.mp3?v=cf726fa254)

### Odd one out

> *Which word has a different vowel sound? A. heart B. calm C. hat D. father*

**Answer: C. hat** (/æ/); the others have /ɑː/.

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Which word has the same vowel sound as 'love'? A. move B. stove C. glove D. rove"}
**C. glove**: /ʌ/, like *love*. *Move* has /uː/; *stove* and *rove* have /əʊ/.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Which word contains a diphthong? A. bit B. bite C. bet D. but"}
**B. bite**: /aɪ/. The others have the pure vowels /ɪ/, /e/ and /ʌ/.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Which word rhymes with 'heard'? A. beard B. bird C. hard D. heart"}
**B. bird**: both *heard* and *bird* end in /ɜːd/. *Beard* has /ɪə/, *hard* has /ɑː/, and *heart* has /ɑː/ with a /t/.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Which word has the vowel /ʊ/? A. fool B. full C. fuel D. foul"}
**B. full**: the short /ʊ/ of *put* and *book*. *Fool* has /uː/, *fuel* has /juː/, *foul* has /aʊ/.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Odd one out: A. key B. quay C. seize D. said"}
**D. said** (/e/). *Key*, *quay* and *seize* all have /iː/. (*Quay* is a landing place for boats, pronounced like *key*.)
:::

:::reveal{title="6. Which word contains the schwa /ə/? A. cat B. about C. hat D. sat"}
**B. about**: its first, unstressed syllable is /ə/: /əˈbaʊt/.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Vowel sound**: a speech sound made with the air flowing freely through the mouth; English has 20.
- **Pure vowel**: a vowel sound that stays the same from start to finish, as in "sit" or "car"; English has 12.
- **Diphthong**: a vowel sound that glides from one vowel to another, as in "day", "boy" or "go".
- **Long vowel**: a vowel sound held longer, marked ː in phonetic symbols, as in /iː/ in "seat".
- **Schwa**: the short, weak vowel /ə/, the most common sound in English, heard in unstressed syllables, as in the first sound of "about".
- **Phonetic symbol**: a symbol that stands for one speech sound, written between slashes, such as /iː/.
:::

## Key points

- English has **20 vowel sounds**: **12 pure vowels** and **8 diphthongs**, written in phonetic symbols.
- **Spelling is not a guide**: say the word and listen.
- Learn the **long/short pairs** (/iː/–/ɪ/, /uː/–/ʊ/, /ɔː/–/ɒ/) and the **a-sounds** (/ɑː/, /æ/, /ʌ/), which are often merged in West African speech.
- The **schwa** /ə/ appears only in unstressed syllables.
- **Rhymes** depend on sound: *said* rhymes with *bed*, not *paid*.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect questions asking you to choose the word with **the same vowel sound** as a given word or letters, words that **rhyme**, the **odd one out**, and words containing a given **symbol**. The vowels most often tested are exactly the pairs that are merged in everyday speech. Practise saying them aloud until you can hear and feel the difference.
:::
