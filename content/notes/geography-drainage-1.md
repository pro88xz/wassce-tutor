The **water cycle**, also called the **hydrological cycle**, is the continuous movement of water between the oceans, the atmosphere and the land. Water **evaporates** from the sea and **transpires** from plants, **condenses** into clouds, falls as **precipitation**, then returns to the sea by **surface runoff** in rivers and by **groundwater flow** through the rocks. The cycle is driven by the Sun's heat and by gravity, and it supplies every drop of fresh water we use. This lesson explains each stage and why the cycle matters.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define the water (hydrological) cycle and name its main stores
- describe the processes of evaporation, transpiration, condensation and precipitation
- describe the processes of interception, infiltration, percolation, runoff and groundwater flow
- explain the importance of the water cycle
- explain how human activities affect the water cycle

## The stages of the water cycle

![The water cycle: evaporation from the sea and transpiration from plants, condensation into clouds that move inland, precipitation, surface runoff in rivers, infiltration and percolation, and groundwater flow back to the sea](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/water-cycle.svg)

*Figure 1. The water (hydrological) cycle. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Process | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Evaporation** | the Sun heats water in seas, lakes and rivers, which turns into water vapour |
| **Transpiration** | plants lose water vapour through tiny pores (stomata) in their leaves |
| **Evapotranspiration** | evaporation and transpiration together |
| **Condensation** | rising water vapour cools, and condenses into tiny droplets that form **clouds** |
| **Precipitation** | droplets join until they are heavy enough to fall as **rain**, snow, hail or sleet |
| **Interception** | leaves and branches catch some rain before it reaches the ground |
| **Infiltration** | water soaks into the soil |
| **Percolation** | water moves further down into porous rock, becoming **groundwater** |
| **Surface runoff** | water flows over the surface into streams and rivers |
| **Groundwater flow** | water moves slowly through rocks towards rivers and the sea |

## Stores of water

About **97%** of the Earth's water is salt water in the **oceans**. Of the fresh water, most is frozen in **ice caps and glaciers**, and much of the rest is **groundwater**. Only a tiny fraction is in rivers, lakes, the soil and the atmosphere at any one time, yet that small amount is what most people depend on.

## The closed system

The water cycle is a **closed system**: the total amount of water on Earth stays almost the same; it only changes form and place. Water moves between **stores** (oceans, atmosphere, ice, groundwater, lakes, rivers) by **flows** (evaporation, precipitation, runoff and so on).

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone is one of the wettest countries in West Africa. Moist south-west monsoon winds from the Atlantic bring very heavy rain between about May and October, with Freetown receiving roughly **3,000 to 3,500 mm** a year. The rain feeds the country's many rivers, fills the **Guma Valley** reservoir that supplies Freetown with water, and drives the turbines at **Bumbuna** on the Rokel River. In the dry season, many communities depend on **groundwater** from wells and boreholes, water that infiltrated during the rains.
:::

## Importance of the water cycle

1. **Supplies fresh water** for drinking, farming and industry, constantly renewed by precipitation.
2. **Feeds rivers and lakes**, used for transport, fishing and hydroelectric power.
3. **Recharges groundwater**, tapped by wells and boreholes.
4. **Distributes heat** around the Earth: evaporation absorbs heat, condensation releases it.
5. **Supports plants and animals** in every ecosystem.
6. **Shapes the land** through rivers, erosion and deposition.

## Human influences on the water cycle

| Activity | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Deforestation** | less interception and transpiration; more and faster runoff; floods and soil erosion; less rainfall locally |
| **Urbanisation** (concrete, roofs, roads) | less infiltration; rapid runoff; flash floods, as in Freetown in the rains |
| **Dams and reservoirs** | water stored; more evaporation; river flow changed downstream |
| **Irrigation** | water taken from rivers and groundwater; more evaporation |
| **Over-pumping of groundwater** | falling water tables; wells dry up |
| **Afforestation** | more interception and infiltration; steadier river flow |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Infiltration** is water soaking **into the soil**; **percolation** is water moving **deeper into the rock** to become groundwater. Do not use the terms as if they mean the same thing.
:::

## Key points

- The **water cycle** is the continuous movement of water between the **oceans, atmosphere and land**, driven by the Sun and gravity.
- Processes: **evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, interception, infiltration, percolation, runoff, groundwater flow**.
- About **97%** of water is in the oceans; most fresh water is ice or groundwater.
- It is a **closed system**: the total water stays the same.
- Deforestation and urbanisation increase runoff and flooding; afforestation reduces it.

## Quick check

1. Define the hydrological cycle.
2. What is transpiration?
3. Distinguish infiltration from percolation.
4. Why does building more roads and roofs increase flooding?
5. Where is most of the Earth's water stored?

**Answers:**

1. The continuous movement of water between the oceans, the atmosphere and the land.
2. The loss of water vapour from plants through pores in their leaves.
3. Infiltration is water soaking into the soil; percolation is water moving down into the rock to become groundwater.
4. Hard surfaces stop infiltration, so more water runs off quickly into drains and rivers.
5. In the oceans (about 97%).
