A wholesaler who sends a sales agent to the provinces, a customer who buys a fridge that does not work, and a family that buys a television by monthly instalments are all covered by special areas of business law. This lesson covers **agency**, the **sale of goods** and **hire purchase**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain how agency is created and the duties of agent and principal
- explain the conditions implied in a contract of sale of goods
- distinguish conditions from warranties and explain *caveat emptor*
- explain hire purchase, its features and the rights of the parties
- distinguish hire purchase from a credit sale and calculate hire purchase charges

## Agency

:::callout{type=definition}
**Agency** is a relationship in which one person (the **agent**) is authorised to act on behalf of another (the **principal**) in making contracts with **third parties**. The contract is between the **principal and the third party**.
:::

**How agency is created:**

1. **Express appointment**: by words or in writing; a **power of attorney** is a formal written appointment.
2. **Implied** from conduct or position: a shop assistant is the shop owner's agent for selling.
3. **Necessity**: in an emergency, someone acts to protect another's goods (a carrier sells perishable goods that would rot).
4. **Ratification**: the principal later **approves** a contract the agent made without authority.
5. **Estoppel**: the principal leads a third party to believe someone is their agent, and cannot later deny it.

| Duties of the agent | Duties of the principal |
|---|---|
| **Obey** lawful instructions | **Pay** the agreed commission or remuneration |
| Act with **care and skill** | **Indemnify** the agent for losses incurred in proper performance |
| Act **personally** (not delegate without permission) | **Reimburse** expenses properly incurred |
| Act in **good faith**: no secret profits or bribes; avoid conflicts of interest | |
| **Account** for money and property received | |
| Keep the principal's information **confidential** | |

**Agency ends by:**

- completion of the task
- agreement
- revocation by the principal
- renunciation by the agent
- death, insanity or bankruptcy of either party
- the end of the agreed period

## Sale of goods

:::callout{type=definition}
A **contract of sale of goods** is a contract in which the **seller transfers** (or agrees to transfer) **ownership** of goods to the buyer for a money consideration called the **price**.
:::

### Implied conditions

The law adds these terms to contracts of sale, even if they are not stated:

| Implied condition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Title** | The seller has the **right to sell** the goods; the buyer gets good ownership |
| **Description** | Goods sold by description must **match the description** |
| **Merchantable (satisfactory) quality** | Goods sold in the course of business must be fit for normal use, free from hidden defects |
| **Fitness for purpose** | If the buyer tells the seller the particular purpose and relies on the seller's skill, the goods must be fit for that purpose |
| **Sample** | Goods sold by sample must **correspond with the sample** |

### Conditions and warranties

| Condition | Warranty |
|---|---|
| A **major** term going to the root of the contract | A **minor** term, subsidiary to the main purpose |
| Breach allows the buyer to **reject the goods** and end the contract, and claim damages | Breach allows only a claim for **damages**; the contract continues |
| Example: a "new" car is second-hand | Example: the car comes without a promised spare tyre |

**Caveat emptor ("let the buyer beware")**: the buyer must examine goods before buying. The implied conditions above have greatly **limited** this old rule. It still applies, for example, to private sales and to defects the buyer saw or should have seen on examination.

**Rights of an unpaid seller:**

- **lien**: keeping the goods until payment
- **stoppage in transit**: recalling goods already sent if the buyer becomes insolvent
- **resale** of the goods
- **suing** for the price or for damages

## Hire purchase

:::callout{type=definition}
**Hire purchase (HP)** is a system in which the buyer pays a **deposit**, takes **possession** of the goods at once and pays the balance plus interest in **instalments**. **Ownership passes only when the last instalment is paid**; until then the buyer is legally a **hirer**.
:::

**Parties:** the **hirer** (buyer); the **owner**, who may be the dealer or a **finance company** that pays the dealer and collects the instalments; and the **dealer** (seller).

### Hire purchase and credit sale

![Two timelines: under hire purchase the seller owns the goods and can repossess them until the last instalment, when ownership passes; under a credit sale the buyer owns the goods from the start](https://wasscetutor.com/img/commerce/hire-purchase.svg)

*Figure 1. When ownership passes under hire purchase and under a credit sale. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Hire purchase | Credit sale |
|---|---|
| Ownership passes on payment of the **last** instalment | Ownership passes **at once**, when the contract is made |
| The seller can **repossess** the goods if the buyer defaults (subject to the law) | The seller **cannot repossess**; can only sue for the debt |
| The buyer **cannot sell** the goods before paying in full | The buyer **can sell** the goods, as they are theirs |
| Usually a deposit is required | May need no deposit |
| Used for durable goods: vehicles, fridges, machines | Used for cheaper goods |

**Rights of the hirer:**

- use the goods
- end the agreement by returning the goods (after paying a minimum amount)
- become the owner on the final payment
- receive a copy of the agreement and statements

**Rights of the owner:**

- receive the instalments
- **repossess** on default, normally by court order once a large part has been paid
- claim for damage beyond fair wear

| Advantages of HP | Disadvantages of HP |
|---|---|
| Buyers enjoy goods **before** paying in full | **Higher total cost** than the cash price (interest) |
| Raises **living standards** | Goods may be **repossessed** |
| Firms acquire machines and vehicles and pay from earnings | Encourages people to **overspend** and fall into debt |
| Increases **sales** and production | Sellers bear the risk of default and need large capital |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A trader buys a freezer on hire purchase.** The cash price is NLe 6,000. The deposit is 20%, followed by 12 monthly instalments of NLe 480.

1. Deposit $= 20\% \times 6000 = $ NLe 1,200
2. Instalments $= 12 \times 480 = $ NLe 5,760
3. **Hire purchase price** $= 1200 + 5760 = $ **NLe 6,960**
4. **HP charge (interest)** $= 6960 - 6000 = $ **NLe 960**
5. Amount borrowed $= 6000 - 1200 = $ NLe 4,800
6. Flat rate of interest for the year $= \dfrac{960}{4800} \times 100 = $ **20%**
:::

**Deferred payment** (a credit sale) is a related system: the goods become the buyer's at once and are paid for over time, usually for cheaper goods.

:::callout{type=local-example}
In Sierra Leone, motorbikes for okada riders and kekes are often bought on **hire purchase** from dealers or finance companies, with daily or weekly payments. If the rider stops paying, the dealer may **repossess** the bike, because ownership has not yet passed.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Under hire purchase, the buyer **possesses** the goods at once but does not **own** them until the last instalment. Selling HP goods before paying in full is selling something you do not own.
:::

## Key points

- **Agency**: the agent makes contracts for the principal with third parties. It is created by **express appointment, implication, necessity, ratification or estoppel**.
- **Implied conditions** in a sale of goods: **title, description, merchantable quality, fitness for purpose, sample**.
- Breach of a **condition** allows rejection; breach of a **warranty** allows only damages.
- **Hire purchase**: possession now, **ownership after the last instalment**; **credit sale**: ownership at once.
- HP charge = HP price − cash price.

## Quick check

1. Give three duties of an agent to the principal.
2. What does "merchantable quality" mean?
3. Give two differences between hire purchase and a credit sale.
4. A radio costs NLe 800 cash, or NLe 200 deposit and 10 instalments of NLe 70. Find the HP charge.

**Answers:** 1. Any three: obey instructions, use care and skill, act personally, act in good faith (no secret profit), account for money, keep confidentiality. 2. Goods sold in business must be fit for their normal use and free from hidden defects. 3. Any two: ownership passes at the last instalment under HP but at once in a credit sale; the HP seller can repossess but the credit seller can only sue; the HP buyer cannot sell before full payment but the credit buyer can. 4. HP price $= 200 + 10 \times 70 = 900$; charge $= 900 - 800 = $ NLe 100.
