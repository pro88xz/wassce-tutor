The jingle on the radio, the billboard at Lumley roundabout, the free sample in the supermarket and the sponsor's name on a football shirt are all promotion: ways of telling customers about a product and persuading them to buy it. This lesson covers advertising, its media and its effects, sales promotion, personal selling, public relations, branding and packaging.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define advertising and explain its purposes
- describe the types of advertising
- compare advertising media and choose a suitable medium
- state the advantages and disadvantages of advertising
- explain sales promotion, personal selling, public relations, branding and packaging

## Advertising

:::callout{type=definition}
**Advertising** is a paid, non-personal message by an identified sponsor that **informs** people about a product, service or idea and **persuades** them to buy or act.
:::

**Purposes of advertising:**

- **introduce** new products
- **increase sales** and market share
- **inform** about price, uses and where to buy
- **remind** customers of existing products
- **build a brand image**
- **counter competitors**

## Types of advertising

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Informative** | Gives facts: price, features, where to buy | A notice of new bus routes and fares |
| **Persuasive** | Convinces people they need the product, often using emotion | "Be strong, drink…" |
| **Competitive** | Shows the product is better than rivals' | "Faster network, more coverage" |
| **Mass (generic or collective)** | Promotes a **type** of product for a whole industry, not a brand | "Eat more local rice" campaign by growers |
| **Institutional (prestige or corporate)** | Builds the image of the **firm**, not a product | A bank sponsoring school sports |
| **Reminder** | Keeps a well-known brand in people's minds | Short repeated radio jingles |

## Advertising media

| Medium | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Radio** | Reaches many people, including the illiterate and rural; cheap; local languages | No pictures; message is brief and quickly forgotten |
| **Television** | Sound, colour and movement; very persuasive | Expensive; reaches mainly urban viewers with power |
| **Newspapers and magazines** | Detailed; a permanent record; can be re-read | Need literacy; limited circulation |
| **Billboards and posters** | Seen repeatedly by passers-by; cheap per viewer | Short messages only; damaged by weather |
| **Internet and social media** | Cheap; targets specific groups; measurable; interactive | Needs data and phones; easy to ignore; fake adverts |
| **SMS and phone messages** | Direct to the customer's phone | Often seen as a nuisance |
| **Cinema** | Captive audience | Small audience |
| **Vehicles and vans, loudspeakers** | Mobile; reaches markets and villages | Noise; short range |
| **Shop window and point-of-sale displays** | Reach customers at the moment they buy | Only people at the shop |
| **Sponsorship** | Links brand to popular events | Costly; indirect |
| **Word of mouth** | Trusted; free | Cannot be controlled |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Choosing a medium by cost per thousand people reached.**

| Medium | Cost of one advert | People reached | Cost per thousand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radio | NLe 900 | 300,000 | $900 \div 300 = $ NLe 3 |
| Newspaper | NLe 1,500 | 25,000 | $1500 \div 25 = $ NLe 60 |
| Billboard (a month) | NLe 6,000 | 400,000 | $6000 \div 400 = $ NLe 15 |

Radio is the cheapest way to reach people, and it reaches rural and non-reading audiences. A newspaper may still be chosen when a **detailed** message must be read, for example a job advert or a tender.
:::

## Effects of advertising

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| **Informs** consumers about products, prices and where to buy | Adds to **costs**, which may raise prices |
| **Increases sales**, allowing large-scale production and lower unit costs | May **mislead** with false or exaggerated claims |
| Introduces **new products** quickly | Persuades people to buy things they **do not need** |
| Creates **jobs** in media and advertising | Encourages **monopoly**: big firms out-advertise small ones |
| **Funds** newspapers, radio, TV and sport | Can harm **culture and morals**; billboards spoil the environment |
| Encourages **competition** and better quality | May promote harmful goods (tobacco, alcohol) |

**Advertising agencies** plan and run campaigns for clients. They conduct research, design adverts, choose and buy media space, and measure results.

**Control of advertising** comes from consumer protection laws, standards bodies, media regulators such as the **Independent Media Commission**, and codes of practice within the industry.

## Other forms of promotion

### Sales promotion

Short-term incentives to buy now:

- **free samples** and **free gifts**
- **price reductions**, coupons and "buy one, get one free"
- **competitions** and raffles
- **demonstrations** and **trade fairs and exhibitions**
- **loyalty cards** and points
- **credit facilities** and **after-sales service**
- **attractive displays** at the point of sale

### Personal selling

Face-to-face selling by **sales representatives**, who explain the product, answer questions, demonstrate, take orders and collect feedback. It suits technical or expensive goods, and goods sold to businesses.

### Public relations (PR)

:::callout{type=definition}
**Public relations** is the effort to create and keep **goodwill** and a good image of the firm among the public.
:::

It uses:

- press releases and news conferences
- sponsorship of sports, education and health
- community projects (corporate social responsibility)
- open days and factory visits
- quick, honest handling of complaints

## Branding and packaging

**Branding** gives a product a name, logo or symbol that makes it **identifiable** and different from rivals. It builds **brand loyalty**, allows **advertising** of one name, and lets a firm charge a **premium price**.

**Functions of packaging:**

- **protects** goods from damage, dirt and theft
- **attracts** buyers with colour and design
- **informs**: contents, weight, price, ingredients, expiry date and instructions
- makes goods **easy to carry, store and display**
- **identifies** the brand

:::callout{type=local-example}
Mobile networks and banks in Sierra Leone sponsor football leagues and school quizzes (public relations), advertise on radio stations across the provinces (radio reaches more people than television), and run promotions with prizes for top-up customers (sales promotion). Market traders rely on **word of mouth** and **attractive displays**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Advertising** and **sales promotion** are not the same. Advertising is a paid message through a **medium**; sales promotion is a **short-term incentive** (sample, discount, gift) to buy now. Both are parts of **promotion**.
:::

## Key points

- **Advertising** is paid, non-personal promotion: informative, persuasive, competitive, generic, institutional or reminder.
- Choose a medium by **audience, cost per thousand, message, literacy and reach**; radio reaches most people in Sierra Leone.
- Advertising **informs and increases sales** but **adds cost and can mislead**.
- Promotion also includes **sales promotion, personal selling and public relations**; **branding and packaging** identify and protect products.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **define advertising**, to **state the types** (especially informative, persuasive, competitive and mass advertising), to **compare media**, to give **advantages and disadvantages of advertising** to consumers and producers, and to **explain sales promotion methods and the functions of packaging**.
:::

## Quick check

1. Distinguish informative advertising from persuasive advertising.
2. Give two advantages of radio as an advertising medium in Sierra Leone.
3. A TV advert costs NLe 4,000 and reaches 160,000 people. Find the cost per thousand.
4. Give three functions of packaging.

**Answers:** 1. Informative advertising gives facts (price, features, where to buy); persuasive advertising tries to convince people to buy, often by appealing to emotions. 2. Any two: reaches many people including rural and illiterate listeners, cheap, local languages, works without mains power. 3. $4000 \div 160 = $ NLe 25 per thousand. 4. Any three: protects, attracts, informs, makes handling easy, identifies the brand.
