# Wind, glacial and coastal erosion processes

## Learning objectives
You should be able to explain how wind, moving ice and sea waves erode the landscape, identify their main processes and recognise the environments in which they operate.

## Wind erosion
Wind is an effective geomorphological agent in dry regions where vegetation is sparse, loose sediment is available and the surface is exposed. It erodes through:
- **Deflation:** removal of loose fine particles from the ground.
- **Abrasion:** sand grains carried by wind strike and wear exposed rock surfaces.

Deflation may lower a surface and leave coarser stones behind as a desert pavement. Wind abrasion can polish or shape rocks, especially near ground level where sand concentration is high. Wind also transports sediment by suspension, saltation and surface creep, then deposits it when wind speed falls.

## Glacial erosion
Glaciers are moving masses of ice formed where snow accumulates faster than it melts over many years. As a glacier moves, it erodes by:
- **Plucking:** ice freezes onto fractured rock and pulls blocks away as it moves.
- **Abrasion:** rock fragments embedded in the ice scrape and grind the bedrock.

Glacial erosion is most effective in cold mountain and high-latitude environments. It can widen and deepen valleys and create distinctive landforms.

## Coastal erosion
Waves attack coastlines through:
- **Hydraulic action:** wave impact compresses air in cracks and loosens rock.
- **Abrasion or corrasion:** rock fragments carried by waves scrape cliffs and shore platforms.
- **Attrition:** transported fragments collide and become smaller and rounder.
- **Solution or corrosion:** seawater dissolves soluble minerals.

The strength of coastal erosion depends on wave energy, fetch, wind, tides, rock resistance, geological structure and the availability of beach sediment. Joints, faults and bedding planes can provide weaknesses along which cliffs retreat.

## Comparing the agents

| Agent | Main erosion processes | Typical environment |
|---|---|---|
| Wind | Deflation and abrasion | Dry, exposed areas with loose sediment |
| Ice | Plucking and abrasion | Glaciated mountains and high latitudes |
| Waves | Hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition and solution | Exposed coasts |

## Transport and deposition
Wind transports sand and dust, glaciers carry debris within or beneath ice, and waves move beach material through swash and backwash and alongshore currents. When energy falls, material may be deposited. Erosion, transport and deposition operate together, so a coastal or desert landscape is continually changing.

## WASSCE examination focus
Do not confuse wind deflation with wind abrasion. In glacial environments, distinguish plucking from abrasion. For coastal erosion, explain how wave energy and rock weaknesses contribute to cliff retreat.

## Quick check
1. What is deflation?
2. Distinguish glacial plucking from abrasion.
3. Name two coastal erosion processes.
4. Why is wind erosion especially effective where vegetation is sparse?

**Answers:** 1. Removal of loose surface particles by wind. 2. Plucking removes blocks attached to ice; abrasion scrapes rock with debris carried in ice. 3. Hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition or solution. 4. Loose material is exposed and less protected by roots or surface cover.
