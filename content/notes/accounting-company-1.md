:::callout{type=definition}
A **limited liability company** is a business that is a **separate legal person** from its owners, the **shareholders**, whose liability for its debts is **limited** to the amount they have agreed to pay for their shares.
:::

Companies raise long-term finance by issuing **shares** (ordinary and preference) and borrowing through **debentures**. Their accounts show special items: **share capital** at several levels, **reserves**, **dividends** and **debenture interest**. This lesson explains the types of company, shares, debentures and reserves, and how a share issue is recorded.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the features of a limited liability company
- distinguish private from public companies
- distinguish ordinary shares, preference shares and debentures
- explain authorised, issued, called-up and paid-up capital
- record an issue of shares at par and at a premium
- distinguish capital reserves from revenue reserves

## Features of a limited company

1. A **separate legal entity**: it can own property, sue and be sued in its own name.
2. **Limited liability** of shareholders.
3. **Perpetual succession**: it continues even if shareholders change or die.
4. Owned by **shareholders** and managed by **directors**.
5. Must be **registered** and file accounts; in Sierra Leone companies are registered with the **Corporate Affairs Commission** under the **Companies Act**.

| Private limited company (Ltd) | Public limited company (Plc) |
|---|---|
| shares **cannot** be offered to the general public | shares **can** be offered to the public and traded |
| fewer shareholders | many shareholders |
| often a family business | larger businesses, banks, telecoms |

## Shares and debentures

| Ordinary shares | Preference shares | Debentures |
|---|---|---|
| owners of the company, with **voting rights** | usually **no** voting rights | **loans** to the company: creditors, not owners |
| **variable** dividend, paid after preference dividends | **fixed** dividend (e.g. 8%), paid before ordinary | **fixed interest**, paid whether or not there is a profit |
| repaid **last** if the company is wound up | repaid before ordinary shareholders | repaid before shareholders; often **secured** on assets |
| dividend is an **appropriation** of profit | dividend is an appropriation | interest is an **expense** |

**Cumulative** preference shares carry forward any unpaid dividend to later years; **redeemable** preference shares can be bought back by the company.

## Levels of share capital

![Four levels of share capital, each equal to or smaller than the one above: authorised, issued, called-up and paid-up capital](https://wasscetutor.com/img/accounting/share-capital-levels.svg)

*Figure 1. Levels of share capital. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Authorised (nominal) capital** | the maximum the company may issue under its constitution |
| **Issued capital** | the shares actually issued to shareholders |
| **Called-up capital** | the part of issued capital the company has asked shareholders to pay |
| **Paid-up capital** | the part actually received; any unpaid amount is **calls in arrears** |

## Issuing shares

Shares have a **nominal (par) value**, such as NLe 1. They may be issued **at par** (for their nominal value) or **at a premium** (for more than nominal value). The premium goes to a **share premium account**, a **capital reserve**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Kamara Holdings Ltd issues 100,000 ordinary shares of NLe 1 each at NLe 1.50, fully paid in cash. Record the issue.**

- Cash received $= 100\,000 \times 1.50 = 150\,000$
- Share capital (nominal) $= 100\,000 \times 1 = 100\,000$
- Share premium $= 100\,000 \times 0.50 = 50\,000$

:::

## Reserves

| Capital reserves | Revenue reserves |
|---|---|
| created from **capital** gains, not trading | profits **kept** in the company |
| **share premium**, **revaluation reserve** | **general reserve**, **retained earnings** |
| **cannot** normally be paid out as dividends | **can** be paid out as dividends |

## Dividends and debenture interest

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A company has 50,000 8% preference shares of NLe 1 and 10% debentures of NLe 40,000. Find the preference dividend and the debenture interest for a year.**

- Preference dividend $= 8\% \times 50\,000 = 4\,000$ (an appropriation of profit)
- Debenture interest $= 10\% \times 40\,000 = 4\,000$ (an **expense**, charged before profit)
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Debenture holders are creditors**, not owners. Debenture interest is an **expense** that must be paid even in a loss-making year, and debentures are shown as a **non-current liability**, not as part of equity.
:::

## Key points

- A company is a **separate legal person**; shareholders have **limited liability**.
- **Ordinary shares:** voting owners, variable dividends. **Preference shares:** fixed dividends paid first. **Debentures:** loans with fixed interest.
- Share capital levels: **authorised ≥ issued ≥ called-up ≥ paid-up**.
- Shares issued **at a premium**: excess to **share premium** (a capital reserve).
- **Capital reserves** cannot normally be distributed; **revenue reserves** can.

## Quick check

1. State three features of a limited company.
2. Give two differences between preference shares and ordinary shares.
3. 20,000 shares of NLe 2 are issued at NLe 2.50. Find the share premium.
4. Is debenture interest an expense or an appropriation?
5. Give one capital reserve and one revenue reserve.

**Answers:**

1. Any three: separate legal entity; limited liability; perpetual succession; owned by shareholders and managed by directors; must be registered.
2. Any two: preference dividends are fixed, ordinary dividends vary; preference shareholders are paid first; ordinary shareholders usually have voting rights, preference shareholders usually do not; preference shares are repaid before ordinary shares in a winding up.
3. $20\,000 \times 0.50 = 10\,000$: NLe 10,000.
4. An expense.
5. Capital reserve: share premium or revaluation reserve. Revenue reserve: general reserve or retained earnings.
