**Latitude** is the distance of a place north or south of the Equator, and **longitude** is its distance east or west of the Prime Meridian at Greenwich, both measured in degrees. Together they give every place on Earth an exact address: Freetown is at about **8½° N, 13° W**. Because the Earth turns 15° of longitude every hour, longitude also lets you calculate the **time** anywhere in the world. This lesson explains both, step by step.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define latitude and longitude and describe the main lines of each
- state the location of a place using latitude and longitude
- calculate distances along a meridian from differences in latitude
- calculate local time from differences in longitude
- explain standard time, time zones and the International Date Line

## Latitude

:::callout{type=definition}
**Latitude** is the angular distance of a place **north or south of the Equator**, measured in degrees from the centre of the Earth. Lines joining places of the same latitude are called **parallels of latitude**; they run **east–west** around the globe.
:::

![Two globes: the first shows parallels of latitude, including the Equator, the tropics and the polar circles; the second shows meridians of longitude meeting at the poles, with the Prime Meridian at Greenwich](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/latitude-longitude.svg)

*Figure 1. Lines of latitude (left) and longitude (right). WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Parallel | Latitude | Importance |
|---|---|---|
| **Equator** | 0° | the longest parallel (a great circle); divides the Earth into northern and southern hemispheres |
| **Tropic of Cancer** | 23½° N | northern limit of the overhead Sun (21 June) |
| **Tropic of Capricorn** | 23½° S | southern limit of the overhead Sun (22 December) |
| **Arctic Circle** | 66½° N | northern limit of 24-hour day or night |
| **Antarctic Circle** | 66½° S | southern limit of 24-hour day or night |
| **North and South Poles** | 90° N and 90° S | points, not lines |

**Uses of latitude:** locating places; measuring distance north–south (1° of latitude ≈ **111 km**); and understanding climate, since temperatures generally fall away from the Equator.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Freetown is at about 8½° N. A town due north of it on the same meridian is at 18½° N. How far apart are they?**

Difference in latitude $= 18\tfrac{1}{2} - 8\tfrac{1}{2} = 10°$. Distance $\approx 10 \times 111 = 1{,}110$ km.

(If one place were north and the other south of the Equator, you would **add** their latitudes instead.)
:::

## Longitude

:::callout{type=definition}
**Longitude** is the angular distance of a place **east or west of the Prime Meridian** (0°), which passes through Greenwich in London. Lines joining places of the same longitude are called **meridians**; they run **north–south** from pole to pole and meet at the poles.
:::

- Longitude is measured up to **180° east** and **180° west**.
- The 180° meridian is roughly where the **International Date Line** runs.
- All meridians are the same length, and each is half of a great circle.

## Longitude and time

The Earth turns **360° in 24 hours**, so:

$$360° \div 24 = 15° \text{ per hour} \qquad 1° = 4 \text{ minutes}$$

Because the Earth rotates from west to east, places to the **east** see the Sun earlier: their time is **ahead**. Places to the **west** are **behind**.

![A longitude scale from 60° W to 60° E: when it is 12 noon at 30° E it is 7 a.m. at 45° W, a difference of 75°, or 5 hours](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/longitude-time.svg)

*Figure 2. Every 15° of longitude is one hour of time. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

**Method for time calculations:**

1. Find the **difference in longitude**: subtract if both places are on the same side of Greenwich, **add** if one is east and the other west.
2. Convert to time: divide by 15 for hours (or multiply by 4 for minutes).
3. **Add** the time if the place you want is **east**; **subtract** if it is **west**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**It is 2:00 p.m. at town A (45° E). What is the time at town B (15° W)?**

Difference $= 45 + 15 = 60°$ (opposite sides of Greenwich). Time $= 60 \div 15 = 4$ hours.

B is west of A, so its time is behind: $2{:}00$ p.m. $- 4$ hours $=$ **10:00 a.m.**
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**When it is 12 noon at Greenwich, it is 3:00 p.m. at town X. Find the longitude of X.**

X is 3 hours ahead, so it is east. $3 \times 15 = 45°$. X is at **45° E**.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Find the local time at Freetown (13° W) when it is 12 noon at Greenwich.**

$13 \times 4 = 52$ minutes. Freetown is west, so local (Sun) time is **11:08 a.m.**
:::

## Standard time and time zones

If every town kept its own local Sun time, timetables would be chaos. Countries therefore use **standard time**: one agreed time for a whole country or zone, usually differing from Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) by whole hours.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone keeps **Greenwich Mean Time (GMT)** all year, even though Freetown's local Sun time is about 52 minutes behind Greenwich. Neighbouring Guinea and Liberia also use GMT, while Nigeria uses **West Africa Time (GMT + 1)**. So when it is 9:00 a.m. in Freetown, it is 10:00 a.m. in Lagos.
:::

**Large countries have several time zones:** for example, the USA mainland has four and Russia has eleven.

## The International Date Line

The **International Date Line** runs roughly along the **180° meridian**, bending to avoid land. Because places east and west of Greenwich differ by up to 12 hours each way, the two sides of the line are a whole **day** apart:

- crossing the line travelling **eastwards**, you **go back one day** (repeat a date)
- crossing it travelling **westwards**, you **go forward one day** (skip a date)

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not reverse the rule. **East is ahead:** add time when the place you want is east. A quick check is that the Sun rises first in the east, so eastern places have later clocks.
:::

## Key points

- **Latitude:** north–south of the Equator; parallels run east–west; 1° ≈ 111 km.
- **Longitude:** east–west of the Prime Meridian (Greenwich); meridians run pole to pole.
- **15° = 1 hour; 1° = 4 minutes.** East is ahead; west is behind.
- Add longitudes on opposite sides of Greenwich; subtract on the same side.
- **Standard time** gives a whole country one time; Sierra Leone uses **GMT**.
- **International Date Line** (about 180°): eastwards, go back a day; westwards, go forward a day.

## Quick check

1. What is the latitude of the Tropic of Capricorn?
2. How long does it take the Earth to turn through 1° of longitude?
3. It is 8:00 a.m. at 30° W. What is the time at 60° E?
4. When it is noon at Greenwich, it is 9:20 a.m. at place P. Find the longitude of P.
5. Which time does Sierra Leone use?

**Answers:**

1. 23½° S.
2. 4 minutes.
3. Difference $90° = 6$ hours; 60° E is east, so 2:00 p.m.
4. 2 h 40 min behind $= 160$ minutes; $160 \div 4 = 40°$; P is west: 40° W.
5. Greenwich Mean Time (GMT).
