# Securities, primary and secondary markets, and investor risk

## Learning objectives
Distinguish shares from debt securities, compare primary and secondary markets and explain basic investment risks.

## Main securities
An **ordinary share** represents ownership in a company. Shareholders may receive dividends if declared and may have voting rights under the company's rules. Dividends are not guaranteed, and share prices can fall.

A **preference share** generally has priority over ordinary shares for specified dividend or capital rights, according to its terms. A **debenture or bond** is a debt instrument: the issuer borrows money and promises interest and repayment under stated conditions. Debt holders are creditors, not owners merely because they hold the security.

## Primary and secondary markets
The **primary market** is where new securities are issued to raise funds for the issuer. An initial public offering is one way a company may offer shares to the public, subject to applicable requirements.

The **secondary market** is where existing securities are traded between investors. The issuer does not normally receive the sale proceeds from an ordinary secondary-market trade, but the market provides liquidity and price information.

## Roles of market participants
An issuer raises capital. Investors supply funds and accept risk. Brokers execute transactions for clients. Dealers may trade as principals. A stock exchange provides an organised market and rule framework. Regulators supervise market conduct and investor protection under the relevant law.

## Investment risk
Risks include market-price movements, issuer default, liquidity constraints, inflation, interest-rate changes and concentration. Diversification spreads exposure across different investments but cannot eliminate all risk. Investors should understand fees, time horizon, risk tolerance and the legal documents before investing.

## WASSCE examination focus
A share is ownership; a bond or debenture is debt. The primary market issues new securities; the secondary market trades existing securities. Do not describe a stock exchange as the issuer of every security traded on it.

## Quick check
1. Distinguish a share from a debenture.
2. What happens in a primary market?
3. Why is a secondary market useful?
4. State three investment risks.

**Answers:** 1. A share is ownership capital; a debenture is borrowed capital. 2. New securities are issued to investors. 3. It provides liquidity and price discovery for existing securities. 4. Market, default, liquidity, inflation and interest-rate risks.
