# Market research, segmentation and the marketing mix

## Learning objectives
Explain market research methods, identify market segments and apply the marketing mix to a product.

## Market research
Market research gathers and analyses information about customers, competitors and market conditions. **Primary research** collects new information directly through surveys, interviews, observation or focus groups. **Secondary research** uses existing information such as published reports, government statistics, trade data and internal sales records.

A good research plan defines the decision to be made, target population, sample, questions, collection method, budget and analysis. A biased sample or leading question can produce misleading conclusions. Results should be treated as evidence, not certainty.

## Market segmentation
Segmentation divides a broad market into groups with similar needs or characteristics. Common bases include:
- **Geographic:** location, climate or settlement.
- **Demographic:** age, income, occupation or household size.
- **Psychographic:** lifestyle, interests and values.
- **Behavioural:** usage rate, loyalty, benefits sought and purchase occasion.

A business selects target segments it can serve effectively and positions its product to communicate a clear benefit.

## The marketing mix: 4 Ps
1. **Product:** features, quality, design, packaging, brand and after-sales support.
2. **Price:** the amount charged and the pricing strategy.
3. **Place:** distribution channels and where customers can obtain the product.
4. **Promotion:** advertising, sales promotion, public relations, personal selling and digital communication.

The elements must fit together. A premium product sold through specialist outlets needs a different price and promotional approach from a low-cost everyday product.

## Measuring marketing performance
Businesses can monitor sales volume, revenue, repeat purchases, customer acquisition cost, market share, complaint rates and campaign response. An increase in social-media views does not necessarily mean profitable sales.

## WASSCE examination focus
For a case study, identify the target customer first, then apply each of the 4 Ps to that customer. Distinguish promotion (the wider communication effort) from advertising (one promotional method).

## Quick check
1. Distinguish primary from secondary research.
2. State four bases of segmentation.
3. List the 4 Ps.
4. Why should a firm measure repeat purchases?

**Answers:** 1. Primary research collects new data; secondary research uses existing data. 2. Geographic, demographic, psychographic and behavioural. 3. Product, price, place and promotion. 4. It indicates whether customers return and can help assess satisfaction and loyalty.
