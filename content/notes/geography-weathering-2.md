**Mass wasting** (also called **mass movement**) is the movement of soil, rock and weathered material **down a slope under the pull of gravity**. It ranges from **soil creep**, too slow to see, to sudden **landslides**, **mudflows** and **rockfalls** that can bury whole communities. In August 2017 a mudslide on Sugar Loaf Mountain above Regent, Freetown, killed or buried more than 1,000 people. This lesson explains the types of mass wasting, their causes and effects, and how they can be prevented.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define mass wasting and explain the role of gravity and water
- describe the main types of mass movement, from slow to very fast
- explain the natural and human causes of mass movement
- state the effects of landslides and mudflows
- suggest ways of preventing and reducing mass movement

## What makes slopes fail?

:::callout{type=definition}
**Mass wasting** is the downslope movement of weathered material as a whole mass, under **gravity**. Unlike erosion by rivers or wind, no moving agent carries the material, although **water** often makes it heavier and more slippery.
:::

On every slope, gravity pulls material downhill, while friction and the strength of the soil and rock hold it in place. When the pull becomes greater than the resistance, the slope fails. Water is the usual trigger: it **adds weight**, **fills the spaces** between particles and acts as a **lubricant**.

## Types of mass movement

![Four slopes showing soil creep with tilted poles, a landslide along a slip plane, a mudflow of saturated soil, and a rockfall from a steep face](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/mass-movement-types.svg)

*Figure 1. Types of mass movement, from very slow to very fast. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Type | Speed | Description | Signs or features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Soil creep** | very slow (millimetres a year) | gradual downhill movement of soil particles, helped by wetting and drying | tilted trees, fences, poles and walls; small steps (terracettes) on slopes |
| **Solifluction** | slow | waterlogged soil flowing over frozen ground | cold regions |
| **Earthflow** | slow to moderate | saturated soil flowing down a gentle slope | tongue-shaped lobes |
| **Mudflow** | fast | soil and debris so full of water that it flows like a river of mud | follows valleys; can travel far |
| **Landslide** | very fast | a mass of rock and soil slides suddenly along a slip plane | scar on the hillside; heap of debris below |
| **Slump** | fast | a block rotates and slides along a curved surface | backward-tilted block |
| **Rockfall** | very fast | rock fragments fall freely from a cliff or steep face | heap of boulders (scree) at the foot |

## Causes of mass movement

**Natural causes:**

1. **Heavy or prolonged rainfall**, which saturates the soil.
2. **Steep slopes.**
3. **Weak or deeply weathered rock**, and layers of clay that become slippery when wet.
4. **Undercutting** of slopes by rivers or waves.
5. **Earthquakes and volcanic eruptions**, which shake slopes loose.

**Human causes:**

1. **Deforestation:** removing trees removes the roots that bind the soil and the canopy that slows rainfall.
2. **Building on steep slopes**, which adds weight and blocks natural drainage.
3. **Quarrying and mining**, which steepen and weaken slopes.
4. **Road cutting** into hillsides.
5. **Poor drainage** of rain and waste water.

## Effects of mass movement

- **Loss of life** and injuries.
- **Destruction of homes**, farms, roads and bridges.
- **Blocking of rivers**, which can cause floods.
- **Loss of fertile soil** from slopes.
- **Disease and displacement** after disasters.
- **Economic costs** of rescue, relief and rebuilding.

:::callout{type=local-example}
On **14 August 2017**, after days of very heavy rain, part of **Sugar Loaf Mountain** above **Regent** in Freetown collapsed. A **mudflow** of rock, soil and water swept down the valley through the communities below, killing or burying more than 1,000 people and destroying hundreds of homes. Investigations pointed to the combination of **steep slopes**, **saturated soil**, **deforestation** of the hillsides and **unplanned building** on them, a clear example of natural and human causes acting together.
:::

## Preventing and reducing mass movement

1. **Afforestation and reforestation:** plant trees and grass to bind soil.
2. **Terracing** of steep slopes.
3. **Drainage channels** to carry rainwater safely off slopes.
4. **Retaining walls**, wire-mesh nets and rock bolts on dangerous slopes and road cuts.
5. **Land-use planning:** ban building on steep, unstable slopes; enforce building regulations.
6. **Monitoring and early warning** during heavy rain, and evacuation plans.
7. **Public education** about the danger signs, such as cracks in the ground and tilting walls.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Mass wasting is not caused by a river or the wind carrying material away. It is movement under **gravity**; water helps, but it is not the transporting agent.
:::

## Key points

- **Mass wasting:** downslope movement of material under **gravity**; water is the usual trigger.
- Types: **soil creep** (very slow), **solifluction**, **earthflow**, **mudflow**, **landslide**, **slump**, **rockfall** (very fast).
- Causes: heavy rain, steep slopes, weak rock, undercutting, earthquakes; **deforestation, building on slopes, quarrying, road cuts**.
- Effects: loss of life, destruction, blocked rivers, soil loss.
- Prevention: **trees, terraces, drainage, retaining walls, planning, early warning**.
- **Freetown, 14 August 2017:** the Regent mudslide.

## Quick check

1. Define mass wasting.
2. Name the slowest type of mass movement and give one sign of it.
3. Give two human causes of landslides.
4. Why does heavy rain trigger mass movement?
5. State two ways of preventing landslides.

**Answers:**

1. The downslope movement of soil, rock and weathered material under gravity.
2. Soil creep; signs include tilted trees, fences and poles, and terracettes.
3. Any two: deforestation; building on steep slopes; quarrying and mining; road cutting; poor drainage.
4. Water adds weight, fills the spaces between particles and acts as a lubricant, reducing friction.
5. Any two: afforestation; terracing; drainage channels; retaining walls; land-use planning; early warning.
