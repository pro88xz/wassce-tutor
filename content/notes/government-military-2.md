From 1991 to 2002, Sierra Leone suffered one of the most brutal civil wars of modern times. Tens of thousands of people were killed, about two million were driven from their homes, and thousands of civilians had limbs cut off. Yet the country also built one of Africa's most studied peace processes. This lesson explains the causes, course and effects of the civil war, and how peace was built.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the causes of the civil war
- outline its main events from 1991 to 2002
- state the effects of the war
- describe the peace agreements and peace-building measures

## Causes of the war

1. **Bad governance**: years of one-party rule, corruption and abuse of power under the APC.
2. **Economic collapse**: unpaid salaries, inflation and poverty in the 1980s.
3. **Youth unemployment and marginalisation**: many young people had no education, jobs or voice, and were easily recruited.
4. **Collapse of education and social services**.
5. **Diamonds**: control of the diamond fields funded the fighting ("blood" or "conflict" diamonds).
6. **External support**: the RUF was backed by **Charles Taylor's** NPFL in Liberia, and its leaders had trained in Libya.
7. **Weak and divided army**, poorly paid and equipped.
8. **Exclusion of rural areas** and resentment of chiefs' abuses.

## Course of the war

![A timeline: 1991 RUF invades from Liberia; 1996 elections and Abidjan Peace Accord; 1997 AFRC coup and junta with the RUF; 1998 ECOMOG restores Kabbah; 1999 attack on Freetown and Lomé Peace Agreement; 1999 UNAMSIL; 2000 peacekeepers taken hostage and British intervention; 2002 war declared over](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/civil-war-timeline.svg)

*Figure 1. War and peace, 1991–2002. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **23 March 1991** | The **Revolutionary United Front (RUF)**, led by **Foday Sankoh**, attacks **Bomaru** in **Kailahun District** from Liberia |
| **1992** | Soldiers from the war front overthrow Momoh (NPRC) |
| **1995** | RUF advances near Freetown; the NPRC hires private military contractors |
| **1996** | Elections bring **Kabbah** to power; the **Abidjan Peace Accord** (November) fails to hold |
| **May 1997** | **AFRC** coup; the AFRC invites the RUF to share power; Kabbah goes into exile in Guinea |
| **February 1998** | **ECOMOG**, the Nigerian-led ECOWAS force, drives out the junta; Kabbah returns in March |
| **6 January 1999** | AFRC and RUF forces invade **Freetown**, killing thousands and burning much of the city |
| **7 July 1999** | **Lomé Peace Agreement**: power-sharing, amnesty, and a role for Sankoh |
| **October 1999** | The UN creates **UNAMSIL**, a peacekeeping mission |
| **May 2000** | The RUF takes hundreds of UN peacekeepers hostage; **British troops intervene**; Sankoh is arrested |
| **2000–2001** | **Abuja ceasefire agreements**; disarmament begins in earnest |
| **18 January 2002** | President Kabbah declares the war **over** |

The **Civil Defence Forces (CDF)**, including the **Kamajors**, community militias based on traditional hunters, fought against the RUF and AFRC alongside government forces.

## Effects of the war

- **Loss of life**: estimates of at least **50,000** deaths.
- **Displacement**: around **two million** people, nearly half the population, displaced, many as refugees in Guinea and Liberia.
- **Atrocities**: amputations, rape and sexual violence, abduction of **child soldiers**.
- **Destruction** of homes, schools, hospitals, roads and the economy.
- **Collapse of state institutions** in much of the country.
- **Trauma** and broken communities.
- **Huge international involvement**: ECOMOG, the UN, Britain and donors.

## Building peace

| Measure | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Disarmament, Demobilisation and Reintegration (DDR)** | More than **70,000** combatants handed in weapons and received help to return to civilian life |
| **UNAMSIL (1999–2005)** | At its height one of the largest UN peacekeeping missions in the world |
| **Truth and Reconciliation Commission (TRC)** | Heard testimony from victims and perpetrators; its **2004** report explained the causes of the war and recommended reforms |
| **Special Court for Sierra Leone (2002–2013)** | Tried those bearing the greatest responsibility; convicted **Charles Taylor** in **2012** |
| **Security sector reform** | Rebuilt the army (RSLAF) and police with British and international help |
| **Elections (2002)** | Restored democratic legitimacy; Kabbah re-elected |
| **Decentralisation (2004)** | Local councils revived to bring government closer to the people |
| **Anti-Corruption Commission (2000)** | Tackled one of the root causes |

:::callout{type=local-example}
The **Truth and Reconciliation Commission** found that the war's root causes lay in **years of bad governance, corruption and the marginalisation of young people and rural areas**, not simply in diamonds or foreign interference. Communities across the country have also used traditional ceremonies and local initiatives to reconcile with former fighters and help them return home.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The **Lomé Peace Agreement (1999)** did **not** end the war. Fighting continued, and in **May 2000** the RUF took UN peacekeepers hostage. The war ended after the **Abuja agreements**, disarmament and the declaration of **18 January 2002**.
:::

## Key points

- Causes: **bad governance, economic collapse, youth marginalisation, diamonds, Liberian (Charles Taylor) support, weak army**.
- Began **23 March 1991** at **Bomaru**, Kailahun; RUF led by **Foday Sankoh**.
- Milestones: **Abidjan (1996)**, **AFRC coup (1997)**, **ECOMOG (1998)**, **Freetown invasion and Lomé (1999)**, **UNAMSIL (1999)**, **British intervention (2000)**, **war over (18 January 2002)**.
- Peace-building: **DDR, UNAMSIL, TRC, Special Court, security reform, elections, decentralisation**.

## WASSCE examination focus

Expect to **explain the causes and effects of the civil war**, to **describe the peace agreements**, and to **explain the measures taken to build peace**. Questions also ask about the roles of ECOWAS (ECOMOG), the UN and the TRC.
:::

## Quick check

1. When and where did the civil war begin?
2. Which ECOWAS force restored President Kabbah in 1998?
3. What was the Lomé Peace Agreement?
4. Name two institutions set up to deal with the legacy of the war.

**Answers:**

1. On 23 March 1991, when the RUF attacked Bomaru in Kailahun District from Liberia.
2. ECOMOG (the ECOWAS Monitoring Group, led by Nigeria).
3. The peace agreement signed on 7 July 1999 between the government and the RUF, providing for power-sharing, an amnesty and the disarmament of fighters.
4. Any two: the Truth and Reconciliation Commission; the Special Court for Sierra Leone; the DDR programme; the Anti-Corruption Commission.
