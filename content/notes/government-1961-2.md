The 1961 Constitution lasted just ten years. In that time Sierra Leone saw a peaceful change of Prime Minister, a bitter debate over a one-party state, a disputed election, three military takeovers and a return to civilian rule. These events tested the constitution and revealed both its strengths and its weaknesses. This lesson evaluates the 1961 Constitution in the light of what happened between 1961 and 1971.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the strengths of the 1961 Constitution
- state its weaknesses
- describe the political events of 1961 to 1971
- explain why the constitution was replaced in 1971

## Events from 1961 to 1971

*Figure 1. From independence to republic. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1964** | **Sir Milton Margai** dies; his brother **Albert Margai** becomes Prime Minister |
| **1966** | Albert Margai proposes a **one-party state** and a **republic**; strong opposition from the APC, the press and many citizens |
| **1967 (March)** | The **APC** wins the general election; the Governor-General swears in **Siaka Stevens** as Prime Minister; **Brigadier David Lansana** declares martial law |
| **1967** | Army officers overthrow Lansana and form the **National Reformation Council (NRC)** under **Lt-Col Andrew Juxon-Smith** |
| **1968 (April)** | Junior ranks overthrow the NRC in the **Sergeants' revolt**; civilian rule returns and **Stevens** becomes Prime Minister |
| **1971 (March)** | An attempted coup against Stevens fails |
| **1971 (19 April)** | Sierra Leone becomes a **republic** |

## Strengths of the 1961 Constitution

1. **Protected fundamental human rights.**
2. Provided for a **multi-party democracy** with a recognised opposition.
3. **Independent judiciary**, with appeals to an impartial court abroad.
4. **Public Service and Judicial Service Commissions** kept appointments free of politics.
5. **Entrenched clauses** made it hard for a ruling party to change the constitution for its own ends.
6. **Recognised chieftaincy** and customary law, linking tradition with modern government.
7. **Peaceful transfer of power** was possible through elections, as the 1967 result showed.

## Weaknesses of the 1961 Constitution

1. **Foreign head of state**: the British monarch remained head of state, which many saw as incomplete independence.
2. **Final appeals to the Privy Council** in London: justice was expensive and distant.
3. **Imported Westminster model** that did not suit local conditions, such as ethnic and regional politics.
4. **Governor-General's discretion** in appointing a Prime Minister could be disputed, as in **1967**.
5. **No safeguards against military intervention**, and the army was drawn into politics.
6. **Fusion of powers** allowed a dominant party to control both the executive and the legislature.
7. **Paramount Chief members** were not directly elected, and tended to support the government of the day.

## Why it was replaced

- Political leaders wanted a **Sierra Leonean head of state** and full sovereignty.
- The **APC** had long favoured a **republic** with an **executive president**.
- Stevens wanted a **strong executive** to deal with coup attempts and instability.
- Many African states, such as Ghana in 1960, had already become republics.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Albert Margai's plan for a **one-party state** in **1966** provoked strong opposition. Critics feared it would end democracy. The proposal was dropped, and the **1967 election** became a contest between the SLPP and the APC. When the APC won and the army intervened, the crisis showed that constitutional rules alone could not protect democracy without respect for election results and a politically neutral army.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**"Assess the 1961 Constitution."** Structure your essay:

- **Introduction:** what the constitution was (Westminster model, 1961–1971).
- **Strengths** with examples: rights, multi-party system, independent courts, peaceful change of government possible.
- **Weaknesses** with examples: foreign head of state, Privy Council, Governor-General's discretion (1967), no protection against coups.
- **Conclusion:** it provided a democratic framework, but political leaders and the army did not respect it, and it was replaced in 1971.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The **National Reformation Council** did not take power from Albert Margai directly. **Brigadier Lansana** intervened first, declaring martial law; other officers then removed Lansana and formed the **NRC**.
:::

## Key points

- **1964**: Albert Margai succeeds Milton Margai; **1966**: one-party proposal; **1967**: APC wins, **Lansana** intervenes, **NRC** rules; **1968**: **Sergeants' revolt** restores civilian rule under **Stevens**.
- **Strengths**: rights, multi-party democracy, independent judiciary, service commissions, entrenched clauses, chieftaincy recognised.
- **Weaknesses**: **foreign head of state**, **Privy Council appeals**, imported model, **Governor-General's discretion**, no safeguard against coups.
- Replaced by the **1971 Republican Constitution**.

## Quick check

1. Who succeeded Sir Milton Margai as Prime Minister in 1964?
2. What was the National Reformation Council?
3. State two weaknesses of the 1961 Constitution.
4. How did civilian rule return in 1968?

**Answers:**

1. Albert Margai.
2. The military government that ruled Sierra Leone from 1967 to 1968, led by Lt-Col Andrew Juxon-Smith.
3. Any two: foreign head of state; final appeals to the Privy Council; imported Westminster model; Governor-General's discretion in appointing a Prime Minister; no safeguard against coups; fusion of powers allowed party dominance; Paramount Chief members not directly elected.
4. Junior ranks of the army overthrew the NRC in the Sergeants' revolt (April 1968) and restored civilian rule, with Siaka Stevens as Prime Minister.
