Freetown was founded as a home for freed Black people: the "Black Poor" from London, Nova Scotian settlers who had fought for Britain in the American War of Independence, Jamaican Maroons, and tens of thousands of Africans freed from slave ships. Over the nineteenth century this settlement became a British **Colony**, and in 1896 Britain declared a **Protectorate** over the vast hinterland. This lesson explains how Sierra Leone came under British rule and how the Colony and the Protectorate were governed differently.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- outline the founding of Freetown and the creation of the Colony
- explain who the Liberated Africans were and how Krio society developed
- explain why and how the Protectorate was declared in 1896
- describe the Hut Tax War of 1898
- distinguish the government of the Colony from that of the Protectorate

### The Liberated Africans and the Krio

After Britain abolished the slave trade in **1807**, the Royal Navy intercepted slave ships off West Africa and brought the freed captives to Freetown. These **Liberated Africans** came from many parts of West Africa. Over time they and the earlier settlers formed the **Krio** community, with its own language, churches and schools, such as **Fourah Bay College** (1827). Krio professionals became teachers, clergy, lawyers, doctors and traders across British West Africa.

## The Protectorate, 1896

On **31 August 1896**, Britain declared a **Protectorate** over the territory behind the Colony. Reasons included:

- **Competition with France**, which was expanding in Guinea and could block Freetown's trade routes.
- **Protecting trade** with the interior, which was disrupted by wars between chiefdoms.
- **Ending the slave trade** and slave raiding in the hinterland.
- **Securing the boundaries** agreed with France (1895) and Liberia.

The Protectorate was divided into **districts**, each under a British **District Commissioner**, and ruled **indirectly through the chiefs**. A **Frontier Police** force kept order.

## The Hut Tax War of 1898

To pay for administering the Protectorate, Governor **Frederic Cardew** imposed a **house (hut) tax** from **1898**: **five shillings** a year on each house of up to four rooms and **ten shillings** on larger houses.

**Causes of the war:**

1. Chiefs saw the tax as a sign that they had **lost their land and sovereignty**.
2. People had little cash and were forced to **work or sell crops** to pay.
3. **Brutal collection** by the Frontier Police, who flogged and humiliated chiefs who could not pay.
4. **Loss of chiefs' powers** to District Commissioners.
5. Resentment of the **Krio traders and missionaries** associated with British rule.

**Course:** In the north, **Bai Bureh** of Kasseh led a skilful guerrilla war from February 1898. In April, a coordinated **rising in Mende country** attacked British officials, Krio traders and missionaries. British troops crushed the resistance by the end of the year. Bai Bureh was captured in November 1898 and exiled; other leaders were executed.

**Results:** British control of the Protectorate was secured; the tax remained; a royal commission under **Sir David Chalmers** criticised the tax and its methods of collection; and suspicion grew between the Protectorate and the Colony.

## The Colony and the Protectorate compared

| | Colony | Protectorate |
|---|---|---|
| **Area** | Freetown peninsula and nearby coast | The hinterland |
| **Legal status** | British territory; inhabitants British subjects | Under British protection; inhabitants "British protected persons" |
| **System of rule** | **Direct rule**: Governor, Executive and Legislative Councils | **Indirect rule** through Paramount Chiefs under District Commissioners |
| **Law** | English law and courts | Mainly customary law and native courts |
| **Population** | Mainly Krio, with others | Mende, Temne, Limba and other groups |
| **Education** | Many schools; Fourah Bay College | Few schools until later |

:::callout{type=local-example}
The division between **Colony** and **Protectorate** shaped Sierra Leone's politics for decades. It produced different laws, unequal access to education, and rivalry between Krio leaders in Freetown and the educated leaders of the Protectorate, which surfaced sharply in the debates over the **1951 constitution**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Freetown did not become a Crown Colony in 1787 or 1792. It was first run by a private company, the **Sierra Leone Company**, and became a **Crown Colony on 1 January 1808**. The Protectorate came much later, in **1896**.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Colony**: the Freetown peninsula and nearby coast, British territory ruled directly, whose inhabitants were British subjects.
- **Direct rule**: rule through British officials, the Governor and his councils, rather than through traditional rulers.
- **Liberated Africans** (recaptives): Africans freed from slave ships by the Royal Navy and landed at Freetown from 1808.
:::

## Key points

- **1787** Province of Freedom; **1792** Nova Scotians found **Freetown**; **1800** Maroons; **1808** Crown Colony.
- **Liberated Africans** landed after **1807** helped form the **Krio** community.
- **Protectorate** declared on **31 August 1896**, partly to block **French** expansion.
- The **Hut Tax** (from 1898) caused the **Hut Tax War**: **Bai Bureh** in the north and a **Mende rising** in the south.
- The **Colony** had **direct rule**; the **Protectorate** had **indirect rule** through chiefs.

## Quick check

1. When did Sierra Leone become a British Crown Colony?
2. Who were the Liberated Africans?
3. State two reasons why Britain declared the Protectorate in 1896.
4. Which governor imposed the hut tax?

**Answers:**

1. On 1 January 1808.
2. Africans freed from slave ships by the Royal Navy after the abolition of the slave trade in 1807 and landed in Freetown.
3. Any two: to stop French expansion; to protect trade with the interior; to end slave raiding; to secure the boundaries with French Guinea and Liberia.
4. Governor Frederic Cardew.
