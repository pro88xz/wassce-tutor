Freetown had a city council in the 1890s, decades before most of Africa. Yet for much of Sierra Leone's history after independence, power was concentrated in the capital, and local councils were abolished altogether in 1972. They were revived only after the civil war, as part of rebuilding the state. This lesson traces the history of local government in Sierra Leone and explains how local councils and chiefdoms work together today.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- trace the development of local government in Sierra Leone
- explain why local councils were revived in 2004
- describe the structure and functions of local councils today
- explain the relationship between local councils and chiefdom authorities
- evaluate decentralisation in Sierra Leone

## Milestones in local government

![A timeline: 1893 Freetown municipality; 1896 Protectorate ruled through chiefs; 1937 Native Administration reforms; 1940s District Councils and the Protectorate Assembly; 1972 local councils abolished; 2004 Local Government Act; 2017 190 chiefdoms; 2022 Local Government Act](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/local-gov-history.svg)

*Figure 1. Local government through the years. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Year | Development |
|---|---|
| **1893** | **Freetown** becomes a municipality with an elected city council, one of the earliest in British West Africa |
| **1896** | The Protectorate is ruled **through chiefs** (indirect rule) |
| **1937** | **Native Administration** reforms create chiefdom treasuries |
| **1940s** | **District Councils** and the **Protectorate Assembly** are created |
| **1972** | **Local councils abolished**; power centralised in Freetown |
| **2004** | **Local Government Act** revives local councils; elections held |
| **2017** | New chiefdoms bring the total to **190** |
| **2022** | **Local Government Act 2022** replaces the 2004 Act |

## Why councils were revived in 2004

1. **Lessons of the war**: the Truth and Reconciliation Commission and others linked the war to **over-centralisation** and the neglect of rural areas.
2. **Participation**: to give people a say in decisions that affect them.
3. **Better services**: to deliver health, education and other services closer to communities.
4. **Accountability**: local leaders can be judged by local voters.
5. **Donor support** for decentralisation as part of post-war recovery.

## Local councils today

- **22 local councils**: **district councils** in rural areas and **city or municipal councils** in towns.
- Each council is headed by a **directly elected** **mayor** (cities) or **chairperson** (districts).
- **Councillors** represent wards; in **2023** they were elected by **district block proportional representation**.
- Each ward has a **ward committee** that links the council with communities.
- A **Chief Administrator** heads the council's staff.
- Council terms are **five years**.

**Devolved functions** transferred from ministries to councils include **primary health care**, **primary education facilities**, **agricultural extension**, **feeder roads**, **water and sanitation** in some areas, and **solid waste management**.

## Councils and chiefdoms

| Local councils | Chiefdom authorities |
|---|---|
| Created by statute (Local Government Act) | Rooted in custom; guaranteed by the Constitution |
| Elected for five-year terms | Paramount Chiefs elected for life by councillors |
| Plan development and deliver devolved services | Keep order, manage land and custom |
| Receive grants and raise local revenue | Help collect local tax, shared with councils |

**Cooperation:** Paramount Chiefs sit on or work with councils and ward committees, help mobilise communities, and share local tax revenue with councils.

**Tension:** disputes can arise over **revenue sharing**, **land** and **who controls local development**.

## Evaluation of decentralisation

**Achievements:** councils have built and maintained schools, clinics, markets and feeder roads; citizens have more contact with local leaders; local revenue has grown in some cities through property tax reform.

**Challenges:** councils depend heavily on **central grants**, which are often late; **some ministries have been slow** to hand over functions and funds; **skilled staff** are scarce; and **political interference** and **corruption** persist.

:::callout{type=local-example}
The first local council elections under the **2004 Act** were held in **May 2004**, the first in more than thirty years. Since then council elections have been held alongside or close to national elections. **Freetown City Council**, with roots going back to **1893**, remains the country's best-known council and has worked on waste collection, tree planting and property tax reform.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Local councils were not created for the first time in 2004. Freetown had a council from **1893**, and District Councils existed from the **1940s**. What happened in **2004** was the **revival** of local councils that had been **abolished in 1972**.
:::

## Key points

- **1893** Freetown municipality; **1940s** District Councils; **1972** councils abolished; **2004** councils revived; **2022** new Act.
- Revived to **reverse over-centralisation**, improve services and participation after the war.
- **22 councils**: district and city/municipal; **directly elected mayors and chairpersons**; **ward committees**; **devolved functions**.
- Councils and chiefdoms **cooperate** (revenue sharing, mobilisation) but can **clash** over revenue and land.

## Quick check

1. In what year were local councils abolished in Sierra Leone?
2. Which Act revived local councils?
3. Name two functions devolved to local councils.
4. Give one area of cooperation and one area of conflict between councils and chiefdoms.

**Answers:**

1. 1972.
2. The Local Government Act 2004.
3. Any two: primary health care; primary education facilities; agricultural extension; feeder roads; water and sanitation; solid waste management.
4. Cooperation: sharing local tax revenue, mobilising communities, ward committees. Conflict: revenue sharing, land, control of local development.
