Two farmers with the same land, rain and seed can end the year very differently: one with savings and a plan for next season, the other in debt and unsure why. The difference is usually **management**: planning what to produce, organising the work, keeping **records**, and checking the results against the plan. This lesson covers the functions of a farm manager, how to plan a farm and choose enterprises, the layout of a farm, the types and uses of farm records, and simple farm accounts including gross margin and depreciation.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define farm management and state the functions of a farm manager
- describe the steps in farm planning and the factors that affect the choice of enterprises
- plan a simple farm layout
- name the types of farm records and state their importance
- calculate gross margin and depreciation
- describe a simple profit and loss account

## Farm management

:::callout{type=definition}
**Farm management** is the organisation and control of the farm's resources (land, labour, capital) to achieve the farmer's goals, usually the **highest profit** that can be sustained.
:::

**Functions of a farm manager:**

- **Planning**: deciding in advance what, how much, when and how to produce.
- **Organising**: arranging land, labour, inputs and equipment.
- **Directing and coordinating**: guiding workers and making sure activities fit together.
- **Controlling**: checking results against the plan and correcting problems.
- **Decision making** and **risk bearing**.
- **Record keeping** and **marketing**.

## Farm planning

### Steps

1. **Set goals**: food for the family, income, or both.
2. **Take stock of resources**: land (area, soil, water), labour, capital, equipment, skills.
3. **Study the market**: what sells, where, at what price and when.
4. **Choose enterprises** and decide how much of each.
5. **Prepare a budget**: expected costs and returns.
6. **Make a work calendar**: when each operation will happen and who will do it.
7. **Carry out** the plan, **keep records**, and **review** at the end of the season.

### Factors affecting the choice of enterprise

- **Climate and soil** (rainfall, soil type, drainage, slope).
- **Market demand** and prices; distance to market; transport.
- **Capital** available and access to credit.
- **Labour** supply, especially at peak periods.
- The farmer's **skills, interest and experience**.
- **Risk**: pests, diseases, price swings.
- **Land tenure**: tree crops need secure, long-term access to land.
- **Government policy**: subsidies, input supply, extension services.
- **Complementarity**: enterprises that help each other (crops and livestock, fish and rice).

## Farm layout

**Principles:** the house, store and office near the **road** and entrance; livestock houses **near the store** but **downwind** of the house; crop plots divided for **rotation**; fish ponds and irrigated crops near **water**; tree crops on land that will not be needed for other uses; **paths** linking every part; fire breaks where needed.

## Farm records

| Type | What it records |
|---|---|
| **inventory record** | all assets: land, buildings, machines, tools, animals, stores, at a date |
| **production records** | crop yields per plot; eggs, milk, weights; litter sizes |
| **input records** | seed, fertiliser, feed, chemicals used and their cost |
| **labour record** | workers, days worked, wages paid |
| **livestock records** | births, deaths, breeding, vaccinations, treatments |
| **sales and purchases** (cash book) | money received and paid out, with dates |
| **farm diary** | daily activities and events (rain, pests, visitors) |
| **financial records** | profit and loss account, balance sheet |

**Importance of records:** show whether the farm makes a **profit**; help in **planning** and decision making; show which enterprise is most profitable; help detect problems (falling yields, high mortality); needed for **loans** and credit; help in tax and inheritance matters; prevent theft and fraud.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Keeping records in your head.** Memory is unreliable, especially months later. A simple **exercise book** with dated entries for money in, money out, inputs used and yields is far better than nothing, and lenders and cooperatives will ask for it.
:::

## Simple farm accounts

### Gross margin

:::callout{type=definition}
$$\text{gross margin} = \text{gross output (value of production)} - \text{variable costs}$$

Gross margin is used to **compare enterprises** on the same farm. It ignores fixed costs, which are shared by all enterprises.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Compare 1 ha of maize and 1 ha of groundnut.**

| | Maize | Groundnut |
|---|---|---|
| yield × price | 40 bags × NLe 200 = **8000** | 25 bags × NLe 400 = **10 000** |
| seed | 600 | 1500 |
| fertiliser | 1500 | 500 |
| labour | 1800 | 2800 |
| chemicals | 300 | 400 |
| **total variable costs** | **4200** | **5200** |
| **gross margin** | $8000 - 4200 = $ **3800** | $10\,000 - 5200 = $ **4800** |

Groundnut gives the higher gross margin per hectare, and it also adds nitrogen for the next crop.
:::

### Depreciation

Machines, tools and buildings lose value as they are used. **Depreciation** spreads their cost over their working life.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A farmer group buys a power tiller for NLe 40 000. It is expected to last 6 years and then be sold for NLe 4000. Find the yearly depreciation (straight-line method).**

$$\text{depreciation per year} = \frac{\text{cost} - \text{scrap value}}{\text{life}} = \frac{40\,000 - 4000}{6} = \text{NLe } 6000$$
:::

### Profit and loss account

A **profit and loss account** lists all the farm's **income** (sales of produce, value of produce consumed at home, increase in stock) and all its **expenses** (variable costs, fixed costs including depreciation) for a year. **Profit** = income − expenses; if expenses are greater, there is a **loss**.

A **balance sheet** shows what the farm **owns** (assets) and what it **owes** (liabilities) at a given date; the difference is the farmer's **net worth**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Records and the cooperative loan.** A women's vegetable group in Bombali that kept a simple cash book and yield record for two seasons could show their bank and cooperative exactly how much they earned per bed and per season. That record was what got them a loan for a water pump, and with dry-season irrigation their income rose. Records are not paperwork for its own sake: they are evidence.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. State four functions of a farm manager."}
Any four: planning, organising, directing and coordinating, controlling, decision making and risk bearing, record keeping, marketing.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Give five factors that affect a farmer's choice of enterprise."}
Any five: climate and soil; market demand and prices; capital and credit; labour; skills and interest; risk; land tenure; government policy; complementarity of enterprises.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. State four importances of farm records."}
Any four: show profit or loss; help planning and decisions; show the most profitable enterprise; detect problems; needed for loans; tax and inheritance; prevent theft.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. A poultry unit's sales are NLe 25 000 and its variable costs are NLe 17 500. Find the gross margin."}
$25\,000 - 17\,500 = $ NLe 7500.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. A sprayer costs NLe 3000, lasts 5 years and has no scrap value. Find its yearly depreciation."}
$3000 \div 5 = $ NLe 600 per year.
:::

## Key points

- **Farm management**: planning, organising, directing, controlling, deciding, recording, marketing.
- **Planning**: goals → resources → market → enterprises → budget → calendar → review.
- Enterprise choice depends on **climate, soil, market, capital, labour, skills, risk, tenure, policy**.
- **Records**: inventory, production, inputs, labour, livestock, cash book, diary, accounts.
- **Gross margin** = gross output − variable costs; **depreciation** = (cost − scrap) ÷ life.
- **Profit and loss account**: income − expenses for the year.
