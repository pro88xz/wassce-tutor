The **peoples of West Africa** belong to hundreds of **ethnic groups**, speaking languages from three of Africa's language families: **Niger-Congo** (the largest, including Mande, Wolof, Fula, Temne, Mende, Akan, Yoruba and Igbo), **Afro-Asiatic** (including Hausa and the Tuareg's Tamasheq) and **Nilo-Saharan** (including Kanuri and Songhai). Many groups trace their origins to **migrations**, and peoples have long mixed through **trade, marriage, war and religion**. This lesson introduces the major peoples of West Africa and of Sierra Leone, their origins and their ways of life.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name the major peoples of West Africa and where they live
- describe the main language families of West Africa
- explain how migration shaped the distribution of peoples
- name the ethnic groups of Sierra Leone
- describe common features of West African societies before colonial rule

## Language families

| Family | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Niger-Congo** | Mande (Mandinka, Soninke, Mende, Kono, Vai), Atlantic (Wolof, Fula, Temne, Bullom/Sherbro), Kwa and related (Akan, Ewe, Yoruba, Igbo), Gur (Mossi) |
| **Afro-Asiatic** | Chadic (Hausa), Berber (Tuareg), Arabic |
| **Nilo-Saharan** | Kanuri, Songhai |

Related languages show that peoples share **common origins** or long **contact**: for example, Mende, Kono and Vai in Sierra Leone and Liberia are Mande languages, like Mandinka and Soninke further north.

## Peoples of Sierra Leone

Sierra Leone has about **sixteen ethnic groups**. The two largest are the **Temne**, mainly in the north and north-west, and the **Mende**, mainly in the south and east. Others include the **Limba**, **Kono**, **Koranko**, **Loko**, **Sherbro (Bullom)**, **Susu**, **Fula**, **Mandingo**, **Kissi**, **Yalunka**, **Vai**, **Krim**, **Gola** and the **Krio** of the Western Area.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Oral traditions and early Portuguese records suggest that peoples such as the **Bullom (Sherbro)**, **Limba** and **Temne** were living in Sierra Leone by the sixteenth century, while **Mande-speaking** groups, including the ancestors of the **Mende**, **Kono** and **Vai**, moved in from the interior over several centuries. These movements help to explain the pattern of peoples in Sierra Leone today.
:::

## Common features of pre-colonial societies

1. **Kinship:** the family, lineage and clan were the basis of society; descent could be **patrilineal** or **matrilineal**.
2. **Political systems:** some peoples formed **centralised states** under kings (Asante, Oyo, Mali); others were **decentralised (stateless)**, governed by elders and age grades (many Igbo communities).
3. **Economy:** farming, herding, fishing, crafts (weaving, iron working, pottery) and **trade**.
4. **Religion:** traditional religions honouring a supreme being, spirits and ancestors; **Islam** spread in the savanna from about the 11th century, and **Christianity** spread in the 19th century.
5. **Secret societies:** such as the **Poro** (men's) and **Sande** (women's) societies of Sierra Leone and Liberia, which educated the young, kept order and checked rulers.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
"Stateless" or decentralised societies were **not** without government. They were governed by **councils of elders, lineage heads, age grades and secret societies**, without a single king.
:::

## Key points

- Major peoples include the **Wolof, Soninke, Mandinka, Fulani, Songhai, Hausa, Kanuri, Akan, Yoruba, Edo, Igbo** and **Tuareg**.
- Language families: **Niger-Congo, Afro-Asiatic, Nilo-Saharan**.
- **Migration**, trade and conquest shaped where peoples live.
- Sierra Leone: about **sixteen** ethnic groups; **Temne** and **Mende** are the largest.
- Societies were **centralised** (kingdoms) or **decentralised** (elders, age grades, secret societies).

## Quick check

1. Which people founded ancient Ghana?
2. Name the two largest ethnic groups in Sierra Leone.
3. Which language family includes Hausa?
4. What is a decentralised (stateless) society?
5. Name the men's and women's secret societies of Sierra Leone.

**Answers:**

1. The Soninke.
2. The Temne and the Mende.
3. Afro-Asiatic (Chadic branch).
4. A society governed by elders, lineage heads, age grades and secret societies, without a single king or central government.
5. The Poro (men's) and the Sande (women's) societies.
