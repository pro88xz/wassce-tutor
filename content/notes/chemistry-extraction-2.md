Sierra Leone is rich in metal ores: iron ore at Tonkolili and Marampa, rutile (titanium ore) in the south, bauxite (aluminium ore) and gold. Turning an ore into a usable metal needs chemistry, and the method depends on where the metal sits in the reactivity series. This lesson covers ores, the stages of extraction, the choice of method, and alloys.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define an ore and name the main ores of common metals
- describe the stages in extracting a metal from its ore
- explain how the method of extraction depends on the reactivity of the metal
- define an alloy and give the composition and uses of common alloys
- explain why alloys are often more useful than pure metals
- discuss the environmental effects of mining

## Ores

:::callout{type=definition}
An **ore** is a naturally occurring rock or mineral from which a metal can be **extracted profitably**.
:::

| Metal | Ore | Main compound |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron** | Haematite; magnetite | Fe₂O₃; Fe₃O₄ |
| **Aluminium** | **Bauxite** | Al₂O₃·2H₂O |
| **Titanium** | **Rutile**; ilmenite | TiO₂; FeTiO₃ |
| **Copper** | Copper pyrites; malachite | CuFeS₂; CuCO₃·Cu(OH)₂ |
| **Zinc** | Zinc blende | ZnS |
| **Lead** | Galena | PbS |
| **Tin** | Cassiterite | SnO₂ |
| **Sodium** | Rock salt | NaCl |
| **Calcium** | Limestone | CaCO₃ |
| **Gold, silver** | Found **native** (as the free metal) | Au, Ag |

## Stages of extraction

1. **Mining** the ore.
2. **Concentrating** the ore: removing earthy waste (**gangue**) by gravity separation, magnetic separation or **froth flotation** (used for sulphide ores).
3. **Roasting** sulphide ores in air to turn them into oxides: 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂.
4. **Reduction** of the oxide or compound to the metal (the method depends on reactivity).
5. **Refining** (purifying) the metal, for example copper by electrolysis.

## The method depends on reactivity

| Metals | Method | Example equation |
|---|---|---|
| **K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al** (most reactive) | **Electrolysis of the molten compound**; carbon cannot reduce their oxides | Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al (from Al₂O₃ in molten cryolite) |
| **Zn, Fe, Sn, Pb** (middle) | **Reduction of the oxide by carbon or carbon(II) oxide** | ZnO + C → Zn + CO; Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ |
| **Cu, Hg** | **Heating** the ore (roasting), sometimes with limited air | HgS + O₂ → Hg + SO₂ |
| **Ag, Au** (least reactive) | Found **native**; separated physically or dissolved and recovered | — |

The **more reactive** the metal, the **more energy** is needed to extract it. That is why aluminium, though very common in the earth's crust, was a costly metal until electrolysis made it cheap.

**Titanium** is a special case. Its oxide, from rutile, is first turned into titanium(IV) chloride, which is then reduced with **magnesium or sodium** in an inert argon atmosphere: TiCl₄ + 2Mg → Ti + 2MgCl₂.

## Alloys

:::callout{type=definition}
An **alloy** is a **mixture** of a metal with one or more other elements (usually metals, sometimes carbon), made by melting them together.
:::

| Alloy | Composition | Special properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Steel** (mild) | Iron + up to 0.25% carbon | Strong, tough | Building, vehicles, roofing sheets |
| **Stainless steel** | Iron + chromium + nickel | **Does not rust** | Cutlery, sinks, surgical tools |
| **Brass** | Copper + zinc | Hard, gold-coloured, does not corrode | Door handles, ornaments, musical instruments |
| **Bronze** | Copper + tin | Hard, does not corrode | Statues, medals, bearings, bells |
| **Solder** | Tin + lead (now tin + silver + copper) | **Low melting point** | Joining wires and pipes |
| **Duralumin** | Aluminium + copper + magnesium | **Light and strong** | Aircraft bodies, bicycle frames |
| **Amalgam** | Mercury + another metal | Soft, sets hard | Dental fillings (formerly); gold extraction |

**Why alloy?** Atoms of different sizes **disrupt the regular layers** of the pure metal, so layers cannot slide easily. Alloys are therefore usually **harder and stronger**. Alloying can also **prevent corrosion** (stainless steel), **lower the melting point** (solder) or change the **colour** (brass).

## Mining and the environment

| Problem | Example |
|---|---|
| **Land destruction** | Open pits, removed forest and topsoil; abandoned pits fill with water |
| **Water pollution** | Silt and chemicals in rivers; **mercury** from artisanal gold mining |
| **Air pollution** | Dust; SO₂ from roasting sulphide ores (acid rain) |
| **Health** | Lung disease from dust; mercury poisoning |
| **Waste heaps** (tailings) | May leak toxic metals |

**Reducing the harm:** **reclamation** (refilling and replanting land), treating waste water, **banning mercury**, **recycling** metals (recycling aluminium uses only about 5% of the energy needed to extract it), and enforcing environmental laws.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Sierra Rutile**, in the Moyamba and Bonthe districts, mines **rutile** (titanium(IV) oxide) from mineral sands. Titanium dioxide is the brilliant white pigment in paint, toothpaste and paper. Some artisanal gold miners mix **mercury** with gold-bearing sediment to form an **amalgam**, then heat it to drive the mercury off. This releases toxic mercury vapour and contaminates rivers and fish, which is why safer, mercury-free methods are encouraged.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
An alloy is a **mixture**, not a compound. Its composition can vary (steels with different carbon contents), and the metals in it are not chemically combined in a fixed ratio.
:::

## Key points

- An **ore** is a mineral from which a metal is extracted profitably: haematite (Fe), **bauxite** (Al), **rutile** (Ti), galena (Pb), zinc blende (Zn).
- Stages: **mining → concentration → roasting (sulphides) → reduction → refining**.
- **Most reactive (K–Al): electrolysis. Middle (Zn–Pb): reduction with C or CO. Cu, Hg: heating. Ag, Au: native.**
- **Alloys** are mixtures, usually harder or more useful: **steel, stainless steel, brass, bronze, solder, duralumin**.
- Mining causes **land, water and air pollution**; reclamation, recycling and banning mercury reduce the harm.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **name ores**, to **relate the method of extraction to reactivity**, to **describe the stages of extraction** with equations, and to **give the composition and uses of alloys**, explaining why alloys are harder than pure metals. Questions about the effects of mining are common.
:::

## Quick check

1. Name the ore of aluminium and its main compound.
2. Why is sodium extracted by electrolysis and not by heating its oxide with carbon?
3. Give the composition of brass and one use.
4. Why are alloys usually harder than the pure metal?

**Answers:**

1. Bauxite, Al₂O₃·2H₂O.
2. Sodium is above carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon cannot reduce its oxide.
3. Copper and zinc; door handles, ornaments or musical instruments.
4. Atoms of different sizes disrupt the regular layers, so the layers cannot slide over each other easily.
