Iron roofing sheets rusting, charcoal burning, bleach removing stains, a car battery delivering current: all are **redox** reactions, in which one substance is oxidised while another is reduced. This lesson builds up the four ways of recognising oxidation and reduction, and the oxidising and reducing agents that cause them.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define oxidation and reduction in terms of oxygen, hydrogen, electrons and oxidation number
- explain why oxidation and reduction always occur together
- identify oxidising and reducing agents in a reaction
- write half-equations for redox reactions
- describe tests for oxidising and reducing agents
- explain displacement reactions as redox reactions

## Four definitions

| Definition | Oxidation | Reduction |
|---|---|---|
| **Oxygen** | **Gain** of oxygen | **Loss** of oxygen |
| **Hydrogen** | **Loss** of hydrogen | **Gain** of hydrogen |
| **Electrons** | **Loss** of electrons | **Gain** of electrons |
| **Oxidation number** | **Increase** in oxidation number | **Decrease** in oxidation number |

A useful memory aid for the electron definition is **OIL RIG**: **O**xidation **I**s **L**oss, **R**eduction **I**s **G**ain (of electrons).

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Copper(II) oxide heated in hydrogen.**

$$\text{CuO(s)} + \text{H}_2\text{(g)} \rightarrow \text{Cu(s)} + \text{H}_2\text{O(l)}$$

- CuO **loses oxygen**: it is **reduced** to copper.
- H₂ **gains oxygen**: it is **oxidised** to water.

**Hydrogen sulphide and chlorine.**

$$\text{H}_2\text{S(g)} + \text{Cl}_2\text{(g)} \rightarrow 2\text{HCl(g)} + \text{S(s)}$$

- H₂S **loses hydrogen**: it is **oxidised** to sulphur.
- Cl₂ **gains hydrogen**: it is **reduced** to HCl.
:::

The **electron** definition is the most general: it covers reactions with no oxygen or hydrogen at all.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Sodium burning in chlorine.**

$$2\text{Na(s)} + \text{Cl}_2\text{(g)} \rightarrow 2\text{NaCl(s)}$$

- Na → Na⁺ + e⁻: sodium **loses electrons**, so it is **oxidised**.
- Cl₂ + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻: chlorine **gains electrons**, so it is **reduced**.
:::

## Redox: always together

Electrons lost by one substance must be gained by another, so **oxidation and reduction always happen together**. Such a reaction is called a **redox** reaction.

## Oxidising and reducing agents

:::callout{type=definition}
- An **oxidising agent** causes another substance to be oxidised; it is **itself reduced** (it **gains** electrons).
- A **reducing agent** causes another substance to be reduced; it is **itself oxidised** (it **loses** electrons).
:::

In CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O, **CuO is the oxidising agent** and **H₂ is the reducing agent**.

| Common oxidising agents | Common reducing agents |
|---|---|
| Oxygen, O₂ | Hydrogen, H₂ |
| Chlorine and the halogens | Carbon and carbon(II) oxide |
| Acidified potassium manganate(VII), KMnO₄ | Reactive metals (Na, Mg, Zn, Fe) |
| Acidified potassium dichromate(VI), K₂Cr₂O₇ | Hydrogen sulphide, H₂S |
| Concentrated nitric and sulphuric acids | Sulphur(IV) oxide, SO₂ (as sulphite) |
| Hydrogen peroxide, H₂O₂ | Iron(II) salts; potassium iodide |

### Tests

| Test for | Reagent | Positive result |
|---|---|---|
| **A reducing agent** | Acidified potassium manganate(VII) | **Purple → colourless** |
| **A reducing agent** | Acidified potassium dichromate(VI) | **Orange → green** |
| **An oxidising agent** | Potassium iodide solution | **Colourless → brown** (iodine released); turns starch blue-black |
| **An oxidising agent** | Hydrogen sulphide | Yellow deposit of **sulphur** |

## Half-equations

A redox reaction can be split into two **half-equations**, one for oxidation and one for reduction. Each must balance in atoms **and** charge.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Zinc in copper(II) sulphate solution.** The blue colour fades and brown copper forms on the zinc.

$$\text{Zn(s)} + \text{CuSO}_4\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{ZnSO}_4\text{(aq)} + \text{Cu(s)}$$

- Oxidation half-equation: Zn(s) → Zn²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻
- Reduction half-equation: Cu²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → Cu(s)
- Ionic equation (adding them; the electrons cancel and SO₄²⁻ is a spectator):

$$\text{Zn(s)} + \text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + \text{Cu(s)}$$

Zinc is the **reducing agent**; Cu²⁺ is the **oxidising agent**.
:::

## Displacement reactions

A more reactive element **displaces** a less reactive one from its compounds. These are redox reactions.

- **Metals:** Mg, Zn and Fe displace copper from copper(II) salts, because they are more reactive.
- **Halogens:** a more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive one from its halide:

$$\text{Cl}_2\text{(aq)} + 2\text{Br}^-\text{(aq)} \rightarrow 2\text{Cl}^-\text{(aq)} + \text{Br}_2\text{(aq)}$$

The solution turns orange-brown as bromine forms. Chlorine is reduced and bromide ions are oxidised.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Household bleach sold in Sierra Leone contains **sodium hypochlorite (sodium chlorate(I))**, a strong **oxidising agent**. It removes stains by oxidising coloured compounds into colourless ones, and it kills germs, which is why diluted bleach is used to disinfect water and surfaces. Never mix bleach with acid-based toilet cleaners: the redox reaction releases poisonous chlorine gas.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The **oxidising agent is reduced**, and the **reducing agent is oxidised**. Students often say the oxidising agent is oxidised. It does the oxidising, so it must itself gain the electrons.
:::

## Key points

- **Oxidation:** gain of oxygen, loss of hydrogen, **loss of electrons**, increase in oxidation number. **Reduction:** the opposite.
- **OIL RIG:** Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain of electrons.
- Oxidation and reduction occur **together** (redox).
- The **oxidising agent is reduced**; the **reducing agent is oxidised**.
- Tests: reducing agents decolourise **KMnO₄** (purple → colourless) and turn **K₂Cr₂O₇** green; oxidising agents release **iodine** from KI.
- **Displacement** reactions of metals and halogens are redox reactions.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **define oxidation and reduction** in more than one way, to **identify the oxidising and reducing agents** in an equation, to **write half-equations**, and to **describe tests** for oxidising and reducing agents with colour changes. Displacement of copper by zinc and of bromine by chlorine are standard examples.
:::

## Quick check

1. Define oxidation in terms of electrons.
2. In Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂, which substance is the reducing agent?
3. What colour change shows that a substance is a reducing agent when added to acidified potassium dichromate(VI)?
4. Write the two half-equations for Mg + 2H⁺ → Mg²⁺ + H₂.

**Answers:**

1. Oxidation is the loss of electrons.
2. Carbon(II) oxide, CO: it gains oxygen and reduces the iron(III) oxide.
3. Orange to green.
4. Oxidation: Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻; reduction: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂.
