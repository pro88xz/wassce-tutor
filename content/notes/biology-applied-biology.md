Biology is not only something to learn; it is something people **use** to grow more food, make medicines, process and preserve food, treat waste and produce energy. When a farmer chooses improved rice seed, when a woman ferments cassava to make gari, when a fisher smokes bonga at Tombo, when a bakery's dough rises, or when a hospital gives insulin made by bacteria, biology is at work. This lesson surveys the applications of biology in **agriculture** (crop and animal improvement, soil fertility, fish farming and bee-keeping) and in **industry** (fermentation, food processing, medicines, biotechnology and waste treatment), with examples from Sierra Leone.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain how biology improves crop and animal production
- describe selective breeding, hybridisation and tissue culture
- describe aquaculture, apiculture and other agricultural enterprises
- explain fermentation and its industrial uses
- describe the use of microorganisms in medicine, waste treatment and biogas
- outline genetic engineering and its benefits and concerns

## Biology in agriculture

### Improving crops

| Method | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **selection** | choosing seed from the best plants to sow next season | farmers saving seed from the highest-yielding rice plants |
| **hybridisation** | crossing two varieties to combine their good traits | **NERICA** rice, from African and Asian rice: higher yield and drought tolerance |
| **resistant varieties** | bred to resist pests or diseases | cassava varieties resistant to mosaic disease |
| **tissue culture** | growing many identical plantlets from small pieces of tissue in sterile nutrient jelly | disease-free planting material of banana, cassava and pineapple |
| **genetic engineering** | inserting a useful gene from another organism | insect-resistant cotton and maize (not yet widely grown in West Africa) |

### Improving soil fertility

- **Fertilisers**: NPK supply nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium.
- **Organic manure and compost**: add nutrients and humus, improve soil structure and water retention.
- **Legumes** in rotation fix nitrogen through *Rhizobium*.
- **Liming** corrects acid soils.

### Improving animals

- **Selective breeding** and **cross-breeding**: e.g. crossing local N'Dama cattle (tolerant of trypanosomiasis) with higher-yielding breeds.
- **Artificial insemination**: semen from a high-quality male used for many females.
- **Good feeding, housing and veterinary care**: vaccination, deworming, clean water.

### Other agricultural enterprises

- **Aquaculture** (fish farming): rearing tilapia or catfish in ponds; fed with farm by-products; provides protein and income, and eases pressure on wild fish.
- **Apiculture** (bee-keeping): honey and beeswax; bees also **pollinate** crops, increasing yields of fruit trees and oil palm.
- **Poultry**: eggs and meat in a short time.
- **Snail and grasscutter rearing**: produce bushmeat without hunting wild animals.

## Biology in industry

### Fermentation

![Yeast ferments sugar to ethanol and carbon dioxide, used in bread and in palm wine and beer; lactic acid bacteria ferment sugar to lactic acid, used in yoghurt and cheese and in gari and fufu, which reduces cyanide in cassava](https://wasscetutor.com/img/biology/fermentation-uses.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
**Fermentation** is the breakdown of sugars by microorganisms **without oxygen** (anaerobic respiration).

- **Yeast:** glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide + energy
- **Lactic acid bacteria:** glucose → lactic acid + energy
:::

| Product | Microorganism | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **bread** | yeast | CO₂ bubbles make the dough rise |
| **palm wine**, beer, ethanol | yeast (and bacteria in palm wine) | ethanol |
| **yoghurt**, cheese | lactic acid bacteria | lactic acid thickens and sours milk, and preserves it |
| **gari**, **fufu** | lactic acid bacteria and other microbes | soften and sour the cassava and help remove poisonous **cyanide** compounds |
| vinegar | acetic acid bacteria | turn ethanol into acetic acid |

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Making gari.** Cassava roots contain compounds that release poisonous cyanide. To make gari, women peel and grate the roots, leave the mash to **ferment** in sacks for two or three days, press out the liquid, sieve and then **roast** it. Fermentation by microorganisms and the pressing and heating together remove most of the cyanide, and the fermentation gives gari its sour taste. Cassava that is not properly processed can cause poisoning, so each step matters.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Saying yeast makes bread rise by making alcohol.** It is the **carbon dioxide** gas from fermentation that makes the dough rise. The small amount of ethanol evaporates during baking.
:::

### Food processing and preservation

Biology explains why food spoils and how to stop it: **smoking** and **drying** fish (Tombo, Shenge and other coastal towns), **salting**, **refrigeration**, **canning**, and **parboiling** rice, which hardens the grain so that fewer grains break in milling.

### Medicine

- **Antibiotics** from microorganisms: penicillin from the mould *Penicillium* (discovered by Alexander Fleming in 1928), streptomycin from *Streptomyces*.
- **Vaccines** made from killed or weakened pathogens or their parts.
- **Human insulin** for diabetics, made by bacteria carrying the human insulin gene.
- Traditional plant medicines studied for active compounds (quinine from cinchona bark was the first important malaria drug; artemisinin comes from the sweet wormwood plant).

### Waste treatment and energy

- **Sewage treatment**: bacteria break down organic matter so that treated water can be released safely.
- **Composting**: microbes turn kitchen and farm waste into manure.
- **Biogas**: in a sealed **digester**, anaerobic bacteria break down animal dung and plant waste, producing **methane** gas for cooking and lighting; the remaining sludge is a good fertiliser.

## Genetic engineering

**Genetic engineering** (genetic modification) is moving a gene from one organism to another so that it makes a useful product.

**Steps (insulin example):** cut out the human insulin gene → insert it into a **plasmid** (a small loop of DNA) from a bacterium → put the plasmid into bacteria → grow the bacteria in large **fermenters** → extract and purify the insulin.

| Benefits | Concerns |
|---|---|
| cheap, pure medicines (insulin, vaccines) | possible effects on the environment and other species |
| crops resistant to pests, disease or drought | genes might spread to wild relatives |
| crops with added nutrients | cost of seed and control by large companies |
| | safety testing and public trust |

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Define fermentation and give the products of yeast fermentation."}
The anaerobic breakdown of sugars by microorganisms. Yeast produces **ethanol and carbon dioxide** (and energy).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Why is cassava fermented when making gari?"}
To help remove poisonous **cyanide** compounds (with pressing and roasting), soften the pulp, and give the characteristic sour taste.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Give two advantages of tissue culture in agriculture."}
Any two: many identical plantlets quickly; disease-free planting material; plants with desired traits; production all year round in a small space.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. What is biogas, and how is it produced?"}
**Methane** gas, made by **anaerobic bacteria** breaking down dung and plant waste in a sealed digester. It is used as fuel.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Name two products of biotechnology used in medicine."}
Any two: antibiotics (penicillin), vaccines, human insulin made by bacteria.
:::

## Key points

- Crop improvement: **selection, hybridisation (NERICA), resistant varieties, tissue culture, genetic engineering**.
- Soil fertility: fertilisers, manure, legumes, liming. Animals: selective breeding, artificial insemination, good care.
- **Aquaculture, apiculture, poultry, snail and grasscutter rearing** add food and income.
- **Fermentation**: yeast (bread, palm wine) and lactic acid bacteria (yoghurt, gari).
- Microbes make **antibiotics, vaccines, insulin**; treat **sewage**; produce **biogas**.
- Genetic engineering moves useful genes between organisms; it has benefits and concerns.
