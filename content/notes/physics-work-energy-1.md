Energy is what makes things happen: it moves vehicles, lights homes, cooks food and keeps you alive. Physics gives it an exact meaning and a law that has never been broken: energy is never created or destroyed, only changed from one form into another.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define work, energy and power and state their units
- calculate work done by a force, including from a force–distance graph
- calculate kinetic and gravitational potential energy
- apply the principle of conservation of energy

## Work

:::callout{type=definition}
**Work is done** when a force moves its point of application in the direction of the force:

$$W = F \times s$$

where $s$ is the distance moved **in the direction of the force**. The unit is the **joule (J)**: 1 J is the work done when a force of 1 N moves its point of application 1 m in the direction of the force.
:::

If the force acts at angle θ to the direction of motion, only its component along the motion does work:

$$W = Fs\cos\theta$$

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
No work is done if there is **no movement**, or if the movement is **perpendicular** to the force. A man holding a heavy bag still does no work on it (though his muscles tire); a waiter carrying a tray horizontally at constant height does no work on the tray against gravity.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A boy pulls a box 20 m along the floor with a rope inclined at 60° to the horizontal. The tension is 50 N. Find the work done by the boy.**

$$W = Fs\cos\theta = 50 \times 20 \times \cos 60° = 500\ \text{J}$$
:::

### Work from a graph

The **area under a force–distance graph** is the work done.

![Force against extension for a spring: a straight line from the origin; the shaded triangle under it represents the work done, half F times x](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/work-area-graph.svg)

*Figure 1. Work done in stretching a spring is the area under the graph. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

For a spring stretched by $x$ with a final force $F$: $W = \tfrac{1}{2}Fx = \tfrac{1}{2}kx^2$ (this is the elastic potential energy stored).

## Energy

:::callout{type=definition}
**Energy** is the **capacity (ability) to do work**. Its unit is also the **joule (J)**.
:::

Forms of energy include kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, chemical, heat (thermal), electrical, light, sound and nuclear energy.

### Kinetic energy

:::callout{type=definition}
**Kinetic energy** is the energy a body has because of its **motion**:

$$E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$$
:::

### Gravitational potential energy

:::callout{type=definition}
**Gravitational potential energy** is the energy a body has because of its **position** (height) in a gravitational field:

$$E_p = mgh$$
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A 1200 kg car moves at 15 m s⁻¹. Find its kinetic energy. How high would it have to be lifted to have the same potential energy? ($g = 10$ m s⁻²)**

$$E_k = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 1200 \times 15^2 = 135\,000\ \text{J}$$

$$mgh = 135\,000 \Rightarrow h = \dfrac{135\,000}{12\,000} = 11.25\ \text{m}$$

A crash at 54 km h⁻¹ is like falling from a three- or four-storey building.
:::

## The principle of conservation of energy

:::callout{type=definition}
**Principle of conservation of energy:** energy can **neither be created nor destroyed**; it can only be **changed from one form to another**. The total energy of an isolated system remains constant.
:::

For a body falling freely (no air resistance), potential energy turns into kinetic energy:

$$mgh = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{2gh}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A 2 kg stone falls from rest from a height of 20 m. Find its kinetic energy and speed just before it hits the ground, and its kinetic energy halfway down. ($g = 10$ m s⁻²)**

At the top: $E_p = 2 \times 10 \times 20 = 400$ J, $E_k = 0$.

Just before landing: all 400 J is kinetic, so $\tfrac{1}{2}(2)v^2 = 400 \Rightarrow v = 20$ m s⁻¹.

Halfway (10 m down): $E_p = 200$ J, so $E_k = 400 - 200 = 200$ J. The total stays 400 J throughout.
:::

### Energy transformations

| Device or process | Energy change |
|---|---|
| Hydroelectric station (e.g. Bumbuna) | gravitational PE of water → KE → electrical |
| Generator (gen-set) | chemical (fuel) → heat → kinetic → electrical |
| Solar panel | light → electrical |
| Electric fan | electrical → kinetic (+ some heat and sound) |
| Battery charging a phone | electrical → chemical |
| Photosynthesis | light → chemical |
| Burning firewood or charcoal | chemical → heat and light |

## Power

:::callout{type=definition}
**Power** is the **rate of doing work** or the rate of transferring energy:

$$P = \dfrac{W}{t} = \dfrac{E}{t}$$

Its unit is the **watt (W)**: 1 W = 1 J s⁻¹. (1 horsepower ≈ 746 W.)
:::

For a body moving at constant speed $v$ against a force $F$:

$$P = \dfrac{Fs}{t} = Fv$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A 60 kg student runs up a flight of stairs 4 m high in 5 s. Find her power. ($g = 10$ m s⁻²)**

$$P = \dfrac{mgh}{t} = \dfrac{60 \times 10 \times 4}{5} = 480\ \text{W}$$
:::

## Key points

- Work $W = Fs\cos\theta$ (J); no movement in the direction of the force means no work.
- Area under a force–distance graph = work done.
- $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$; $E_p = mgh$.
- Energy is conserved: it only changes form.
- Power $P = W/t = Fv$ (W).

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
**Define work, energy and power** with units, **state the principle of conservation of energy**, and solve problems on falling bodies, pendulums and inclines using $mgh = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$. Questions also ask for the **energy changes** in devices (a hydroelectric station, a generator, a solar panel) and for **power** in climbing stairs or lifting loads.
:::

## Quick check

1. State the SI unit of power.
2. A 0.5 kg ball moves at 8 m s⁻¹. Find its kinetic energy.
3. A crane lifts a 500 kg load through 12 m in 20 s. Find its power output. ($g = 10$ m s⁻²)

**Answers:** 1. The watt (W). 2. $\tfrac{1}{2}(0.5)(64) = 16$ J. 3. $\dfrac{500 \times 10 \times 12}{20} = 3000$ W.
