# Parts of speech: word classes and their roles in sentences

## Learning objectives
You should be able to identify the main word classes; explain their functions; distinguish words that change class according to context; and analyse how word classes build clear sentences.

## Why word classes matter
Parts of speech are categories of words grouped by their grammatical behaviour and function. A word's class is determined by how it is used in a sentence, not by its spelling alone. For example, “work” is a noun in “The work is difficult” and a verb in “They work every day”.

## The major classes
Nouns name people, places, things, substances, qualities and ideas. Verbs express actions, events or states: run, decide, become, seem. A verb may be finite, showing tense and agreeing with a subject, or non-finite, as in infinitives and participles. Verbs may be transitive (taking an object) or intransitive (not taking an object in that use).

Adjectives describe or classify nouns and pronouns: a careful driver, a wooden table. They may appear before a noun or after a linking verb: “The road is narrow.” Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, other adverbs or whole clauses: “She spoke quietly”; “very useful”; “almost certainly”. Not every adverb ends in -ly, and not every -ly word is an adverb.

Pronouns stand in for noun phrases or refer to participants: she, them, who, someone, ours. Their forms and case matter: “She invited him,” not “Her invited he.” Determiners occur with nouns and help identify quantity, possession or reference: the, a, this, each, my, several. Traditional school grammar may group some determiners under adjectives; use the classification expected by the course or question.

Prepositions express relationships such as place, time, direction or cause: in, under, before, through, because of. Conjunctions link words, phrases or clauses. Coordinating conjunctions include and, but and or; subordinating conjunctions include although, because, if and when. Interjections express reactions or exclamations, such as “Oh!” or “Hurray!”

## Form and function in context
In “The extremely careful nurse checked the patient,” “nurse” is a noun, “careful” an adjective, “extremely” an adverb, and “checked” a verb. “The” is a determiner. In “They waited outside,” “outside” functions as an adverb; in “outside the building,” it functions as a preposition. Context determines the analysis.

## Avoiding common errors
Adjectives modify nouns; adverbs commonly modify verbs and adjectives. Compare “a quick response” with “respond quickly”. Pronouns should have clear antecedents. A conjunction should connect grammatically compatible elements. Prepositions are normally followed by a noun phrase or another appropriate complement.

## WASSCE examination focus
For each word-class question, read the full sentence and state the word's function. Be prepared to identify verbs, nouns, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns, prepositions and conjunctions in context. When correcting a sentence, check both word class and grammatical position.

## Check your understanding
1. Identify the adjective and adverb: “The careful driver moved slowly.”
2. What is the function of “because” in “We stayed inside because it rained”?
3. Classify “light” in “Turn on the light” and “a light bag”.
4. State the difference between a noun and a verb.
5. Identify the preposition in “The keys are under the chair.”

## Answers for self-assessment
1. Adjective: careful; adverb: slowly.
2. It introduces a subordinate clause of reason.
3. Noun in the first sentence; adjective in the second.
4. A noun names; a verb expresses an action, event or state.
5. Under.
