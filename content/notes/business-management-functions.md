Follow a bag of cement from a factory to a building site. Someone buys the limestone and fuel, someone runs the kiln, someone finds customers and sets the price, someone collects the money and pays the workers, and someone recruits and trains those workers. Each of these is a **function of business**, a set of activities every business must carry out, whether one person does them all or each has a whole department.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define the functions of business
- explain the main functions: production, marketing, finance, human resources and purchasing
- show how the functions depend on one another

## What are the functions of business?

:::callout{type=definition}
**Functions of business** are the main groups of activities that every business must perform to produce and sell its goods or services and reach its objectives.
:::

In a small business the owner carries out every function. In a large business each function is usually a **department** with its own manager.

## The main functions

### 1. Production (operations)

:::callout{type=definition}
**Production** is the process of changing inputs (materials, labour and machines) into goods or services that satisfy wants.
:::

The production function decides **what** to produce, **how much**, **how** and to what **quality**. It plans the use of machines and workers and keeps waste low.

### 2. Marketing

:::callout{type=definition}
**Marketing** is the process of finding out what customers want and making, pricing, promoting and distributing goods to satisfy those wants at a profit.
:::

Marketing covers **market research**, **product** design, **pricing**, **promotion** (advertising and sales promotion) and **distribution** (getting goods to where customers buy them).

### 3. Finance

The finance function **raises money** (capital) for the business, **budgets** for spending, **keeps accounts**, pays bills and wages, collects debts and checks that the business is making a profit.

### 4. Human resources (personnel)

This function deals with the **people** in the business: recruiting and selecting workers, training them, setting pay, keeping them safe and motivated, and handling discipline and disputes.

### 5. Purchasing (procurement)

Purchasing buys the **materials, goods and equipment** the business needs, at the right **price**, **quality**, **quantity** and **time**, and keeps them safely in store until they are used.

### 6. Administration and records

Keeping files and records, handling letters and messages, and running the office so that the other functions have the information they need.

## How the functions depend on one another

| If this function fails | What happens to the others |
|---|---|
| Purchasing buys poor materials | Production makes poor goods; marketing struggles to sell them |
| Marketing does not find customers | Goods pile up in store; finance runs short of cash |
| Finance does not pay wages on time | Workers lose motivation; production falls |
| Human resources hires untrained staff | Production and service quality suffer |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Name the function.** In a rice-milling business in Bo:

1. The miller signs a contract to buy paddy from farmers at harvest. **Purchasing**
2. Workers dry, mill and bag the rice. **Production**
3. The business puts up posters and gives a discount to shops buying 20 bags. **Marketing**
4. The accountant prepares the monthly income statement. **Finance**
5. The manager interviews three applicants for a machine operator's job. **Human resources**
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not mix up the **functions of business** (production, marketing, finance, human resources, purchasing) with the **functions of management** (planning, organising, leading, controlling). Managers perform the management functions **within** each business function.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
In a large company such as a bank in Freetown, each function is a separate department: operations, marketing, finance, human resources and procurement. In a small tailoring shop in Makeni, the tailor does all of them herself: she buys cloth, sews, tells customers about her prices, keeps the money and trains her apprentice.
:::

## Key points

- **Functions of business** are the main groups of activities every business performs.
- **Production** turns inputs into goods and services; **marketing** finds and satisfies customers; **finance** raises and controls money; **human resources** manages people; **purchasing** buys materials; **administration** keeps records.
- The functions depend on one another: a failure in one harms the rest.
- Do not confuse them with the functions of management.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **list and explain the functions of business**, or to **explain the work of one function**, such as marketing or finance, in detail. Questions may also ask how the functions are **related**. Use a short example from a real or imagined business for each function.
:::

## Quick check

1. Define the functions of business.
2. Which function decides the price of a product?
3. Which function recruits and trains workers?
4. Give one way a failure in purchasing affects production.

**Answers:** 1. The main groups of activities a business must perform to produce and sell its goods and reach its objectives. 2. Marketing. 3. Human resources. 4. Poor or late materials lead to poor-quality goods or stop production.
