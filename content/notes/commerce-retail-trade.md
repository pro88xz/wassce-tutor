# Retail trade: functions, outlets and modern retailing

## Learning objectives
Define retail trade, explain retailer functions and compare common retail outlets.

## Meaning
Retail trade is the sale of goods or services in relatively small quantities to final consumers for personal or household use. A retailer is the trader who sells to the final user. A common distribution channel is **Producer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Final consumer**. Not every product passes through all these stages: a manufacturer may sell directly to a consumer or a large retail chain. The key distinction is that a retailer's main customer is the final user, not another trader buying for resale.

## Functions of retailers
Retailers make goods available in convenient locations and quantities. They break bulk by buying larger quantities and selling smaller units, display products for comparison, provide information and advice, hold stock near consumers and communicate customer preferences to suppliers. Some provide delivery, credit or after-sales services under their policies and applicable law.

Retailers also bear risks such as theft, damage, deterioration and unsold stock. They must manage stock levels carefully: too little stock causes lost sales, while excessive stock ties up money and increases storage costs.

## Types of retail outlet

| Outlet | Main feature |
|---|---|
| Independent shop | Usually owned by an individual or small business |
| Market stall | Sells goods in a market, often in small quantities |
| Department store | Offers a broad range in separate departments |
| Supermarket | Self-service outlet focused mainly on food and household goods |
| Convenience shop | Small outlet emphasising location and convenience |
| Chain store | Several outlets under common ownership or control |
| Online retailer | Takes orders through a website or digital platform |

## Choosing a retail format
Location, customer income, foot traffic, product type, rent, competition, security, supply reliability and operating costs influence the choice. Fresh produce may suit a market stall with rapid turnover. Electronics may require secure premises, demonstrations and after-sales support. Online selling can reach distant customers but requires reliable delivery, accurate descriptions, digital payment and complaint handling.

A neighbourhood shop may buy cartons of soap from a wholesaler and sell individual bars to households. The retailer provides convenience and breaks bulk, while the wholesaler allows the shop to buy from one supplier instead of contacting many manufacturers.

## WASSCE examination focus
Explain each retailer function and its benefit. Do not confuse a wholesaler, who mainly sells to traders for resale, with a retailer, who sells to final consumers.
