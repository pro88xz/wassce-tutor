# Plate tectonics: plate boundaries and movement

## Learning objectives
You should be able to explain the theory of plate tectonics, identify the main types of plate boundary, and relate plate movement to major landforms and hazards.

## The lithosphere and tectonic plates
The lithosphere consists of the crust and the rigid uppermost mantle. It is broken into large and small tectonic plates that move slowly over the weaker, deformable asthenosphere beneath. Plate movement is generally measured in centimetres per year.

The theory of plate tectonics explains the movement and interaction of these plates. Evidence includes the fit of continental margins, matching fossils and rock belts on now-separated continents, evidence of past climates, seafloor spreading, magnetic stripes on the ocean floor and the global distribution of earthquakes and volcanoes.

Plate movement is associated with mantle convection, slab pull and ridge push. These mechanisms act together; no single simple conveyor-belt picture explains every plate movement.

## Three main types of plate boundary

### Divergent or constructive boundaries
Two plates move apart. Mantle material rises, melts as pressure decreases and may solidify to form new oceanic crust. Mid-ocean ridges and rift valleys are associated with divergence. Shallow earthquakes and basaltic volcanic activity may occur.

### Convergent or destructive boundaries
Two plates move towards each other. Where dense oceanic lithosphere meets another plate, it may descend beneath it in a process called subduction. This can produce deep ocean trenches, volcanic arcs and powerful earthquakes. Where two continental plates collide, crust may crumple and thicken to form fold mountains.

### Transform or conservative boundaries
Two plates slide horizontally past each other. Crust is neither created nor destroyed along the boundary. Friction may lock sections of the fault until stress is released suddenly as an earthquake.

## Plate boundaries and landforms
At a divergent boundary, a long rift valley may form on land or a mid-ocean ridge beneath the sea. At an oceanic–continental convergent boundary, a trench and volcanic mountain chain may develop. Continental collision can produce high fold mountains. Transform boundaries are associated with major fault zones.

## African and global context
The East African Rift System is an important example of continental divergence, where the African Plate is being divided into the Nubian and Somali plates. The boundary is associated with rift valleys, volcanic activity and earthquakes. The Atlantic Ocean contains a mid-ocean ridge where new oceanic crust forms.

## WASSCE examination focus
Define plate tectonics as the theory that the lithosphere is divided into moving plates whose interactions shape the Earth's surface. Distinguish divergent, convergent and transform boundaries by the direction of movement and the resulting features.

## Quick check
1. What is the lithosphere?
2. State the three main types of plate boundary.
3. What happens at a subduction zone?
4. Why are earthquakes common along transform boundaries?

**Answers:** 1. The crust and rigid uppermost mantle. 2. Divergent, convergent and transform. 3. One plate descends beneath another into the mantle. 4. Friction locks the moving plates, stress builds and sudden slip releases energy.
