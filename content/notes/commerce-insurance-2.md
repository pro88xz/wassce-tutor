# Insurance claims, policy terms and risk management

## Learning objectives
Explain how insurance contracts operate, identify common policy terms and describe the steps in making a claim.

## The insurance contract
Insurance transfers specified financial risks from a policyholder to an insurer in exchange for a premium, subject to the policy's terms, limits, exclusions and conditions. It does not remove the event or guarantee payment for every loss.

Important terms:
- **Premium:** amount paid for cover.
- **Sum insured:** amount stated as the insured value or limit, subject to policy wording.
- **Excess or deductible:** part of a claim the policyholder must bear.
- **Exclusion:** a risk or circumstance not covered.
- **Indemnity:** placing the insured, as far as money can, in a similar financial position to that immediately before the insured loss, subject to the contract.
- **Insurable interest:** a recognised financial or legal relationship to the subject matter that may be affected by loss.

## Principles
The principle of utmost good faith requires material information to be disclosed as required by the law and policy. Indemnity limits recovery to the covered loss rather than allowing profit from insurance. Contribution may apply where the same risk is insured with more than one insurer. Subrogation allows an insurer, after paying a covered claim, to pursue responsible third parties to the extent permitted.

Life insurance is not generally a contract of indemnity in the same way as property insurance.

## Making a claim
The policyholder should:
1. notify the insurer within the required time;
2. take reasonable steps to prevent further loss;
3. preserve evidence and report incidents where required;
4. complete claim forms accurately;
5. provide receipts, valuations, photographs or other requested documents;
6. cooperate with inspection and assessment.

A claim may be reduced or declined if exclusions, non-disclosure or policy conditions apply.

## Risk management
Businesses should identify hazards, estimate likelihood and impact, reduce avoidable risks, transfer suitable risks through insurance and retain risks they can reasonably bear. Good security, maintenance and staff training can prevent losses before insurance is needed.

## WASSCE examination focus
Distinguish risk prevention from risk transfer. Insurance transfers financial consequences of specified risks; it does not prevent fire, theft or accidents from occurring.
