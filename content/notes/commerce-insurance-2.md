A transporter needs cover for lorries, a shop owner for stock and buildings, an importer for cargo at sea, and a family for the loss of a breadwinner. Each needs a different kind of policy. This lesson describes the main types of insurance, how to take out a policy and make a claim, and the average clause, which punishes under-insurance.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe life assurance and its types
- describe the main types of general insurance: fire, marine, motor and accident
- explain the procedure for taking out insurance and making a claim
- calculate premiums and claims under the average clause
- explain reinsurance, double insurance and social insurance

## Life assurance

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Whole life** | The sum assured is paid when the insured **dies**, whenever that is; premiums are paid for life or to a set age |
| **Endowment** | The sum assured is paid at the **end of a fixed period** (say 20 years) or on earlier death; a form of saving |
| **Term (temporary) assurance** | Pays only if death occurs **within a set period**; nothing if the insured survives; cheapest |
| **With-profit policy** | The insured shares in the insurer's profits through bonuses |
| **Annuity** | A lump sum paid to the insurer in exchange for a regular income for life |

## General (non-life) insurance

### Fire insurance

Covers loss of or damage to buildings, stock, furniture and machinery by **fire**, and often by lightning and explosion. **Consequential loss (business interruption)** insurance covers the profits lost while a business cannot trade after a fire.

### Marine insurance

| Type | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Hull insurance** | The **ship** itself |
| **Cargo insurance** | The **goods** carried |
| **Freight insurance** | The **freight charges** the shipowner would lose if cargo is lost |
| **Ship owner's liability** | Damage the ship causes to other ships or property |

**Marine policies:**

- a **voyage policy** covers a single voyage
- a **time policy** covers a fixed period, usually a year
- a **mixed policy** covers a voyage within a time period
- a **floating (open) policy** covers a total value of shipments over a period, declared as each shipment is made
- a **fleet policy** covers several ships

**Marine losses:**

- **total loss**: everything is lost
- **general average**: cargo thrown overboard (jettisoned) to save the ship; the loss is shared by everyone with goods on board
- **particular average**: a partial loss borne by the owner of the damaged goods

### Motor insurance

| Type | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Third party only** | Injury or damage the vehicle causes to **other people** and their property; **compulsory by law** |
| **Third party, fire and theft** | Third party, plus the owner's vehicle if burnt or stolen |
| **Comprehensive** | Third party, fire and theft, plus **damage to the owner's own vehicle** in an accident; dearest |

### Accident and other insurance

| Type | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Burglary and theft** | Goods stolen by forced entry |
| **Fidelity guarantee** | Loss from **dishonest employees** (theft or fraud by cashiers and storekeepers) |
| **Cash (money) in transit** | Money stolen while being carried, for example to the bank |
| **Goods in transit** | Goods lost or damaged while carried by road or rail |
| **Personal accident** | Injury or death from accidents |
| **Employer's liability (workmen's compensation)** | Injury to employees at work |
| **Public liability** | Injury to members of the public on the business's premises |
| **Bad debts (credit) insurance** | Customers who fail to pay |
| **Plate glass** | Breakage of shop windows |
| **Health insurance** | Medical bills |

## Taking out insurance and making a claim

**Taking out a policy:**

1. The insured completes a **proposal form**, disclosing all material facts.
2. The insurer assesses the risk and fixes the **premium**.
3. The insured pays the premium and receives a **cover note** for temporary cover.
4. The insurer issues the **policy**.

**Making a claim:**

1. **Notify** the insurer promptly; report theft or accidents to the **police**.
2. Complete a **claim form**.
3. Provide **evidence**: the police report, receipts, photographs and estimates.
4. A **loss adjuster** investigates and assesses the loss.
5. The insurer **pays** (cash, repair or replacement) in line with the policy and the principles of insurance.

## Calculating premiums and claims

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Premium.** A warehouse worth NLe 400,000 is insured against fire at **NLe 5 per NLe 1,000** insured.

$$\text{premium} = \dfrac{400000}{1000} \times 5 = \text{NLe } 2{,}000 \text{ a year}$$
:::

### The average clause

If property is **under-insured** (insured for less than its full value), the **average clause** makes the insured bear part of any loss. The insurer pays only the proportion of the loss that the sum insured bears to the full value.

$$\text{claim} = \dfrac{\text{sum insured}}{\text{value of property}} \times \text{loss}$$

![Bar comparison: shop worth NLe 100,000 insured for NLe 60,000; a fire loss of NLe 40,000 gives a claim of NLe 24,000](https://wasscetutor.com/img/commerce/average-clause.svg)

*Figure 1. Under the average clause the insurer pays only its share of the loss. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A shop worth NLe 100,000 is insured for NLe 60,000. Fire causes a loss of NLe 40,000.**

$$\text{claim} = \dfrac{60000}{100000} \times 40000 = \text{NLe } 24{,}000$$

The owner bears $40000 - 24000 = $ NLe 16,000. Had the shop been insured for its full value, the whole NLe 40,000 would have been paid.

**With an excess:** if the policy also has an excess of NLe 1,000, the insurer pays $24000 - 1000 = $ NLe 23,000.
:::

**Why the average clause exists:** to stop people paying low premiums by insuring for less than full value while still expecting full compensation.

## Reinsurance, double insurance and co-insurance

- **Reinsurance**: an insurer passes **part of a large risk** to another insurer (a reinsurer), so that one big loss will not ruin it.
- **Double insurance**: the **insured** covers the same property with **two or more insurers**. **Contribution** stops the insured collecting more than the loss.
- **Co-insurance**: several insurers **share** one large risk from the start, each taking a stated part.

## Social insurance

**Social insurance** is a compulsory scheme run by the state, to which employers and workers contribute, giving pensions and benefits for old age, disability and death. In Sierra Leone it is run by the **National Social Security and Insurance Trust (NASSIT)**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
A transporter in Bo must carry at least **third-party motor insurance**. Many also choose **comprehensive** cover for new vehicles bought on credit, because the lender demands it. An importer bringing goods into Freetown usually takes a **marine cargo policy**, and the bank financing the letter of credit will ask for the **certificate of insurance**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Reinsurance** is arranged by the **insurer**; **double insurance** is arranged by the **insured**. Students often confuse them.
:::

## Key points

- **Life assurance**: whole life, endowment, term; pays an agreed sum.
- **General insurance**: **fire, marine** (hull, cargo, freight), **motor** (third party compulsory; comprehensive) and **accident** (burglary, fidelity guarantee, cash in transit, liability).
- Procedure: **proposal form → premium → cover note → policy**; claims need notice, a claim form, evidence and assessment.
- **Average clause**: claim = (sum insured ÷ value) × loss.
- **Reinsurance** spreads the insurer's risk; **double insurance** is by the insured.

## Quick check

2. Which type of motor insurance is compulsory?
3. Goods worth NLe 80,000 are insured for NLe 60,000. A loss of NLe 20,000 occurs. Find the claim under the average clause.
4. What is fidelity guarantee insurance?

**Answers:** 1. Whole life pays the sum assured on death, whenever it happens; endowment pays at the end of a fixed period or on earlier death. 2. Third-party insurance. 3. $\dfrac{60000}{80000} \times 20000 = $ NLe 15,000. 4. Insurance against loss caused by the dishonesty (theft or fraud) of employees.
