Iron is the backbone of modern life: roofing sheets, reinforcing rods, cars, machines and bridges. Copper carries electricity into every building, and zinc protects iron from rust. This lesson covers the extraction of iron in the blast furnace, steelmaking, iron compounds and their tests, and the extraction, properties and uses of copper and zinc.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name the raw materials of the blast furnace and describe the reactions in it
- distinguish cast iron, wrought iron and steel, and describe steelmaking
- describe the properties of iron(II) and iron(III) compounds and tests for Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺
- describe the extraction, properties and uses of copper
- describe the extraction, properties and uses of zinc

## Iron: the blast furnace

![Blast furnace: iron ore, coke and limestone enter at the top; hot air is blown in near the bottom; carbon(II) oxide reduces the ore; limestone forms slag; molten iron and slag are tapped off at the bottom](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/blast-furnace.svg)

*Figure 1. Extracting iron in the blast furnace. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

**Raw materials:**

| Material | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Iron ore** (haematite, Fe₂O₃) | Source of iron |
| **Coke** (carbon) | Fuel (provides heat) and source of the reducing agent CO |
| **Limestone** (CaCO₃) | Removes the acidic impurity **silica** (sand) as slag |
| **Hot air** | Provides oxygen for the coke to burn |

**Reactions:**

1. **Coke burns** in the hot air near the bottom, raising the temperature to about 1900 °C:

$$\text{C(s)} + \text{O}_2\text{(g)} \rightarrow \text{CO}_2\text{(g)}$$

2. **Carbon(II) oxide forms** as CO₂ rises through more hot coke:

$$\text{CO}_2\text{(g)} + \text{C(s)} \rightarrow 2\text{CO(g)}$$

3. **The ore is reduced** by carbon(II) oxide, the main reducing agent:

$$\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3\text{(s)} + 3\text{CO(g)} \rightarrow 2\text{Fe(l)} + 3\text{CO}_2\text{(g)}$$

4. **Slag forms:** limestone decomposes and the calcium oxide (basic) reacts with silica (acidic):

$$\text{CaCO}_3 \rightarrow \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2 \qquad \text{CaO} + \text{SiO}_2 \rightarrow \text{CaSiO}_3$$

Molten **slag** (calcium silicate) floats on the molten iron, protecting it from re-oxidation. Both are tapped off at the bottom. Slag is used for road-building and cement.

## Types of iron and steel

| Type | Carbon content | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pig iron / cast iron** | About 3–4% | Hard but **brittle**; low melting point; easily cast | Cooking pots, drain covers, engine blocks |
| **Wrought iron** | Almost pure iron | Soft, malleable, tough | Ornamental gates (now rare) |
| **Mild steel** | Up to about 0.25% | Strong, **malleable**, easily shaped; rusts | Roofing sheets, rods, car bodies, nails |
| **High-carbon steel** | 0.5–1.5% | Very **hard** but more brittle | Cutting tools, knives, springs |
| **Stainless steel** | Iron + chromium + nickel | **Does not rust** | Cutlery, sinks, surgical tools |

**Steelmaking (basic oxygen process):** pure **oxygen** is blown onto molten pig iron. It **oxidises** the excess carbon (to CO and CO₂), silicon and phosphorus. The oxides of silicon and phosphorus react with added **lime** to form slag. The carbon content is then adjusted, and other metals are added to make alloy steels.

## Compounds of iron

| | Iron(II) compounds | Iron(III) compounds |
|---|---|---|
| **Example** | FeSO₄·7H₂O (green crystals) | FeCl₃ (yellow-brown) |
| **Colour in solution** | Pale green | Yellow-brown |
| **With NaOH or NH₃** | **Dirty green** precipitate, Fe(OH)₂, which turns brown in air | **Red-brown** precipitate, Fe(OH)₃ |
| **With potassium thiocyanate** | No colour (or faint) | **Blood-red** colour |
| **Behaviour** | **Reducing agent**: easily oxidised to iron(III) | More stable in air |

**Iron(II) to iron(III):** chlorine, hydrogen peroxide or acidified potassium manganate(VII) **oxidise** iron(II): 2Fe²⁺ + Cl₂ → 2Fe³⁺ + 2Cl⁻.

**Iron(III) to iron(II):** hydrogen sulphide or zinc and acid **reduce** iron(III).

## Copper

**Extraction:** from **copper pyrites** (CuFeS₂). The concentrated ore is roasted in air to remove sulphur and iron, giving impure copper. This is purified by **electrolytic refining** (impure copper anode, pure copper cathode, copper(II) sulphate electrolyte).

**Properties:**

- reddish-brown, malleable and ductile
- an **excellent conductor** of heat and electricity
- **unreactive**: no reaction with water or dilute HCl; forms black CuO on heating in air
- over years outdoors, a green coating (basic copper carbonate, "verdigris") forms
- reacts with **nitric acid** and hot concentrated sulphuric acid

**Uses:**

- **electrical wiring** (most important)
- water pipes and cooking pots
- **alloys**: **brass** (Cu + Zn) and **bronze** (Cu + Sn)
- coins
- **copper(II) sulphate** as a fungicide

## Zinc

**Extraction:** **zinc blende** (ZnS) is **roasted** to zinc oxide, then **reduced with carbon** (coke):

$$2\text{ZnS} + 3\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{ZnO} + 2\text{SO}_2 \qquad \text{ZnO} + \text{C} \rightarrow \text{Zn} + \text{CO}$$

Zinc is also extracted by electrolysis of zinc sulphate solution.

**Properties:** bluish-white; fairly reactive (reacts with acids, giving hydrogen; burns with a bluish-green flame); its oxide and hydroxide are **amphoteric**; zinc oxide is **yellow when hot and white when cold**.

**Uses:**

- **galvanising** iron (roofing sheets, buckets), giving **sacrificial protection**
- **brass**
- the **casing (negative electrode) of dry cells**
- **zinc oxide** in ointments, sunscreen, paints and rubber

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's iron ore from **Tonkolili** and **Marampa** is mostly **haematite** (Fe₂O₃), shipped from the coast for smelting in blast furnaces abroad. The "**zinc**" roofing sheets on homes across the country are in fact **mild steel coated with zinc**: the zinc corrodes first and protects the iron underneath.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
In the blast furnace, the main reducing agent is **carbon(II) oxide**, not coke itself. Coke burns to give heat and then forms the CO that reduces the ore.
:::

## Key points

- **Blast furnace:** raw materials **haematite, coke, limestone, hot air**. C + O₂ → CO₂; CO₂ + C → 2CO; **Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂**; CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ (slag).
- **Cast iron** (3–4% C) is brittle; **steel** has less carbon and is made by blowing **oxygen** into pig iron; **stainless steel** contains Cr and Ni.
- **Fe²⁺** gives a green precipitate with NaOH; **Fe³⁺** gives a red-brown precipitate and a **blood-red** colour with thiocyanate.
- **Copper:** from pyrites, refined by electrolysis; used for **wiring, pipes, brass and bronze**.
- **Zinc:** from zinc blende (roast, then reduce with carbon); used for **galvanising, brass and dry cells**.

## Quick check

1. State the purpose of limestone in the blast furnace.
2. Write the equation for the reduction of iron(III) oxide in the blast furnace.
3. How would you distinguish iron(II) ions from iron(III) ions using sodium hydroxide?
4. Give two uses of zinc.

**Answers:**

1. It decomposes to calcium oxide, which removes the silica impurity as slag (calcium silicate).
2. Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂.
3. Fe²⁺ gives a dirty-green precipitate; Fe³⁺ gives a red-brown precipitate.
4. Any two: galvanising iron, making brass, dry-cell casings, zinc oxide in ointments and paints.
