A **departmental trading and profit and loss account** is set out in **columns**, one for each department and often one for the total, so that the **gross profit** and **net profit** of every department can be read side by side. Sales, purchases and inventories are recorded for each department, direct expenses are **allocated**, and shared expenses are **apportioned** on suitable bases. This lesson works through a full columnar departmental account and shows how to interpret the results.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- set out a columnar departmental income statement
- calculate the gross profit of each department
- allocate and apportion expenses to find each department's net profit
- calculate departmental gross profit margins
- interpret the results to advise management

## Worked example

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Kamara Superstore has two departments, Clothing (C) and Electronics (E). For the year ended 31 December 2026 (NLe):**

- Sales: C 120,000; E 180,000
- Opening inventory: C 15,000; E 25,000
- Purchases: C 70,000; E 110,000
- Closing inventory: C 18,000; E 30,000
- Sales staff wages (direct): C 12,000; E 15,000
- Rent 24,000, apportioned by floor area C : E = 2 : 1
- Advertising 10,000, apportioned by sales
- Electricity 6,000, apportioned by floor area
:::

**Workings for apportionment:**

- Rent: C $\frac{2}{3} \times 24\,000 = 16\,000$; E $8\,000$
- Advertising: sales ratio $120 : 180 = 2 : 3$; C $4\,000$; E $6\,000$
- Electricity: C $\frac{2}{3} \times 6\,000 = 4\,000$; E $2\,000$

**Departmental income statement for the year ended 31 December 2026**

| | Clothing | Electronics |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | 120,000 | 180,000 |
| Opening inventory | 15,000 | 25,000 |
| Add purchases | 70,000 | 110,000 |
| Less closing inventory | (18,000) | (30,000) |
| **Cost of sales** | **(67,000)** | **(105,000)** |
| **Gross profit** | **53,000** | **75,000** |
| Sales staff wages | 12,000 | 15,000 |
| Rent | 16,000 | 8,000 |
| Advertising | 4,000 | 6,000 |
| Electricity | 4,000 | 2,000 |
| **Total expenses** | **(36,000)** | **(31,000)** |
| **Net profit** | **17,000** | **44,000** |

**Check on the totals:** sales $300\,000$; cost of sales $172\,000$; gross profit $128\,000$; expenses $12\,000 + 15\,000 + 24\,000 + 10\,000 + 6\,000 = 67\,000$; net profit $128\,000 - 67\,000 = 61\,000 = 17\,000 + 44\,000$ ✓

## Interpreting the results

$$\text{Gross profit margin} = \frac{\text{Gross profit}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100\%$$

| | Clothing | Electronics |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | $\frac{53\,000}{120\,000} \approx 44.2\%$ | $\frac{75\,000}{180\,000} \approx 41.7\%$ |
| Net profit margin | $\frac{17\,000}{120\,000} \approx 14.2\%$ | $\frac{44\,000}{180\,000} \approx 24.4\%$ |

Clothing earns a **slightly higher gross margin**, but its **net margin is much lower**, because it bears two-thirds of the rent and electricity for its larger floor space. Management might **reduce the space** given to clothing, raise its sales per square metre, or review whether the floor area basis is fair.

## Presenting departmental accounts

- Use **one column per department**; a total column is optional.
- Show the **apportionment workings** separately or in brackets.
- Head the statement with the business name and the period.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not apportion **purchases** or **inventories**. Each department's purchases, sales and inventories are recorded **directly**; only shared **expenses** are apportioned.
:::

## Key points

- Departmental accounts are set out in **columns**, one per department.
- Sales, purchases and inventories are **direct**; shared expenses are **apportioned**.
- Compare departments with **gross and net profit margins**.
- Results depend on the **apportionment bases**, so interpret them with care.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **prepare a columnar departmental trading and profit and loss account** with apportioned expenses, and to **comment on the performance** of each department. Always show how each shared expense was apportioned.
:::

## Quick check

1. Why is a departmental account set out in columns?
2. Department X has sales of NLe 80,000 and cost of sales of NLe 50,000. Find its gross profit margin.
3. Rent of NLe 15,000 is shared by floor area 3 : 2. Find each share.
4. Which items are not apportioned in departmental accounts?
5. Why might a department with a high gross margin have a low net margin?

**Answers:**

1. So that the results of each department can be compared side by side.
2. Gross profit $= 30\,000$; margin $= \frac{30\,000}{80\,000} \times 100\% = 37.5\%$.
3. NLe 9,000 and NLe 6,000.
4. Sales, purchases and inventories (and direct expenses), which are recorded for each department directly.
5. Because it bears a large share of the apportioned expenses, such as rent for a large floor area.
