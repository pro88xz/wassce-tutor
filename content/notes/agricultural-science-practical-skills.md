Agricultural Science is a practical subject. The WASSCE practical paper presents **specimens**: soils, seeds, tools, pests, feeds, fertilisers, animal products. It asks you to identify them, state their uses or features, and carry out simple calculations. On the farm, the same skills decide whether a job is done well: measuring a plot correctly, laying out straight rows, calculating seed and fertiliser, handling animals safely. This lesson gathers the practical skills of Agricultural Science and shows how to answer practical questions for full marks.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- measure farm plots and calculate their areas in square metres and hectares
- lay out plots and planting rows correctly
- calculate plant population, seed and fertiliser requirements for a plot
- identify common practical specimens and state their uses or features
- handle and restrain farm animals safely
- follow safety rules in practical work

## Measuring land

**Units:** 1 hectare (ha) $= 10\,000$ m² (a square of 100 m × 100 m); 1 acre ≈ 0.4 ha.

**Tools:** measuring tape or chain, **ranging poles** (to mark straight lines), **pegs and line**, and **pacing** (counting steps of known length) for quick estimates.

:::callout{type=definition}
| Shape of plot | Area |
|---|---|
| rectangle or square | length × breadth |
| triangle | ½ × base × height |
| trapezium | ½ × (sum of parallel sides) × distance between them |
| irregular plot | divide it into rectangles and triangles, and add |
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A school farm plot is a trapezium with parallel sides of 50 m and 30 m, 40 m apart. (a) Find its area in m² and in hectares. (b) How many cassava cuttings are needed at 1 m × 1 m? (c) How much NPK is needed at 250 kg/ha?**

**(a)** Area $= \tfrac12 \times (50 + 30) \times 40 = 1600$ m² $= \dfrac{1600}{10\,000} = $ **0.16 ha**.

**(b)** Each plant needs 1 m², so **1600 cuttings**.

**(c)** NPK $= 250 \times 0.16 = $ **40 kg**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Forgetting to convert m² to hectares** before using a rate given per hectare. A rate of 250 kg/ha applied as "250 kg" to a 0.16 ha plot puts more than six times too much fertiliser on the land.
:::

### Laying out a plot and rows

1. Mark one side with **ranging poles** and a line.
2. Make **right angles** at the corners with the **3-4-5 method**: measure 3 m along one side and 4 m along the other; when the diagonal between the two marks is exactly 5 m, the corner is a right angle.
3. Peg out rows at the chosen spacing with a line marked at the plant spacing.
4. Mark paths between beds for access.

## Practical calculations

You may be asked for any of these (see the crop and animal lessons for more examples):

- **Plant population** $= \dfrac{\text{area (m}^2)}{\text{area per plant (m}^2)} \times$ plants per stand.
- **Seed rate** from population, seeds per hole and seed weight.
- **Germination percentage** $= \dfrac{\text{seeds germinated}}{\text{seeds sown}} \times 100$.
- **Fertiliser** needed from nutrient percentage and rate.
- **Feed mixing** by Pearson square; **FCR**; **laying percentage**.
- **Gross margin**, **profit**, **depreciation**.

## Identifying specimens

The practical paper shows specimens labelled A, B, C… For each, you usually: **name** it, give **features** you can see that identify it, and state its **uses** or importance (or harmful effects and control for pests and weeds).

| Specimen type | Examples | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| **soil samples** | sand, clay, loam | texture by feel; water retention; crops suited |
| **farm tools** | cutlass, hoe, sickle, budding knife, secateurs, rake, watering can, knapsack sprayer | name; two uses; maintenance |
| **seeds and planting materials** | maize, rice, cowpea, groundnut, cassava cutting, yam sett, plantain sucker | name; crop; method of planting; viability features |
| **crop pests** | grasshopper, weevil, stem borer larva, aphid, termite | name; type of mouthpart; damage; control |
| **weeds** | spear grass, *Chromolaena*, nutgrass | name; type (grass, broadleaf, sedge); harmful effects; control |
| **fertilisers and chemicals** | urea, NPK, single superphosphate, lime | name; nutrient supplied; method and time of application |
| **feeds** | maize, groundnut cake, fish meal, bone meal, oyster shell | name; class (energy, protein, mineral); animal fed |
| **animal products** | egg, hide, wool, honey | name; uses; processing |
| **parasites** | tick, tapeworm, liver fluke | name; host; effect; control |

:::callout{type=tip}
**Write what you can see.** "Specimen B is a cassava cutting: a woody stem piece about 25 cm long with 6 nodes and buds" earns more than just "cassava". Observable features show the examiner you identified it, not guessed it.
:::

## Handling animals

- Approach **calmly and quietly** from the side, not suddenly from behind.
- **Cattle**: use a halter or rope, a crush or a strong post; keep clear of the hind legs.
- **Goats and sheep**: hold under the jaw and around the body, or by a collar; never by the horns or wool alone.
- **Pigs**: use a **pig board** to guide them; a snout rope for restraint.
- **Poultry**: catch at night or in dim light; hold the bird against your body with its wings folded and legs held.
- Wash hands after handling animals.

## Safety in practical work

- Wear suitable clothing and **boots**; use **gloves and masks** with chemicals.
- Carry sharp tools with blades down; never leave them lying on the ground.
- Read labels; mix chemicals outdoors; never eat or drink during practical work.
- Report injuries at once; keep a **first-aid kit** on the farm.
- Clean and put away tools and equipment after use.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**The school farm as a laboratory.** Many secondary schools in Sierra Leone have a school farm. Using it to measure plots, lay out rows with the 3-4-5 method, compare fertilised and unfertilised plots, keep a farm diary and record yields gives students exactly the practice the WASSCE practical paper tests, and the habits of a good farm manager.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. A rectangular plot measures 60 m by 25 m. Find its area in hectares."}
$60 \times 25 = 1500$ m² $= 0.15$ ha.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. How do you make a right angle at the corner of a plot without a protractor?"}
Use the **3-4-5 method**: mark 3 m along one side and 4 m along the other; adjust until the diagonal between the marks is exactly 5 m.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Maize is to be planted at 75 cm × 25 cm on a 0.15 ha plot, one seed per hole. How many seeds are needed?"}
Area per plant $= 0.1875$ m². Seeds $= 1500 \div 0.1875 = 8000$.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. A specimen is a fine grey powder labelled 'NPK 15-15-15'. State the nutrients it supplies and one method of applying it."}
**Nitrogen, phosphorus (as P₂O₅) and potassium (as K₂O)**, 15% of each. Applied by band or side placement, ring placement, or broadcasting before planting (any one).
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Give three safety rules when handling agrochemicals in practical work."}
Any three: read the label; wear gloves and a mask; mix outdoors; do not eat, drink or smoke; wash hands afterwards; store safely.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Plant population**: the number of plants on an area: area divided by the area per plant, times the plants per stand.
- **Seed rate**: the quantity of seed needed to plant a given area.
- **Germination percentage**: the number of seeds that germinate as a percentage of the seeds sown.
- **Specimen**: a sample, such as a soil, seed, pest, weed or animal product, to be identified and described in a practical examination.
- **Feed conversion ratio** (FCR): the amount of feed eaten divided by the weight gained; the lower it is, the better the animal uses its feed.
- **Laying percentage**: the number of eggs laid in a day as a percentage of the number of hens.
:::

## Key points

- **1 ha = 10 000 m²**; areas of rectangles, triangles and trapeziums; divide irregular plots.
- Lay out plots with **ranging poles, line and pegs**, and right angles by **3-4-5**.
- Convert to hectares before using **per-hectare** rates.
- In specimen questions: **name, observable features, uses** (or damage and control).
- Handle animals **calmly** with the right restraint; follow **safety rules** with tools and chemicals.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
The practical paper tests identification of specimens (soils, tools, seeds, pests, weeds, fertilisers, feeds, animal products), their features and uses, and calculations of area, plant population, seed and fertiliser requirements. Write clear, specific answers with observable features, and show every step of your calculations with units.
:::
