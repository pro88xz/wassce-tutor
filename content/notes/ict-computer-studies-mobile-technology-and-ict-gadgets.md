:::callout{type=definition}
**Mobile technology** is technology that people carry and use while moving about, such as smartphones, tablets and smartwatches, connected by wireless and mobile phone networks. An **ICT gadget** is a small, usually portable, device built for one main job with information or communication, such as a smartwatch, an e-reader, a drone or a power bank.
:::

In Sierra Leone more people reach the Internet through a mobile phone than through any other device, and many pay school fees, buy electricity and receive money through **mobile money**. This lesson explains the **generations of mobile networks** (1G to 5G), the parts and features of a **smartphone**, the SIM card and IMEI number, wireless technologies such as **Bluetooth**, **GPS**, **NFC** and hotspots, **mobile money**, common **ICT gadgets**, and how to use and care for mobile devices.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define mobile technology and name common ICT gadgets and their uses
- describe the generations of mobile networks from 1G to 5G
- identify the parts and features of a smartphone, and explain the SIM card and IMEI
- explain Bluetooth, GPS, NFC, a mobile hotspot and the Internet of Things
- describe mobile money and how it is used
- state the advantages and disadvantages of mobile devices, and how to care for them

## How a mobile network works

:::callout{type=definition}
**Cellular (mobile) network:** a network that covers an area with many small zones called cells, each served by a base station (mast) that links phones by radio to the operator's network.

**Base station (mobile mast):** a tower with antennas that sends and receives the radio signals of the mobile phones in its cell.
:::

As you move, your call or data connection is **handed over** from one mast to the next without being cut. Mobile network operators in Sierra Leone include **Orange**, **Africell** and **Qcell**.

| Generation | Introduced | What it added |
|---|---|---|
| **1G** | 1980s | analogue voice calls; poor quality, no security |
| **2G** (GSM) | 1990s | digital calls, **SMS** text messages, basic data |
| **3G** | 2000s | mobile Internet, video calls, email on phones |
| **4G** (LTE) | 2010s | fast mobile broadband: video streaming, online classes, apps |
| **5G** | 2020s | very high speeds and very low delay (latency), for many connected devices |

## The smartphone

:::callout{type=definition}
**Smartphone:** a mobile phone with a touch screen, an operating system and Internet access that runs apps and works as a small general-purpose computer.

**Feature phone:** a basic mobile phone, usually with buttons, used mainly for calls and SMS, with few or no apps.

**Mobile app (application):** a program designed to run on a smartphone or tablet, installed from an app store such as the Google Play Store or Apple's App Store.
:::

![The parts of a smartphone: on the front, the touch screen, front camera, earpiece speaker, microphone and USB-C charging port; on the back, the rear cameras, flash and fingerprint sensor, with the SIM card tray on the side; inside, the battery, processor, RAM and storage, GPS receiver and accelerometer](https://wasscetutor.com/img/ict/smartphone-parts.svg)

*Figure 2. The main parts of a smartphone. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

A smartphone combines the work of many separate gadgets: telephone, camera, music player, radio, torch, calculator, map, alarm clock, diary and computer. Its **operating system** (Android or iOS) is covered in the lesson on operating systems.

**Sensors in a smartphone:** the **accelerometer** and **gyroscope** detect movement and tilt (turning the screen sideways, counting steps); the **proximity sensor** turns the screen off when the phone is at your ear; the **light sensor** adjusts brightness; the **fingerprint sensor** or **face recognition** unlocks the phone.

### SIM card and IMEI

:::callout{type=definition}
**SIM card (subscriber identity module):** a small chip card that identifies a subscriber to a mobile network and holds the phone number; an eSIM does the same job built into the phone.

**IMEI (International Mobile Equipment Identity):** a unique 15-digit number that identifies a mobile phone handset itself; dialling *#06# shows it, and operators can use it to block a stolen phone.
:::

| SIM card | IMEI |
|---|---|
| identifies the **subscriber** (you and your number) | identifies the **handset** (the phone) |
| can be moved to another phone | stays with the phone |
| issued by the network operator | given by the phone's maker |

## Wireless technologies on mobile devices

:::callout{type=definition}
**Bluetooth:** a short-range radio technology, working over about 10 metres, that links nearby devices without cables, such as a phone and wireless earphones.

**GPS (Global Positioning System):** a system of satellites whose signals let a receiver work out its exact position on Earth, used for maps, directions and tracking.

**NFC (near-field communication):** very short-range wireless communication, over a few centimetres, used to pay by tapping a phone or card on a reader.

**Mobile hotspot (tethering):** a feature that lets a phone share its mobile data connection with other devices by Wi-Fi.

**Internet of Things (IoT):** everyday objects, such as meters, cameras, lights and household machines, fitted with sensors and connected to the Internet so they can send data and be controlled remotely.
:::

**Wi-Fi** connects a phone to a nearby wireless network; **mobile data** (3G, 4G, 5G) connects it through the operator's masts.

## Mobile money

:::callout{type=definition}
**Mobile money:** a service that lets people store, send and receive money, pay bills and buy goods using a mobile phone account, without a bank account.

**USSD (Unstructured Supplementary Service Data):** a menu service reached by dialling a short code that starts with * or # and ends with #, which works on any phone, even without Internet access.
:::

Mobile money services in Sierra Leone include **Orange Money** and **Afrimoney**. With them people:

- **send and receive money** to and from family members anywhere in the country
- **pay bills**, such as prepaid electricity and school fees
- **buy airtime** and data bundles
- **withdraw cash** from an agent, and **pay** in shops
- **save** small amounts, and receive salaries and payments

Each transaction is protected by a secret **PIN**. Mobile money has brought banking services to many people with no bank account, a step called **financial inclusion**.

## Advantages, disadvantages and care

| Advantages of mobile devices | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| communication anywhere, at any time | cost of handsets, data and repairs |
| learning on the move, with apps and online lessons | distraction, especially during study and while driving |
| mobile money and services without a bank | theft, loss and fraud |
| maps, news, weather and emergency help | small screens and short battery life |
| photos and videos to record events | spreading of false news; online bullying |

**Caring for a mobile device:** use a **case and screen protector**; keep it **dry** and away from heat; charge with the **right charger**; set a **screen lock** (PIN, fingerprint or face); install apps only from the **official app store**; keep the system **updated**; and **back up** contacts and photos.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Paying fees by phone.** A parent in Kenema dials the mobile money USSD menu on a basic phone, chooses **Pay bill**, enters the school's merchant number and the amount, and confirms with a PIN. The school receives the money at once and the parent gets an SMS receipt, without travelling or queuing at a bank.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Mixing up the SIM card and the IMEI.** The **SIM** identifies **you** (your number) and moves with you to a new phone; the **IMEI** identifies the **phone** and stays with it.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. State one feature that each generation, 2G and 4G, added to mobile phones."}
**2G**: digital calls and **SMS** text messages. **4G**: **fast mobile broadband** for video streaming and apps.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Distinguish between a SIM card and an IMEI number."}
A **SIM card** identifies the **subscriber** and holds the phone number; it can be moved to another phone. The **IMEI** is a 15-digit number that identifies the **handset** itself.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. State the use of (a) Bluetooth, (b) GPS, (c) NFC."}
(a) **Short-range wireless links** between nearby devices, such as earphones. (b) Finding your **exact position** for maps and directions. (c) **Contactless payment** by tapping a phone on a reader.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Give three uses of mobile money."}
Any three: **sending and receiving money**; **paying bills** (electricity, school fees); **buying airtime**; **withdrawing cash** from agents; **paying in shops**; **saving**.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. State two ways of protecting a smartphone and the data on it."}
Any two: a **screen lock** (PIN, fingerprint, face); installing apps only from the **official store**; keeping the system **updated**; **backing up** data; a **case and screen protector**.
:::

## Key points

- **Mobile technology** works through **cellular networks** of cells and **base stations**.
- Generations: **1G** analogue voice, **2G** digital and SMS, **3G** mobile Internet, **4G** fast broadband, **5G** very fast, low delay.
- A **smartphone** runs apps on an operating system; the **SIM** identifies the subscriber, the **IMEI** the handset.
- Wireless: **Bluetooth** (short range), **GPS** (position), **NFC** (tap to pay), **hotspot** (share data), **IoT** (connected objects).
- **Mobile money** sends money, pays bills and buys airtime by phone, through apps or **USSD** codes.
- Gadgets: tablets, smartwatches, e-readers, cameras, smart TVs, drones, power banks.
