Who should own the water company, the electricity supply or the port: the government or private investors? Over the years West African governments have taken businesses over, sold them off, and told state firms to run like private ones. They have also tried to put more of the economy in citizens' hands. This lesson explains **public enterprises, nationalisation, privatisation, commercialisation and indigenisation**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe public enterprises and their features
- explain nationalisation, with its reasons and disadvantages
- explain privatisation, its methods, reasons and disadvantages
- explain commercialisation and how it differs from privatisation
- explain indigenisation and local content policy

## Public enterprises

:::callout{type=definition}
A **public enterprise (public corporation, parastatal)** is a business **owned and controlled by the government**, set up by law to provide goods or services to the public.
:::

**Features:**

- owned by the **state** on behalf of the public
- set up by an **Act of Parliament** or decree
- run by a **board of directors** appointed by the minister
- capital from **government**
- main aim is **public service**, though many are expected to cover costs
- has a separate **legal personality**
- **accountable** to Parliament through the minister

**Examples in Sierra Leone:**

- Guma Valley Water Company
- the Electricity Distribution and Supply Authority (EDSA)
- the Sierra Leone Ports and Harbours Authority
- the Sierra Leone Road Transport Corporation (SLRTC)
- SALPOST
- the Sierra Leone Broadcasting Corporation

**Problems of public enterprises:**

- inefficiency
- political interference
- overstaffing
- corruption
- losses that must be met by the taxpayer
- poor service
- slow decision-making

## Nationalisation

:::callout{type=definition}
**Nationalisation** is the **transfer of ownership and control** of a privately owned business to the **government**, usually with compensation to the former owners.
:::

**Reasons for nationalisation:**

1. Control **strategic industries** (power, water, ports, defence).
2. Provide **essential services** to all, even where unprofitable (rural areas).
3. Prevent **private monopolies** exploiting consumers.
4. Protect **jobs** in failing firms.
5. Ensure that **profits** stay in the country and go to the public.
6. Attract the **large capital** some projects need, which private firms cannot raise.
7. **National pride** and economic independence.

**Disadvantages:**

- inefficiency and losses
- political interference
- a heavy burden on government funds
- lack of competition
- loss of foreign investors' confidence
- the cost of compensation

## Privatisation

:::callout{type=definition}
**Privatisation** is the **transfer of ownership** (all or part) of a government-owned enterprise **to private individuals or firms**.
:::

**Methods:**

| Method | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sale of shares** to the public | Shares are sold on the stock exchange or to employees |
| **Sale to a private investor** | The enterprise is sold to a company, often a foreign one |
| **Management contract** | A private firm runs the enterprise for a fee; the government still owns it |
| **Concession or lease** | A private firm operates the asset for a period (e.g. a port terminal) and pays the government |
| **Public–private partnership (PPP)** | Government and private firms share investment, risk and returns |
| **Deregulation** | Opening a state monopoly to private competition |

In Sierra Leone, privatisation is managed by the **National Commission for Privatisation (NCP)**, set up in **2002**. The **container terminal at the Freetown port**, for example, has been run by a private operator under a concession.

**Reasons for privatisation:**

1. **Efficiency**: private owners seek profit and cut waste.
2. **Reduce the burden** on government funds; government receives money from the sale.
3. **End political interference**.
4. **Attract investment** and new technology.
5. **Competition** improves service and choice.
6. **Wider share ownership**.

**Disadvantages:**

- **job losses** as firms cut staff
- **higher prices** and the neglect of unprofitable areas
- a public monopoly may become a **private monopoly**
- **foreign control** of strategic assets
- assets may be **sold too cheaply**
- **profits leave** the country

## Commercialisation

:::callout{type=definition}
**Commercialisation** is the reorganisation of a public enterprise so that it is run on **commercial (business) lines**: it must **cover its costs and make a profit**, and it gets **less or no government subsidy**, but it **remains government-owned**.
:::

- **Full commercialisation**: the enterprise must fund itself completely, including new investment.
- **Partial commercialisation**: it must cover running costs, but government still funds major investment.

| Privatisation | Commercialisation |
|---|---|
| **Ownership** passes to private hands | Ownership **stays with government** |
| Private owners take the profit | Profit goes to the government enterprise |
| Shares are sold | No shares are sold |

**Advantages of commercialisation:** greater efficiency, less dependence on subsidies, better service and charges that reflect costs. **Disadvantages:** higher charges for the poor, and possible job cuts.

## Indigenisation and local content

:::callout{type=definition}
**Indigenisation** is a government policy to **transfer the ownership and control** of businesses from **foreigners to citizens** of the country.
:::

**Methods:**

- **reserving** certain businesses (often small retail and services) for citizens
- requiring a **minimum share of local ownership** in foreign firms
- requiring firms to **employ and train** citizens in management posts
- **local content rules**: firms must buy local goods and services

**Reasons:**

1. Give citizens **control** of their economy.
2. Keep **profits** in the country.
3. Develop **local entrepreneurs** and skills.
4. Create **jobs** for citizens at all levels.
5. Reduce **foreign domination**.

**Problems:**

- citizens may **lack the capital and skills** to take over
- **fronting**: citizens act as fronts for foreign owners
- foreign investment is **discouraged**
- efficiency may fall

:::callout{type=local-example}
Nigeria's **Indigenisation Decrees** of the 1970s are the best-known West African example. In Sierra Leone, the **Local Content Agency**, set up under a 2016 law, promotes the use of **Sierra Leonean workers, goods and services** by companies, especially in mining and large projects.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Commercialisation is not privatisation.** A commercialised enterprise is still **owned by the government**; only its management style and financing change.
:::

## Key points

- **Public enterprises** are state-owned and run for public service; they suffer from inefficiency and interference.
- **Nationalisation**: private → state, for strategic control and essential services.
- **Privatisation**: state → private, for efficiency and to relieve government funds. Methods include share sales, sale to investors, management contracts, concessions and PPPs.
- **Commercialisation**: state-owned but run for profit without subsidy.
- **Indigenisation**: foreign → citizen ownership; **local content** favours local workers, goods and services.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
WASSCE often asks you to **define and distinguish privatisation and commercialisation**, to **give reasons for and against privatisation or nationalisation**, and to **explain indigenisation**. The features and problems of public enterprises are also common. Give examples from your country.
:::

## Quick check

1. Distinguish privatisation from commercialisation.
2. Give two reasons for nationalisation.
3. Give two methods of privatisation.
4. What is indigenisation?

**Answers:** 1. Privatisation transfers ownership of a state enterprise to private owners; commercialisation keeps it state-owned but makes it run on commercial lines to cover costs and make a profit. 2. Any two: control of strategic industries, essential services for all, prevent private monopolies, protect jobs, keep profits in the country, large capital needs, national pride. 3. Any two: sale of shares, sale to a private investor, management contract, concession or lease, public–private partnership, deregulation. 4. A policy of transferring ownership and control of businesses from foreigners to citizens.
