## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define an economic system
- describe the features of capitalism and socialism
- state the advantages and disadvantages of each

## What is an economic system?

:::callout{type=definition}
An **economic system** is the way a society organises the ownership and use of its resources to answer the basic economic problems: **what** to produce, **how** to produce and **for whom** to produce.
:::

The main systems are **capitalism** (the free market economy), **socialism** (the command or planned economy) and the **mixed economy**. This lesson covers the first two; the mixed economy is in the next lesson.

## Capitalism (the free market economy)

:::callout{type=definition}
**Capitalism** is an economic system in which the factors of production are owned and controlled mainly by **private individuals and firms**, and the basic economic problems are solved by the **price mechanism** (the forces of demand and supply).
:::

### Features of capitalism

1. **Private ownership** of land, factories and other means of production.
2. **Profit motive**: firms produce in order to make a profit.
3. **The price mechanism** decides what is produced: goods that consumers want, and are willing to pay for, earn a profit and are produced.
4. **Consumer sovereignty**: consumers "vote" with their money for the goods they want.
5. **Free enterprise**: people are free to start businesses and choose their jobs.
6. **Competition** among firms for customers.
7. **Little government interference** in economic activity.

### Advantages of capitalism

- Competition encourages **efficiency**, better quality and lower prices.
- The profit motive encourages **hard work, innovation** and new products.
- Consumers enjoy a **wide variety** of goods and services.
- People have **freedom** to own property, start businesses and choose occupations.
- There is no need for a large, costly planning bureaucracy.

### Disadvantages of capitalism

- **Inequality** of income and wealth: the rich can become richer while the poor fall behind.
- **Monopolies** may form and exploit consumers with high prices.
- Goods that do not make a profit, such as street lighting or roads in remote villages, may **not be provided**.
- Firms may **neglect the environment** or workers' welfare in search of profit.
- Booms and slumps can lead to **unemployment**.
- Firms may waste resources on **advertising** and on producing luxuries for the rich.

## Socialism (the command economy)

:::callout{type=definition}
**Socialism** is an economic system in which the factors of production are owned and controlled mainly by the **state**, and the basic economic problems are solved by **central planning**.
:::

### Features of socialism

1. **State ownership** of land, factories, mines and other means of production.
2. **Central planning**: a planning authority decides what, how and for whom to produce.
3. **Social welfare** is the aim, rather than profit.
4. **Prices are fixed** by the government, not by demand and supply.
5. **Little or no competition** between producers.
6. **More equal distribution** of income.

### Advantages of socialism

- **Less inequality**: income and wealth are shared more evenly.
- **Essential goods** (food, health care, education, housing) can be provided for everyone.
- Resources can be directed to **national priorities**, such as heavy industry or defence.
- Planning can reduce **unemployment** and avoid wasteful competition.

### Disadvantages of socialism

- **Lack of incentive**: without profit, workers and managers may not work hard or innovate.
- **Bureaucracy and inefficiency**: planning everything centrally is slow and costly.
- **Limited choice** for consumers; shortages and queues may occur.
- **Restriction of freedom**: people cannot freely start businesses or own property.
- **Wrong decisions** by planners affect the whole economy.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not write that under capitalism "there is no government". Even capitalist countries have governments that make laws, defend the country and enforce contracts. The point is that the government **does not direct** most economic activity.
:::

## Comparing the two

| | Capitalism | Socialism |
|---|---|---|
| Who owns resources? | Private individuals and firms | The state |
| Who decides what to produce? | Consumers, through the price mechanism | Central planners |
| Main motive | Profit | Social welfare |
| How prices are set | Demand and supply | Government |
| Consumer choice | Wide | Limited |
| Income distribution | Unequal | More equal |

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Common questions ask you to **describe the features**, or **state the advantages and disadvantages**, of capitalism or socialism, or to **compare** the two. Write each point as a short heading followed by a sentence of explanation. Four well-explained points usually earn more than eight bare ones.
:::

## Quick check

1. What decides what is produced in a capitalist economy?
2. Give two advantages of socialism.
3. Why might a capitalist economy fail to provide street lights in a small village?

**Answers:** 1. The price mechanism (consumers' demand and producers' supply). 2. For example: less inequality; essential goods for all; planning towards national priorities. 3. Because no private firm can make a profit from them: people cannot easily be charged for using street lights.
