# Transportation in commerce: modes, choice and logistics

## Learning objectives
Explain the importance of transport, identify the main modes and select a suitable mode for different goods and journeys.

## Meaning and importance
Transportation is the movement of people and goods from one place to another. In commerce it creates place utility by moving products from where they are produced to where they are needed. It connects suppliers, factories, warehouses, markets and consumers. Reliable transport reduces delivery delays and stock shortages; unreliable transport increases costs and can cause spoilage.

## Main modes

| Mode | Advantages | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Road | Flexible routes; door-to-door delivery | Traffic, road conditions, fuel costs and capacity limits |
| Rail | Efficient for large volumes on established routes | High infrastructure cost and fixed routes |
| Water | Often economical for bulky international cargo | Slower; depends on ports, waterways and weather |
| Air | Very fast for urgent or valuable goods | High cost and cargo restrictions |
| Pipeline | Continuous movement of suitable liquids or gases | Expensive to build and limited to specific products |

The best mode depends on distance, speed, nature and value of goods, quantity, cost, reliability, security and infrastructure. A container may travel by sea between countries and by road from a port to a warehouse, combining modes.

## Transport and logistics
Logistics is broader than transportation. It includes planning and controlling the movement and storage of goods, inventory, information and related services from origin to destination. A transport decision should consider packaging, loading, insurance, tracking, delivery schedules and stock held while goods are in transit.

Fresh fish requires fast delivery and suitable temperature control. Heavy building materials may be moved in bulk using a lower-cost mode where the route allows. Urgent medical supplies may justify air transport despite the expense.

## Sierra Leone context
Road transport connects inland production areas with urban markets, while ports support international trade. Route conditions, seasonal weather, port handling and vehicle availability affect delivery reliability. A trader should plan for these constraints rather than compare modes by speed alone.

## WASSCE examination focus
When comparing modes, state an advantage and a limitation, then relate each to the cargo or route. No mode is always cheapest or fastest; the result depends on distance, infrastructure and goods.
