**Agriculture** is the growing of crops and the rearing of animals for food, raw materials and income. In Sierra Leone it is everywhere: rice in the inland valley swamps, cassava on the uplands, cocoa and coffee in the east, cattle in the north, and fishing boats coming in at Tombo. This lesson answers the question *what is agriculture?*, then sets out its branches, its main types and why it matters so much to Sierra Leone.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define agriculture and explain where the word comes from
- name and describe the main branches of agriculture
- distinguish subsistence agriculture from commercial agriculture
- explain the importance of agriculture to the individual and to the nation
- state the main problems that limit agricultural production in Sierra Leone

## What is agriculture?

:::callout{type=definition}
**Agriculture** is the art and science of **cultivating crops** and **rearing animals** for the use of people. It covers the whole chain, from preparing the land and choosing seed or breeding stock to harvesting, storing, processing and marketing the produce.
:::

The word comes from two Latin words: ***ager***, meaning "field" or "land", and ***cultura***, meaning "cultivation". Agriculture is therefore, literally, *the cultivation of the field*.

It is called both an **art** and a **science**:

- an **art**, because it uses skills learnt by practice: making ridges, planting, weeding, milking, handling animals
- a **science**, because it applies knowledge from biology, chemistry, physics and economics: soil testing, plant breeding, animal nutrition, pest control, farm records

Agriculture includes more than farming crops. Rearing poultry, keeping bees, managing a fish pond, planting and harvesting trees and processing palm fruit into oil are all agriculture.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**"Agriculture means growing crops."** Crop growing is only one part. A good definition always includes **both crops and animals**, and the purpose: food, raw materials or income. "Agriculture is farming" earns little in an examination.
:::

## Branches of agriculture

Agriculture is divided into branches, each a field of study and of work.

| Branch | What it deals with | Sierra Leone example |
|---|---|---|
| **Crop production** (agronomy) | Growing field crops: cereals, roots and tubers, legumes | Rice, cassava, maize, groundnut |
| **Animal production** (animal husbandry) | Rearing livestock for meat, milk, eggs, hides and work | Cattle, goats, sheep, poultry, pigs |
| **Horticulture** | Fruits, vegetables, flowers and ornamental plants | Pepper, garden eggs, oranges, mangoes |
| **Forestry** | Establishing, managing and conserving trees and forests | Timber, firewood, forest reserves |
| **Fisheries and aquaculture** | Catching wild fish and farming fish in ponds | Artisanal fishing, tilapia ponds |
| **Soil science** | Soil formation, properties, fertility and conservation | Testing soil before planting |
| **Crop protection** | Controlling pests, diseases and weeds | Protecting stored rice from weevils |
| **Agricultural economics** | Costs, prices, markets, credit and farm management | Deciding which crop pays best |
| **Agricultural engineering** (mechanisation) | Machines, tools, irrigation, farm buildings | Power tillers, rice mills |
| **Agricultural extension** | Teaching farmers new ideas and methods | Extension officers visiting farm groups |

:::callout{type=tip}
The branches are connected. A farmer choosing a rice variety must think about soil (soil science), pests (crop protection), labour and machines (engineering), and price at the market (economics), not only about the seed.
:::

## Types of agriculture: subsistence and commercial

| | Subsistence agriculture | Commercial agriculture |
|---|---|---|
| **Main aim** | To feed the farmer and family | To sell produce for profit |
| **Size of farm** | Usually small | Usually large |
| **Labour** | Mainly family labour | Hired labour |
| **Tools** | Simple hand tools: hoe, cutlass | Machines, improved inputs |
| **Crops and animals** | Many kinds together (mixed cropping) | Often one or a few (specialisation) |
| **Output** | Low; little or nothing left to sell | High; mostly sold |

Most farmers in Sierra Leone are smallholders producing mainly for their households, but many sell part of what they grow, so the two types shade into each other.

## Importance of agriculture

Agriculture matters to the farmer, to the community and to the nation.

1. **Food.** It provides the food we eat: rice, cassava, palm oil, vegetables, fish, meat and eggs.
2. **Employment.** It is the largest source of work in Sierra Leone, especially in rural areas, for farmers, labourers, traders, transporters and processors.
3. **Income.** Farmers earn money by selling crops, animals and their products.
4. **Raw materials for industry.** Oil palm for soap and cooking oil, cocoa for chocolate, timber for furniture, hides for leather.
5. **Foreign exchange.** Exports such as cocoa and coffee earn money from other countries.
6. **Government revenue.** Taxes and levies on agricultural trade and exports.
7. **Market for industrial goods.** Farmers buy tools, fertilisers, feeds, sacks and machines made by industry.
8. **Clothing and shelter.** Cotton, hides, timber, bamboo and thatch.
9. **Rural development.** Farming communities need roads, markets, schools and clinics, which in turn help agriculture.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Follow one bag of rice from an inland valley swamp near Bo. The farmer and hired labourers are **employed** to grow it; it **feeds** the household; part is **sold** for income; a rice mill **processes** it; a lorry driver and a trader earn by **transporting and selling** it; and the farmer uses the money to buy **tools and seed** from shops. One crop supports many livelihoods.
:::

## Problems limiting agriculture in Sierra Leone

- uncertain rainfall and dry spells
- low soil fertility and soil erosion
- pests and diseases of crops and animals
- poor access to improved seed, fertilisers and tools
- shortage of credit (loans) for farmers
- poor storage, so produce spoils after harvest
- bad roads and high transport costs to markets
- labour shortage as young people move to towns
- weak market information and low, unstable prices

A problem should be understood before a solution is chosen: more fertiliser will not help a rice field whose real problem is poor drainage.

## Key points

- **Agriculture** is the art and science of cultivating crops and rearing animals for food, raw materials and income.
- The word comes from Latin ***ager*** (field) and ***cultura*** (cultivation).
- Its **branches** include crop production, animal production, horticulture, forestry, fisheries, soil science, crop protection, economics, engineering and extension.
- **Subsistence** agriculture feeds the family; **commercial** agriculture produces for sale and profit.
- Agriculture provides **food, employment, income, raw materials, foreign exchange and revenue**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **define agriculture**, **name and explain its branches**, **compare subsistence and commercial agriculture**, and **state the importance of agriculture** to the nation. When you explain an importance, link the activity to an outcome (for example, "cocoa exports earn foreign exchange"), and do not confuse a branch of agriculture with a farm operation such as weeding or harvesting.
:::
