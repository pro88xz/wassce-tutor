The **Mali** and **Songhai** empires followed Ghana as the great powers of the Western Sudan. **Mali** was founded by **Sundiata Keita**, who defeated the Sosso king **Sumanguru** at the **Battle of Kirina** around **1235**, and reached its height under **Mansa Musa** (ruled about **1312–1337**), whose **pilgrimage to Mecca in 1324** made Mali famous for its gold. **Songhai** rose on the **Niger bend** under **Sunni Ali** (1464–1492) and was reorganised by **Askia Muhammad** (1493–1528), before falling to a **Moroccan invasion** at the **Battle of Tondibi in 1591**. This lesson explains the rise, achievements and fall of both empires.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the rise of Mali under Sundiata Keita
- describe the achievements of Mansa Musa and the importance of his pilgrimage
- explain the decline of Mali
- explain the rise of Songhai under Sunni Ali and Askia Muhammad
- describe the administration of Songhai and explain its fall

## The Mali Empire

**Founding:** **Sundiata Keita** of the **Mandinka (Malinke)** united the Mande clans and defeated **Sumanguru Kante** of Sosso at the **Battle of Kirina** (about **1235**). His story is preserved in the oral **Epic of Sundiata**, recited by griots.

**Factors in the rise of Mali:**

1. Leadership of **Sundiata** and later strong **mansas** (kings).
2. Control of the **gold fields** of Bure and the trans-Saharan **trade routes**.
3. Fertile land along the **upper Niger**.
4. A strong **army**, including cavalry.
5. **Islam**, which linked Mali to North Africa and provided literate officials.

### Mansa Musa

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Mansa Musa** (ruled about **1312–1337**) made the **pilgrimage (hajj) to Mecca in 1324** with a huge caravan carrying gold. In Cairo he spent and gave away so much gold that, according to Arab writers, its value there **fell for years** afterwards. He brought back scholars and the architect **as-Sahili**, who is credited with building the **Djinguereber Mosque** in **Timbuktu**. His journey put Mali on European maps: the **Catalan Atlas of 1375** shows "Musa Mali" holding a gold nugget.
:::

**Importance of Mansa Musa's reign:** it **advertised Mali's wealth**, strengthened ties with the Muslim world, made **Timbuktu** a centre of Islamic learning and trade, and extended the empire to its greatest size, including Timbuktu and Gao.

The traveller **Ibn Battuta** visited Mali in **1352–1353** and praised its **justice and security**: travellers could move safely without fear of robbers.

**Decline of Mali** (from the late 14th century):

1. **Weak rulers** and **succession disputes** after Mansa Musa and Mansa Sulayman.
2. **Revolts** by subject peoples, including the **Songhai** at Gao.
3. **Attacks** by the **Tuareg** (who took Timbuktu in 1433) and the **Mossi**.
4. The **size** of the empire made it hard to control.
5. The rise of **Songhai**, which absorbed most of Mali's lands.

## The Songhai Empire

**Sunni Ali** (ruled **1464–1492**) turned the Songhai kingdom of **Gao** into an empire. A brilliant soldier with a river **fleet** on the Niger and strong **cavalry**, he captured **Timbuktu (1468)** and **Djenné** (about 1473).

**Askia Muhammad** (ruled **1493–1528**), also called Askia the Great, seized power after Sunni Ali's death and reorganised the empire:

| Reform | Description |
|---|---|
| **Provinces** | divided the empire into provinces under **governors** loyal to him |
| **Ministries** | appointed officials for finance, justice, agriculture, the navy and so on |
| **Standing army** | created a **professional army** in place of seasonal levies |
| **Islam** | promoted Islam and **Islamic law**; went on **pilgrimage** in 1496–1497 |
| **Learning** | supported scholars at **Sankore** in Timbuktu |
| **Trade** | standardised **weights and measures**; protected trade routes |

**Fall of Songhai:**

1. **Succession disputes** and civil war among Askia's descendants.
2. **Moroccan invasion:** Sultan **Ahmad al-Mansur** of Morocco, seeking control of the **gold** and the **salt mines of Taghaza**, sent an army under **Judar Pasha** across the Sahara.
3. At the **Battle of Tondibi (1591)**, Moroccan **firearms** defeated Songhai's cavalry and spearmen.
4. Afterwards the empire broke up, and trade and learning declined.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not confuse **Sunni Ali** and **Askia Muhammad**. Sunni Ali was the **conqueror** who built the empire by war; Askia Muhammad was the **organiser** who reformed its government and promoted Islam.
:::

## Key points

- **Mali:** founded by **Sundiata Keita** after **Kirina (c. 1235)**; height under **Mansa Musa** (hajj **1324**); visited by **Ibn Battuta (1352–53)**; declined through weak rulers, revolts and attacks.
- **Songhai:** built by **Sunni Ali (1464–1492)**; reformed by **Askia Muhammad (1493–1528)**; fell after the Moroccan victory at **Tondibi (1591)**.
- Both empires grew rich from **trans-Saharan trade** and were linked to the Islamic world.

## Quick check

1. Who founded the Mali empire, and at which battle?
2. Why was Mansa Musa's pilgrimage important?
3. Which traveller visited Mali in 1352–53?
4. Name three reforms of Askia Muhammad.
5. Why did Songhai lose the Battle of Tondibi?

**Answers:**

1. Sundiata Keita, at the Battle of Kirina (about 1235).
2. It advertised Mali's wealth, strengthened ties with the Muslim world, brought scholars and builders to Mali, and put Mali on European maps.
3. Ibn Battuta.
4. Any three: provinces under governors; ministries; a standing army; promotion of Islam and Islamic law; support for scholars; standard weights and measures.
5. The Moroccan army had firearms, which Songhai's cavalry and spearmen could not match; Songhai was also weakened by internal disputes.
