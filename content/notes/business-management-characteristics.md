A provision shop, a fishing company at Tombo and a mobile money agency look very different. Yet they share the same basic features: each sells to customers, each hopes for profit, each runs the risk of loss, and each must keep going day after day. These shared features are the **characteristics of business**. Knowing them helps you recognise a business and understand what keeps it alive.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state and explain the main characteristics of business
- use the characteristics to tell a business from a non-business activity
- explain why risk and profit go together

## The characteristics of business

### 1. Profit motive

The main reason a business is set up is to earn a **profit**. Without profit, the owner has no reward for the money and effort put in, and the business cannot grow.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Profit motive** is the desire to earn a profit, which drives people to start and run businesses.
:::

### 2. Risk and uncertainty

Every business faces the possibility of **loss**. Prices may fall, customers may stop buying, goods may be stolen or damaged, and a new competitor may open nearby. Profit is the owner's reward for bearing this risk.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Business risk** is the possibility that a business will make a loss instead of a profit because of events it cannot fully control.
:::

### 3. Production or distribution of goods and services

A business either **makes** goods (a bakery, a block factory), **distributes** them (a wholesaler, a transporter) or **provides** a service (a barber, a bank).

### 4. Exchange (buying and selling)

Goods and services are produced **for sale** to others, not for the owner's own use. There must be a buyer and a seller and something of value exchanged, usually money.

### 5. Regularity (continuity)

Business is carried on **regularly**, not once. A trader who sells goods every market day is in business; a person who sells an old radio once is not.

### 6. Customer satisfaction

A business survives only if customers keep buying. It must offer the goods customers want, at a price they can pay, of good quality and in the right place.

### 7. Use of resources

Every business combines **resources**: land or premises, labour, capital (money, tools and machines) and the skill of the owner, called **entrepreneurship**.

### 8. Legal existence

A business must operate **within the law**: it registers where required, pays taxes and obeys rules on health, safety and consumer protection. Selling stolen goods or illegal drugs is not business.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not write "a business must make a profit" as a characteristic. Many businesses make losses in some years. The characteristic is the **profit motive**: the business **aims** at profit and bears the **risk** of loss.
:::

## Using the characteristics

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Applying the characteristics.** A youth group sells cold water at a football match once a year to raise money for a trip.

- Exchange: yes, water is sold.
- Profit motive: partly, but the money goes to a trip, not owners.
- Regularity: **no**, it happens once a year.

So it is a **fund-raising activity**, not a business. If the same youths set up a stall that sells cold water every day for profit, all the characteristics would be present and it would be a business.
:::

## Why risk and profit go together

The greater the risk, the higher the profit people expect before they will invest. A trader importing goods from abroad faces more risk (exchange rates, delays at the port, theft) than one selling bread in her own street, so she expects a larger profit on each item. If the expected profit is too small for the risk, people will not start that business at all.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Rice importers in Freetown face the risk that the leone falls against the US dollar while their rice is at sea, making the next shipment dearer. They set their prices and keep reserves of money with that risk in mind. A market woman who buys rice from them and sells by the cup faces smaller risks and earns a smaller margin per bag.
:::

## Key points

- The **characteristics of business** are: profit motive, risk, production or distribution, exchange, regularity, customer satisfaction, use of resources and legal existence.
- **Profit** is the reward for bearing **risk**; higher risk calls for a higher expected profit.
- An activity missing exchange, regularity or a profit motive is not a business.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
WASSCE asks you to **state and explain** the characteristics of business, often "five" or "seven". Give a heading and one or two sentences of explanation for each, with an example. A list of words without explanation earns few marks.
:::

2. Why is regularity a characteristic of business?
3. Give two risks faced by a provision shop.
4. Why does a business importing goods expect a higher profit than a street trader?

Any two of theft, falling prices, competition, goods spoiling, customers not paying debts. 4. It bears more risk, and profit is the reward for risk.
