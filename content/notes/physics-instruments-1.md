Your eye is a living camera: a lens that focuses light onto a light-sensitive screen, with an automatic shutter and focus. Understanding it explains why some people need glasses — and how a camera copies its design.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the structure of the human eye and the function of each part
- explain accommodation, the near point and the far point
- explain short sight and long sight and how they are corrected
- compare the eye with the camera

## The structure of the eye

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **Cornea** | transparent front window; does most of the bending (refraction) of light |
| **Iris** | coloured ring of muscle that controls the size of the pupil |
| **Pupil** | the hole through which light enters; smaller in bright light, larger in dim light |
| **Eye lens** | converging lens of variable thickness; fine-focuses the image |
| **Ciliary muscles** | change the thickness (focal length) of the lens |
| **Suspensory ligaments** | hold the lens in place |
| **Retina** | light-sensitive layer where the image forms; contains rods (dim light) and cones (colour) |
| **Fovea (yellow spot)** | most sensitive part of the retina, where vision is sharpest |
| **Blind spot** | where the optic nerve leaves; no light-sensitive cells |
| **Optic nerve** | carries signals from the retina to the brain |
| **Aqueous and vitreous humour** | transparent fluids that keep the eye's shape |

The image on the retina is **real, inverted and diminished**. The brain interprets it as upright.

## Accommodation

:::callout{type=definition}
**Accommodation** is the ability of the eye to **change the focal length of its lens** so that objects at different distances are focused clearly on the retina.
:::

- **Distant object**: ciliary muscles **relax**, the lens becomes **thinner** (longer focal length).
- **Near object**: ciliary muscles **contract**, the lens becomes **thicker** (shorter focal length).

:::callout{type=definition}
- The **near point** is the closest point at which an object can be seen clearly. For a normal adult eye it is about **25 cm** (the **least distance of distinct vision**).
- The **far point** is the furthest point that can be seen clearly. For a normal eye it is at **infinity**.
:::

## Defects of vision

![Short sight: rays from a distant object focus in front of the retina and a diverging lens corrects it. Long sight: rays from a near object focus behind the retina and a converging lens corrects it](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/eye-defects.svg)

*Figure 1. Short sight and long sight, and their correction. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

### Short sight (myopia)

- **Symptom:** near objects are clear; **distant** objects are blurred. The far point is closer than infinity.
- **Cause:** the eyeball is **too long**, or the lens is too strong (converges too much).
- **Effect:** rays from a distant object focus **in front of** the retina.
- **Correction:** a **diverging (concave) lens**, which spreads the rays slightly before they enter the eye.

### Long sight (hypermetropia)

- **Symptom:** distant objects are clear; **near** objects are blurred. The near point is further than 25 cm.
- **Cause:** the eyeball is **too short**, or the lens is too weak.
- **Effect:** rays from a near object would focus **behind** the retina.
- **Correction:** a **converging (convex) lens**, which converges the rays a little before they enter the eye.

### Other defects

- **Presbyopia**: with age the lens stiffens and cannot accommodate well, so older people struggle to read; corrected with converging (reading) glasses or bifocals.
- **Astigmatism**: the cornea is unevenly curved, so lines in some directions are blurred; corrected with cylindrical lenses.
- **Cataract**: the lens becomes cloudy; treated by surgically replacing the lens.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Free eye-screening camps run by health NGOs in Sierra Leone often find students who cannot read the board from the back of the class — short sight, quickly fixed with diverging spectacles. Cataract surgery restores sight to many older people.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A short-sighted student cannot see clearly beyond 2.0 m. What lens does she need, and what is its power?**

She needs a diverging lens that forms a virtual image, at 2.0 m, of very distant objects ($u = \infty$). So $v = -2.0$ m:

$$\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{\infty} + \dfrac{1}{-2.0} \Rightarrow f = -2.0\ \text{m}, \quad P = -0.5\ \text{D}$$
:::

## The eye and the camera compared

| Eye | Camera |
|---|---|
| eye lens and cornea | camera lens (converging) |
| retina | film or digital sensor |
| iris and pupil | diaphragm and aperture |
| eyelid | shutter |
| focusing by changing the **thickness** of the lens (accommodation) | focusing by changing the **distance** between lens and film |
| image real, inverted, diminished | image real, inverted, diminished |
| dark choroid layer absorbs stray light | inside of the camera painted black |

## Key points

- The cornea and lens focus a real, inverted, diminished image on the retina.
- Accommodation: ciliary muscles change the lens thickness; near point ≈ 25 cm, far point at infinity.
- Short sight: focus in front of the retina; diverging lens. Long sight: focus behind the retina; converging lens.
- The camera focuses by moving the lens; the eye by changing its shape.

## Quick check

1. Which part of the eye controls how much light enters?
2. Which type of lens corrects short sight?
3. How does a camera focus objects at different distances?

**Answers:** 1. The iris (by changing the size of the pupil). 2. A diverging (concave) lens. 3. By moving the lens towards or away from the film or sensor.
