## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define production and describe the three types (stages) of production
- explain division of labour and specialisation
- state their advantages, disadvantages and limits

## What is production?

:::callout{type=definition}
**Production** is the creation of goods and services to satisfy human wants. It is complete only when the goods reach the **final consumer**, so transport, storage and selling are part of production too.
:::

## Types of production

Production is usually grouped into three stages:

| Type | What it involves | Examples in Sierra Leone |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** (extractive) | Taking resources from nature | Farming rice and cassava, fishing, mining diamonds and iron ore, logging |
| **Secondary** (manufacturing and construction) | Turning raw materials into finished or semi-finished goods | Milling rice, making palm oil, building houses, bottling drinks |
| **Tertiary** (services) | Providing services, often to help goods reach consumers | Trading, transport, banking, insurance, teaching, nursing |

Tertiary production is often split into:

- **commercial services**, which help trade (trade, transport, banking, insurance, warehousing, advertising)
- **direct (personal) services**, given straight to people (teachers, doctors, lawyers, hairdressers)

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Following a bag of rice.** A farmer in Torma Bum grows the paddy (**primary**). A mill dries and husks it (**secondary**). A lorry carries it to Freetown and a trader sells it at Dove Cut market (**tertiary**). Only when the rice reaches the family who will eat it is production complete.
:::

## Division of labour

:::callout{type=definition}
**Division of labour** means breaking a production process into separate tasks, with each worker doing one task or a small number of tasks.
:::

In a tailor's workshop, one person may cut the cloth, another sew, another fix buttons and another iron. Each becomes very good at their own task.

**Specialisation** is the wider idea: individuals, firms, regions or even countries concentrating on what they do best. Division of labour is specialisation within a production process.

### Advantages of division of labour

1. **Greater output**: workers become skilled and fast at their task.
2. **Saving of time**: no time is lost moving from one task to another.
3. **Use of machines**: simple, repeated tasks can be done by machines.
4. **Use of talents**: each worker does what suits them best.
5. **Lower cost per unit**, so goods can be cheaper.
6. **Shorter training**: learning one task is quicker than learning the whole craft.

### Disadvantages of division of labour

1. **Monotony**: repeating one task all day is boring and lowers morale.
2. **Loss of craftsmanship**: no worker can make the whole product.
3. **Interdependence**: if one stage stops (a strike, an absent worker), the whole process stops.
4. **Risk of unemployment**: a worker with one narrow skill may find it hard to get another job, especially if a machine replaces them.
5. **Standardised goods**: products become uniform, with less variety.

### Limits to division of labour

Division of labour cannot be used everywhere. It is limited by:

- **the size of the market**: if few goods are sold, there is no point splitting the work into many tasks (the most important limit)
- **the nature of the product**: some goods, such as a hand-carved mask, need one skilled person from start to finish
- **the amount of capital**: splitting tasks often needs machines and buildings
- **the supply of labour**: there must be enough workers to fill the separate tasks

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not write that division of labour "always reduces cost". It reduces cost only when output is large enough. A village tailor making a few shirts a week gains little from splitting the work among five people: the market is too small.
:::

## Key points

- Production is complete only when goods reach the **final consumer**.
- Three types: **primary** (extraction), **secondary** (manufacturing and construction), **tertiary** (services).
- **Division of labour** splits production into tasks; it raises output but causes monotony and interdependence.
- Its main limit is **the size of the market**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Questions ask you to **explain the types of production with examples**, to **state the advantages and disadvantages of division of labour**, and to **explain the factors that limit it**. Use local examples, and remember that commercial services such as transport and trade are part of production.
:::

## Quick check

1. Is a bus driver engaged in primary, secondary or tertiary production?
2. Give two advantages of division of labour.
3. What is the main factor that limits division of labour?

**Answers:** 1. Tertiary (a commercial service: transport). 2. For example: greater output; saving of time; use of machines; lower cost per unit. 3. The size of the market.
