Suppose Party A wins 46% of the votes nationwide, Party B 34% and Party C 20%. How many seats should each get? The answer depends on the **electoral system**: the rules that turn votes into seats. Under one system Party C might win nothing; under another it wins a fair share. Sierra Leone has switched between systems several times. This lesson explains the main electoral systems and the work of the electoral commission.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define an electoral system
- explain the simple majority (first-past-the-post), absolute majority and proportional representation systems
- state the merits and demerits of each system
- describe the electoral systems used in Sierra Leone
- explain the functions and problems of an electoral commission

## What is an electoral system?

:::callout{type=definition}
An **electoral system** is the set of rules by which **votes are cast, counted and converted into seats** or offices.
:::

## The main systems

### Simple majority (first-past-the-post)

The country is divided into **single-member constituencies**. The candidate with the **most votes** wins, even without a majority of all votes cast.

- **Merits:** simple to understand and operate; quick results; a close link between an MP and a constituency; usually produces stable single-party governments.
- **Demerits:** a candidate can win with a minority of votes; many votes are "wasted"; small parties are under-represented; seats may not reflect national vote shares.

### Absolute majority (two-round system)

A candidate must win **more than half** of the votes (or another set share). If no one does, a **run-off** is held between the leading candidates.

- **Merits:** the winner has majority support and greater legitimacy.
- **Demerits:** expensive and slow; a second round can raise tension.

### Proportional representation (PR)

Seats are shared among parties **in proportion to their votes**. Voters usually choose a **party list**, and seats go to candidates in the order of the list.

- **List PR (national):** the whole country is one constituency.
- **District block PR:** each district elects several members, shared among parties by their votes in that district.
- **Merits:** fair representation of parties; fewer wasted votes; minorities and small parties win seats; lists can include more women.
- **Demerits:** weak link between MPs and voters; party leaders control lists; may produce coalitions and instability; more complex to count.

### Other systems

- **Mixed systems** combine constituency seats with PR seats (for example, Germany).
- **Indirect election**: an electoral college chooses the officeholder (for example, the US President formally).

## Electoral systems in Sierra Leone

| Election | System |
|---|---|
| **President** | Two-round system: at least **55%** of valid votes to win in round one; otherwise a run-off between the top two |
| **Parliament, 1996** | National list PR |
| **Parliament, 2002** | District block PR |
| **Parliament, 2007, 2012, 2018** | First-past-the-post in single-member constituencies |
| **Parliament and local councillors, 2023** | District block PR |
| **Mayors and council chairpersons** | First-past-the-post |

:::callout{type=local-example}
In the **2018** presidential election no candidate reached **55%** in the first round, so a **run-off** was held on **31 March 2018** between **Julius Maada Bio** (SLPP) and **Samura Kamara** (APC). Bio won with about **51.8%** of the vote. For the **2023** elections, the move to district block PR for Parliament was controversial, and the **Public Elections Act 2022** also required parties to nominate **at least one woman for every three candidates** on their lists.
:::

## The electoral commission

**Functions of an electoral commission** (in Sierra Leone, the ECSL):

1. **Delimiting** constituencies and wards.
2. **Registering voters** and keeping the register up to date.
3. **Conducting** all public elections and referendums.
4. **Recruiting and training** electoral officials.
5. **Receiving nominations** of candidates.
6. **Providing ballot papers**, boxes and other materials.
7. **Counting votes** and **declaring results**.
8. **Voter education.**

**Problems facing electoral commissions in West Africa:**

- Lack of **independence** from the government
- Inadequate **funding** and late release of funds
- **Logistical** difficulties: poor roads, late materials
- Inaccurate **voter registers**
- **Violence** and intimidation of staff
- **Mistrust** by opposition parties

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not confuse the **ECSL** with the **PPRC**. The **Electoral Commission for Sierra Leone** runs elections; the **Political Parties Regulation Commission** registers and regulates political parties.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **First-past-the-post**: the system in which the candidate with the most votes in a constituency wins, even without a majority.
- **Proportional representation**: a system in which parties win seats in proportion to their share of the votes.
- **Two-round system**: the system for electing Sierra Leone's President: 55% of valid votes to win in the first round, otherwise a run-off between the top two.
:::

## Key points

- An **electoral system** turns votes into seats.
- **First-past-the-post**: most votes wins a single-member seat; simple but can be unrepresentative.
- **Absolute majority**: requires more than half (or a set share), with a **run-off** if needed.
- **PR**: seats in proportion to votes; fair but weakens MP–voter links.
- Sierra Leone: President by **two-round (55%)**; Parliament by **district block PR in 2023**; **2018 run-off** won by **Bio**.
- The **ECSL** conducts elections; the **PPRC** regulates parties.

## Quick check

1. In the simple majority system, how is the winner decided?
2. State two merits of proportional representation.
3. What share of valid votes must a presidential candidate in Sierra Leone obtain to avoid a run-off?
4. State three functions of the ECSL.

**Answers:**

1. The candidate with the most votes in the constituency wins, even if this is less than half of the votes.
2. Any two: fair representation of parties; fewer wasted votes; small parties and minorities win seats; easier to include women on lists.
3. At least 55%.
4. Any three: delimiting constituencies; registering voters; conducting elections and referendums; training officials; receiving nominations; providing materials; counting votes and declaring results; voter education.
