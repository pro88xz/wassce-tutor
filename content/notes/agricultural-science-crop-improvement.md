A farmer who plants an improved variety can double the yield from the same land with the same work. **Crop improvement** (plant breeding) is the science of producing better varieties: higher yielding, resistant to pests, diseases and drought, quicker to mature, or more nutritious. Sierra Leone's research centres have released improved varieties of rice, cassava and other crops, and getting good **certified seed** of them to farmers is one of the most powerful ways to raise production. This lesson explains the aims and methods of crop improvement, the steps in a breeding programme, and how seed of a new variety reaches farmers.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the aims of crop improvement
- describe introduction, selection, hybridisation, mutation breeding and genetic engineering
- explain hybrid vigour and backcrossing
- outline the steps in a plant breeding programme
- describe the classes of seed and the importance of certified seed
- name the institutions that develop improved varieties

## Aims of crop improvement

- **Higher yield** per hectare.
- **Resistance** to pests and diseases (e.g. cassava mosaic, rice blast).
- **Tolerance** of drought, flooding, acid or salty soils, and poor fertility.
- **Early maturity**, to fit the rainy season and avoid the hungry season.
- **Better quality**: taste, cooking quality, oil or protein content, longer storage.
- **Better nutrition**: for example, yellow cassava and orange-fleshed sweet potato rich in vitamin A.
- **Suitability for mechanization**: uniform height and ripening, plants that do not lodge (fall over).
- **Adaptation** to local conditions.

## Methods of crop improvement

### Introduction

Bringing in a variety from another place or country, testing it under local conditions, and releasing it if it performs well. Quick and cheap, but the variety may not suit local soils, pests or tastes, and plants must pass **quarantine** checks.

### Selection

Choosing the best plants and using their seed or cuttings for the next crop.

- **Mass selection**: many plants with the desired features are chosen, and their seed is bulked together. Simple; farmers have done this for thousands of years.
- **Pure-line selection**: the seed of **single** outstanding plants of a self-pollinated crop (rice, cowpea) is grown separately; the best line becomes a new, uniform variety.

### Hybridisation

**Crossing** two parent plants with different good characteristics, so that the offspring combine them. For example, crossing a high-yielding variety with a disease-resistant one, then selecting among the offspring over several generations for plants that have both traits.

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Hybrid vigour (heterosis)**: the offspring (F₁) of two unrelated parents is often **stronger and higher yielding** than either parent. Hybrid maize uses this, but farmers must buy **new seed every season**, because the vigour falls in the next generation.
- **Backcrossing**: crossing the offspring back to one parent to transfer one useful gene (such as disease resistance) into an otherwise good variety.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**NERICA rice.** African rice (*Oryza glaberrima*) tolerates weeds, drought and poor soils but yields little; Asian rice (*Oryza sativa*) yields more but is less hardy. Breeders crossed them and selected among the offspring for many generations, producing the **NERICA** ("New Rice for Africa") varieties, which combine better yield with tolerance of African conditions. NERICA varieties have been tested and grown in Sierra Leone.
:::

### Other methods

- **Mutation breeding**: treating seeds with radiation or chemicals to cause mutations, then selecting useful ones.
- **Polyploidy**: doubling the chromosome number, often giving larger plants and fruits (many bananas are polyploid).
- **Tissue culture**: growing many disease-free plantlets from tiny pieces of tissue; fast multiplication of new varieties.
- **Genetic engineering**: inserting a specific gene from another organism (e.g. insect resistance); fast and precise, but costly and raises safety and ownership concerns.

## Steps in a breeding programme

1. Set the **objective** (e.g. a high-yielding, blast-resistant rice).
2. **Collect** varieties (germplasm) with the needed traits.
3. **Cross** parents and grow the offspring.
4. **Select** the best plants over several generations.
5. **Test** promising lines on research stations and farmers' fields in different areas and seasons (**trials**).
6. **Release** the best as a new, named variety.
7. **Multiply** the seed and distribute it to farmers.

The whole process often takes **eight to twelve years**.

## From breeder to farmer: seed classes

![Seed classes from breeder to farmer: a new variety is bred and tested; breeder seed is multiplied to foundation seed, then to certified seed which is inspected and labelled before farmers grow it](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/seed-multiplication.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Breeder seed**: produced in small quantities by the breeder; the purest.
- **Foundation seed**: grown from breeder seed under strict control.
- **Certified seed**: grown from foundation seed by approved seed growers, **inspected** in the field and tested for **purity, germination and freedom from disease**, then labelled and sold to farmers.
:::

**Why use certified seed?** It is true to type, has high germination, is free of weed seeds and seed-borne diseases, and gives higher, more uniform yields. Saving seed from a poor or mixed crop slowly lowers yields.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Saving seed from hybrid maize.** Seed harvested from an F₁ hybrid gives a mixed, weaker crop the next season. Hybrid seed must be **bought new** each season; open-pollinated varieties can be saved if fields are kept pure.
:::

## Research institutions

- The **Sierra Leone Agricultural Research Institute (SLARI)**, with research centres including **Rokupr** (rice, especially mangrove and swamp rice) and **Njala** (roots and tubers, such as improved cassava and sweet potato), and centres for tree crops and livestock.
- **Njala University** (teaching and research in agriculture).
- International centres such as **AfricaRice** (NERICA) and the **International Institute of Tropical Agriculture** (IITA, cassava, yam and cowpea).

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Improved cassava against mosaic disease.** When cassava mosaic disease spread through West Africa, it cut yields of local varieties sharply. Research centres in the region, including Njala, tested and released **mosaic-resistant** cassava varieties and set up multiplication sites so that farmers could get **clean cuttings**. Where farmers adopted them, cassava yields recovered strongly, showing how breeding and seed systems work together.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Mass selection**: choosing many plants with the desired features and bulking their seed together.
- **Pure-line selection**: growing the seed of single outstanding plants of a self-pollinated crop separately and keeping the best line.
- **Hybrid vigour** (heterosis): the greater strength and yield of the offspring of two unrelated parents compared with either parent.
- **Mutation breeding**: treating seeds with radiation or chemicals to cause mutations, then selecting useful ones.
- **Polyploidy**: doubling the chromosome number, which often gives larger plants and fruits.
- **Lodging**: the falling over of a crop, which improved varieties are bred to resist.
:::

## Key points

- Aims: **yield, resistance, tolerance, early maturity, quality, nutrition, mechanization**.
- Methods: **introduction, selection (mass, pure-line), hybridisation, mutation, polyploidy, tissue culture, genetic engineering**.
- **Hybrid vigour** gives strong F₁ plants, but hybrid seed must be bought new each season.
- Breeding programme: objective → collect → cross → select → test → release → multiply.
- Seed classes: **breeder → foundation → certified**; certified seed is inspected and labelled.
- In Sierra Leone: **SLARI** (Rokupr, Njala and other centres), Njala University, AfricaRice, IITA.
