# International trade documentation: payment, risk and delivery terms

## Learning objectives
Explain how key trade documents support international transactions and distinguish common payment and delivery arrangements.

## Documents and their functions
- **Commercial invoice:** records the seller, buyer, goods, quantities, prices and sale terms; it supports customs valuation and payment.
- **Packing list:** describes packages, weights, dimensions and contents to help handling and inspection.
- **Bill of lading:** issued by a carrier for sea transport; it evidences receipt of goods and the contract of carriage and may function as a document of title depending on its form.
- **Air waybill:** evidence of an air-carriage contract and receipt of cargo; it is generally not a negotiable document of title.
- **Certificate of origin:** identifies the country in which goods originate for customs and trade-policy purposes.
- **Insurance certificate or policy:** evidences insurance cover for specified risks.
- **Letter of credit:** a bank's undertaking to pay the seller when stipulated documentary conditions are met.

## Payment methods
**Advance payment** gives the exporter strong payment security but exposes the importer to non-delivery risk. **Open-account trade** allows the buyer to pay later and favours the importer, but exposes the exporter to credit risk. **Documentary collection** uses banks to handle documents and payment instructions but does not provide the same payment undertaking as a letter of credit.

Under a documentary letter of credit, banks examine documents rather than the physical goods. Discrepancies between documents and credit terms can delay or prevent payment.

## Delivery terms
Incoterms published by the International Chamber of Commerce allocate specified costs, tasks and risks between seller and buyer. They do not by themselves determine ownership transfer or every aspect of payment.

For example, under FOB (Free On Board) for sea or inland-waterway transport, the seller's delivery and risk-transfer obligations are tied to placing goods on board the nominated vessel at the named port of shipment. Under CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight), the seller arranges and pays freight and specified insurance to the named destination port, while risk generally transfers when goods are on board at shipment.

## WASSCE examination focus
Do not confuse a bill of lading with an air waybill or assume every shipping document transfers ownership. Read the named place and version of the delivery rule carefully.

## Quick check
1. State three functions of a commercial invoice.
2. Distinguish a bill of lading from an air waybill.
3. What does a letter of credit provide?
4. Under CIF, who arranges freight and insurance?

**Answers:** 1. Identifies parties, describes goods and records quantities, values and terms. 2. A bill of lading may be a document of title; an air waybill generally is not. 3. A bank undertaking to pay against compliant documents. 4. The seller.
