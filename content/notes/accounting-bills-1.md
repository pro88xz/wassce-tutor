:::callout{type=definition}
A **bill of exchange** is an **unconditional order in writing**, addressed by one person (the **drawer**) to another (the **drawee**), signed by the drawer, requiring the drawee to pay a **certain sum of money** on demand or at a **fixed or determinable future time** to a named person or to bearer.
:::

In trade, a seller who gives credit draws a bill on the buyer; once the buyer **accepts** it by signing, the bill becomes a **negotiable** document that can be **held**, **discounted** with a bank for immediate cash, or **endorsed** to someone else. This lesson explains bills, their parties and features, due dates and discounting.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a bill of exchange and name its parties
- state the features and advantages of bills of exchange
- distinguish a bill of exchange from a promissory note and a cheque
- explain acceptance, endorsement, discounting, honour and dishonour
- calculate the due date and the proceeds of a discounted bill

## Parties to a bill

| Party | Role |
|---|---|
| **Drawer** | the person who **writes** (draws) the bill, usually the **creditor** (seller) |
| **Drawee** | the person on whom it is drawn, usually the **debtor** (buyer) |
| **Acceptor** | the drawee **after** signing "accepted" on the bill |
| **Payee** | the person to be paid (often the drawer) |
| **Endorser / endorsee** | the holder who signs it over to another person / the person it is signed over to |

## The life of a bill

![A bill drawn by the drawer and accepted by the drawee; the drawer may hold it to maturity, discount it with a bank or endorse it to a creditor; on the due date it is either honoured or dishonoured](https://wasscetutor.com/img/accounting/bill-of-exchange-cycle.svg)

*Figure 1. The life of a bill of exchange. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

- **Acceptance:** the drawee signs the bill, agreeing to pay on the due date.
- **Holding to maturity:** the drawer keeps the bill and presents it for payment on the due date.
- **Discounting:** the drawer sells the bill to a bank **before** it is due and receives the face value **less a discount** (interest for the time remaining).
- **Endorsement:** the drawer signs the bill over to one of **their own creditors** to settle a debt.
- **Honour:** the acceptor pays on the due date.
- **Dishonour:** the acceptor fails to pay. A **noting charge** may be paid to a notary to record the dishonour officially.
- **Retiring a bill:** the acceptor pays **before** the due date, usually getting a rebate.
- **Renewal:** the acceptor cannot pay, and a new bill is drawn for a later date, usually with interest added.

## Features and advantages

1. It is a **legal document**, so the debt is easy to prove.
2. It is **negotiable**: it can be transferred by endorsement and delivery.
3. The seller can get **cash before the due date** by discounting.
4. The buyer gets **time to pay**.
5. It fixes the **date** of payment.

| Bill of exchange | Promissory note | Cheque |
|---|---|---|
| an **order** to pay | a **promise** to pay | an order on a **bank** |
| three parties (drawer, drawee, payee) | two parties (maker, payee) | drawn on a bank |
| needs **acceptance** | no acceptance needed | payable on demand |

## Due date

The due date is found by adding the **tenor** (term) of the bill to the date of drawing or acceptance. Under the traditional rule taught for WASSCE, **three days of grace** are added to a bill payable at a future date (not to bills payable on demand).

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A three-month bill is drawn and accepted on 10 March 2026. Find the due date, adding three days of grace.**

10 March + 3 months = 10 June; + 3 days of grace = **13 June 2026**.
:::

## Discounting a bill

**Discount = face value × annual rate × (months to maturity ÷ 12)**

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A bill for NLe 10,000 due in 3 months is discounted with a bank after 1 month at 12% a year. Find the discount and the proceeds.**

Time to maturity $= 2$ months.

Discount $= 10\,000 \times 12\% \times \frac{2}{12} = 200$

Proceeds $= 10\,000 - 200 = 9\,800$

The NLe 200 is a **discounting charge**, an expense in the drawer's income statement.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The discount on a bill is calculated on the **time remaining until maturity**, not on the full term of the bill. If the bill has already run one of its three months, only two months' discount is charged.
:::

## Key points

- A **bill of exchange** is an unconditional written order to pay a fixed sum at a fixed or determinable time.
- Parties: **drawer** (creditor), **drawee/acceptor** (debtor), **payee**.
- The holder may **hold, discount or endorse** the bill; on the due date it is **honoured** or **dishonoured**.
- **Discount = face value × rate × time remaining.**

## Quick check

1. Define a bill of exchange.
2. Who is the drawer of a bill?
3. What is meant by discounting a bill?
4. A bill for NLe 6,000 with 4 months to run is discounted at 15% a year. Find the proceeds.
5. Give two differences between a bill of exchange and a promissory note.

**Answers:**

1. An unconditional order in writing, signed by the drawer, requiring the drawee to pay a certain sum on demand or at a fixed or determinable future time to a named person or bearer.
2. The person who writes the bill, usually the creditor.
3. Selling the bill to a bank before it is due, for its face value less a discount.
4. Discount $= 6\,000 \times 15\% \times \frac{4}{12} = 300$; proceeds $= 5\,700$: NLe 5,700.
5. Any two: a bill is an order to pay, a note is a promise; a bill has three parties, a note two; a bill needs acceptance, a note does not.
