**Sir Albert Margai's government (1964–1967)** was a turbulent period that ended in Sierra Leone's first **military takeover**. Albert, a lawyer and Milton's younger brother, was more **forceful and ambitious**. He pushed **development projects**, but was accused of **corruption**, **favouring the Mende**, and trying to entrench himself through a **one-party state** and a **republican constitution (1966)**, which met strong opposition. In the **March 1967 election** the **APC** won the most seats, but after **Siaka Stevens** was sworn in, **Brigadier David Lansana** declared **martial law**, and army officers formed the **National Reformation Council (NRC)**. The NRC ruled until the **Sergeants' revolt of April 1968** restored civilian rule under **Stevens**. This lesson explains **the Albert Margai government, the 1967 election crisis and the NRC**.

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain how Albert Margai became Prime Minister

## Albert Margai becomes Prime Minister

When Sir Milton Margai died in **April 1964**, the **Governor-General, Sir Henry Lightfoot Boston**, appointed **Albert Margai** as Prime Minister. The choice was **disputed**: other SLPP leaders, such as **Dr John Karefa-Smart**, had support, and some felt the party had not been properly consulted.

## Policies and achievements

1. **Development projects**: roads, bridges and public buildings; expansion of the **port** at Freetown.
2. **Education**: more secondary schools and support for **Njala University College**.
3. **Africanisation**: more Sierra Leoneans appointed to senior posts in the civil service and army.
4. **Assertive foreign policy**: closer ties with other African states and strong support for African liberation.

## Why Albert Margai became unpopular

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **One-party state** | in **1966** he proposed making the SLPP the only legal party, which the APC, students, lawyers and the press strongly opposed |
| **Republican constitution** | his plan for a **republic with an executive president** was seen as a way to keep himself in power |
| **Corruption** | accusations that ministers enriched themselves |
| **Ethnic favouritism** | critics said he favoured the **Mende** in appointments, including in the **army** |
| **Economic difficulties** | debts, falling revenue and rising prices |
| **Treatment of opponents** | the arrest of opponents and army officers accused of plotting a **coup** in early **1967**, including **Brigadier John Bangura** |

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **17 March 1967** | **general election**: the **APC** won the most seats; the SLPP and independents disputed the result |
| **21 March 1967** | the **Governor-General swore in Siaka Stevens** as Prime Minister |
| **21 March 1967** | the army commander, **Brigadier David Lansana**, a supporter of Albert Margai, declared **martial law**, saying the Governor-General had acted before all results (including paramount chief members) were known; Stevens and the Governor-General were detained |
| **23 March 1967** | other army and police officers arrested Lansana and set up the **National Reformation Council (NRC)** |
| **1967–1968** | the NRC, under **Lieutenant-Colonel Andrew Juxon-Smith**, ruled by **decree**, suspended the constitution and banned parties |

## The NRC government (1967–1968)

**Actions:**

1. Set up **commissions of inquiry** into the 1967 election (the **Dove-Edwin Commission**, which found that the APC had won) and into the finances of the previous government.
2. Tried to **cut government spending** and fight **corruption**.
3. Promised a **return to civilian rule**, but delayed it.

**Why it was overthrown:**

1. It **delayed** the promised handover to civilians.
2. **Juxon-Smith** became increasingly **authoritarian** and unpopular.
3. Ordinary soldiers resented the **privileges of officers**.
4. The **Dove-Edwin report** showed the APC had won the 1967 election.

## The Sergeants' revolt (April 1968)

In **April 1968**, **non-commissioned officers and privates**, calling themselves the **Anti-Corruption Revolutionary Movement**, arrested the NRC officers. They recalled **Brigadier John Bangura**, who formed a **National Interim Council** and handed power back to civilians. On **26 April 1968**, **Siaka Stevens** was sworn in as **Prime Minister**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Why the 1967 crisis mattered**

The crisis showed that Sierra Leone's democracy was **fragile**: the **army** had overturned the result of an election. It began a pattern of **military involvement** in politics, deepened the **SLPP–APC rivalry** along regional lines, and convinced Siaka Stevens that he needed a **strong executive presidency** and control of the army, which led to the **republic of 1971** and, later, the **one-party state**.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
The 1967 election is often remembered as the first time in **independent Africa** that a ruling party was defeated in a general election, even though the army prevented the immediate transfer of power. It is an important lesson for Sierra Leone's democracy today, where peaceful changes of government took place in **2007** and **2018**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not confuse the three military groups of 1967–1968. **Lansana** declared martial law on **21 March 1967**; the **NRC**, soon led by **Juxon-Smith**, took over on **23 March 1967**; and the **Sergeants' revolt** (Anti-Corruption Revolutionary Movement) overthrew the NRC in **April 1968**.
:::

## Key points

- **Albert Margai** became PM in **1964**, appointed by **Governor-General Lightfoot Boston**; the choice was disputed.
- Achievements: **development projects, Africanisation, education**.
- Unpopular because of the **one-party and republican proposals (1966)**, **corruption**, **ethnic favouritism**, **economic problems** and the treatment of opponents.
- **1967**: APC wins; Stevens sworn in; **Lansana's martial law (21 March)**; **NRC (23 March)** under **Juxon-Smith**.
- **April 1968**: **Sergeants' revolt**; **Stevens Prime Minister (26 April 1968)**.

## Quick check

1. Who appointed Albert Margai as Prime Minister in 1964?
2. Give three reasons why Albert Margai's government became unpopular.
3. Which party won the most seats in the 1967 election?
4. What did Brigadier David Lansana do on 21 March 1967?
5. Who led the NRC government?
6. How did civilian rule return in 1968?

**Answers:**

1. The Governor-General, Sir Henry Lightfoot Boston.
2. Any three: the one-party state proposal; the republican constitution plan; corruption; ethnic favouritism; economic difficulties; the arrest of opponents.
3. The All People's Congress (APC).
4. He declared martial law and detained Siaka Stevens and the Governor-General.
5. Lieutenant-Colonel Andrew Juxon-Smith.
6. Junior ranks of the army overthrew the NRC in the Sergeants' revolt (April 1968), and Siaka Stevens was sworn in as Prime Minister on 26 April 1968.
