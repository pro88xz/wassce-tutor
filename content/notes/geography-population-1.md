**Population distribution** is the way people are spread out over an area, and **population density** is the average number of people living in each square kilometre: total population divided by land area. Some places, such as river valleys, coasts and cities, are **densely populated**; others, such as deserts, high mountains and thick forests, are **sparsely populated**. This lesson explains why, how a population is counted in a **census**, and how to calculate density, with Sierra Leone examples.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define population, population distribution and population density
- calculate population density
- explain the physical and human factors that influence population distribution
- describe areas of dense and sparse population in the world, West Africa and Sierra Leone
- explain the terms over-population, under-population and optimum population
- explain the importance and problems of a population census

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition}
**Population** is the total number of people living in an area at a particular time. **Population distribution** describes where they live: how they are spread over the area. **Population density** is the average number of people per square kilometre:

$$\text{population density} = \frac{\text{total population}}{\text{land area (km}^2)}$$
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Sierra Leone has an area of about 71,740 km². The 2021 mid-term census counted about 7.5 million people. Find the population density.**

$$\frac{7\,500\,000}{71\,740} \approx 105 \text{ people per km}^2$$

The average hides big differences: the **Western Area**, with Freetown, is far more crowded than rural districts such as Koinadugu or Bonthe.
:::

The world's population passed **8 billion** in **November 2022**. Asia holds about 60% of it; Africa is the fastest-growing continent.

## Factors influencing population distribution

### Physical factors

| Factor | Attracts people | Repels people |
|---|---|---|
| **Climate** | moderate temperatures and reliable rainfall | very hot, very cold or very dry climates |
| **Relief** | lowlands, plains and gentle slopes | high, steep mountains |
| **Soil** | fertile soils (river valleys, deltas, volcanic soils) | poor, leached or rocky soils |
| **Water supply** | rivers, lakes, springs, groundwater | deserts with no water |
| **Vegetation** | open savanna and cleared land | dense forest, swamps |
| **Disease** | healthy areas | areas of tsetse fly (sleeping sickness) or river blindness |
| **Natural resources** | minerals, fishing grounds | no resources |

### Human factors

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Economic activities** | mining, industry, trade and commercial farming attract people (Koidu grew with diamond mining) |
| **Transport and communication** | towns grow along roads, railways and at ports |
| **Government policy** | capitals and planned settlements attract people; resettlement schemes move them |
| **Historical factors** | old trading centres and colonial headquarters remain populous |
| **Social amenities** | schools, hospitals, water and electricity attract people |
| **Security** | people move away from war and conflict, and towards safety |

## Dense and sparse areas

**Densely populated:** the river valleys and deltas of Asia (Ganges, Yangtze, Nile), western Europe, the north-eastern USA; in West Africa, south-western Nigeria, the Kano region, the coastal towns and the cocoa belt of Ghana.

**Sparsely populated:** the Sahara, the Kalahari, the Amazon and Congo forests, the high Andes and the polar lands.

:::callout{type=local-example}
In Sierra Leone, population is densest in the **Western Area** (Freetown and its surroundings), around the regional cities of **Bo, Kenema, Makeni** and **Koidu**, and in the diamond-mining areas of **Kono**. It is sparser in the north-eastern highlands and in the swampy coastal districts. Many people moved into Freetown during the civil war (1991–2002), and the capital has kept growing since.
:::

## Over-population, under-population and optimum population

- **Over-population:** too many people for the resources available, with unemployment, poverty and pressure on land and services.
- **Under-population:** too few people to make full use of the resources.
- **Optimum population:** the number that gives the **highest standard of living** with the resources and technology available.

These ideas depend on **resources and technology**, not just numbers: a densely populated rich country may not be over-populated.

## The population census

:::callout{type=definition}
A **census** is an official count of all the people in a country at a particular time, recording details such as age, sex, occupation, education and place of residence. Most countries hold one every ten years.
:::

**Importance:** planning schools, hospitals, roads and housing; sharing resources and drawing electoral boundaries; estimating the labour force; studying population growth and migration.

**Problems in West Africa:** high cost; poor roads to remote areas; illiteracy; incomplete birth records; suspicion and false information; political interference in the results; shortage of trained staff.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's **2015 census** counted about **7.1 million** people. A **mid-term census in 2021** gave about **7.5 million**, but its results were disputed by some political parties, an example of the political problems that can affect a census.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Population **density** is not the same as population **size**. A small country can have a high density and a huge country a low one. Always divide by the area.
:::

## Key points

- **Population density** $= \dfrac{\text{population}}{\text{area}}$; Sierra Leone ≈ **105 per km²**.
- Physical factors: **climate, relief, soil, water, vegetation, disease, resources**.
- Human factors: **economic activity, transport, government, history, amenities, security**.
- **Over-, under- and optimum population** depend on resources and technology.
- A **census** counts everyone; it is vital for planning but costly and difficult.

## Quick check

1. Define population density.
2. A district has 420,000 people on 6,000 km². Find its density.
3. Give two physical factors that attract people to an area.
4. What is optimum population?
5. State two problems of conducting a census in West Africa.

**Answers:**

1. The average number of people living in each square kilometre of an area.
2. $420\,000 \div 6\,000 = 70$ people per km².
3. Any two: moderate climate; flat land; fertile soil; water supply; minerals; healthy conditions.
4. The population size that gives the highest standard of living with the available resources and technology.
5. Any two: cost; poor roads; illiteracy; poor records; suspicion; political interference; lack of trained staff.
