:::callout{type=definition}
A **joint venture** is a **temporary partnership** between two or more persons (the **co-venturers**) to carry out a **specific business project**, such as buying and selling one consignment of goods, after which the venture ends and the profit is shared in an agreed ratio.
:::

Usually no separate set of books is opened: each co-venturer records **their own** part of the venture in a **joint venture account with the other party**, and a **memorandum joint venture account** is drawn up to find the **total profit**. This lesson explains joint ventures and works through the memorandum method.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a joint venture and distinguish it from a partnership
- state the features of a joint venture
- prepare a memorandum joint venture account to find the profit
- prepare each co-venturer's joint venture account and the final settlement

## Features of a joint venture

1. It is formed for a **specific project** and ends when the project is completed.
2. The co-venturers share profits and losses in an **agreed ratio** (equally if no ratio is agreed).
3. It usually has **no firm name** and no separate premises.
4. Each co-venturer may keep their **own records** of the venture.
5. It is **temporary**, unlike a partnership, which is a continuing business.

| Joint venture | Partnership |
|---|---|
| **temporary**, for one project | **continuing** business |
| usually **no firm name** | trades under a **firm name** |
| often **no separate books** | keeps a **full set of books** |
| ends when the project ends | continues until dissolved |

## The memorandum method

Each co-venturer opens a **joint venture with [other party] account** in their own books and records only **their own transactions**:

- **Debit:** goods supplied, expenses paid, share of profit.
- **Credit:** sales made, goods taken over.

A **memorandum joint venture account** (not part of the double entry) combines both parties' figures to find the **total profit**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Sesay and Bah agree to buy and sell a consignment of generators, sharing profit equally. Sesay buys the generators for NLe 20,000 and pays transport of NLe 2,000. Bah pays storage of NLe 1,000 and sells all the generators for NLe 30,000. Find the profit and the settlement.**

**Memorandum joint venture account**

| Debit | NLe |
|---|---|
| Goods bought (Sesay) | 20,000 |
| Transport (Sesay) | 2,000 |
| Storage (Bah) | 1,000 |
| **Profit: Sesay ½** | **3,500** |
| **Profit: Bah ½** | **3,500** |
| **Total** | **30,000** |

| Credit | NLe |
|---|---|
| Sales (Bah) | 30,000 |
| **Total** | **30,000** |

Profit $= 30\,000 - (20\,000 + 2\,000 + 1\,000) = 7\,000$, shared $3\,500$ each.
:::

## Settlement

**In Sesay's books: joint venture with Bah account**

| Debit | NLe | Credit | NLe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goods | 20,000 | Bank (from Bah) | 25,500 |
| Transport | 2,000 | | |
| Profit share | 3,500 | | |

Sesay is owed his costs **plus** his share of profit: $20\,000 + 2\,000 + 3\,500 = 25\,500$.

**In Bah's books: joint venture with Sesay account**

| Debit | NLe | Credit | NLe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Storage | 1,000 | Sales | 30,000 |
| Profit share | 3,500 | | |
| Bank (to Sesay) | 25,500 | | |

Bah collected the NLe 30,000, keeps $1\,000 + 3\,500 = 4\,500$, and pays Sesay **NLe 25,500**. Check: $4\,500 + 25\,500 = 30\,000$ ✓.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The **memorandum joint venture account** is **not** part of either party's double entry: it is prepared only to work out the total profit. Each party's own books contain only the **joint venture with the other party** account.
:::

## Key points

- A **joint venture** is a **temporary** arrangement for one project, with profit shared in an agreed ratio.
- Each co-venturer keeps a **joint venture with [other party]** account of their own transactions.
- A **memorandum joint venture account** finds the **total profit**.
- **Settlement:** the party holding the cash pays the other their **costs plus profit share**.

## Quick check

1. What is a joint venture?
2. Give two differences between a joint venture and a partnership.
3. In what ratio is profit shared if none is agreed?
4. What is the purpose of the memorandum joint venture account?
5. Total costs NLe 15,000, sales NLe 21,000, shared 2 : 1. Find each share of profit.

**Answers:**

1. A temporary partnership between two or more persons for a specific business project, with profit shared in an agreed ratio.
2. Any two: a joint venture is temporary, a partnership continuing; a joint venture usually has no firm name; a joint venture often keeps no separate books.
3. Equally.
4. To combine both co-venturers' figures and find the total profit or loss of the venture.
5. Profit $= 6\,000$: NLe 4,000 and NLe 2,000.
