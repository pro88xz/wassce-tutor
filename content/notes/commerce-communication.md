# Communication in commerce: channels and effective business information

## Learning objectives
Explain why communication is essential to business, compare communication channels and select a suitable channel for a business message.

## Meaning and role
Communication is the exchange of information, instructions, ideas or feedback between people and organisations. In commerce it allows buyers and sellers to ask about prices, place orders, confirm payment, arrange delivery, resolve complaints and maintain relationships. Poor communication can cause wrong orders, missed deadlines, disputes and financial loss.

## Main channels
**Oral communication** includes face-to-face conversations, meetings and telephone calls. It is quick and allows immediate questions, but important details may be forgotten unless recorded.

**Written communication** includes letters, invoices, notices, reports and contracts. It creates a record and can present detailed information, but may take longer to prepare and deliver.

**Electronic communication** includes email, websites, messaging services, video meetings and business platforms. It can be fast and reach distant customers, but depends on devices, connectivity, digital skills and suitable security.

**Postal and courier services** deliver physical documents and parcels. They remain useful where original documents or physical goods are required, but delivery may take time.

## Choosing a channel
The choice depends on urgency, complexity, confidentiality, cost, accessibility, need for a permanent record and the recipient's ability to use the channel. A quick stock query may be handled by telephone. A formal purchase order should be recorded in an agreed written format. Sensitive account information should not be sent through an insecure public channel.

Business communication should be clear, accurate, complete, courteous and timely. Messages should identify the sender, state the required action and include relevant dates, quantities, prices and reference numbers. Organisations should keep appropriate records and protect customer data.

## Digital communication and risk
Digital channels support online marketing, mobile banking, electronic orders and customer support. They also create risks such as phishing, impersonation, accidental disclosure and mistaken payments. Businesses should verify payment instructions through trusted channels, restrict access to confidential information, use strong authentication and train staff to recognise suspicious messages.

A Sierra Leone supplier may use a phone to confirm an order, send a written invoice and use an approved payment service. The call is convenient, but the invoice and receipt provide a clearer record.

## WASSCE examination focus
When asked for advantages and disadvantages, relate the point to business use. Do not treat every electronic message as secure or legally sufficient for every agreement.

1. Define communication.
2. State three uses of communication in commerce.
3. Give two advantages and two limitations of electronic communication.
4. Why should a business confirm changed bank details through a trusted channel?

Speed and wide reach; connectivity dependence, security risks and digital exclusion. 4. To reduce the risk of impersonation causing payment to be sent to the wrong account.
