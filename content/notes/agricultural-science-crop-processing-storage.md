Growing a good crop is only half the work. Between the field and the plate, grain can shatter, rot, be eaten by insects and rats, or be spoiled by poor drying, and fresh produce can bruise and decay within days. These **post-harvest losses** can take a large share of a harvest, and they fall hardest on farm families, who then have less to eat and less to sell. This lesson explains the causes of post-harvest losses, the operations that turn harvested crops into safe, valuable products, and the principles, structures and pest control of good storage.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define post-harvest losses and state their causes
- describe the post-harvest operations for grain crops
- explain why moisture content matters and calculate weight change on drying
- describe the processing of named crops and the reasons for processing
- state the conditions for safe storage and describe storage structures
- describe the control of storage pests

## Post-harvest losses

:::callout{type=definition}
**Post-harvest losses** are reductions in the **quantity or quality** of produce between harvest and consumption.
:::

![Post-harvest operations and the losses at each step: shattering and birds at harvest, spillage and breakage in threshing, chaff and stones in cleaning, mould if drying is poor, poor bags, insects and rats in storage, and damage in transport](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/postharvest-chain.svg)

**Causes:** harvesting too early or too late; rough handling; poor threshing; inadequate drying (moulds, aflatoxins, sprouting); **insects** (weevils, moths, bruchids); **rodents**; birds; heat and moisture in stores; poor packaging and transport; delays in marketing.

## Post-harvest operations for grain

1. **Harvesting** at the right maturity, in dry weather.
2. **Threshing**: separating grain from straw or pods (beating on a hard surface, treading, or a thresher).
3. **Cleaning and winnowing**: removing chaff, stones and broken grain (winnowing in the wind, sieves).
4. **Drying**: in the sun on clean tarpaulins, mats or raised platforms, or in a dryer, until the grain is hard and at a safe moisture.
5. **Grading and sorting**: removing damaged, mouldy or insect-infested grain; grading by size and quality.
6. **Packaging**: clean, strong bags; airtight bags for long storage.
7. **Storage** and **marketing**.

### Moisture content

Freshly harvested grain may contain 20–25% moisture. Grain is **safe to store** at about **12–14%** moisture (lower for long storage and for oil seeds such as groundnut). Damp grain heats up, grows moulds, sprouts and attracts insects.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A farmer harvests 1000 kg of paddy at 22% moisture and dries it to 14%. What will it weigh after drying?**

The **dry matter** does not change: $1000 \times (100 - 22)\% = 780$ kg.

After drying, this dry matter is $100 - 14 = 86\%$ of the new weight:

$$\text{new weight} = \frac{780}{0.86} \approx 907 \text{ kg}$$

About 93 kg of water has been removed. (A buyer paying the same price per kg for wet grain is paying for water.)
:::

## Processing crops

**Why process?** To make produce **safe to store** for longer, remove poisons (cassava), make it easier to transport and use, **add value** and earn more, and create employment.

| Crop | Main processing steps | Products |
|---|---|---|
| **rice** | thresh, dry, (parboil), mill, grade | milled rice; bran (feed); husk (fuel) |
| **maize** | dry, shell, clean, mill | grain, flour, animal feed |
| **cassava** | peel, grate, ferment, press, roast or dry | gari, fufu, flour, starch, chips |
| **oil palm** | sterilise, thresh, digest, press, clarify; crack nuts | palm oil, kernel oil, kernel cake |
| **cocoa** | ferment 5–7 days, dry, grade | cocoa beans |
| **groundnut** | dry, shell, (roast), press | nuts, oil, paste, cake |
| **fruits** | wash, peel, juice or dry | juice, jam, dried fruit |
| **vegetables** | sort, dry | dried pepper and okra |

The processing of cassava, cocoa and oil palm is described in the crop lessons.

## Storage

### Aims and conditions

Storage keeps produce **in good condition** until it is eaten, sold or planted, allowing sale when prices are higher and keeping seed for the next season.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Conditions for safe storage:**

- produce **dry** (safe moisture) and **clean**, sorted free of damaged and infested grain
- a store that is **dry, cool, well ventilated** (except airtight stores) and **clean**
- protection from **rain, damp floors, insects, rodents and birds**
- regular **inspection**
:::

### Storage structures

![Storage structures: a raised, roofed maize crib with metal rat guards on its legs; a hermetic bag with two airtight liners in which insects die without oxygen; and a sealed metal or concrete silo for large quantities](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/storage-structures.svg)

| Structure | Used for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **crib** | maize cobs | narrow, slatted, raised on legs with **rat guards**; air dries the cobs |
| **barn** | yam and other tubers | shaded, airy; tubers tied to frames or racks |
| **kitchen loft** (above the fire) | rice in panicles, maize, seeds | smoke and heat keep produce dry and repel insects |
| **bags** on pallets | grain | raised off the floor, away from walls |
| **hermetic (airtight) bags** | grain, beans | insects die from lack of oxygen; no chemicals needed |
| **silos** | large amounts of grain | metal or concrete; sealed |
| **warehouses** | bagged produce | for traders, cooperatives and government |
| **cold stores** | fish, meat, some fruits and vegetables | need reliable power |

### Controlling storage pests

- **Dry** the produce properly and **clean** the store before filling.
- **Sort** out damaged and infested grain.
- Use **airtight** storage.
- Traditional protectants: **neem leaves** or powder, wood **ash**, smoke (kitchen loft).
- **Rat guards**, traps and a clean surrounding area for rodents.
- Approved storage insecticides at the right dose.
- **Fumigation** of large stores, **only by trained, licensed people**, because fumigants are deadly.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Mixing old and new harvest, or storing on a bare floor.** Insects from old, infested grain spread into the new grain, and bags on a damp floor absorb moisture and go mouldy. Clean out the store completely, and stack bags on pallets away from the walls.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Rice over the kitchen fire.** In many rural homes in Sierra Leone, seed rice and some food rice are stored **in the panicle, above the kitchen fire**. The rising heat and smoke keep the rice dry and drive away insects, so the seed stays viable until planting. It is a cheap, effective use of the same principles (dryness and pest control) that modern stores rely on.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Define post-harvest losses and give four causes."}
Reductions in the quantity or quality of produce between harvest and consumption. Causes: any four of late or early harvesting, poor threshing, poor drying, insects, rodents, birds, rough handling, poor storage, poor transport.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. List the post-harvest operations for rice in order."}
Harvesting, threshing, cleaning and winnowing, drying, (parboiling), milling, grading, packaging, storage.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. 500 kg of maize at 20% moisture is dried to 12%. Find its new weight."}
Dry matter $= 500 \times 0.80 = 400$ kg. New weight $= 400 \div 0.88 \approx 455$ kg.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. State four conditions for safe storage of grain."}
Any four: grain dry and clean; store dry, cool and well ventilated; protection from rain and damp floors; protection from insects, rodents and birds; regular inspection.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. How do hermetic (airtight) bags protect stored grain?"}
They keep out air, so the oxygen inside is used up by the insects and grain; the insects **die from lack of oxygen** and new ones cannot enter, without any chemicals.
:::

## Key points

- **Post-harvest losses** reduce quantity and quality between harvest and use.
- Grain operations: **harvest → thresh → clean → dry → grade → pack → store → market**.
- Safe moisture for grain: about **12–14%**; dry matter stays constant when calculating weight loss.
- Processing makes produce **safe, storable and more valuable**.
- Storage needs **dry, clean produce** in a **dry, cool, clean, pest-proof** store; use cribs, barns, hermetic bags, silos.
- Control storage pests by **drying, cleaning, sorting, airtight storage, rat guards** and approved protectants.
