Every breath you take is a mixture of gases. This lesson looks at air: what it contains, why it is a mixture and not a compound, how to measure its oxygen content, and its role in burning. It ends with the main air pollutants and the danger of running a generator in a closed room.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the composition of air by volume
- give evidence that air is a mixture
- describe an experiment to find the percentage of oxygen in air
- explain combustion, the fire triangle, and complete and incomplete combustion
- describe methods of fighting fires
- name common air pollutants, their sources and their effects

## Composition of air

![Donut chart of dry air: about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, about 1% argon and other gases, 0.04% carbon(IV) oxide, plus variable water vapour](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/air-composition.svg)

*Figure 1. The composition of dry air by volume. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Gas | % by volume (dry air) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen | 78 | Dilutes oxygen; raw material for fertilisers |
| Oxygen | 21 | Respiration and combustion |
| Argon and other noble gases | 0.93 | Unreactive |
| Carbon(IV) oxide | 0.04 | Photosynthesis; greenhouse gas |
| Water vapour | Variable (up to about 4%) | Weather; humidity |

## Air is a mixture

| Evidence | Reason it shows a mixture |
|---|---|
| Its **composition varies** from place to place (more CO₂ in towns, more water vapour by the sea) | A compound has a fixed composition |
| Its gases can be **separated by physical means** (fractional distillation of liquid air) | Compounds need chemical means |
| **No heat change** occurs when its gases are mixed in the right proportions | Forming a compound usually involves energy change |
| Liquid air has **no fixed boiling point**: it boils over a range | A pure compound boils at a fixed temperature |
| Each gas **keeps its own properties** (oxygen still supports burning) | A compound has new properties |
| Air **cannot be given a formula** | A compound has a fixed formula |
| Oxygen and nitrogen **dissolve in water in a different ratio** from that in air | A compound would dissolve unchanged |

## Finding the percentage of oxygen in air

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Using copper and two gas syringes.** 100 cm³ of air is passed back and forth over **heated copper** in a silica tube. The copper turns black as it reacts with the oxygen: 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO. When cooled, the volume is constant at **79 cm³**.

- Volume of oxygen removed $= 100 - 79 = 21$ cm³
- Percentage of oxygen $= \dfrac{21}{100} \times 100 = 21\%$

The remaining 79 cm³ is mainly nitrogen. It does not support burning and does not turn lime water milky.
:::

**Alternative:** damp **iron wool** pushed into the top of an inverted measuring cylinder over water. As the iron rusts, it uses up the oxygen and water rises about **one fifth** of the way up the cylinder.

## Combustion

:::callout{type=definition}
**Combustion (burning)** is a chemical reaction in which a substance reacts rapidly with **oxygen**, giving out **heat and light**.
:::

The **fire triangle**: a fire needs **fuel**, **oxygen** (air) and **heat** (to reach the ignition temperature). Remove any one and the fire goes out.

| Complete combustion | Incomplete combustion |
|---|---|
| Plenty of oxygen | Limited oxygen |
| Carbon → **carbon(IV) oxide**; hydrogen → water | Carbon → **carbon(II) oxide** (poisonous) and **soot** (carbon) |
| Blue, clean, hot flame | Yellow, smoky, cooler flame |
| CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O | 2CH₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO + 4H₂O |

**Respiration** is a slow combustion inside cells: glucose + oxygen → carbon(IV) oxide + water + energy.

## Fighting fires

| Method | Which side of the triangle it removes | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Water** | Heat (cools below ignition temperature) | Wood, paper, cloth fires; **never** for oil or electrical fires |
| **Sand, fire blanket** | Oxygen (smothers) | Small chemical or cooking-oil fires |
| **Carbon(IV) oxide extinguisher** | Oxygen (dense gas blankets the fire) | Electrical and liquid fires |
| **Foam extinguisher** | Oxygen | Oil and petrol fires |
| **Dry powder** | Oxygen (and interrupts the reaction) | Most fires, including electrical |
| **Fire breaks; switching off gas** | Fuel | Bush fires; gas leaks |

## Air pollution

| Pollutant | Main sources | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbon(II) oxide, CO** | Incomplete burning in vehicles, **generators**, charcoal stoves | Combines with haemoglobin and stops it carrying oxygen; **can kill** |
| **Sulphur(IV) oxide, SO₂** | Burning fuels containing sulphur; smelting | Breathing problems; **acid rain** |
| **Oxides of nitrogen**, NOₓ | Vehicle engines | Acid rain; smog; lung irritation |
| **Smoke and particulates** | Burning refuse, firewood, charcoal, diesel | Lung and heart disease; dirty buildings |
| **Lead compounds** | Old leaded petrol, battery recycling | Brain and kidney damage, especially in children |
| **Excess carbon(IV) oxide** | Burning fossil fuels, deforestation | **Global warming** |

The **Chemistry and the Environment** topic covers acid rain and the greenhouse effect in detail.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Every year in Sierra Leone, people die from **carbon(II) oxide poisoning** after running a **petrol generator inside a closed room** or burning a charcoal stove indoors to keep warm. CO has no colour and no smell, so victims fall asleep and do not wake. Generators must always be run **outdoors**, away from windows and doors.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not use **water** on burning oil or kerosene: the water sinks, boils and spreads the burning oil. Use sand, a fire blanket, foam or a CO₂ extinguisher instead.
:::

## Key points

- Dry air: **78% N₂, 21% O₂, about 1% argon, 0.04% CO₂**, plus water vapour.
- Air is a **mixture**: variable composition, separated physically, no formula, no fixed boiling point.
- Heating copper in 100 cm³ of air leaves 79 cm³, so air is **21% oxygen**.
- **Fire triangle:** fuel, oxygen, heat. Incomplete combustion gives poisonous **CO** and soot.
- Pollutants: **CO, SO₂, NOₓ, smoke, lead, excess CO₂**; never run generators indoors.

## Quick check

1. Give two reasons why air is regarded as a mixture.
2. 80 cm³ of air passed over hot copper leaves 63.2 cm³. Find the percentage of oxygen.
3. Name the three sides of the fire triangle.
4. Why is it dangerous to run a generator in a closed room?

**Answers:**

1. Any two: variable composition; separated by physical means; no formula; no heat change on mixing; no fixed boiling point; each gas keeps its properties.
2. Oxygen = 80 − 63.2 = 16.8 cm³; % = 16.8 ÷ 80 × 100 = 21%.
3. Fuel, oxygen and heat.
4. It produces carbon(II) oxide, a colourless, odourless poisonous gas that stops blood carrying oxygen.
