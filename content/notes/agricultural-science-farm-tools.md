Most farming in Sierra Leone is still done by hand. A rice farmer in an inland valley swamp, a cassava grower in Bombali and a vegetable gardener in Waterloo all depend on the same few tools: the cutlass, the hoe, the shovel and the sickle. Choosing the right tool for each job, using it safely and keeping it in good condition save time, money and injuries. This lesson classifies farm tools by what they do, explains the use of each common tool, and describes how to maintain them and use them safely.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- classify farm tools and implements by their uses
- identify common hand tools and state the use of each
- describe the maintenance of farm tools
- state the safety precautions in using farm tools
- distinguish hand tools, animal-drawn implements and machines

## Tools, implements and machines

:::callout{type=definition}
- A **farm tool** is a simple hand-held device worked by human energy: cutlass, hoe, shovel.
- A **farm implement** is a larger device usually pulled or pushed by an animal or tractor: plough, harrow, ridger.
- A **farm machine** has its own source of power (an engine or motor): tractor, power tiller, rice thresher, maize sheller.
:::

:::callout{type=tip}
WASSCE often shows a **drawing** of a tool and asks you to **name it** and **state two uses**. Learn to recognise each tool by its shape: the long curved blade of a cutlass, the crescent of a sickle, the flat blade set at an angle on a hoe.
:::

### Measuring and other equipment

- **Measuring tape** and **ranging poles**: measuring and marking out plots.
- **Line and pegs**: marking straight rows for planting.
- **Spring balance** and **weighing scale**: weighing produce, feed and fertiliser.
- **Rain gauge** and **thermometer**: recording weather on the farm.

## Animal-drawn implements

In some parts of West Africa, oxen pull **ploughs, ridgers and carts**. This is called **animal traction**. It is cheaper than a tractor and faster than hand work, and the animals also give manure. It needs trained animals, feed and care, and suits fairly level land without many stumps.

## Maintaining farm tools

Good maintenance makes tools last longer, work better and stay safe.

:::callout{type=definition}
1. **Clean** tools after use: remove soil and plant sap, which hold moisture and cause rust.
2. **Dry** them and **oil or grease** metal parts to prevent rust.
3. **Sharpen** blades (cutlass, hoe, sickle, axe) with a file or sharpening stone.
4. **Repair or replace** loose or broken handles; tighten loose parts.
5. **Store** tools in a **dry shed**, hung up or in a rack, not left in the field.
6. **Paint** metal parts that are not cutting edges.
7. **Wash out** sprayers after use, with clean water, away from wells and streams.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Leaving tools in the field overnight.** Dew and rain rust the metal, the sun cracks wooden handles, and tools get lost or stolen. A tool that is put away clean and oiled can last for many years.
:::

## Safety in using tools

- Carry cutlasses and sickles with the **blade pointing down**, away from the body and other people.
- Keep a safe distance from other workers when swinging a cutlass, hoe or axe.
- Wear **boots** to protect the feet, and **gloves** where needed.
- Use the **right tool** for the job; never use a cutlass as a hammer.
- Keep blades sharp: a blunt tool needs more force and slips more easily.
- When spraying chemicals, wear protective clothing (gloves, mask, long sleeves, boots), do not eat, drink or smoke, and wash afterwards.
- Store chemicals and sharp tools out of reach of children.
- Give first aid for cuts promptly, and keep tetanus vaccinations up to date.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**The swamp rice farmer's kit.** A typical inland valley swamp rice farmer needs a **cutlass** to clear vegetation, a **hoe** to till the swamp and build bunds (low walls) to hold water, a **shovel** to dig drainage and irrigation channels, a **sickle** for harvesting, and a **watering can** or simple bucket for the nursery. With these few tools well maintained, a family can farm a hectare or more of swamp each season.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Distinguish between a farm tool and a farm machine."}
A farm **tool** is a simple hand-held device worked by human energy (e.g. a hoe). A farm **machine** has its own engine or motor (e.g. a tractor or rice thresher).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. State two uses each of the cutlass and the hoe."}
Cutlass: any two of clearing bush, weeding, cutting stems or cuttings, harvesting. Hoe: any two of tilling, making ridges or mounds, weeding, earthing up.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Name the tool used for (a) harvesting rice, (b) turning compost, (c) spraying herbicide."}
(a) sickle (b) garden fork (c) knapsack sprayer.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Give four ways of maintaining farm tools."}
Any four: clean after use; dry and oil metal parts; sharpen blades; repair or replace broken handles; store in a dry shed; paint non-cutting metal parts; wash sprayers after use.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Give three safety precautions when using a cutlass."}
Any three: carry with the blade pointing down; keep a safe distance from others; keep it sharp; wear boots; use it only for cutting; store it safely away from children.
:::

## Key points

- **Tools** are hand-worked; **implements** are pulled by animals or tractors; **machines** have engines.
- Cutting tools: **cutlass, sickle, axe, knife, secateurs**. Digging tools: **hoe, mattock, pickaxe, shovel, spade, fork, trowel**. Others: **rake, watering can, sprayer, wheelbarrow, dibber**.
- **Maintain** tools: clean, dry, oil, sharpen, repair, store in a dry shed.
- **Safety**: carry blades down, keep distance, wear boots, protect against chemicals.
