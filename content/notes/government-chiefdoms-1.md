In the provinces of Sierra Leone, the **Paramount Chief** is still one of the most important people in daily life. Chiefs help settle land disputes, keep the peace, organise community work, support tax collection and speak for their chiefdoms in Parliament. Their office is older than the state itself, and the 1991 Constitution guarantees it. This lesson explains how chiefdoms are governed today.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the structure of chiefdom administration
- explain how a Paramount Chief is elected and removed
- state the functions of Paramount Chiefs and the Chiefdom Council
- explain the role of Paramount Chiefs in national politics
- evaluate the strengths and problems of chieftaincy today

## Chiefdoms today

- Sierra Leone's provinces are divided into **190 chiefdoms** (from **2017**, when new chiefdoms were created; there were **149** before).
- The institution of chieftaincy is **guaranteed by the 1991 Constitution**.
- The **Chieftaincy Act 2009** sets out how chiefs are elected, recognised and removed.

## Structure of a chiefdom

![Ruling houses supply candidates; chiefdom councillors elect the Paramount Chief for life; the chief works with the Chiefdom Council and the Chiefdom Speaker; section chiefs and town and village chiefs rule smaller units](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/chiefdom-structure.svg)

| Office or body | Role |
|---|---|
| **Paramount Chief** | Head of the chiefdom; custodian of land and custom; keeps law and order |
| **Chiefdom Speaker** | Deputy to the Paramount Chief; acts for the chief when absent |
| **Chiefdom Council** | Chiefdom councillors who advise the chief, help make chiefdom bye-laws and elect a new Paramount Chief |
| **Section chiefs** | Govern sections (groups of towns and villages) |
| **Town and village chiefs** | Govern local communities |
| **Chiefdom police** | Help keep order |
| **Regent chief** | Acts as head of the chiefdom while a Paramount Chief's office is vacant |

## Election and removal of a Paramount Chief

1. When the office is vacant, a **regent chief** acts until an election is held.
2. **Candidates** must come from a **recognised ruling house** of the chiefdom, by birth or, where tradition allows, by lineage.
3. The **chiefdom councillors**, whose names are on an approved and published list, **vote** to elect the chief.
4. The election is supervised by the **Provincial Secretary** and the **Electoral Commission**.
5. The winner is **recognised by the President** and holds office **for life** (or until removed).
6. A chief may be **removed** for misconduct or incapacity after an **inquiry**, through procedures set out in the Chieftaincy Act.

## Functions of Paramount Chiefs

1. **Maintaining law and order** in the chiefdom.
2. **Custodian of land**: approving land allocations and settling land disputes according to custom.
3. **Preserving custom and tradition**.
4. **Supporting revenue collection**, such as local tax, which is shared with local councils.
5. **Mobilising communities** for development, sanitation and public health campaigns.
6. **Settling disputes** by mediation.
7. **Representing the chiefdom** to the government and visitors.
8. **Sitting in Parliament** as **Paramount Chief Members of Parliament (PCMPs)**: one for each of the **14 provincial districts**.

## Strengths and problems of chieftaincy

| Strengths | Problems |
|---|---|
| Rooted in tradition and respected by many people | **Life tenure** can lead to abuse of power |
| Closeness to the people and knowledge of local conditions | Elections limited to ruling houses and councillors, not all citizens |
| Useful in mediation, land matters and mobilisation | **Disputes and litigation** over who belongs to ruling houses |
| Helped communities during crises such as Ebola (2014–2016) | **Political interference** by governments and parties |
| Provides continuity and identity | **Conflict with elected local councils** over roles and revenue |
| | Women face barriers to becoming chiefs in some areas |

:::callout{type=local-example}
During the **Ebola** epidemic of **2014–2016**, Paramount Chiefs played a key role in many districts. They passed **chiefdom bye-laws** on safe burials, reporting of sick people and restrictions on movement, and worked with health workers to persuade communities. Their authority helped make public health measures acceptable where outside officials were distrusted.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A Paramount Chief is **not elected by all adult citizens** of the chiefdom. Under the Chieftaincy Act, the voters are the **chiefdom councillors**, and candidates must come from **recognised ruling houses**.
:::

## Key points

- **190 chiefdoms** (since 2017); chieftaincy **guaranteed by the 1991 Constitution**; governed by the **Chieftaincy Act 2009**.
- Structure: **Paramount Chief, Chiefdom Speaker, Chiefdom Council, section chiefs, town and village chiefs**.
- Chiefs are **elected for life** by **chiefdom councillors** from **ruling-house** candidates.
- Functions: **law and order, land, custom, revenue support, mobilisation, mediation, representation; 14 PCMPs in Parliament**.
- Problems: **life tenure, restricted franchise, disputes, political interference, conflict with councils**.

## Quick check

1. How many chiefdoms are there in Sierra Leone today?
2. Who elects a Paramount Chief?
3. What is the role of the Chiefdom Speaker?
4. How many Paramount Chief Members of Parliament are there?

**Answers:**

1. 190 (since 2017).
2. The chiefdom councillors, from candidates belonging to recognised ruling houses.
3. Deputy to the Paramount Chief, acting for the chief when absent.
4. Fourteen, one for each provincial district.
