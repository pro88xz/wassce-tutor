Businesses are not the only ones who organise. Consumers form associations to defend their interests, firms meet buyers at trade fairs, and all businesses are expected to behave honestly and responsibly. This lesson covers consumer associations, trade fairs and exhibitions, and the ethics and social responsibilities of business.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe consumer associations and their functions
- explain the purposes of trade fairs and exhibitions
- explain business ethics and give examples of ethical and unethical practices
- explain the social responsibilities of a business

## Consumer associations

:::callout{type=definition}
A **consumer association** is a voluntary organisation of consumers formed to **protect consumers' interests** and to educate them about their rights.
:::

**Functions:**

1. **Educating consumers** about their rights and how to make complaints.
2. **Testing and comparing products** and publishing the results.
3. **Exposing harmful practices**: adulteration, short measures, fake goods, misleading adverts.
4. **Pressing government** for consumer protection laws and their enforcement.
5. **Taking up complaints** with producers and sellers on behalf of consumers.
6. **Encouraging fair prices** and good service.

Consumer associations are covered more fully in the topic *Consumer Protection*.

## Trade fairs and exhibitions

:::callout{type=definition}
A **trade fair** is an event at which many producers and traders **display their goods** to buyers, other businesses and the public over a few days. An **exhibition** may focus on one industry or theme.
:::

**Purposes:**

1. **Showing new products** to many buyers at once.
2. **Meeting buyers and suppliers**, including foreign buyers.
3. **Comparing products and prices** with competitors.
4. **Taking orders** and making contracts.
5. **Advertising and building a brand**.
6. **Promoting exports** and attracting investment.
7. **Learning about new technology** and methods.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Trade fairs and expos in Freetown bring together local producers, importers and service firms, and help small businesses meet buyers they could never reach individually. Sierra Leone's investment and export promotion agency, SLIEPA, also takes local exporters to regional and international fairs to find new markets.
:::

## Business ethics

:::callout{type=definition}
**Business ethics** are the moral principles that guide the behaviour of businesses: honesty, fairness and respect for customers, workers, competitors, the community and the law.
:::

| Ethical practice | Unethical practice |
|---|---|
| Honest advertising and accurate labels | False or misleading claims |
| Correct weights and measures | Short measures and "fixed" scales |
| Selling safe, genuine goods | Fake, expired or adulterated goods |
| Fair prices and clear terms | Hoarding to create scarcity and raise prices |
| Paying fair wages on time | Exploiting workers; unsafe conditions |
| Paying taxes and following the law | Tax evasion, smuggling, bribery |
| Honouring contracts and guarantees | Refusing to replace faulty goods |
| Fair competition | Spreading false rumours about rivals |

**Why ethics matter to business:** customers trust and return to honest traders; a good reputation attracts investors and partners; ethical firms avoid fines and court cases; and honest dealing keeps markets working for everyone.

## Social responsibility of business

A business has responsibilities to everyone affected by it, its **stakeholders**:

| Stakeholder | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Customers** | Safe, good-quality goods at fair prices; honest information |
| **Workers** | Fair wages, safe conditions, training, fair treatment |
| **Owners and shareholders** | Honest management and a fair return |
| **Suppliers and creditors** | Paying on time; honouring agreements |
| **Government** | Paying taxes; obeying laws and regulations |
| **The community** | Creating jobs, supporting schools and clinics, avoiding nuisance |
| **The environment** | Avoiding pollution; safe disposal of waste; protecting land and rivers |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A mining company near a town.** Ethical and socially responsible behaviour would include: compensating landowners fairly; employing and training local people; restoring land after mining; keeping dust and water pollution low; paying royalties and taxes in full; and supporting community projects agreed with local people.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Social responsibility does not mean a business must give away its profits. It means earning profit **in a fair and lawful way** that does not harm customers, workers, the community or the environment.
:::

## Key points

- **Consumer associations** educate consumers, test products, expose harmful practices and press for laws.
- **Trade fairs** let firms show products, meet buyers, take orders and promote exports.
- **Business ethics** require honesty, fairness and lawfulness; unethical practices include short measures, fake goods and hoarding.
- Firms have **social responsibilities** to customers, workers, owners, government, the community and the environment.

## Quick check

1. Give two functions of a consumer association.
2. State three purposes of a trade fair.
3. Give two examples of unethical business practices.
4. What responsibility does a business have to the environment?

**Answers:** 1. Any two: educating consumers, testing products, exposing harmful practices, pressing for laws, taking up complaints, encouraging fair prices. 2. Any three: showing new products, meeting buyers and suppliers, comparing products, taking orders, advertising, promoting exports, learning about technology. 3. Any two: misleading adverts, short measures, fake or expired goods, hoarding, exploiting workers, tax evasion, bribery. 4. To avoid pollution, dispose of waste safely and protect land, water and air.
