**Sierra Leone became a British Crown Colony on 1 January 1808**, when the bankrupt Sierra Leone Company handed Freetown and the peninsula to the British government. As a Crown Colony it was ruled by a **Governor** appointed by London, and it became the **base for Britain's campaign against the slave trade**: the **Royal Navy's West Africa Squadron** landed tens of thousands of **Liberated Africans (recaptives)** at Freetown, and courts in the city tried captured slave ships. Under **Governor Charles MacCarthy (1814–1824)**, recaptives were settled in **villages** such as **Regent, Gloucester, Wellington, Hastings, Waterloo and Kent**, with churches and schools. This lesson explains **Sierra Leone as a Crown Colony** and the settlement of the **Liberated Africans**.

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

## Why Sierra Leone became a Crown Colony

1. **The Company's failure:** the Sierra Leone Company had **lost money** and could not continue.
2. **Security:** the colony had been attacked by the **French (1794)** and the **Temne (1801–1802)**, and had faced the **settler rising of 1800**.
3. **Abolition:** after the **Abolition Act of 1807**, Britain needed a **naval base** in West Africa and a place to **land and settle captives** freed from slave ships.
4. **Strategic value:** Freetown has one of the **best natural harbours** in West Africa.

:::callout{type=definition}
A **Crown Colony** is a colony ruled directly by the **British government** through a **Governor** appointed by the Crown, rather than by a company or by the settlers themselves.
:::

## Government of the Crown Colony

| Institution | Role |
|---|---|
| **Governor** | appointed by London; head of the colony; the first was **Thomas Perronet Thompson (1808–1810)** |
| **Governor's Council** | advised the Governor |
| **Courts** | a **Vice-Admiralty Court** (from 1808) and, from **1819**, **Courts of Mixed Commission** with Spain, Portugal and the Netherlands, to try captured slave ships |
| **Executive and Legislative Councils** | created in **1863**; the Legislative Council made laws and later included some African members |

## Freetown and the end of the slave trade

1. The Royal Navy's **West Africa Squadron** used Freetown as its **base** from **1808** to patrol the coast.
2. Captured slave ships were brought to Freetown and **tried** in its courts.
3. People freed from the ships, called **Liberated Africans** or **recaptives**, were landed at **King's Yard** in Freetown.
4. Between **1808 and the 1860s**, **tens of thousands** were landed. They came from many parts of West and West-Central Africa, especially **Yoruba** country, **Igbo** country, the **Congo-Angola** region and the Upper Guinea coast.

## The settlement of the Liberated Africans

**Problems:** the recaptives arrived **sick, starved and traumatised**, spoke **many different languages**, and had lost their homes and families. At first some were **"apprenticed"** to settlers as labourers, which critics said was close to slavery; Governor Thompson opposed this and was recalled.

**Governor Charles MacCarthy (1814–1824)** organised a system to settle them:

1. **Villages:** recaptives were settled in villages on the peninsula, named after British places and people, such as **Regent, Gloucester, Leicester, Wilberforce, Bathurst, Charlotte, Kissy, Wellington, Hastings, Waterloo, York and Kent**.
2. **Parishes and missionaries:** each village was a **parish** run by a **Church Missionary Society (CMS)** missionary or superintendent, with a **church and school**.
3. **Food and tools:** the government provided rations, tools and clothing until settlers could support themselves.
4. **Buildings:** MacCarthy built churches, schools and public buildings in Freetown and the villages.
5. **Wider authority:** from **1821** the Governor of Sierra Leone also governed the British forts on the **Gold Coast** and in **The Gambia**. MacCarthy was killed in **1824** fighting the **Asante** on the Gold Coast.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Why MacCarthy's village system mattered**

By giving each group of recaptives a **village, land, a church and a school**, MacCarthy turned thousands of uprooted people from many nations into **farmers, artisans, traders and pupils**. The schools taught **English and Christianity**, which became a common bond. Within one or two generations, the recaptives' children were teachers, clergy, clerks and merchants, and they joined the settlers to form the **Krio** people.
:::

## Expansion of the Colony

The Colony grew beyond the peninsula during the nineteenth century, mainly through **treaties** with local rulers:

1. The **Isles de Los** (1818; later given to France in 1904).
2. **British Koya** and the **Sherbro**, including **Sherbro Island and Bonthe**, in the **1860s**.
3. Other coastal strips, to control **customs revenue** and stop the slave trade.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many of the villages founded for recaptives, such as **Regent, Gloucester, Hastings, Waterloo and Kent**, are now part of Freetown and the **Western Area**. Their old stone churches, such as **St Charles Church in Regent** (built in the time of MacCarthy), are among the oldest buildings in Sierra Leone.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The Liberated Africans did **not** all come from Sierra Leone. Most came from **other parts of Africa**, especially **Yoruba and Igbo** country and the **Congo-Angola** region, because they were freed from slave ships that had loaded captives all along the coast.
:::

## Key figures and terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figures and terms"}
- **Governor Charles MacCarthy**: the governor (1814–1824) who settled Liberated Africans in villages and parishes with churches and schools.
- **Mixed Commission Courts**: the international courts set up in Freetown in 1819 to try captured slave ships.
:::

## Key points

- **1 January 1808:** Sierra Leone became a **Crown Colony** because of the Company's **failure**, **insecurity**, and Britain's need for an **anti-slavery base**.
- First Governor **Thomas Perronet Thompson**; **Vice-Admiralty Court** and **Mixed Commission Courts (1819)**; Executive and Legislative Councils **1863**.
- **Liberated Africans** (recaptives) landed by the **West Africa Squadron**, from 1808 to the 1860s.
- **Governor MacCarthy (1814–1824):** **villages, parishes, CMS churches and schools**.
- The Colony expanded by treaty to **Isles de Los, British Koya and the Sherbro**.
