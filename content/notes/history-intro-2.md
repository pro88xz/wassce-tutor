:::callout{type=definition}
**Chronology** is the arrangement of events in the **order in which they happened**, and it is the backbone of history.
:::

Historians date events using systems such as **BC and AD** (or **BCE and CE**), group years into **decades, centuries and millennia**, and divide the past into **periods** such as the **Stone Age**, the **Iron Age**, the **pre-colonial**, **colonial** and **post-colonial** eras. This lesson explains how historical time is measured, how to work out centuries, the main periods of West African history, and the historian's methods of **cause and effect**, **continuity and change**, and **bias**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain BC/AD and BCE/CE dating
- calculate the century in which a year falls
- describe the main periods of West African history
- explain the Stone Age, the Iron Age and the Nok culture
- explain how historians use cause and effect, continuity and change, and evidence

## Dating systems

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **BC** (before Christ) / **BCE** (before the Common Era) | years **before** year 1; counted **backwards**, so 500 BC is earlier than 200 BC |
| **AD** (anno Domini, "in the year of the Lord") / **CE** (Common Era) | years **from** year 1 onwards |
| **c.** (circa) | "about", used when a date is approximate, for example c. 1235 |
| **Decade** | 10 years |
| **Century** | 100 years |
| **Millennium** | 1,000 years |

The Islamic calendar counts years from the **Hijra** of the Prophet Muhammad to Madinah in AD 622, and is used for many West African Arabic records.

## Working out centuries

The **first century AD** is years 1 to 100, so the **century number is one more than the "hundreds" digits** (except for years ending in 00).

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**In which centuries did these events happen?**

- **1482**, the building of Elmina Castle: 1482 lies between 1401 and 1500, so the **15th century**.
- **1787**, the Province of Freedom in Sierra Leone: **18th century**.
- **1961**, Sierra Leone's independence: **20th century**.
- **1800**: the last year of the **18th century** (the 19th century began in 1801).
- **300 BC**: the **3rd century BC**.
:::

## Periods of West African history

| Period | Approximate time | Main features |
|---|---|---|
| **Stone Age** | until about 2,500 years ago | hunting, gathering, stone tools; later farming and pottery |
| **Iron Age** | from about 2,500 years ago | iron tools and weapons; more farming, larger settlements |
| **Era of states and empires** | about AD 300 to 1600 | Ghana, Mali, Songhai, Kanem-Bornu, Hausa states; trans-Saharan trade; spread of Islam |
| **Era of European contact and the Atlantic trade** | 15th to 19th centuries | coastal forts; Atlantic slave trade; forest states such as Asante, Dahomey, Oyo, Benin |
| **Colonial period** | late 19th century to about 1960 | European partition and rule |
| **Post-colonial (independence) period** | from about 1957 | independent states, nation-building, regional cooperation |

:::callout{type=local-example}
The **Nok culture** of central Nigeria, which flourished more than 2,000 years ago, made striking **terracotta sculptures** and was among the early iron-working societies of West Africa. Iron tools allowed people to clear forest, farm more land and build larger communities, laying the foundation for later states.
:::

## How historians think

| Idea | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Cause and effect** | events have causes and lead to results | the demand for labour in the Americas caused the Atlantic slave trade, which depopulated parts of West Africa |
| **Continuity and change** | some things stay the same while others change | chieftaincy continued under colonial rule, but chiefs' powers changed |
| **Evidence** | conclusions must be supported by sources | Kumbi Saleh's excavated ruins support al-Bakri's description of ancient Ghana |
| **Bias** | a one-sided view in a source | some colonial records describe African rulers unfairly |
| **Interpretation** | historians may explain the same event differently | debates over why the Atlantic slave trade was abolished |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The year **1900** belongs to the **19th century**, not the 20th, because centuries run from year 1 to year 100 (and from 1801 to 1900). The 20th century is 1901 to 2000.
:::

## Key figures and terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figures and terms"}
- **Circa** (c.): "about", written before a date that is approximate, as in c. 1235.
- **Century**: a period of 100 years; the 15th century runs from 1401 to 1500, so 1482 falls in it.
- **Cause and effect**: the historian's idea that every event has causes and leads to results, which must be explained.
- **Continuity and change**: the study of what stays the same and what changes over a period of history.
:::

## Key points

- **BC/BCE** counts back from year 1; **AD/CE** counts forward; **c.** means approximately.
- The century is **one more than the hundreds digits** (1482 → 15th century), except years ending in 00.
- Periods: **Stone Age, Iron Age, states and empires, European contact, colonial, post-colonial**.
- Historians study **cause and effect, continuity and change, evidence and bias**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect objective questions on **dating and centuries**, and essay questions asking you to **explain causes and effects** of events. Training yourself to give **several causes** and **several effects**, each explained, is the key to History essays.
:::

## Quick check

1. In which century is the year 1591?
2. Which is earlier, 400 BC or 100 BC?
3. What does "c. 1235" mean?
4. Name one feature of the Iron Age in West Africa.
5. What is meant by bias in a historical source?

**Answers:**

1. The 16th century.
2. 400 BC.
3. About the year 1235 (the exact date is not certain).
4. Any one: iron tools and weapons; more farming; larger settlements; the Nok culture.
5. A one-sided view that favours or opposes a person, group or idea, so the source does not give a fair picture.
