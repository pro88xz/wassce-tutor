From 1978 to 1991, Sierra Leone had only one lawful political party: the **All People's Congress (APC)**. Elections still took place, but voters could choose only between APC candidates, and the party chose the only presidential candidate. This lesson explains why the one-party state was created, how the **1978 Constitution** worked, and what it meant for the country.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a one-party state
- explain the reasons given for the one-party state in Sierra Leone
- state the features of the 1978 Constitution
- state the merits and demerits of the one-party system
- explain how the one-party state ended

## What is a one-party state?

:::callout{type=definition}
A **one-party state** is one in which only one political party is allowed to exist and contest elections. It is **de jure** when the law or constitution makes it so, and **de facto** when other parties exist legally but cannot compete effectively.
:::

Sierra Leone under the 1978 Constitution was a **de jure one-party state**.

## Reasons given for the one-party state

The APC government argued that a one-party state would:

1. **End political violence** between parties, which had marked the elections of 1973 and 1977.
2. **Promote national unity** by removing parties that divided people along ethnic and regional lines.
3. **Save money** spent on competitive elections.
4. **Allow faster development**, with everyone working together.
5. **Fit African traditions** of consensus under a single leader.
6. **Follow the example** of other African states such as Tanzania and Guinea.

Critics saw a different reason: the wish of the ruling party and President Stevens to **remove all opposition** and stay in power.

## The 1978 Constitution

![The APC as the only lawful party; the President as its single candidate with a seven-year term, assisted by First and Second Vice-Presidents; a Parliament of APC members only](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/one-party-1978.svg)

*Figure 1. The one-party state, 1978 to 1991. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

The constitution was approved in a **referendum in 1978**, with an officially reported vote of about **97% in favour**, a result widely regarded as heavily manipulated.

**Features:**

1. The **APC** was the **only lawful political party**.
2. The **President** was nominated as the **single candidate** of the party and served a **seven-year term**.
3. A **First Vice-President** and a **Second Vice-President** assisted the President; the office of Prime Minister was abolished.
4. **Members of Parliament** had to be **APC members**; several APC candidates could compete for one seat, so voters had some choice of individuals.
5. **Paramount Chief members** remained in Parliament.
6. The party's organs, such as its **Central Committee**, had great influence over government.
7. Fundamental rights remained in the text, but **freedom of association** was limited by the ban on other parties.

## Merits and demerits

| Claimed merits | Demerits |
|---|---|
| Reduced inter-party violence at elections | **No real choice** of government or president |
| Encouraged a sense of national unity | **No opposition** to check the government |
| Lower cost of politics | **Concentration of power** and personal rule |
| Allowed some choice between candidates in constituencies | **Abuse of human rights**; critics silenced |
| | **Corruption** and economic mismanagement went unchecked |
| | **Rigged** elections and apathy |

## The end of the one-party state

- **1985**: Siaka Stevens retired and handed over to **Major-General Joseph Saidu Momoh**, the army commander, who was elected as the single candidate.
- The **economy declined** sharply in the 1980s: shortages, inflation and unpaid salaries.
- From **1989–1990**, the end of the Cold War and pressure from citizens and donors pushed many African states back to multi-party politics.
- In **1990**, President Momoh set up a **Constitutional Review Commission** under **Dr Peter Tucker**.
- In **1991**, a new constitution restoring **multi-party democracy** was approved by referendum.

:::callout{type=local-example}
The **First Vice-President** under the one-party constitution was **S. I. Koroma**, and the **Second Vice-President** was **C. A. Kamara-Taylor**. Both were long-serving APC leaders. The arrangement shows how the APC's senior leadership and the state had become almost the same thing during the one-party period.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Elections did not stop under the one-party state. General elections were held in **1982** and **1986**, but **only APC candidates** could stand, and there was only **one presidential candidate**.
:::

## Key points

- **1978 Constitution**: Sierra Leone a **de jure one-party state** under the **APC**, approved by a **referendum** (officially about 97% yes).
- President: **single party candidate, seven-year term**, with **First and Second Vice-Presidents**; Prime Minister abolished.
- **Only APC members** in Parliament; several APC candidates per seat allowed.
- Demerits: **no choice, no opposition, personal rule, rights abuses, corruption**.
- **Momoh** succeeded Stevens in **1985**; the **Tucker Commission (1990)** led to the **1991 Constitution**.

1. Which party was the only lawful party under the 1978 Constitution?
2. How long was the President's term under the 1978 Constitution?
3. State two reasons the government gave for the one-party state.
4. Who succeeded Siaka Stevens as President in 1985?

**Answers:**

1. The All People's Congress (APC).
2. Seven years.
3. Any two: to end political violence; to promote national unity; to save money; to speed up development; to fit African traditions of consensus; to follow other African states.
