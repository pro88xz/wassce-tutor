Why does sodium react violently with water while neon reacts with nothing? The answer lies in how each atom's electrons are arranged. This lesson builds the picture step by step: from simple shells to the sub-shells and orbitals that WASSCE expects you to use.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe electron shells and state the maximum number of electrons in each
- write the shell arrangements of the first 20 elements
- name the s, p, d and f sub-shells and the number of orbitals and electrons in each
- describe the shapes of s and p orbitals
- state the order in which sub-shells are filled

## Electron shells

Bohr pictured electrons moving in fixed **energy levels (shells)** around the nucleus, labelled K, L, M, N or $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$. Shells nearer the nucleus have **lower energy** and fill first.

**Maximum number of electrons in a shell** $= 2n^2$:

| Shell | n | Maximum electrons |
|---|---|---|
| K | 1 | $2 \times 1^2 = 2$ |
| L | 2 | $2 \times 2^2 = 8$ |
| M | 3 | $2 \times 3^2 = 18$ |
| N | 4 | $2 \times 4^2 = 32$ |

For the first 20 elements, fill the shells 2, 8, 8, 2.

| Elements 1 to 10 | Elements 11 to 20 |
|---|---|
| H (1): 1 | Na (11): 2, 8, 1 |
| He (2): 2 | Mg (12): 2, 8, 2 |
| Li (3): 2, 1 | Al (13): 2, 8, 3 |
| Be (4): 2, 2 | Si (14): 2, 8, 4 |
| B (5): 2, 3 | P (15): 2, 8, 5 |
| C (6): 2, 4 | S (16): 2, 8, 6 |
| N (7): 2, 5 | Cl (17): 2, 8, 7 |
| O (8): 2, 6 | Ar (18): 2, 8, 8 |
| F (9): 2, 7 | K (19): 2, 8, 8, 1 |
| Ne (10): 2, 8 | Ca (20): 2, 8, 8, 2 |

The electrons in the outermost shell are the **valence electrons**. They decide how an atom reacts. Atoms with a full outer shell (He, Ne, Ar) are very **unreactive**.

## Sub-shells and orbitals

Each shell is divided into **sub-shells**, labelled **s, p, d and f**. Each sub-shell contains **orbitals**.

:::callout{type=definition}
An **orbital** is a region of space around the nucleus where there is a **high probability of finding an electron**. Each orbital holds a **maximum of two electrons**.
:::

| Sub-shell | Number of orbitals | Maximum electrons |
|---|---|---|
| s | 1 | 2 |
| p | 3 | 6 |
| d | 5 | 10 |
| f | 7 | 14 |

| Shell | Sub-shells present | Electrons |
|---|---|---|
| n = 1 | 1s | 2 |
| n = 2 | 2s, 2p | 2 + 6 = 8 |
| n = 3 | 3s, 3p, 3d | 2 + 6 + 10 = 18 |
| n = 4 | 4s, 4p, 4d, 4f | 2 + 6 + 10 + 14 = 32 |

### Shapes of orbitals

![An s orbital is a sphere; the three p orbitals are dumbbell shapes along the x, y and z axes at right angles to each other](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/orbital-shapes.svg)

*Figure 1. The shapes of s and p orbitals. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

- An **s orbital** is **spherical**.
- The three **p orbitals** (pₓ, p_y, p_z) are **dumbbell-shaped**, at right angles to each other.
- d orbitals have more complex shapes; you need not draw them.

### Quantum numbers (in brief)

Each electron is described by four quantum numbers:

| Quantum number | Symbol | Describes |
|---|---|---|
| Principal | n | The main shell (1, 2, 3 …) and its energy |
| Azimuthal (subsidiary) | l | The sub-shell: l = 0 (s), 1 (p), 2 (d), 3 (f) |
| Magnetic | mₗ | The orientation of the orbital (e.g. pₓ, p_y, p_z) |
| Spin | mₛ | The direction of spin: +½ or −½ |

## The order of filling: the Aufbau principle

![Energy-level diagram showing sub-shells in order of increasing energy: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p; the 4s sub-shell lies below 3d](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/energy-levels.svg)

*Figure 2. Sub-shells fill in order of increasing energy. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=definition}
**Aufbau ("building-up") principle:** electrons occupy the **lowest-energy** orbitals available first.
:::

Order of filling: **1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, 5s, 4d …**

The **4s** sub-shell has **lower energy than 3d**, so it fills first. That is why potassium is 2, 8, 8, 1 and not 2, 8, 9.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Writing configurations with sub-shells.**

- Na (11): $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^1$
- Cl (17): $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\,3p^5$
- K (19): $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\,3p^6\,4s^1$

Check by adding the superscripts: for Cl, 2 + 2 + 6 + 2 + 5 = 17.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The superscript tells you the **number of electrons**, not the number of orbitals. 2p⁵ means five electrons in the 2p sub-shell, spread over its **three** orbitals.
:::

## Key points

- Shell n holds at most **2n²** electrons; the first 20 elements fill as **2, 8, 8, 2**.
- **Valence electrons** in the outer shell decide chemical behaviour.
- Sub-shells **s, p, d, f** have **1, 3, 5, 7 orbitals** holding **2, 6, 10, 14** electrons; each orbital holds **two** electrons.
- **s orbitals are spherical; p orbitals are dumbbell-shaped**.
- **Aufbau order:** 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p **4s 3d** 4p.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **write electron arrangements** of elements 1 to 20 in shells and in s, p, d notation, to **state the maximum electrons** in a shell or sub-shell, to **sketch s and p orbitals**, and to **explain why 4s fills before 3d**.
:::

## Quick check

1. What is the maximum number of electrons in the M shell?
2. Write the electron arrangement of aluminium (Z = 13) in shells and in sub-shells.
3. How many orbitals are in a d sub-shell?
4. Why does the 19th electron of potassium go into 4s, not 3d?

**Answers:**

1. $2 \times 3^2 = 18$.
2. Shells 2, 8, 3; sub-shells $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\,3p^1$.
3. Five.
4. The 4s sub-shell is lower in energy than 3d, so it fills first (Aufbau principle).
