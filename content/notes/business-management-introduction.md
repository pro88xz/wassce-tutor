Two women open provision shops on the same street in Bo. A year later one shop is always stocked, its accounts balance and it has a second branch; the other is often empty and owes money to suppliers. They sell the same goods to the same customers. The difference is **management**: how each owner plans, organises, leads and checks the work of her business. Business management is the study of how that is done well.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define business and business management
- describe the four functions of management: planning, organising, leading and controlling
- explain the scope of business management
- explain why business management is important to a business, its owners and the country

## What is business management?

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Business**: any organised activity that produces or distributes goods and services in order to make a profit.
- **Management**: the process of getting things done through people and other resources by planning, organising, leading and controlling.
- **Business management**: the planning, organising, leading and controlling of a business's resources (people, money, materials and machines) to achieve its objectives.
:::

A **manager** is anyone responsible for directing the work of others towards the goals of the business. In a small shop the owner is also the manager; in a large company such as a bank or a mining firm, there are many managers at different levels.

## The four functions of management

| Function | What the manager does | Example in a bakery in Makeni |
|---|---|---|
| **Planning** | Sets objectives and decides in advance how to reach them | Decides to bake 2,000 loaves a day and buy flour in bulk each month |
| **Organising** | Arranges people, tasks and resources so the plan can be carried out | Gives each worker a shift and a task: mixing, baking, packing, delivery |
| **Leading (directing)** | Guides, motivates and communicates with workers | Explains the daily target and rewards the best team |
| **Controlling** | Compares results with the plan and corrects any shortfall | Counts loaves sold and flour used each evening and finds the cause of waste |

The four functions form a **cycle**: what the manager learns when controlling feeds into the next round of planning.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not list "making profit" as a function of management. Profit is an **objective** of business; planning, organising, leading and controlling are the **functions** (activities) managers perform to reach it.
:::

## The scope of business management

The **scope** of business management means the range of areas it covers. They match the main departments of a business:

1. **Production (operations) management**: making goods or providing services efficiently and to the right quality.
2. **Marketing management**: finding out what customers want, pricing, promotion and distribution.
3. **Financial management**: raising money, budgeting, keeping accounts and controlling costs.
4. **Human resource management**: recruiting, training, paying and motivating workers.
5. **Purchasing and stores management**: buying materials at the right price and keeping stock safely.
6. **Office and information management**: records, communication and the use of computers.

## Why business management is important

### To the business

- Resources are used **efficiently**, so costs fall and profits rise.
- Clear goals and plans help the business **survive** competition and grow.
- Problems are spotted early and corrected through control.

### To workers and owners

- Workers know their duties, are trained and are rewarded fairly.
- Owners' money is protected and earns a return.

### To the nation

- Well-managed businesses create **jobs**, pay **taxes** and supply goods and services.
- They attract investment and support economic development.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Spotting the management function.** Name the function in each case.

1. A shop owner writes down that she will open a second branch within two years. **Planning**
2. A school canteen manager draws up a duty roster for her six cooks. **Organising**
3. A supervisor praises a worker who served 100 customers in a day. **Leading**
4. A manager compares the month's sales with the target and finds sales were 20% short. **Controlling**
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many of Sierra Leone's small businesses, from market stalls to tailoring shops, are run by their owners without written plans or records. Simple management habits, such as a daily cash book, a monthly stock count and a written target, are often what separate the businesses that grow from those that close.
:::

## Key points

- **Business management** is planning, organising, leading and controlling a business's resources to achieve its objectives.
- The four **functions of management** form a cycle; controlling feeds back into planning.
- The **scope** covers production, marketing, finance, human resources, purchasing and office management.
- Good management makes businesses efficient and profitable, protects workers and owners, and helps the nation through jobs and taxes.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **define business management**, to **explain the functions of management** with examples, and to **state the importance of business management**. Use the words planning, organising, leading (directing) and controlling, and give a short example for each to earn the full marks.
:::
