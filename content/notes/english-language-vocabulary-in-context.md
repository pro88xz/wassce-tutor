No student knows every word in a WASSCE passage, and nobody expects you to. What examiners reward is the ability to work out what an unfamiliar word means **from the words around it**. Good readers do this constantly, almost without noticing. This lesson turns it into a deliberate skill: six kinds of clue that reveal a word's meaning, a step-by-step method, and practice with the kinds of sentences used in the examination.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- use six kinds of context clue to work out the meaning of an unfamiliar word
- check a guessed meaning by substituting it in the sentence
- combine context clues with word formation
- answer vocabulary-in-context questions in the comprehension and objective papers

## Why context matters

:::callout{type=definition}
The **context** of a word is the sentence and passage around it. **Context clues** are hints in that text (a definition, an example, a contrast, a cause) that point to the word's meaning.
:::

Even a word you **do** know may have a different meaning in a new context. *"The company will **launch** a new product"* has nothing to do with boats. Context tells you which meaning is meant.

## Six kinds of context clue

### 1. Definition

The sentence explains the word directly, often after *is*, *means*, *that is*, *or*, or between commas or dashes.

> *The farmer practised **crop rotation**, that is, growing different crops on the same land in different seasons.*

### 2. Synonym (restatement)

A word with a similar meaning appears nearby.

> *The child was **timid**; she was too shy to speak even to her teacher.*

### 3. Antonym (contrast)

A word or phrase of opposite meaning appears, often after *but*, *however*, *unlike*, *whereas*, *instead of*.

> *Unlike his **garrulous** brother, who talks all day, Sahr hardly says a word.*
> → *garrulous* = very talkative.

### 4. Example

Examples point to the meaning, often after *such as*, *for example*, *including*.

> *Many **perishable** goods, such as fish, tomatoes and fresh milk, spoil quickly in the heat.*
> → *perishable* = likely to go bad quickly.

### 5. Cause and effect

The word is the cause or the result of something explained in the sentence.

> *Because the road was **impassable** after the floods, no vehicle could reach the village for a week.*
> → *impassable* = impossible to travel along.

### 6. General sense

The overall situation makes the meaning clear.

> *After walking twelve miles in the sun without water, the travellers were **parched**.*
> → *parched* = extremely thirsty.

:::callout{type=tip}
**Signal words** point to the clue type: *that is, or, means* (definition); *also, similarly* (synonym); *but, however, unlike, although* (contrast); *such as, for example* (example); *because, so, therefore* (cause and effect).
:::

## A step-by-step method

1. **Read the whole sentence**, and the sentences around it.
2. **Identify the clue type**: is there a definition, a synonym, a contrast, an example, a cause or effect?
3. **Decide the word class**: is it a noun, verb, adjective or adverb? (Its position tells you.)
4. **Use word parts**: does it have a prefix or suffix you know? (*im-pass-able*: not able to be passed.)
5. **Guess a meaning** in your own words.
6. **Substitute** your meaning into the sentence. If the sentence still makes sense, you are probably right.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
*"The villagers were **apprehensive** about the new dam; they feared it might flood their farms."*

1. Whole sentence read.
2. Clue: **cause and effect / restatement**: *"they feared…"* explains their feeling.
3. Word class: an **adjective** describing *villagers*.
4. Word parts: not much help here.
5. Guess: **worried, anxious**.
6. Substitute: *"The villagers were **worried** about the new dam…"*: it makes sense.

**Meaning: anxious, fearful about what might happen.**
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Guessing from the sound of the word.** *"Apprehensive"* sounds a little like *"apprehend"* (to arrest), but the villagers are not being arrested. Always let the **context**, not the sound, decide.
:::

## When the word has several meanings

Context also tells you **which** meaning of a familiar word is intended:

| Sentence | Meaning of the word in bold |
|---|---|
| *The teacher **set** a difficult test.* | gave, prepared |
| *The sun **set** behind the hills.* | went down |
| *She bought a new tea **set**.* | a group of things that belong together |
| *The **current** in the river was strong.* | flow of water |
| *Read the **current** issue of the newspaper.* | the latest, present |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Passages about Sierra Leone often use words in specialised senses. *"The **harvest** was **poor**"* (small, not good), *"The council **levied** a market tax"* (imposed, charged), *"The chiefdom **rallied** behind its candidate"* (came together in support). Read for the meaning the situation needs, not the first meaning you learnt.
:::

## Practice

For each word in bold, name the clue type and give the meaning.

:::reveal{title="1. 'The medicine had an immediate effect, unlike the herbs, which took weeks to work.' What does 'immediate' mean?"}
**Contrast clue** (*unlike… took weeks*). **Immediate** = happening at once, without delay.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. 'Musa is very frugal; he never wastes money and always looks for the cheapest option.' What does 'frugal' mean?"}
**Restatement clue** (*never wastes money*). **Frugal** = careful with money, thrifty.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. 'Torrential rain fell all night, and by morning the streets were rivers.' What does 'torrential' mean?"}
**Cause and effect** (the streets became rivers). **Torrential** = extremely heavy (of rain).
:::

:::reveal{title="4. 'Many pests, such as weevils, locusts and rats, destroy stored grain.' What are 'pests'?"}
**Example clue**. **Pests** = insects or animals that damage crops or food.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. 'The witness's account was so ambiguous that the police could not tell what had really happened.' What does 'ambiguous' mean?"}
**Effect clue** (the police could not tell). **Ambiguous** = unclear, able to be understood in more than one way.
:::

:::reveal{title="6. Which meaning of 'charge' is used in: 'The police will charge the suspect with theft'?"}
To **formally accuse** someone of a crime (not *to ask a price* or *to fill a battery*).
:::

## Key points

- **Context clues**: definition, synonym, contrast, example, cause and effect, general sense.
- **Signal words** show which kind of clue is present.
- Method: read the whole sentence, find the clue, decide the word class, use word parts, guess, **substitute to check**.
- Context also tells you **which meaning** of a familiar word is intended.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Vocabulary-in-context skills are tested in the **comprehension** section (word-replacement questions) and in the **objective** paper (nearest and opposite in meaning). Examiners deliberately choose words students may not know, so the **context**, not memory, is what earns the mark.
:::
