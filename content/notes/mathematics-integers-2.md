A freezer is at $-18\,°\text{C}$ and the room is at $27\,°\text{C}$. A mobile money account is NLe 250 overdrawn and someone deposits NLe 600. Questions like these need confident arithmetic with **negative numbers and fractions**. This lesson covers the four operations on integers and rational numbers, the sign rules and the order of operations.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- add, subtract, multiply and divide integers using the sign rules
- apply the order of operations (BODMAS) to expressions with negative numbers
- perform the four operations on rational numbers
- find a rational number between two given rational numbers
- solve word problems involving negative numbers

## Adding and subtracting integers

![A number line from −6 to 6 showing −3 + 4 = 1 as a jump four steps to the right](https://wasscetutor.com/img/mathematics/integer-number-line.svg)

*Figure 1. Adding a positive number moves right; subtracting moves left. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

- Adding a positive number: move **right**. Adding a negative number: move **left**.
- **Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive:** $a - (-b) = a + b$.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Evaluate (a) $-7 + 12$ (b) $-8 - (-3)$ (c) $5 - 11$ (d) $-4 + (-9)$.**

**(a)** $5$ **(b)** $-8 + 3 = -5$ **(c)** $-6$ **(d)** $-13$
:::

## Multiplying and dividing integers

| Signs | Result |
|---|---|
| positive × positive, or negative × negative | positive |
| positive × negative, or negative × positive | negative |

The same rules apply to division.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Evaluate (a) $(-6) \times (-4)$ (b) $(-36) \div 9$ (c) $(-2)^3$ (d) $-3^2$ and $(-3)^2$.**

**(a)** $24$ **(b)** $-4$ **(c)** $(-2)(-2)(-2) = -8$

**(d)** $-3^2 = -(3^2) = -9$, but $(-3)^2 = (-3)(-3) = 9$.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
$-3^2$ means "the negative of $3^2$", which is $-9$. Only $(-3)^2$, with brackets, equals $9$. Calculators follow the same rule, so type the brackets.
:::

## Order of operations

Brackets → Orders (powers and roots) → Division and Multiplication (left to right) → Addition and Subtraction (left to right).

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Evaluate $18 \div (-3) + 4 \times (-2) - (-5)$.**

Division and multiplication first: $18 \div (-3) = -6$ and $4 \times (-2) = -8$.

Then $-6 + (-8) + 5 = -9$.
:::

## Operations on rational numbers

A rational number is any number of the form $\dfrac{a}{b}$ with integers $a$, $b$ and $b \ne 0$. Combine the fraction rules with the sign rules.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Evaluate (a) $-\tfrac{2}{3} + \tfrac{5}{6}$ (b) $\left(-\tfrac{3}{4}\right) \times \tfrac{8}{9}$ (c) $\left(-\tfrac{5}{6}\right) \div \left(-\tfrac{10}{3}\right)$.**

**(a)** $-\tfrac{4}{6} + \tfrac{5}{6} = \tfrac{1}{6}$

**(b)** $-\tfrac{24}{36} = -\tfrac{2}{3}$

**(c)** $-\tfrac{5}{6} \times \left(-\tfrac{3}{10}\right) = \tfrac{15}{60} = \tfrac{1}{4}$ (negative ÷ negative is positive)
:::

### A rational number between two others

The average of two rational numbers always lies between them.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Find a rational number between $\tfrac{1}{3}$ and $\tfrac{1}{2}$.**

$$\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{5}{6} = \frac{5}{12}$$

Check: $\tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{4}{12}$ and $\tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{6}{12}$, so $\tfrac{5}{12}$ lies between them. Because you can always do this again, there are infinitely many rational numbers between any two.
:::

## Word problems

:::callout{type=local-example}
**(a)** A freezer is at $-18\,°\text{C}$ and the kitchen in Kenema is at $27\,°\text{C}$. The difference is $27 - (-18) = 45\,°\text{C}$.

**(b)** A mobile money wallet is NLe 250 overdrawn (balance $-250$). After a deposit of NLe 600, the balance is $-250 + 600 = $ NLe 350.

**(c)** A diver is 12 m below sea level ($-12$ m) and rises 5 m, then descends 9 m. Her position is $-12 + 5 - 9 = -16$ m: 16 m below sea level.
:::

## Key points

- Subtracting a negative = adding: $a - (-b) = a + b$.
- Same signs → **positive**; different signs → **negative** (for × and ÷).
- $-3^2 = -9$ but $(-3)^2 = 9$.
- Follow **BODMAS**; combine fraction rules with sign rules for rational numbers.
- The **average** of two rationals lies between them.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect **expressions mixing negative numbers, fractions and BODMAS**, **temperature and money problems** with negatives, and questions on **ordering and classifying numbers**. Show each step; a single sign error loses the answer mark but not the method marks.
:::

## Practice

Try each question first, then open the answer to check your working.

**1.** Evaluate $-15 - (-7) + 3$.

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
$-15 + 7 + 3 = -5$.
:::

**2.** Evaluate $(-48) \div (-6) \times (-2)$.

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
$8 \times (-2) = -16$.
:::

**3.** Evaluate $-\tfrac{3}{5} - \tfrac{1}{4}$.

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
$-\tfrac{12}{20} - \tfrac{5}{20} = -\tfrac{17}{20}$.
:::

**4.** Evaluate $-2^4 + (-2)^4$.

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
$-16 + 16 = 0$.
:::

**5.** The temperature at night is $-3\,°\text{C}$ and rises by $11\,°\text{C}$ by noon. Find the noon temperature.

:::reveal{title="Show the answer"}
$-3 + 11 = 8\,°\text{C}$.
:::
