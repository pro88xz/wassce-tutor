English is full of words that **sound** the same but are spelt differently (*pair, pear, pare*), words that are **spelt** the same but mean different things (*bank* of a river, *bank* for money), and words spelt the same but **pronounced** differently (*lead* the team, *lead* in a pencil). These are the homophones, homonyms and homographs, and they are tested in both the objective paper and Oral English. This lesson sorts them out clearly, gives you the most important examples, and shows you how to avoid the spelling errors they cause.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define homophones, homonyms and homographs and tell them apart
- choose the correct spelling of a homophone from its context
- explain the different meanings of common homonyms
- pronounce common homographs correctly according to their meaning

## Three related ideas

:::callout{type=definition}
| Term | Same sound? | Same spelling? | Different meaning? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Homophones** | yes | **no** | yes | *see / sea*, *right / write* |
| **Homonyms** (in the strict sense) | yes | yes | yes | *bat* (animal) / *bat* (for cricket) |
| **Homographs** | **no** | yes | yes | *lead* /liːd/ (to guide) / *lead* /led/ (a metal) |

*Homo-* means **same**; *-phone* means **sound**, *-graph* means **writing**, *-nym* means **name**.
:::

:::callout{type=tip}
Many textbooks use **homonym** loosely to mean any of these. In the examination, read the question carefully: if it asks for words that *"sound the same but are spelt differently"*, it wants **homophones**, whatever name it uses.
:::

## Homophones: the spelling trap

These cause errors in essays because a spell-checker in your head cannot catch them. Learn them in pairs with their meanings.

| Words | Meanings |
|---|---|
| **allowed / aloud** | permitted / out loud |
| **bare / bear** | uncovered / the animal; to carry or tolerate |
| **brake / break** | to stop a vehicle / to smash; a rest |
| **cell / sell** | a small room; a unit of life / to exchange for money |
| **cite / sight / site** | to quote / vision / a place |
| **coarse / course** | rough / a route; a programme of study |
| **fair / fare** | just; light-coloured / the price of a journey |
| **flour / flower** | ground grain / part of a plant |
| **hear / here** | to listen / in this place |
| **hole / whole** | an opening / complete |
| **knew / new** | past of *know* / not old |
| **mail / male** | letters / a man or boy |
| **meat / meet** | flesh as food / to come together |
| **pail / pale** | a bucket / light in colour |
| **peace / piece** | calm; no war / a part |
| **plain / plane** | simple; flat land / an aircraft; a flat surface |
| **pray / prey** | to talk to God / an animal hunted for food |
| **principal / principle** | head; main / a rule or belief |
| **rain / reign / rein** | water from clouds / a ruler's time / a strap for a horse |
| **right / rite / write** | correct; opposite of left / a ceremony / to put words on paper |
| **role / roll** | a part played / to turn over; a bread roll |
| **sole / soul** | only; bottom of the foot / the spirit |
| **stair / stare** | a step / to look hard |
| **steal / steel** | to take dishonestly / a strong metal |
| **tail / tale** | the end of an animal / a story |
| **weak / week** | not strong / seven days |
| **weather / whether** | rain, sun, wind / if |
| **which / witch** | what one / a woman said to use magic |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sentences like these, from Sierra Leonean life, show how much the spelling matters:

- *The okada driver applied the **brake** before the junction.* (not *break*)
- *The poda-poda **fare** to Waterloo has gone up again.* (not *fair*)
- *The **reign** of the Paramount Chief lasted forty years.* (not *rain* or *rein*)
- *The new market will be built on this **site**.* (not *sight* or *cite*)
:::

## Homonyms: one spelling, several meanings

| Word | Meaning 1 | Meaning 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **bank** | a place for money | the side of a river |
| **bark** | the sound a dog makes | the outer covering of a tree |
| **bat** | a flying animal | a club used to hit a ball |
| **fair** | just; light-coloured | a market or exhibition |
| **light** | not heavy | brightness |
| **match** | a stick for making fire | a game |
| **mine** | belonging to me | a place where minerals are dug |
| **spring** | the season | a source of water; a coil of metal |
| **well** | in good health; satisfactorily | a hole for water |
| **ruler** | a person who rules | an instrument for measuring |

## Homographs: one spelling, two pronunciations

Here the **meaning decides the pronunciation**, and often the **stress** too:

| Word | Pronunciation 1 | Pronunciation 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **lead** | /liːd/ to guide | /led/ a soft metal |
| **read** | /riːd/ present tense | /red/ past tense |
| **tear** | /tɪə/ water from the eye | /teə/ to rip |
| **wind** | /wɪnd/ moving air | /waɪnd/ to turn (a clock, a road) |
| **live** | /lɪv/ to be alive | /laɪv/ happening now (a *live* broadcast) |
| **close** | /kləʊz/ to shut | /kləʊs/ near |
| **bow** | /baʊ/ to bend the body | /bəʊ/ a knot; a weapon for arrows |
| **present** | **PRE**sent (a gift) | pre**SENT** (to give) |
| **record** | **RE**cord (noun) | re**CORD** (verb) |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
*"The road began to **wind** up the hill as the **wind** grew stronger."*

The first *wind* is a **verb** (/waɪnd/, to curve); the second is a **noun** (/wɪnd/, moving air). An Oral English question might ask which pronunciation the first one has.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Choose: 'Students are not (allowed / aloud) to talk (allowed / aloud) during the examination.'"}
**allowed**, then **aloud**.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Choose: 'I don't know (weather / whether) the (weather / whether) will be good for the trip.'"}
**whether**, then **weather**.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Correct the spelling errors: 'The hole class went to the sight of the new hospital to here the minister speak.'"}
*The **whole** class went to the **site** of the new hospital to **hear** the minister speak.*
:::

:::reveal{title="4. How is 'tear' pronounced in: 'Be careful not to tear the certificate'?"}
**/teə/** (to rip), rhyming with *bear*, not /tɪə/ (from the eye).
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Give two meanings of 'bank' and a sentence for each."}
*I keep my savings in a **bank** in Bo.* / *Children played on the **bank** of the river.*
:::

## Key points

- **Homophones**: same sound, different spelling and meaning (*peace / piece*). They cause **spelling errors** in essays.
- **Homonyms**: same spelling and sound, different meanings (*bank*, *bat*). The **context** gives the meaning.
- **Homographs**: same spelling, different sounds (*lead*, *tear*, *wind*). The **meaning** decides the pronunciation.
- When unsure of a homophone, think of the **meaning** first, then the spelling.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Homophones appear in **gap-filling** and spelling questions in the objective paper; homographs appear in **Oral English** questions on pronunciation and stress. In essays, homophone errors (*there/their*, *weather/whether*, *brake/break*) are among the commonest mechanical errors. Each one costs marks.
:::
