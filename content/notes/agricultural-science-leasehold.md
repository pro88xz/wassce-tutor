An oil palm company wants 5,000 hectares in Pujehun District. It cannot buy the land, but the landowning families agree to let the company use it for 50 years in return for a yearly payment. When the lease ends, the land returns to the families. This is **leasehold**: the right to use land for a fixed period without owning it.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define leasehold and the parties to a lease
- state what a good lease agreement should contain
- state the advantages and disadvantages of leasehold for the lessee and the owner
- explain how leasehold is used in Sierra Leone

## What is leasehold?

:::callout{type=definition}
**Leasehold** is a land tenure system in which the owner allows another person or organisation to use land for an **agreed number of years**, in return for a payment called **rent**, under a written agreement called a **lease**.
:::

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Lessor**: the landowner who grants the lease.
- **Lessee**: the person or organisation that receives the right to use the land under a lease.
:::

At the end of the lease the land **returns to the owner**, together with anything permanently attached to it, unless the lease says otherwise or is renewed.

## What a lease agreement should contain

A good lease is written, signed and witnessed, and states:

1. the names of the **lessor** and **lessee**
2. the exact **plot** and its boundaries, with a survey plan
3. the **length** of the lease (for example 25 or 50 years) and whether it can be **renewed**
4. the **rent**, how often it is paid and how it may be reviewed
5. what the land may be **used** for
6. who pays for **improvements**, and what happens to them at the end
7. how the lease may be **ended** early, and how disputes will be settled

## Advantages of leasehold

### To the lessee (the user)

1. Access to land **without the high cost of buying** it.
2. **Long leases** give enough security for tree crops and buildings.
3. A registered lease can sometimes be used as **security for a loan**.
4. Investors and settlers can obtain land where freehold is not available.

### To the owner (lessor)

1. A regular **income** from land that might otherwise be idle.
2. Ownership is **kept**: the land returns at the end of the lease.
3. Land is developed and kept in use.

## Disadvantages of leasehold

1. **Short leases** discourage long-term improvements such as tree crops and soil conservation.
2. The lessee **loses all improvements** when the lease ends, unless the lease provides compensation.
3. **Rent increases** at review may make farming unprofitable.
4. Disputes arise over boundaries, unpaid rent or uses not allowed by the lease.
5. Large leases can take land away from local farmers who depended on it, if the community was not fully consulted or fairly paid.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not confuse **leasehold** with ordinary **renting**. Both involve paying for land use, but a lease is a **formal, long-term written agreement** (often 21 to 99 years), while renting land for farming is usually for **a season or a year**, often by word of mouth.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
In the provinces of Sierra Leone, land cannot be sold to outsiders, so mining companies, plantation companies and many individual investors obtain land by **lease** from the landowning families, through the chiefdom authorities. The rent is paid each year and shared under customary arrangements. Leases for large plantations have sometimes caused disputes where communities felt they were not fully consulted, which is why clear, written and fair agreements matter.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Should a lessee plant cocoa?** A farmer is offered two leases: 5 years or 30 years. Cocoa takes about 3 to 5 years to bear fruit and then yields for over 30 years.

- On a 5-year lease, the farmer would get almost no harvest before the land returns to the owner: **not worth it**.
- On a 30-year lease, the farmer would enjoy about 25 years of harvests: **worth it**.

The length of the lease decides whether long-term investment pays.
:::

## Key points

- **Leasehold**: the right to use another's land for a fixed number of years in return for **rent**, under a written **lease**.
- **Lessor** = owner; **lessee** = user. The land returns to the lessor at the end.
- A good lease states the plot, length, rent, use, improvements and how disputes are settled.
- Advantages: access without purchase, security on long leases, income for the owner who keeps the land.
- Disadvantages: short leases discourage investment; improvements may be lost; rent rises; disputes.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
WASSCE asks you to **define leasehold**, to **state its advantages and disadvantages**, and to **compare it with freehold**: freehold is permanent ownership, leasehold is use for a fixed period. Mention **lessor** and **lessee** correctly; examiners notice when they are swapped.
:::
