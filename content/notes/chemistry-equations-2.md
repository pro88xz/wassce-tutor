A balanced equation tells you more than which substances react: it gives the exact **mole ratio**. From it you can work out how much lime a kiln will produce from a tonne of limestone, how much hydrogen a reaction will give, which reactant runs out first and how pure a sample is. This is **stoichiometry**, the quantitative heart of chemistry.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- use the mole ratio from a balanced equation
- calculate reacting masses, and masses and volumes of gases
- identify the limiting reagent and the excess reagent
- calculate percentage yield
- calculate percentage purity of a sample

## The method

Every stoichiometry problem follows the same four steps:

1. Write the **balanced equation**.
2. Convert the **known quantity to moles**.
3. Use the **mole ratio** from the equation to find the moles of the substance asked for.
4. Convert those moles to the **quantity asked for** (mass, volume or concentration).

## Reacting masses

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**What mass of calcium oxide is produced by heating 50 g of calcium carbonate?** (Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16)

$$\text{CaCO}_3\text{(s)} \rightarrow \text{CaO(s)} + \text{CO}_2\text{(g)}$$

1. Moles of CaCO₃ $= 50 \div 100 = 0.50$ mol.
2. Mole ratio CaCO₃ : CaO = 1 : 1, so CaO = 0.50 mol.
3. Mass of CaO $= 0.50 \times 56 = 28$ g.

By proportion, 100 g of CaCO₃ gives 56 g of CaO, so 1 tonne of pure limestone gives 0.56 tonnes of quicklime.
:::

## Masses and gas volumes

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**What volume of hydrogen, at s.t.p., is produced when 6.5 g of zinc reacts with excess dilute hydrochloric acid?** (Zn = 65)

$$\text{Zn(s)} + 2\text{HCl(aq)} \rightarrow \text{ZnCl}_2\text{(aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{(g)}$$

1. Moles of Zn $= 6.5 \div 65 = 0.10$ mol.
2. Ratio Zn : H₂ = 1 : 1, so H₂ = 0.10 mol.
3. Volume $= 0.10 \times 22.4 = 2.24$ dm³ at s.t.p.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**What mass of magnesium is needed to react completely with 50 cm³ of 2.0 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid?** (Mg = 24)

$$\text{Mg(s)} + 2\text{HCl(aq)} \rightarrow \text{MgCl}_2\text{(aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{(g)}$$

1. Moles of HCl $= 2.0 \times 50 \div 1000 = 0.10$ mol.
2. Ratio Mg : HCl = 1 : 2, so Mg $= 0.10 \div 2 = 0.050$ mol.
3. Mass of Mg $= 0.050 \times 24 = 1.2$ g.
:::

## Limiting reagent

:::callout{type=definition}
The **limiting reagent** is the reactant that is **used up first**. It decides how much product forms. The other reactant is **in excess**: some of it is left over.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**4 g of hydrogen is burned with 16 g of oxygen. Which is the limiting reagent, and what mass of water forms?** (H = 1, O = 16)

$$2\text{H}_2\text{(g)} + \text{O}_2\text{(g)} \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O(l)}$$

1. Moles: H₂ $= 4 \div 2 = 2$ mol; O₂ $= 16 \div 32 = 0.5$ mol.
2. 2 mol of H₂ would need 1 mol of O₂, but only 0.5 mol is available. **Oxygen is the limiting reagent.**
3. Water formed $= 2 \times 0.5 = 1$ mol $= 18$ g.
4. Hydrogen used $= 1$ mol; hydrogen left over $= 2 - 1 = 1$ mol $= 2$ g.
:::

## Percentage yield

The **theoretical yield** is the mass calculated from the equation. The **actual yield** is the mass obtained in practice, which is usually less because of incomplete reaction, side reactions and losses in filtering or transferring.

$$\text{percentage yield} = \dfrac{\text{actual yield}}{\text{theoretical yield}} \times 100$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Heating 50 g of calcium carbonate gave 25.2 g of calcium oxide. Find the percentage yield.**

Theoretical yield = 28 g (from the earlier example).

$$\text{percentage yield} = \dfrac{25.2}{28} \times 100 = 90\%$$
:::

## Percentage purity

$$\text{percentage purity} = \dfrac{\text{mass of pure substance}}{\text{mass of impure sample}} \times 100$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**12.5 g of impure limestone reacts with excess hydrochloric acid to give 2.24 dm³ of carbon(IV) oxide at s.t.p. Find the percentage purity of the limestone.**

$$\text{CaCO}_3 + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{CaCl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2$$

1. Moles of CO₂ $= 2.24 \div 22.4 = 0.10$ mol.
2. Ratio CaCO₃ : CO₂ = 1 : 1, so pure CaCO₃ = 0.10 mol $= 0.10 \times 100 = 10$ g.
3. Percentage purity $= \dfrac{10}{12.5} \times 100 = 80\%$.

The impurities (sand, clay) did not react with the acid.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Stoichiometry decides costs in local industry. A soap maker who knows the balanced equation for saponification can work out how much caustic soda each drum of palm kernel oil needs. Too little leaves greasy, unreacted oil; too much leaves a harsh, alkaline soap that burns the skin.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not use the mass ratio from the equation. Use the **mole ratio**. In Zn + 2HCl, 1 mol of zinc reacts with 2 mol of HCl, not "1 g with 2 g".
:::

## Key points

- Method: **balanced equation → moles of known → mole ratio → moles of unknown → required quantity**.
- Gas volumes: moles × **22.4 dm³** at s.t.p.; solutions: moles = **C × V**.
- The **limiting reagent** is used up first and fixes the amount of product.
- **Percentage yield** = actual ÷ theoretical × 100.
- **Percentage purity** = pure mass ÷ sample mass × 100.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Stoichiometry appears in both papers every year. Expect **reacting-mass and gas-volume calculations**, **limiting reagent** problems, and **percentage purity or yield** of limestone, metals and salts. Marks are given for each step, so show the equation, the moles and the ratio, not just the final answer.
:::

## Quick check

1. What mass of carbon(IV) oxide is produced when 20 g of CaCO₃ is heated?
2. What volume of oxygen at s.t.p. is needed to burn 6 g of carbon completely? (C + O₂ → CO₂)
3. 2.4 g of magnesium is added to 0.10 mol of HCl. Which reactant is limiting?
4. 5.0 g of impure zinc gives 1.12 dm³ of hydrogen at s.t.p. with excess acid. Find the percentage purity of the zinc.

**Answers:**

1. 0.20 mol CaCO₃ → 0.20 mol CO₂ → $0.20 \times 44 = 8.8$ g.
2. 0.50 mol C needs 0.50 mol O₂: $0.50 \times 22.4 = 11.2$ dm³.
3. Mg = 0.10 mol needs 0.20 mol HCl; only 0.10 mol is present, so HCl is limiting.
4. H₂ = 0.050 mol, so Zn = 0.050 mol = 3.25 g; purity $= 3.25 \div 5.0 \times 100 = 65\%$.
