## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the features and sources of monopoly and find the monopolist's equilibrium
- explain price discrimination and the control of monopoly
- describe monopolistic competition and oligopoly, and compare all four market structures

## Monopoly

:::callout{type=definition}
A **monopoly** is a market in which there is a **single seller** of a product that has **no close substitute**, and entry by other firms is **blocked**.
:::

### Sources (causes) of monopoly

1. **Legal rights**: patents, copyrights and licences.
2. **Ownership of a scarce resource**: control of all the supply of a raw material.
3. **Government ownership** of essential services, such as water and electricity.
4. **Huge capital requirements**: a new firm cannot afford to compete.
5. **Mergers and takeovers** that remove competitors.
6. **Natural monopoly**: one firm can supply the whole market more cheaply than several firms, as with a water pipe network.

### The monopolist's equilibrium

The monopolist **is** the industry, so it faces the **downward-sloping market demand curve**. To sell more, it must lower the price on **all** units, so **MR is less than price** (AR).

:::callout{type=worked-example}
A monopolist has the same costs as the firm in the previous lesson (total cost 140, 170, 190, 220, 270 and 340 for 1 to 6 units). Profit is TR minus TC.

| $Q$ | Price (AR) | TR | MR | MC | Profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 40 | −40 |
| 2 | 90 | 180 | 80 | 30 | 10 |
| 3 | 80 | 240 | 60 | 20 | 50 |
| **4** | **70** | **280** | **40** | **30** | **60** |
| 5 | 60 | 300 | 20 | 50 | 30 |
| 6 | 50 | 300 | 0 | 70 | −40 |

- MR is above MC up to the **4th unit** (40 > 30); the 5th unit adds 20 to revenue but 50 to cost.
- The monopolist produces **4 units** and charges **NLe 70**.
- Maximum profit: $280 - 220 = \text{NLe } 60$.
:::

Because entry is blocked, the monopolist can keep **abnormal profit in the long run**. Compared with perfect competition, it usually produces **less** and charges a **higher price**.

### Price discrimination

:::callout{type=definition}
**Price discrimination** is when a seller charges **different prices to different buyers for the same product**, for reasons not connected with differences in cost.
:::

**Conditions needed:** the seller must be a monopolist (or have market power); the markets must be **separated** so goods cannot be resold; and the **elasticities of demand** in the markets must differ (the higher price is charged where demand is **less elastic**).

*Examples: electricity tariffs that differ for homes and factories; lower fares for students; different prices for local and foreign visitors at tourist sites.*

### Advantages and disadvantages of monopoly

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Economies of scale can lower costs | Higher prices and lower output |
| Abnormal profits can fund research | Consumers have little choice |
| Avoids wasteful duplication (e.g. two water networks) | Inefficiency, because there is no competition |
| Essential services can be provided by government | Exploitation of consumers |

### Control of monopoly

Governments may **regulate prices**, pass **anti-monopoly laws**, **nationalise** the monopoly, remove barriers such as **import restrictions** to allow foreign competition, or **tax** abnormal profits.

## Monopolistic competition

:::callout{type=definition}
**Monopolistic competition** is a market with **many sellers** producing **differentiated products** (similar but not identical), with fairly **free entry**.
:::

Firms differentiate their products by **brand names, packaging, quality, location and service**, and spend heavily on **advertising**. Each firm has a slightly downward-sloping demand curve. In the short run it can earn abnormal profit; in the long run new firms enter, so profit falls to **normal**.

*Examples: hairdressing salons, restaurants, bakeries and clothing shops in a town.*

## Oligopoly

:::callout{type=definition}
An **oligopoly** is a market dominated by **a few large sellers**. Each firm's decisions affect its rivals, so firms are **interdependent**. A market with only two sellers is a **duopoly**.
:::

**Features:** high barriers to entry; heavy **non-price competition** (advertising, promotions, free gifts); **price rigidity** (firms avoid price wars); and the possibility of **collusion**, where firms agree to fix prices or share the market (a **cartel**).

:::callout{type=local-example}
Mobile phone services in Sierra Leone are supplied by a small number of large network operators. Each watches the others closely and competes mainly through data bundles, promotions and advertising, rather than by repeatedly cutting basic prices: typical oligopoly behaviour.
:::

## Comparing the four structures

| Feature | Perfect competition | Monopolistic competition | Oligopoly | Monopoly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of firms | Very many | Many | Few | One |
| Product | Identical | Differentiated | Identical or differentiated | Unique |
| Entry | Free | Fairly free | Difficult | Blocked |
| Control over price | None (price taker) | Some | Considerable | Great (price maker) |
| Long-run profit | Normal | Normal | Can be abnormal | Abnormal |
| Advertising | None | Heavy | Heavy | Little needed |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A monopolist cannot charge **any** price it likes **and** sell **any** quantity it likes. It can choose the price **or** the quantity, but buyers' demand decides the other.
:::

## Key points

- **Monopoly**: one seller, no close substitutes, blocked entry; produces where **MC = MR** and keeps abnormal profit.
- **Price discrimination** needs separate markets with different elasticities.
- **Monopolistic competition**: many firms, differentiated products, normal profit in the long run.
- **Oligopoly**: a few interdependent firms, non-price competition, possible collusion.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect **"State five sources of monopoly power"**, **"Explain the conditions necessary for price discrimination"**, and **"Compare perfect competition with monopoly"**. Comparison answers score best as matched points or a table. For the monopoly diagram, draw AR and MR sloping down (MR below AR), mark MC = MR, read the price up to the AR curve and shade abnormal profit.
:::

## Quick check

1. Why is MR below AR for a monopolist?
2. Give two conditions necessary for price discrimination.
3. Name one difference between monopolistic competition and oligopoly.

**Answers:** 1. To sell an extra unit, the monopolist must lower the price on all units, so the extra revenue is less than the price. 2. For example: separate markets with no resale between them, and different elasticities of demand in each market. 3. Monopolistic competition has many firms; oligopoly has only a few, interdependent, firms.
