:::callout{type=definition}
The **classification of computers** is the grouping of computers by the way they handle data (analogue, digital or hybrid), by their size and power (supercomputer, mainframe, minicomputer or microcomputer) and by their purpose (general-purpose or special-purpose).
:::

Not every computer looks like a laptop. The computer that forecasts the weather fills a building; the one in a bank serves thousands of customers at once; the one in a washing machine does a single job and is hidden inside. This lesson sorts these **types of computers** in the three ways WASSCE asks about: **by type**, **by size** and **by purpose**, with examples of each.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- classify computers by type, size and purpose
- explain the difference between analogue, digital and hybrid computers, with examples
- describe supercomputers, mainframes, minicomputers and microcomputers, with their uses
- distinguish general-purpose from special-purpose computers
- name the types of microcomputer

## Classification by type: analogue, digital and hybrid

This is how a computer **represents and handles data**.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Analogue computer:** a computer that works with **continuous** data, measuring physical quantities such as speed, temperature, pressure or voltage, and usually shows its result on a dial or scale.

**Digital computer:** a computer that works with **discrete** data, numbers and characters represented by the digits 0 and 1.

**Hybrid computer:** a computer that combines the features of analogue and digital computers, taking in continuous measurements and processing them digitally.
:::

| | Analogue computer | Digital computer |
|---|---|---|
| Data | continuous (changes smoothly) | discrete (separate values, 0 and 1) |
| What it does | measures | counts and calculates |
| Output | a pointer on a dial, a graph | numbers, text, pictures |
| Accuracy | limited by the measurement | very accurate |
| Memory | little or none | large |
| Examples | analogue speedometer, fuel gauge, slide rule | desktop and laptop computers, calculators, smartphones |

**Examples of hybrid computers:**

- the machines in a hospital intensive care unit, which measure a patient's heartbeat, blood pressure and temperature (analogue) and then process and display the readings as numbers and graphs (digital)
- a modern **petrol pump**, which measures the flow of fuel (analogue) and works out and shows the litres and the price in digits (digital)

:::callout{type=tip}
**Almost every computer you use is digital.** Laptops, phones, ATMs and calculators are all digital computers. Analogue computers are now rare, and many devices that once used them now measure with sensors and process the readings digitally.
:::

## Classification by size: supercomputer to microcomputer

This is how **big and powerful** a computer is, and how many people it can serve at once.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Supercomputer:** the largest, fastest and most expensive type of computer, used for problems that need enormous numbers of calculations.

**Mainframe computer:** a large, powerful computer used by a big organisation to process huge amounts of data for many users at the same time.

**Minicomputer:** a medium-sized computer, smaller and cheaper than a mainframe, that serves a small organisation or one department with a number of users.

**Microcomputer:** a small computer built around a single microprocessor and used by one person at a time; also called a personal computer (PC).
:::

| Type | Size and speed | Users | Uses | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Supercomputer** | largest and fastest: the fastest perform over a billion billion (10¹⁸) calculations a second | scientists and governments | weather forecasting, climate research, space and nuclear research, medicine design | Frontier (USA, 2022), one of the first to pass 10¹⁸ calculations a second |
| **Mainframe** | very large, very powerful, very reliable | thousands at once, through terminals | banks, airlines, insurance companies, large government offices, universities | IBM Z series |
| **Minicomputer** | medium-sized ("mid-range") | tens to hundreds | a factory, a hospital, a department of a company | PDP-8 (1965), today's mid-range servers |
| **Microcomputer** | small, cheap | one person at a time | home, school, office, business | desktop PC, laptop, tablet, smartphone |

### Types of microcomputers

- **Desktop computer:** sits on a desk; separate system unit, monitor, keyboard and mouse (or all-in-one).
- **Laptop (notebook) computer:** portable, with screen, keyboard and battery in one case.
- **Tablet:** a flat computer with a touch screen and no physical keyboard.
- **Smartphone:** a mobile phone that is also a small computer, running apps.
- **Workstation:** a powerful desktop used for engineering design, graphics and video work.

## Classification by purpose: general-purpose and special-purpose

:::callout{type=definition}
**General-purpose computer:** a computer that can do many different jobs, depending on the program it runs.

**Special-purpose computer:** a computer designed to do one particular job only; its program is usually fixed.

**Embedded computer:** a special-purpose computer built inside another machine to control it.
:::

- **General-purpose** examples: a laptop used for typing a letter, calculating a budget, browsing the Internet and playing music.
- **Special-purpose** examples: an **ATM** (automated teller machine), a digital watch, a calculator, a traffic-light controller, the control unit of a washing machine or microwave oven, the engine computer in a car.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Special-purpose computers you meet every day.** The ATM outside a bank in Freetown or Bo, the prepaid electricity meter in a house and the point-of-sale machine in a supermarket each do one job, controlled by a computer built inside them.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Thinking a smartphone is special-purpose because it is a phone.** A smartphone runs many different apps for many tasks, so it is a **general-purpose** microcomputer.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. State three ways of classifying computers."}
By **type** (analogue, digital, hybrid), by **size** (supercomputer, mainframe, minicomputer, microcomputer) and by **purpose** (general-purpose, special-purpose).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. State two differences between analogue and digital computers."}
Any two: analogue computers handle **continuous** data, digital computers handle **discrete** data (0s and 1s); analogue computers **measure**, digital computers **count**; analogue output is on a **dial or graph**, digital output is in **digits, text or pictures**; digital computers are more **accurate** and have much more **memory**.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Give one example of a hybrid computer and explain why it is hybrid."}
A hospital **intensive care monitor**: it measures continuous quantities such as heartbeat and temperature (analogue) and processes and displays them as numbers (digital). A modern **petrol pump** is another example.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Name the type of computer used for weather forecasting, and why."}
A **supercomputer**, because forecasting needs an enormous number of calculations done very quickly.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Is an ATM a general-purpose or a special-purpose computer? Explain."}
**Special-purpose**: it is designed to do one job only, banking transactions such as giving out cash and showing balances.
:::

## Key points

- By **type**: **analogue** (continuous data, measures), **digital** (discrete data, counts), **hybrid** (both).
- By **size**: **supercomputer** (fastest; weather, research), **mainframe** (large organisations, many users), **minicomputer** (medium), **microcomputer** (one user: desktop, laptop, tablet, smartphone).
- By **purpose**: **general-purpose** (many tasks) and **special-purpose** (one task, such as an ATM or a digital watch).
- Most computers in use today are **digital microcomputers**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Classification questions usually ask you to classify computers, to give examples of analogue, digital and hybrid computers, to compare analogue and digital computers, and to name the type of computer suited to a job (supercomputer for weather forecasting, mainframe for a bank, microcomputer for a home). Give a clear example with each type, and for comparisons set the two side by side, point for point.
:::
