The **final accounts of a limited company** follow the same pattern as a sole trader's, with extra items: **debenture interest** and **directors' fees** as expenses, **tax** on the company's profit, **dividends** paid to shareholders, transfers to **reserves**, and an **equity** section showing share capital and reserves. Profit kept in the company after tax and dividends is added to **retained earnings**. This lesson prepares the income statement, the changes in retained earnings, and the statement of financial position of a limited company.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- prepare a company income statement showing profit before and after tax
- calculate dividends on preference and ordinary shares
- prepare a statement of changes in retained earnings
- prepare the statement of financial position of a limited company
- calculate earnings per share

## Special items

| Item | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Debenture interest** | expense in the income statement; any unpaid interest is a current liability |
| **Directors' fees and salaries** | expenses |
| **Tax on profit** | deducted from profit before tax; unpaid tax is a current liability |
| **Dividends paid** | deducted from **retained earnings** (not an expense) |
| **Transfer to general reserve** | moves profit from retained earnings to the general reserve |

## Worked example

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Kamara Holdings Ltd, year ended 31 December 2026 (NLe):**

- Profit before tax (after debenture interest of 4,000): 90,000
- Tax on profit: 22,500 (unpaid at the year end)
- Retained earnings at 1 January 2026: 30,000; general reserve at 1 January: 10,000
- Dividends paid: 8% on 50,000 preference shares of NLe 1; NLe 0.10 per share on 200,000 ordinary shares of NLe 1
- Transfer to general reserve: 15,000
- Non-current assets: property 220,000; equipment (net) 80,000
- Current assets: inventory 40,000; trade receivables 35,000; bank 31,000
- 10% debentures 40,000; trade payables 10,000
:::

**Profit after tax:**

$90\,000 - 22\,500 = 67\,500$

**Dividends:**

- Preference $= 8\% \times 50\,000 = 4\,000$
- Ordinary $= 0.10 \times 200\,000 = 20\,000$

| Less transfer to general reserve | (15,000) |
| **Retained earnings at 31 December 2026** | **58,500** |

## Earnings per share

**Earnings per share (EPS) = (profit after tax − preference dividend) ÷ number of ordinary shares**

For Kamara Holdings: $\frac{67\,500 - 4\,000}{200\,000} = 0.3175$, about **NLe 0.32** per ordinary share.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not put **dividends** in the income statement as an expense. Dividends are a **distribution of profit** to shareholders and are deducted in the **statement of changes in retained earnings** (equity). Debenture interest, by contrast, **is** an expense.
:::

## Key points

- **Debenture interest** and **directors' fees** are expenses; **tax** is deducted to give **profit after tax**.
- **Dividends** and **transfers to reserves** are deducted from **retained earnings**.
- **Equity = share capital + reserves**; debentures are **non-current liabilities**; unpaid tax is a **current liability**.
- **EPS = (profit after tax − preference dividend) ÷ ordinary shares.**

## Quick check

1. Where do dividends paid appear?
2. Profit after tax NLe 50,000; preference dividend NLe 5,000; 100,000 ordinary shares. Find EPS.
3. Where is unpaid tax shown?
4. Retained earnings b/f NLe 20,000; profit after tax NLe 40,000; dividends NLe 15,000; transfer to reserve NLe 5,000. Find retained earnings c/f.
5. In which section of the statement of financial position do debentures appear?

**Answers:**

1. In the statement of changes in retained earnings (equity), not in the income statement.
2. $\frac{50\,000 - 5\,000}{100\,000} = 0.45$: NLe 0.45 per share.
3. As a current liability.
4. $20\,000 + 40\,000 - 15\,000 - 5\,000 = 40\,000$: NLe 40,000.
5. Non-current liabilities.
