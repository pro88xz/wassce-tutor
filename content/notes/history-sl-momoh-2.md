The **Sierra Leone Civil War (1991–2002)** was fought between the government and the **Revolutionary United Front (RUF)**, led by **Foday Sankoh**, which invaded from Liberia on **23 March 1991** with the support of **Charles Taylor**. Its root causes were **bad governance, corruption, the one-party state, economic collapse, the exclusion of youth**, and the struggle for **diamonds**. The war became infamous for the **amputations**, **child soldiers** and attacks on civilians; about **50,000** people were killed and over **two million** displaced. It ended through **ECOMOG**, the **Lomé Agreement (1999)**, the UN mission **UNAMSIL**, **British intervention (2000)** and **disarmament**, and was declared over on **18 January 2002**. This lesson explains **the causes, course and effects of the Sierra Leone Civil War**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the causes of the civil war
- describe the RUF and its methods
- describe the main stages of the war from 1991 to 2002
- explain the role of diamonds in the conflict
- explain the effects of the war on Sierra Leone
- describe how the war ended and how the country sought justice and reconciliation

## Causes of the civil war

| Cause | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Bad governance** | **one-party rule**, abuse of power and the absence of accountability under the APC |
| **Corruption** | public money and mineral wealth were stolen by a small elite |
| **Economic collapse** | unpaid salaries, inflation, unemployment and the collapse of services in the 1980s |
| **Exclusion of youth** | many young people, especially in the provinces and mining areas, had **no education, jobs or voice** |
| **Diamonds** | easily mined **alluvial diamonds** in Kono and the east gave rebels a way to **finance** the war |
| **The Liberian war** | **Charles Taylor's NPFL** supported the RUF, partly to punish Sierra Leone for hosting **ECOMOG** forces fighting him |
| **Weak army** | the army was poorly paid, trained and equipped |

## The RUF and its methods

The **RUF** was led by **Foday Sankoh**, a former army corporal. It claimed to fight against **corruption and injustice**, but in practice:

1. it **abducted** thousands of children and young people as **child soldiers** and labourers;
2. it used **terror**: killings, rape, burning of villages and, especially, the **amputation of hands and arms**;
3. it seized the **diamond fields** to buy arms, often through Liberia;
4. it **looted** property and food.

Some government soldiers, called "**sobels**" (soldiers by day, rebels by night), also committed abuses, and local **Civil Defence Forces (CDF)**, including the **Kamajors**, were formed to defend communities.

## Main stages of the war

*Figure 1. Key places in the Sierra Leone Civil War (positions approximate). WASSCE Tutor map.*

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **23 March 1991** | RUF attacks **Bomaru**, Kailahun District |
| **29 April 1992** | **NPRC** coup; the war continues and spreads |
| **1995** | RUF advances near Freetown; the NPRC hires **Executive Outcomes**, which pushes the RUF back |
| **1996** | elections; **Ahmad Tejan Kabbah** becomes President; **Abidjan Accord** (November) |
| **25 May 1997** | **AFRC** coup under **Major Johnny Paul Koroma**; the AFRC invites the RUF to share power |
| **February 1998** | **ECOMOG** drives out the junta; Kabbah returns in March |
| **6 January 1999** | AFRC and RUF forces **invade Freetown**, killing thousands |
| **7 July 1999** | **Lomé Peace Agreement**: power-sharing and amnesty |
| **October 1999** | the UN sets up **UNAMSIL** |
| **May 2000** | RUF takes **UN peacekeepers hostage**; **British troops** intervene; **Sankoh arrested** |
| **2000–2001** | **Abuja ceasefire agreements**; **disarmament** of about **72,000** fighters |
| **18 January 2002** | President Kabbah declares the **war over** |

## Effects of the war

1. **Loss of life:** about **50,000** people killed.
2. **Displacement:** over **two million** people, nearly half the population, became **refugees** or internally displaced.
3. **Amputations and injuries:** thousands of civilians mutilated.
4. **Child soldiers:** thousands of children forced to fight, many traumatised.
5. **Destruction:** towns, villages, schools, hospitals, roads and power lines destroyed.
6. **Economic collapse:** mining, farming and trade stopped; Sierra Leone fell to the bottom of world development rankings.
7. **Social damage:** sexual violence, broken families and loss of trust between communities.

## Justice and reconciliation after the war

| Institution | Work |
|---|---|
| **Truth and Reconciliation Commission (TRC)** | heard testimony from victims and perpetrators; its **2004 report** named bad governance, corruption and the exclusion of youth as root causes |
| **Special Court for Sierra Leone** | a court set up with the UN in **2002** to try those bearing the **greatest responsibility**; in **2012** it convicted former Liberian President **Charles Taylor** |
| **Disarmament, Demobilisation and Reintegration (DDR)** | collected weapons and helped ex-fighters return to civilian life |
| **Reforms** | the **Anti-Corruption Commission (2000)**, local councils (**2004**) and army and police reforms |

:::callout{type=local-example}
"**Blood diamonds**" from Sierra Leone and other war zones led to the **Kimberley Process Certification Scheme (2003)**, an international system to stop diamonds that fund wars from being sold. Sierra Leone's experience helped to change the global diamond trade.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not write that the war was caused only by diamonds. Diamonds **financed and prolonged** the war, but its **root causes** were **bad governance, corruption, economic collapse and the exclusion of young people**, as the TRC concluded.
:::

## Key points

- War began **23 March 1991** at **Bomaru**; **RUF** led by **Foday Sankoh**, backed by **Charles Taylor**.
- Causes: **bad governance, corruption, economic collapse, exclusion of youth, diamonds, Liberia, a weak army**.
- RUF methods: **child soldiers, amputations, terror, diamond mining**.
- Stages: **NPRC (1992)**, **Executive Outcomes (1995)**, **AFRC (1997)**, **ECOMOG (1998)**, **Freetown invasion (1999)**, **Lomé (1999)**, **UNAMSIL**, **British intervention (2000)**, **war over (18 January 2002)**.
- Effects: about **50,000 dead**, **two million displaced**, destruction; **TRC**, **Special Court**, **DDR** and reforms afterwards.

## Quick check

1. Who led the RUF, and when did the war begin?
2. Give four causes of the civil war.
3. What role did diamonds play in the war?
4. What happened on 6 January 1999?
5. Give three effects of the war.
6. What was the work of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission?

**Answers:**

1. Foday Sankoh; on 23 March 1991.
2. Any four: bad governance and one-party rule; corruption; economic collapse; exclusion of youth; diamonds; support from Charles Taylor in Liberia; a weak army.
3. Rebels seized the diamond fields and used diamonds to buy arms, which financed and prolonged the war.
4. AFRC and RUF forces invaded Freetown, killing thousands and burning much of the city.
5. Any three: about 50,000 killed; over two million displaced; amputations; child soldiers; destruction of property and services; economic collapse; social damage.
6. It heard testimony from victims and perpetrators, explained the causes of the war in its 2004 report and recommended reforms.
