# Rock cycle, rock properties and the distribution of rocks

## Learning objectives
You should be able to explain the rock cycle, identify processes that transform one rock group into another, and interpret how rock structure influences landscapes and human activity.

## The rock cycle
The rock cycle describes the continuous transformation of rocks through geological processes. It is not a fixed sequence in which every rock must pass through every stage. Any rock may be uplifted, weathered, buried, heated, melted or altered depending on its environment.

A simplified sequence is:
1. Existing rocks are exposed and broken down by weathering and erosion.
2. Sediments are transported and deposited in layers.
3. Burial, compaction and cementation may turn sediments into sedimentary rock.
4. Burial and increased heat and pressure may transform igneous or sedimentary rock into metamorphic rock.
5. Further heating may cause melting, producing magma.
6. Cooling and crystallisation of magma produce igneous rock.
7. Uplift and exposure bring rocks back to the surface, where weathering can begin again.

The processes are powered by different energy sources. Solar energy and gravity drive much of weathering, erosion and transport. Internal heat drives melting, metamorphism, tectonic movement and uplift.

## Rock properties and identification
Geographers and geologists use texture, grain size, mineral composition, structure and field relationships to identify rocks.

- **Texture** refers to the size, shape and arrangement of mineral grains.
- **Mineral composition** identifies the minerals present.
- **Porosity** is the proportion of a rock's volume occupied by pores.
- **Permeability** describes how easily water can pass through connected pores or fractures.
- **Hardness** is resistance to scratching; it is not identical to resistance to breaking.
- **Bedding** is the layering common in many sedimentary rocks.
- **Foliation** is the planar arrangement of minerals in many metamorphic rocks.

A rock can be porous but poorly permeable if its pores are not connected. This distinction is important when explaining groundwater storage and movement.

## How geology influences relief
Rock resistance affects the rate at which landscapes are worn down. Resistant rock may remain as ridges, hills or steep escarpments while less resistant rock is lowered into valleys. Alternating hard and soft strata can produce uneven slopes and differential erosion.

Joints, faults and bedding planes create lines of weakness. Water can enter these openings and accelerate weathering. In limestone regions, slightly acidic rainwater may dissolve the rock and create caves, underground drainage and sinkholes; this landscape is known as karst.

## Human and economic importance
Rock resources supply building stone, aggregates, cement raw materials and industrial minerals. Geological structures influence where groundwater is stored and where mineral deposits occur. However, quarrying and mining can cause dust, noise, habitat loss, unstable slopes and polluted runoff if not properly managed.

In Sierra Leone, the distribution of mineral resources is related to the country's varied geology. When discussing a named deposit, distinguish the mineral resource from the rock unit or geological process that hosts it; do not assume every rock contains an economically workable concentration.

## Worked interpretation
A valley contains soft sedimentary rocks between two ridges of resistant rock. The likely explanation is differential erosion: the softer strata have been removed more rapidly, while the resistant layers remain elevated. A complete answer should refer to differences in resistance and the action of weathering and running water.

## WASSCE examination focus
In a rock-cycle diagram, label processes as well as rock groups. Do not draw the cycle as a one-way chain. When asked how rocks influence relief, explain differential erosion and refer to resistance, structure and joints.

## Quick check
1. Name two processes that can turn exposed rock into sediment.
2. How does sediment become sedimentary rock?
3. Distinguish porosity from permeability.
4. Explain how alternating hard and soft rocks can produce ridges and valleys.

**Answers:** 1. Weathering and erosion. 2. Burial, compaction and cementation, or chemical/biological precipitation. 3. Porosity is pore-space proportion; permeability is the ability of connected spaces to transmit water. 4. Softer rocks are eroded faster into valleys while resistant layers remain as ridges.
