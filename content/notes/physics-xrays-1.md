In 1895, while experimenting with an evacuated tube, Röntgen noticed a screen glowing across the room — and then saw the bones of his wife's hand. X-rays and the cathode rays that make them opened the door to modern physics and modern medicine.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe thermionic emission and the production of cathode rays
- state the properties of cathode rays
- describe the cathode-ray oscilloscope and its uses
- explain how cathode rays were shown to be electrons

## Thermionic emission

:::callout{type=definition}
**Thermionic emission** is the emission of **electrons from the surface of a hot metal** (cathode). Heating gives the free electrons in the metal enough energy to escape from its surface.
:::

A cathode is heated by a low-voltage **filament**. Coating it with metal oxides lowers the work function, so electrons escape more easily.

## Cathode rays

When a high p.d. is applied between a heated cathode and an **anode** in an **evacuated** glass tube, the emitted electrons are accelerated towards the anode. Some pass through a hole in it as a beam, historically called **cathode rays**.

### Properties of cathode rays

1. They travel in **straight lines** (they cast sharp shadows of an obstacle).
2. They carry **negative charge**.
3. They are **deflected by electric fields** (towards the positive plate).
4. They are **deflected by magnetic fields** (in the direction given by Fleming's left-hand rule for negative charge).
5. They cause **fluorescence** when they strike certain materials (zinc sulphide glows).
6. They have **momentum and energy**: they can turn a small paddle wheel and heat the target.
7. They produce **X-rays** when they strike a hard metal target.
8. They can **ionise** gases.

All these properties show that cathode rays are **streams of fast-moving electrons**. J. J. Thomson (1897) measured their charge-to-mass ratio, $e/m$, by balancing electric and magnetic deflections — the discovery of the **electron**.

## Energy of the electrons

An electron (charge $e$) accelerated through a p.d. $V$ gains kinetic energy

$$\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = eV$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Electrons are accelerated through 2000 V. Find (a) their kinetic energy, (b) their speed. (e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, m = 9.1 × 10⁻³¹ kg)**

(a) $E_k = eV = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 2000 = 3.2 \times 10^{-16}$ J.

(b) $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2eV}{m}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \times 3.2 \times 10^{-16}}{9.1 \times 10^{-31}}} = \sqrt{7.03 \times 10^{14}} = 2.65 \times 10^{7}$ m s⁻¹ (about 9% of the speed of light).
:::

## The cathode-ray oscilloscope (CRO)

The CRO is an instrument that draws a graph of a changing voltage on a screen.

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **Electron gun** (heated cathode, grid, anodes) | produces a fine beam of electrons; the **grid** controls **brightness**, the **anodes** accelerate and **focus** the beam |
| **Y-plates** (horizontal) | deflect the beam **up and down** in proportion to the input voltage |
| **X-plates** (vertical) | deflect the beam **sideways**; a **time base** sweeps it steadily from left to right and flies back |
| **Fluorescent screen** | glows where the beam strikes |
| **Evacuated tube** | stops electrons colliding with air molecules |

**Uses of the CRO:**

- measuring **d.c. and a.c. voltages** (peak value from the trace height and the Y-gain setting)
- displaying **waveforms** (sound from a microphone, heartbeats in an ECG)
- measuring **frequency** and **time intervals** (from the time base)
- comparing phases of signals

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A CRO's Y-gain is 2 V per division. An a.c. trace has a peak-to-peak height of 6 divisions. Find the peak voltage.**

Peak-to-peak $= 6 \times 2 = 12$ V, so the peak voltage $= 6$ V.
:::

## Key points

- Thermionic emission: electrons leave a hot metal surface.
- Cathode rays are streams of electrons: they travel in straight lines, are negatively charged, are deflected by electric and magnetic fields, cause fluorescence and produce X-rays.
- Electron energy gained through p.d. $V$: $\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = eV$.
- CRO: electron gun, deflecting plates, time base, fluorescent screen; measures voltages and displays waveforms.
