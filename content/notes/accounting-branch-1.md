:::callout{type=definition}
**Branch accounts** are the accounts kept by a business that trades in **more than one place**, to find the **profit of each branch**.
:::

A **dependent branch** does not keep a full set of books: **head office** sends it goods, pays its main expenses, receives its cash and keeps its accounts. An **independent branch** keeps its own full set of books. This lesson explains the types of branch and works through the **branch account (debtors) method** for a dependent branch that receives goods **at cost**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the meaning and purposes of branch accounts
- distinguish dependent from independent branches
- describe the flows of goods and cash between head office and a dependent branch
- prepare a branch account when goods are sent at cost
- check the branch profit with a branch trading account

## Purposes of branch accounts

1. To find the **profit or loss of each branch**.
2. To **control** the inventory, cash and receivables held at each branch.
3. To compare **branch performance** and decide whether to expand or close a branch.
4. To detect **losses** of goods or cash at branches.

## Dependent and independent branches

| Dependent branch | Independent branch |
|---|---|
| does **not** keep full books | keeps a **full set** of books |
| receives goods from **head office** | may buy goods **itself** as well |
| sends all **cash** to head office | keeps and banks its own cash |
| head office prepares its accounts | prepares its own accounts, combined at the year end |

## The branch account (debtors) method: goods at cost

All transactions with the branch go through one **branch account** in head office books. Assets at the branch at the start (inventory, receivables) and everything head office sends or pays are **debited**; everything received back, and assets at the end, are **credited**. The balancing figure is the **branch profit**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A Freetown business has a dependent branch in Bo. For 2026 (NLe):**

- Branch inventory 1 January 8,000; branch receivables 1 January 3,000
- Goods sent to the branch at cost 60,000
- Branch expenses paid by head office 9,000
- Cash sales remitted to head office 52,000
- Cash collected from branch receivables and remitted 25,000
- Branch inventory 31 December 10,000; branch receivables 31 December 5,000

**Bo branch account (head office books)**

| Debit | NLe |
|---|---|
| Inventory at 1 January | 8,000 |
| Receivables at 1 January | 3,000 |
| Goods sent to branch | 60,000 |
| Branch expenses | 9,000 |
| **Profit (balancing figure)** | **12,000** |
| **Total** | **92,000** |

| Credit | NLe |
|---|---|
| Cash remitted: cash sales | 52,000 |
| Cash remitted: from receivables | 25,000 |
| Inventory at 31 December c/d | 10,000 |
| Receivables at 31 December c/d | 5,000 |
| **Total** | **92,000** |

Profit $= 92\,000 - (8\,000 + 3\,000 + 60\,000 + 9\,000) = 12\,000$
:::

**Check with a branch trading account:**

- Credit sales $= 25\,000 + 5\,000 - 3\,000 = 27\,000$; total sales $= 52\,000 + 27\,000 = 79\,000$
- Cost of sales $= 8\,000 + 60\,000 - 10\,000 = 58\,000$
- Gross profit $= 79\,000 - 58\,000 = 21\,000$; net profit $= 21\,000 - 9\,000 = 12\,000$ ✓

The profit of NLe 12,000 is transferred to the main income statement: **debit branch account, credit income statement**.

## Goods sent to branch account

Head office also keeps a **goods sent to branch account**: it **credits** this account with goods sent (debit branch account). At the year end the balance is transferred to head office **purchases** (or trading account), so that goods sent to the branch are not counted as head office sales or inventory.

:::callout{type=local-example}
A Freetown wholesaler opening outlets in Bo, Kenema and Makeni would use branch accounts to see which towns earn the most. Because the branches only sell and send cash back, they are **dependent** branches, and head office keeps all their records.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
In the branch account, the **opening** inventory and receivables are **debits** (assets at the branch at the start) and the **closing** ones are **credits** carried down. Swapping them is a common error.
:::

## Key points

- **Branch accounts** find the profit of each branch.
- A **dependent branch** gets goods and has its records kept by head office; an **independent branch** keeps its own books.
- **Branch account method:** debit opening assets, goods sent and expenses; credit cash remitted and closing assets; the balance is **branch profit**.
- Check the profit with a **branch trading account**.

## Quick check

1. Distinguish a dependent branch from an independent branch.
2. Give two purposes of branch accounts.
3. On which side of the branch account are goods sent to the branch?
4. Opening branch receivables NLe 2,000, cash collected NLe 18,000, closing receivables NLe 4,000. Find credit sales.
5. How is the branch profit transferred?

**Answers:**

1. A dependent branch does not keep full books and is supplied and controlled by head office; an independent branch keeps a full set of books of its own.
2. Any two: find each branch's profit; control inventory, cash and receivables at branches; compare branches; detect losses.
3. The debit side.
4. $18\,000 + 4\,000 - 2\,000 = 20\,000$: NLe 20,000.
5. Debit the branch account, credit the income statement.
