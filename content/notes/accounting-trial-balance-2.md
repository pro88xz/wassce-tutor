:::callout{type=definition}
A **wrongly prepared trial balance** is one in which some balances have been placed on the **wrong side**, so the totals do not agree.
:::

Redrafting it correctly is a common WASSCE task: you must place each balance on its **correct side** using the rules of debit and credit, then find any missing figure. This lesson shows a step-by-step method for **correcting a trial balance**, how to **find missing figures**, and how to tell whether a difference is caused by misplaced balances or by errors in the books.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- identify balances placed on the wrong side of a trial balance
- redraft a trial balance with every balance on its correct side
- find a missing capital or other figure from a redrafted trial balance
- explain the effect on the totals of a balance placed on the wrong side
- distinguish misplaced balances from errors in the ledger

## The effect of a misplaced balance

If a balance is placed on the **wrong side**, one total is **too large** by that amount and the other **too small** by the same amount, so the difference between the totals is **twice** the misplaced amount.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A rent received balance of NLe 3,000 is wrongly listed as a debit. What is the effect?**

The debit total is 3,000 too high and the credit total 3,000 too low, so the totals differ by $2 \times 3\,000 = 6\,000$.

A clue for examiners and candidates: when a trial balance difference is **even**, look for a balance of **half** the difference that may be on the wrong side.
:::

## Method for correcting a trial balance

1. Go through the list **one balance at a time**.
2. Decide whether it is an **asset, expense, drawings** (debit) or a **liability, capital, income** (credit).
3. Write it in the correct column.
4. **Total** both columns.
5. If a figure such as **capital** is missing or wrong, it is the **difference** between the totals.

## Misplaced balances or ledger errors?

| Misplaced balance | Error in the ledger |
|---|---|
| the ledger is correct; only the **list** is wrong | the **ledger account** itself is wrong |
| corrected by **moving** the balance to the other column | corrected by a **journal entry** (and often the suspense account) |
| difference is **twice** the misplaced amount | difference depends on the error |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A **bank overdraft** is a **credit** balance, even though it appears in the bank column of the cash book. Many candidates put it with "bank" on the debit side. An overdraft is money **owed to** the bank: a liability.
:::

## Key points

- A balance on the **wrong side** changes the difference between the totals by **twice** its amount.
- Correct a trial balance by classifying **each balance**: assets, expenses, drawings **debit**; liabilities, capital, income **credit**.
- A **missing capital** figure is the difference between the corrected totals.
- Misplaced balances are fixed by **redrafting**; ledger errors need **journal entries**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
"**Redraft the trial balance correctly**" is a frequent question. Set out a clean two-column trial balance, put each item on its correct side, total both columns, and show how you found any missing figure. Watch especially for returns, discounts, the bank overdraft, drawings and income items.
:::

## Quick check

1. A discount received balance of NLe 900 is put on the debit side. By how much do the totals differ?
2. On which side should carriage outwards appear?
3. On which side should rent received appear?
4. Corrected debits total NLe 96,000 and credits other than capital NLe 61,000. Find the capital.
5. How is a misplaced balance corrected?

**Answers:**

1. $2 \times 900 = 1\,800$: NLe 1,800.
2. The debit side (it is an expense).
3. The credit side (it is income).
4. $96\,000 - 61\,000 = 35\,000$: NLe 35,000.
5. By moving it to the correct column when the trial balance is redrafted; no journal entry is needed because the ledger is correct.
