:::callout{type=definition}
**Practical computer skills** are the hands-on abilities needed to use computers safely and well: working safely in a computer laboratory, sitting and typing correctly, connecting devices to the right ports, installing software, caring for equipment and solving common problems.
:::

Knowing what a computer is matters less if you cannot sit at one for an hour without backache, find the right port for a projector, or fix a printer that will not print. This lesson covers **computer laboratory rules and safety**, **ergonomics** and the health problems of computer use, **touch typing** and the home row keys, **computer ports** and connecting devices, **installing and uninstalling software**, the care of computers, and **basic troubleshooting** of common problems.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the rules and safety precautions for a computer laboratory
- explain ergonomics and describe a healthy computer workstation
- describe health problems linked to computer use and how to prevent them
- place the fingers on the home row keys and explain the benefits of touch typing
- identify common ports and the devices they connect
- install and uninstall software, take a screenshot and solve common computer problems

## Computer laboratory rules and safety

- **No food or drink** near computers: crumbs and spills damage keyboards and can cause short circuits.
- Enter, move and leave **calmly**; do not run or play in the laboratory.
- Handle equipment with **dry hands**; never open a system unit or touch exposed wires.
- Keep **bags and cables** off the floor and away from walkways, so no one trips.
- Use **only your own account**, and do not change settings or install software without permission.
- **Report** faults, sparks or burning smells to the teacher at once; do not try to repair them yourself.
- **Shut down** properly and cover the computers at the end of the lesson.
- Know where the **fire extinguisher** is: a **carbon dioxide (CO₂)** extinguisher is used for electrical fires, never water.

## Ergonomics and health

:::callout{type=definition}
**Ergonomics:** the study of designing equipment, furniture and the way people work so that they are comfortable, safe and efficient, and do not injure themselves.

**Repetitive strain injury (RSI):** pain and damage to the muscles, tendons and nerves of the hands, wrists, arms or neck, caused by repeating the same movements, such as typing or using a mouse, for long periods.

**Computer vision syndrome (eye strain):** tired, dry, sore eyes, blurred vision and headaches caused by looking at a screen for a long time without rest.
:::

![A healthy computer workstation seen from the side, with numbered points: 1, the top of the screen at or just below eye level; 2, the screen about an arm's length away, 50 to 70 centimetres; 3, the back upright and supported by the chair; 4, elbows at about 90 degrees with forearms level; 5, wrists straight with the keyboard and mouse close; 6, thighs level and knees at about 90 degrees; 7, feet flat on the floor or on a footrest. Every 20 minutes, look at something 6 metres away for 20 seconds, and take regular breaks](https://wasscetutor.com/img/ict/ergonomic-workstation.svg)

*Figure 1. Sitting correctly at a computer. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Health problem | Cause | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| **Back and neck pain** | poor posture; a screen too low or too high | adjustable chair with back support; screen at eye level; sit upright |
| **RSI** in wrists and hands | long periods of typing and clicking | keep wrists straight; take breaks; use a wrist rest; stretch the hands |
| **Eye strain** | staring at a screen; glare; small text | the **20-20-20 rule**; reduce glare; adjust brightness and text size |
| **Headaches** | eye strain, glare, poor lighting, stress | good lighting; regular breaks |
| **Electric shock and fire** | damaged cables, overloaded sockets | check cables; do not overload sockets; keep liquids away |

The **20-20-20 rule**: every **20** minutes, look at something about **20** feet (6 metres) away for **20** seconds.

## Touch typing

:::callout{type=definition}
**Touch typing:** typing with all ten fingers without looking at the keyboard, each finger responsible for its own set of keys.

**Home row keys:** the middle row of letter keys, A S D F for the left hand and J K L ; for the right hand, where the fingers rest between keystrokes.
:::

![The home row keys: the left little, ring, middle and index fingers rest on A, S, D and F; the right index, middle, ring and little fingers rest on J, K, L and semicolon; the thumbs rest on the space bar. Small raised bumps on F and J let the index fingers find the home row without looking](https://wasscetutor.com/img/ict/home-row-keys.svg)

*Figure 2. Finger positions on the home row keys. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

**Benefits of touch typing:** faster typing; fewer errors; less tiredness, because the eyes stay on the screen or the document; and better posture.

Typing speed is measured in **words per minute (WPM)**, where a "word" is counted as five characters. A beginner may type 15 to 20 WPM; a practised touch typist 40 WPM or more. Free typing-tutor programs and websites help you practise.

## Ports and connecting devices

:::callout{type=definition}
**Port:** a socket on a computer or other device into which a cable or plug is connected, so that data or power can pass between the devices.

**USB (Universal Serial Bus):** a standard port and cable used to connect many kinds of device, such as flash drives, keyboards, mice, printers and phones, and to charge them.

**HDMI (High-Definition Multimedia Interface):** a port that carries digital video and sound together, to a monitor, TV or projector.

**Plug and play:** the ability of a computer to recognise a new device as soon as it is connected and to set it up automatically.
:::

![Eight common ports and what they connect: USB-A for flash drives, mouse, keyboard and printer; USB-C for charging, data and video on new devices; HDMI for digital video and sound to a TV or projector; VGA for older analogue video to monitors and projectors; Ethernet (RJ-45) for a wired network cable; the 3.5 mm audio jack for headphones, speakers and microphone; the SD card slot for camera and phone memory cards; and the power (DC in) socket for the charger](https://wasscetutor.com/img/ict/computer-ports.svg)

*Figure 3. Common ports on a computer. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

**Connecting a projector for a presentation:** switch on the projector; connect the laptop's **HDMI** (or older **VGA**) port to the projector with the right cable; press **Windows + P** and choose **Duplicate** to show the same screen on both; adjust the focus.

## Installing and uninstalling software

**Installing:** download the program from the **official website** or the app store (or use the installation disc); run the setup file; read and accept the **licence agreement**; choose where to install it; finish and restart if asked. You may need an **administrator** password.

**Uninstalling:** open **Settings → Apps** (or **Control Panel → Programs and Features**), choose the program and click **Uninstall**. Do not just delete the program's folder, because that leaves pieces of the program behind.

**Keep software updated** so that faults and security weaknesses are fixed.

## Caring for computers

- keep computers in a **cool, dry, dust-free** room; cover them with **dust covers** after use
- do not block the **air vents**; overheating damages parts
- clean the screen with a **soft, dry microfibre cloth**, never with water sprayed on it
- connect computers through a **stabiliser or surge protector** and a UPS
- handle **flash drives and discs** carefully and **scan** them for viruses
- carry laptops in a **padded bag**, and do not lift them by the screen

## Basic troubleshooting

:::callout{type=definition}
**Troubleshooting:** finding the cause of a problem with a computer or device by checking the most likely causes one at a time, and then fixing it.

**Screenshot (screen capture):** a picture of what is on the screen, saved as an image file.
:::

| Problem | What to check or do |
|---|---|
| **Computer will not switch on** | is the plug in and switched on at the wall? Is the power cable firm? Is the laptop battery charged? |
| **No picture on the monitor** | is the monitor switched on? Is its cable connected to the right port? Is the brightness turned down? |
| **A program has frozen** | wait a moment; then open **Task Manager** (Ctrl + Shift + Esc), choose the program and click **End task** |
| **No Internet** | is Wi-Fi on, or the network cable plugged in? Is there mobile data credit? Restart the router |
| **Printer will not print** | is it on, with paper and ink or toner? Is it connected and chosen as the printer? Clear any **paper jam** and stuck jobs in the print queue |
| **Mouse or keyboard not working** | check the cable or USB receiver, and the batteries if wireless; try another USB port |
| **Computer very slow** | close unused programs; restart; free up disk space; run a virus scan |

**Taking a screenshot:** in Windows press **PrtScn** (the whole screen is copied, ready to paste) or **Windows + Shift + S** (choose an area); on most Android phones press **Power + Volume down** together.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Getting the school projector to work.** Before an assembly in Port Loko the laptop showed nothing on the projector. The ICT prefect checked the **HDMI** cable (loose), pushed it in firmly, pressed **Windows + P** and chose **Duplicate**, and the slides appeared: troubleshooting by checking the simplest cause first.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Using water on an electrical fire.** Water conducts electricity and can cause an electric shock. Switch off the power if it is safe to, and use a **CO₂** extinguisher.
:::

## Key points

- Follow **laboratory rules**: no food or drink, dry hands, tidy cables, report faults, **CO₂** extinguisher for electrical fires.
- **Ergonomics**: screen at eye level and an arm's length away, back supported, elbows and knees about 90°, wrists straight, feet flat.
- Health problems: **back pain, RSI, eye strain, headaches**; use the **20-20-20 rule** and take breaks.
- **Touch typing** from the **home row** (ASDF JKL;) is faster and more accurate; speed is measured in **WPM**.
- Ports: **USB, USB-C, HDMI, VGA, Ethernet, audio jack, SD slot, power**; **plug and play** sets devices up automatically.
- Install from official sources; uninstall through **Settings**; **troubleshoot** the simplest causes first.
