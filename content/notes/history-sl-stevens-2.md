The **economy of Sierra Leone under Siaka Stevens (1968–1985)** declined badly despite the country's rich **diamonds, iron ore, rutile and bauxite**. **Diamond smuggling** and illegal mining drained state revenue, **corruption and patronage** enriched a small group of insiders, the **Marampa iron-ore mine closed in 1975**, and the **oil price rises** of the 1970s and the cost of the **1980 OAU summit** added to the crisis. The results were **shortages**, **inflation**, **unpaid salaries**, the **collapse of schools and hospitals**, and the emigration of educated people: conditions that helped to produce the **civil war of 1991**. This lesson explains **the economic and social problems of the Stevens era** and their consequences.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe Sierra Leone's main economic resources in the 1970s and 1980s
- explain the causes of economic decline under Siaka Stevens
- describe the effects of economic decline on ordinary people
- explain the role of corruption and patronage in the political economy
- describe the social changes of the period
- explain how economic decline contributed to later conflict

## Sierra Leone's resources

| Resource | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Diamonds** | mined in **Kono** and around **Tongo**; the **National Diamond Mining Company (NDMC)** was formed in **1970**, when the government took a **51% share** of the old SLST |
| **Iron ore** | mined at **Marampa** by **DELCO** until the mine **closed in 1975** |
| **Rutile** | mined from **1967** by **Sierra Rutile** in **Moyamba and Bonthe** districts; became a major export |
| **Bauxite** | mined at **Mokanji** in Moyamba District |
| **Agriculture** | **coffee, cocoa, palm kernels, ginger**; rice for food, though the country increasingly **imported rice** |

## Causes of economic decline

![Why the economy declined under Stevens: causes were diamond smuggling, corruption and patronage, mining decline including the closure of the Marampa iron-ore mine in 1975, oil price rises in 1973 and 1979, and the 1980 OAU summit; effects were shortages of fuel, rice and power, inflation, unpaid salaries, the collapse of schools, hospitals and roads, and a brain drain](https://wasscetutor.com/img/history/stevens-economy.svg)

*Figure 1. Why the economy declined under Stevens. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Cause | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Diamond smuggling** | much of the diamond output was **mined illegally and smuggled** out of the country, so the state lost revenue; official diamond exports fell sharply in the 1970s and 1980s |
| **Corruption and patronage** | ministers, officials and business allies, some of them powerful **Lebanese** businessmen, gained control of trade and mining deals; public money was diverted |
| **Mining decline** | the closure of the **Marampa iron-ore mine (1975)** and falling official diamond output cut exports and jobs |
| **Oil price rises** | the **1973 and 1979** oil shocks raised the cost of fuel and imports |
| **The OAU summit (1980)** | huge spending on hotels, villas and cars, and scandals over payments (the "**Vouchergate**" affair of 1981) |
| **Mismanagement of state enterprises** | marketing boards and state companies made losses; farmers received **low prices** and grew less |
| **Debt** | the government borrowed heavily and fell behind on repayments |

## Effects on ordinary people

1. **Shortages** of **fuel, rice, electricity** and other basic goods; long queues.
2. **Inflation**: the **leone lost value**, and prices rose much faster than wages.
3. **Unpaid salaries**: teachers, nurses and civil servants went **months without pay**.
4. **Collapse of public services**: schools, hospitals, roads and water supplies deteriorated.
5. **Brain drain**: many **educated people emigrated** to other countries.
6. **Youth unemployment**: young people in the towns and mining areas had **few jobs or prospects**.
7. **Protests**: the **1981 general strike** called by the **Sierra Leone Labour Congress** against rising prices and shortages.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Patronage** is a system in which those in power give **jobs, contracts, money or favours** to supporters in return for **loyalty**, rather than on merit. Under Stevens, patronage kept the APC in power but weakened the state and encouraged corruption.
:::

## Social changes

1. **Urbanisation**: people moved to **Freetown** and the mining towns looking for work.
2. **Growth of informal trade and illegal mining**, especially among young men in **Kono**.
3. **Decline of education quality**, though student numbers grew.
4. **Growing gap** between a small wealthy elite and the poor majority.
5. Increasing **frustration among youth**, many of whom felt excluded from politics and the economy.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**How economic decline helped to cause the civil war**

By the late 1980s, many young Sierra Leoneans were **unemployed, poorly educated and angry** at a corrupt one-party government that seemed to offer them nothing. Public services had collapsed, and the **army was poorly paid and weak**. When the **RUF** invaded from Liberia in **1991**, it found recruits among these young people, and diamonds gave it a way to **finance the war**. The Truth and Reconciliation Commission later named **bad governance, corruption and the marginalisation of youth** as root causes of the conflict.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many Sierra Leoneans who left during this period formed a large **diaspora** in Britain, the United States and other countries. Today their **remittances** (money sent home to families) are an important source of income for many households in Sierra Leone.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Sierra Leone's economic decline was **not** caused by a lack of natural resources. The country was rich in **diamonds, rutile, bauxite and iron ore**. The problem was **how the resources were managed**: smuggling, corruption, patronage and poor policies.
:::

## Key figures and terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figures and terms"}
- **Diamond smuggling**: taking diamonds out of the country illegally, which robbed the government of revenue.
- **Brain drain**: the emigration of educated and skilled people, such as doctors and teachers, to work abroad.
:::

## Key points

- Resources: **diamonds (NDMC, 1970)**, **iron ore (Marampa, closed 1975)**, **rutile (from 1967)**, **bauxite**, cash crops.
- Causes of decline: **diamond smuggling, corruption and patronage, mining decline, oil shocks, OAU summit costs, mismanagement, debt**.
- Effects: **shortages, inflation, unpaid salaries, collapse of services, brain drain, youth unemployment**, the **1981 general strike**.
- Economic decline and **exclusion of youth** helped to create the conditions for the **civil war (1991)**.
