Angles are the starting point of all geometry. Almost every geometry question in WASSCE, whether about triangles, polygons, circles, bearings or construction, ends with *"find the angle marked $x$"*, and it is solved by combining a small set of facts. This lesson teaches those facts, the reason each is true, and how to set out your working so that every step earns its mark.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name types of angle and measure them in degrees
- use angles on a straight line, at a point, and vertically opposite
- identify corresponding, alternate and co-interior angles on parallel lines
- use the angle sum of a triangle and the exterior angle theorem
- solve multi-step angle problems, giving a reason for each step

## Types of angle

| Name | Size |
|---|---|
| acute | between $0°$ and $90°$ |
| right | exactly $90°$ |
| obtuse | between $90°$ and $180°$ |
| straight | exactly $180°$ |
| reflex | between $180°$ and $360°$ |

Two angles are **complementary** if they add up to $90°$, and **supplementary** if they add up to $180°$.

## Three basic facts

![Three panels: angles a and b on a straight line add to 180 degrees; angles p, q and r at a point add to 360 degrees; vertically opposite angles x and x are equal, as are y and y](https://wasscetutor.com/img/mathematics/angle-facts.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
1. **Angles on a straight line** add up to $180°$.
2. **Angles at a point** add up to $360°$.
3. **Vertically opposite angles** (formed where two straight lines cross) are **equal**.
:::

## Parallel lines

When a straight line (a **transversal**) crosses two **parallel** lines, it makes eight angles, and they are linked:

![Three panels: corresponding angles in an F shape are equal; alternate angles in a Z shape are equal; co-interior angles in a C shape add up to 180 degrees](https://wasscetutor.com/img/mathematics/angles-parallel-lines.svg)

| Pair | Shape | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **corresponding** | **F** | equal |
| **alternate** | **Z** | equal |
| **co-interior** (allied) | **C** or **U** | add up to $180°$ |

:::callout{type=tip}
Arrows (›) on two lines in a diagram mean they are **parallel**. If there are no arrows and the question doesn't say so, you **cannot** assume lines are parallel.
:::

## Triangles

![Triangle ABC with interior angles a, b and c, and side BC extended past C to show the exterior angle at C, which equals a + b](https://wasscetutor.com/img/mathematics/triangle-angles.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
- The **angles of a triangle** add up to $180°$.
- The **exterior angle** of a triangle equals the **sum of the two interior opposite angles**.
- In an **isosceles** triangle, the angles opposite the equal sides are equal.
- In an **equilateral** triangle, every angle is $60°$.
:::

**Why the exterior angle rule works:** the exterior angle at C and the interior angle $c$ lie on a straight line, so exterior $= 180° - c$. But $a + b = 180° - c$ too. So the exterior angle $= a + b$.

## Setting out your working

Examiners award marks for **reasons**. Write each step as *statement (reason)*:

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**In the diagram, $AB \parallel CD$. A transversal makes an angle of $65°$ with $AB$ at P, and meets $CD$ at Q. Find the angle $x$ between the transversal and $CD$ on the same side (co-interior), and the angle $y$ alternate to the $65°$.**

$x = 180° - 65° = 115°$ (co-interior angles, $AB \parallel CD$)

$y = 65°$ (alternate angles, $AB \parallel CD$)
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**In triangle PQR, $\angle P = 2x$, $\angle Q = 3x$ and $\angle R = 4x$. Find the angles.**

$$2x + 3x + 4x = 180° \quad\text{(angle sum of a triangle)}$$
$$9x = 180° \;\Rightarrow\; x = 20°$$

**$\angle P = 40°$, $\angle Q = 60°$, $\angle R = 80°$.**
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**An isosceles triangle has $AB = AC$ and $\angle A = 48°$. Side $BC$ is extended to D. Find $\angle ACD$.**

$\angle B = \angle C = \dfrac{180° - 48°}{2} = 66°$ (base angles of an isosceles triangle)

$\angle ACD = 180° - 66° = 114°$ (angles on a straight line)

**Check with the exterior angle theorem:** $\angle ACD = \angle A + \angle B = 48° + 66° = 114°$ ✓
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Using "Z angles" or "F angles" as the reason.** These shapes help you **spot** the pairs, but the reason you write should be the proper name: *alternate angles*, *corresponding angles*, *co-interior angles*, and you should state that the lines are **parallel**.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Roof trusses on houses across Sierra Leone are triangles of timber. A carpenter making a symmetrical truss whose top angle is $100°$ knows each base angle is $\dfrac{180° - 100°}{2} = 40°$, the angle at which the rafters must be cut to sit on the wall plate.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Two angles on a straight line are (3x + 10)° and (2x − 5)°. Find x and both angles."}
$3x + 10 + 2x - 5 = 180 \Rightarrow 5x + 5 = 180 \Rightarrow x = 35$.

Angles: $3(35) + 10 = 115°$ and $2(35) - 5 = 65°$. ($115 + 65 = 180$ ✓)
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Angles at a point are 90°, 125°, x and 2x. Find x."}
$90 + 125 + 3x = 360 \Rightarrow 3x = 145 \Rightarrow x = 48\tfrac13°$.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. In triangle ABC, ∠A = 52° and the exterior angle at C is 118°. Find ∠B."}
Exterior angle $= \angle A + \angle B$: $118 = 52 + \angle B \Rightarrow \angle B = 66°$.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One of a pair of co-interior angles is 4 times the other. Find both."}
$x + 4x = 180 \Rightarrow x = 36$. **$36°$ and $144°$.**
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Find the complement and the supplement of 37°."}
Complement $= 90 - 37 = 53°$; supplement $= 180 - 37 = 143°$.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Acute angle**: an angle less than $90°$.
- **Obtuse angle**: an angle between $90°$ and $180°$.
- **Reflex angle**: an angle between $180°$ and $360°$.
- **Vertically opposite angles**: the equal angles opposite each other where two straight lines cross.
- **Corresponding angles**: angles in matching positions where a line crosses parallel lines (an F shape); they are equal.
- **Alternate angles**: angles on opposite sides of a line crossing parallel lines (a Z shape); they are equal.
- **Co-interior angles**: angles between parallel lines on the same side of a crossing line (a C or U shape); they add up to $180°$.
- **Supplementary angles**: two angles that add up to $180°$.
- **Complementary angles**: two angles that add up to $90°$.
:::

## Key points

- **Straight line**: $180°$. **At a point**: $360°$. **Vertically opposite**: equal.
- **Parallel lines**: corresponding (F) and alternate (Z) are **equal**; co-interior (C) add to $180°$.
- **Triangle**: angles add to $180°$; **exterior angle = sum of the interior opposite angles**.
- Isosceles: base angles equal. Equilateral: all $60°$.
- Give a **reason** for every step, and state when lines are parallel.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Angle facts appear as objective questions and inside almost every geometry theory question. Examiners award marks for **correct reasons** as well as correct values, so write *"(alternate angles, $AB \parallel CD$)"* rather than leaving a bare number.
:::
