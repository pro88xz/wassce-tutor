In the early Colony, the Governor ruled with a few British officials, and Sierra Leoneans had almost no say in making the laws that governed them. Change came slowly, through a series of **colonial constitutions** that gradually admitted Africans into government. This lesson covers the first two milestones: the **1863 Constitution**, which created the Executive and Legislative Councils, and the **1924 Slater Constitution**, which introduced the first elected members.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe Crown Colony government in Sierra Leone
- state the features of the 1863 Constitution
- state the features, merits and demerits of the 1924 Slater Constitution
- explain the importance of these constitutions in Sierra Leone's political development

## Crown Colony government

:::callout{type=definition}
**Crown Colony government** was the system under which a British colony was ruled by a **Governor** appointed by the British Crown, assisted by councils made up mainly of **colonial officials**, with final authority resting with the **Secretary of State for the Colonies** in London.
:::

## The 1863 Constitution

In **1863**, letters patent created two councils for the Colony of Sierra Leone:

| Council | Composition | Functions |
|---|---|---|
| **Executive Council** | The Governor and senior colonial officials | Advised the Governor on administration and policy |
| **Legislative Council (LegCo)** | The Governor, official members and a few **nominated unofficial members** | Made laws (ordinances) for the Colony, subject to the Governor's approval and London's control |

**Features of the 1863 Constitution:**

1. The **Governor** presided over both councils and had a **veto** over legislation.
2. **Official majority** in the Legislative Council: colonial officials outnumbered unofficial members.
3. **Unofficial members were nominated** by the Governor, not elected; some were Krio merchants and professionals.
4. Laws applied only to the **Colony**.
5. The British Crown could **disallow** any ordinance.

**Significance:** for the first time, a few Africans sat in the colonial legislature, even though they had little power.

## The 1924 Slater Constitution

![The 1924 Legislative Council: the Governor presiding; official members forming the majority; unofficial members including 3 elected by Colony voters, 3 nominated Paramount Chiefs and others nominated by the Governor](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/slater-legco.svg)

*Figure 1. The Legislative Council under the 1924 Slater Constitution. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

The constitution introduced under Governor **Alexander Ransford Slater** in **1924** followed demands by the **National Congress of British West Africa** (NCBWA) for elected representation.

**Features:**

1. The Legislative Council kept an **official majority**.
2. For the first time, **three members were elected**: two for Freetown and one for the rural area of the Colony.
3. The **franchise was limited** by income or property and literacy, so few people could vote.
4. **Three Paramount Chiefs** were **nominated** to represent the **Protectorate**: the first Protectorate representation in the legislature.
5. Other unofficial members were **nominated** by the Governor.
6. The **Governor** presided and kept **veto and reserved powers**.
7. The **Executive Council** remained made up of officials.

### Merits

- Introduced the **elective principle** into Sierra Leone's government for the first time.
- Gave the **Protectorate** a voice in the legislature for the first time.
- Encouraged **political activity**: the NCBWA won all three elected seats in 1924.
- Brought **Colony and Protectorate representatives** together in one council.

### Demerits

- The **official majority** meant officials could always outvote Africans.
- **Limited franchise**: only a small, wealthy, educated minority could vote.
- **Paramount Chiefs were nominated, not elected**, and could not easily oppose the Governor who appointed them.
- The **Protectorate**, with most of the population, had only three nominated members.
- The **Governor's veto** and London's control remained.
- **No African in the Executive Council**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
The three elected seats in **1924** were all won by candidates of the **National Congress of British West Africa**, whose Sierra Leone section included the Krio doctor and politician **H. C. Bankole-Bright**. He became one of the most outspoken members of the Legislative Council and, in 1950, founded the **National Council of Sierra Leone**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The 1924 constitution introduced **elections** but **not** an African majority or African control. Officials still held the majority. The first constitution to give Africans a **majority** in the Legislative Council was the **1951** constitution.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Executive Council**: the Governor and senior colonial officials, who advised him on government.
- **Legislative Council** (LegCo): the colonial law-making body of the Governor, official members and a few nominated unofficial members.
- **Official majority**: the arrangement in which colonial officials outnumbered unofficial members in the Legislative Council.
- **Limited franchise**: a right to vote held only by a small, wealthy, educated minority.
- **Slater Constitution** (1924): the constitution that brought the first three elected members, chosen on a limited franchise in the Colony, and three nominated Paramount Chiefs.
:::

## Key points

- **Crown Colony government**: a Governor ruling with councils of officials, under London.
- **1863**: **Executive and Legislative Councils** created; official majority; nominated unofficial members; Governor's veto.
- **1924 Slater Constitution**: **first 3 elected members** (Colony, limited franchise); **3 Paramount Chiefs nominated** for the Protectorate; **official majority** retained.
- The NCBWA won the first elections; Bankole-Bright was a leading member.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **state the features of the 1863 and 1924 constitutions**, to **give the merits and demerits of the Slater Constitution**, and to **explain its importance** in Sierra Leone's constitutional development. Questions often ask what was "new" in 1924: the elective principle and Protectorate representation.
:::

## Quick check

1. Which two councils did the 1863 Constitution create?
2. How many elected members did the 1924 Constitution introduce?
3. How was the Protectorate represented under the 1924 Constitution?
4. State two demerits of the Slater Constitution.

**Answers:**

1. The Executive Council and the Legislative Council.
2. Three, elected by voters in the Colony.
3. By three Paramount Chiefs nominated by the Governor.
4. Any two: official majority; limited franchise; Paramount Chiefs nominated, not elected; Protectorate under-represented; Governor's veto retained; no African in the Executive Council.
