The **economy of colonial Sierra Leone** in the early twentieth century was built on **exports of raw materials** carried by the new **Sierra Leone Government Railway**, begun in **1896** and extended to **Bo (1903)** and **Pendembu (1908)**, with a branch to **Makeni**. At first the main exports were **palm kernels**, **kola nuts** and **piassava**; from the **1930s**, **minerals** took over, with **iron ore** mined at **Marampa** (exported from **1933**) and **diamonds** discovered in **Kono (1930)**. Trade was increasingly controlled by **European firms** and **Lebanese traders**, while **schools** such as **Bo School (1906)** and health services grew slowly. This lesson explains **the economic and social development of colonial Sierra Leone up to 1945**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

## The Sierra Leone Government Railway

![Map of Sierra Leone showing the narrow-gauge Government Railway begun in 1896, running from Freetown through Bauya and Bo (1903) to Pendembu (1908), with a branch from Bauya to Makeni; the Marampa–Pepel mining railway of 1933; and the Kono diamond fields, where diamonds were found in 1930](https://wasscetutor.com/img/history/sl-railway-map.svg)

*Figure 1. The Sierra Leone Government Railway and early mining (routes simplified). WASSCE Tutor map.*

| Date | Development |
|---|---|
| **1896** | construction begins at **Freetown** |
| **1903** | railway reaches **Bo** |
| **1908** | main line reaches **Pendembu**, near the Liberian border, about **365 km** from Freetown |
| **1910s** | branch line from **Bauya** to **Makeni** in the north |
| **1970s** | the railway is closed and its track removed |

**Why it was built:** to carry **palm kernels and other produce** to Freetown for export, to **open the Protectorate** to trade, and to move **troops and officials** quickly after the Hut Tax War.

**Effects of the railway:**

1. **Export trade grew**, especially in palm kernels from Mende country.
2. **New towns grew** along the line, such as **Bo**, **Kenema**, **Moyamba** and **Makeni**; Bo became the main town of the south.
3. **Krio traders' monopoly broke down** as European and Lebanese firms opened shops at railway stations.
4. People moved for **work and trade**, mixing ethnic groups.
5. It was **narrow-gauge and slow**, ran at a loss, and was later replaced by **roads**.

## Agricultural exports

| Product | Importance |
|---|---|
| **Palm kernels and palm oil** | the **main export** until the 1930s; used in Europe for soap, margarine and cattle feed |
| **Kola nuts** | sold to other West African countries |
| **Piassava** | a palm fibre for brooms and brushes, from the Sherbro |
| **Ginger** | grown in the south |
| **Cocoa and coffee** | grown in the east (Kailahun and Kenema), growing in importance later |

Farmers grew **rice and cassava** for food, but colonial policy encouraged **cash crops for export** rather than industry.

## Minerals

1. **Diamonds:** discovered in **Kono** in **1930** by the colonial **Geological Survey**. From **1935** the **Sierra Leone Selection Trust (SLST)**, a subsidiary of a British company, was given a **monopoly** to mine diamonds for ninety-nine years.
2. **Iron ore:** found at **Marampa** (near Lunsar) in the **1920s**. The **Sierra Leone Development Company (DELCO)** built a mining railway to the port of **Pepel** and began exports in **1933**.
3. **Gold, platinum and chromite** were also mined in small amounts in the 1930s.

Minerals soon became Sierra Leone's **most valuable exports**, but most **profits went abroad**, and mining areas saw **little local development**.

## European firms and Lebanese traders

1. **European firms** such as the **United Africa Company (UAC, 1929)**, **Paterson Zochonis** and **G. B. Ollivant** controlled **import and export trade**, bought produce and set prices.
2. **Lebanese ("Syrian") traders** arrived from the **1890s**; they opened shops in Protectorate towns and along the railway, buying produce and selling goods, and soon dominated **retail trade**.
3. The **Krio traders**, who had led trade in the nineteenth century, were **pushed out**, which caused resentment, shown in the **1919 riots** in Freetown.

## Education and health

1. **Bo School (1906)** was founded for the **sons and nominees of chiefs**, to train future chiefs and clerks for the Protectorate.
2. **Mission schools** expanded in the Protectorate, run by the CMS, Methodists, Catholics and the American missions.
3. **Prince of Wales School (1925)**, a government secondary school, opened in Freetown.
4. **Fourah Bay College** continued as the main higher institution.
5. **Health** services were limited: hospitals in Freetown and a few dispensaries in the Protectorate. In **1926**, **Milton Margai** became the **first doctor from the Protectorate**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Assessing colonial economic policy**

**Benefits:** a railway, roads, ports, mines, new towns, growth of trade and some schools and health services.

**Drawbacks:** the economy depended on a few **raw-material exports**; there was **no industry**; **profits from minerals went abroad**; **European firms and Lebanese traders** controlled trade; and services were **concentrated in Freetown**, leaving the Protectorate poor. These weaknesses remained at independence.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
The old railway still lives on in Freetown's memory: the **Sierra Leone National Railway Museum** at **Cline Town** displays locomotives and carriages from the line, including a coach built for a royal visit, and tells the story of the railway that once linked Freetown to the east.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Diamonds were **not** discovered in Sierra Leone until **1930**. In the early colonial period, the main exports were **agricultural**, especially **palm kernels**. Minerals became dominant only in the **1930s**.
:::

## Key points

- **Railway:** begun **1896**; **Bo 1903**; **Pendembu 1908**; branch to **Makeni**; built for trade and control; closed in the **1970s**.
- Agricultural exports: **palm kernels** (main), kola, piassava, ginger, later **cocoa and coffee**.
- Minerals: **diamonds in Kono (1930)**, **SLST monopoly (1935)**; **iron ore at Marampa**, **DELCO**, exports from **1933**.
- Trade controlled by **European firms (UAC)** and **Lebanese traders**; Krio pushed out.
- **Bo School (1906)**, **Prince of Wales School (1925)**; **Milton Margai** first Protectorate doctor (**1926**).

## Quick check

1. When was the railway begun, and when did it reach Bo?
2. Give three effects of the railway.
3. What was the main export of Sierra Leone before the 1930s?
4. When and where were diamonds discovered?
5. Which company mined iron ore at Marampa?
6. Why was Bo School founded?

**Answers:**

1. In 1896; it reached Bo in 1903.
2. Any three: growth of export trade; growth of towns along the line; the Krio trade monopoly broke down; movement and mixing of people; faster movement of troops and officials.
3. Palm kernels.
4. In 1930, in Kono.
5. The Sierra Leone Development Company (DELCO).
6. To educate the sons and nominees of chiefs as future chiefs and clerks for the Protectorate.
