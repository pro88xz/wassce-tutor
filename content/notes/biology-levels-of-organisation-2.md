# Levels of organisation: organ systems, coordination and examples

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- trace the organisation of named structures in animals and plants
- explain how organ systems depend on one another
- relate specialised structure to function at more than one level
- interpret biological organisation diagrams and answer comparison questions

## Following one function through several levels

The levels of organisation become easier to understand when you follow a function from a specialised cell to the whole organism.

Consider the transport of oxygen in a human:

1. **Cell:** a red blood cell contains haemoglobin, which combines reversibly with oxygen.
2. **Tissue:** blood is a connective tissue. Its plasma transports dissolved substances, while red blood cells carry most of the oxygen.
3. **Organ:** the heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood.
4. **Organ system:** the circulatory system consists of the heart, blood and blood vessels. It distributes oxygen to tissues.
5. **Organism:** the human body depends on this system, together with the respiratory system, to supply oxygen to cells throughout the body.

The levels are linked, not isolated. A failure at one level can affect the levels above it. If the heart cannot pump effectively, tissues may receive insufficient oxygen, reducing the ability of the whole organism to function.

Consider photosynthesis in a flowering plant:

1. **Cell:** a palisade mesophyll cell contains numerous chloroplasts.
2. **Tissue:** palisade mesophyll tissue forms much of the photosynthetic region of a leaf.
3. **Organ:** the leaf receives light and exchanges gases with the atmosphere.
4. **Organ systems:** the root system supplies water and mineral ions; the shoot system positions leaves and transports materials.
5. **Organism:** the plant uses the products of photosynthesis in respiration, growth and storage.

This example shows why the functions of plant organs are interdependent.

## Organ systems do not operate independently

The digestive, respiratory and circulatory systems cooperate to supply cells with materials for respiration. Digestion breaks large, insoluble food molecules into smaller soluble molecules. The small intestine absorbs many of these nutrients into the blood. The lungs transfer oxygen into the blood. The circulatory system carries glucose and oxygen to cells, where aerobic respiration releases energy in a usable form.

The excretory system also supports the internal environment. The kidneys remove nitrogenous waste and regulate water and salt levels in the blood. The nervous and endocrine systems coordinate activities by transmitting nerve impulses and releasing hormones.

In plants, the root and shoot systems cooperate. Water absorbed by roots moves through xylem towards leaves. Sugars produced in photosynthetic tissues move through phloem to growing regions, roots, fruits and storage organs. The plant's transport tissues connect organs that may be physically far apart.

## Structure and function at different levels

A useful examination technique is to connect a structural feature to what it enables.

| Level | Example | Structure-function link |
|---|---|---|
| Cell | Root hair cell | Long projection increases surface area for absorption |
| Tissue | Xylem | Hollow, strengthened vessels conduct water and support the plant |
| Organ | Leaf | Broad, thin surface helps light absorption and gas exchange |
| Organ system | Human circulatory system | Heart and vessels maintain blood flow around the body |
| Organism | Flowering plant | Root and shoot systems coordinate water uptake, support and food production |

Avoid vague statements such as “the organ helps the body”. State the actual function and explain how the structure contributes to it.

## Growth and increasing organisation

In multicellular organisms, growth commonly involves cell division followed by cell enlargement and differentiation. Differentiation is the process by which an unspecialised cell develops features suited to a particular role. As cells differentiate, they can form specialised tissues and organs.

In plants, meristems contain cells that continue dividing. Cells produced by these regions may differentiate into tissues such as xylem, phloem and epidermis. In animals, cell division and differentiation contribute to growth, repair and development, although many mature specialised cells have limited ability to divide.

The organisation of a body is maintained by coordination and communication. Cells exchange chemical signals; nerves transmit impulses; hormones travel in body fluids. These mechanisms help organs respond in a coordinated way.

## Interpreting diagrams and answering questions

A diagram may show a hierarchy as nested boxes, a flow chart or a named example. Read the labels carefully. If the question asks for the level represented by the heart, the correct answer is an **organ**, not an organ system. If it asks for the circulatory system, name the heart, blood and blood vessels as its main components.

For a question such as “Explain how cells are organised to form an organ”, a complete response should say that specialised cells form tissues, and different tissues combine and cooperate to perform the organ's function. A named example, such as the leaf or heart, makes the explanation clearer.

For a question asking you to compare a unicellular and a multicellular organism, state that a unicellular organism performs all life processes within one cell, whereas a multicellular organism has many cells, often specialised and organised into tissues and organs.

## WASSCE examination focus

You may be asked to define levels of organisation, arrange them in order, identify a level from an example, or explain how different systems cooperate. Use the correct biological terms. Do not call a group of organs a tissue, or a single organ an organ system.

When explaining an example, follow the chain from the specialised cell to the tissue, organ and system. Link each level to the function being discussed rather than simply listing names.

## Quick check

1. Classify blood, the heart and the circulatory system at their correct levels.
2. Explain how the respiratory and circulatory systems cooperate.
3. What is differentiation?
4. Why is a leaf an organ in a flowering plant?

**Answers:** 1. Blood is a tissue; the heart is an organ; the circulatory system is an organ system. 2. The lungs transfer oxygen into the blood and the circulatory system transports it to body cells. 3. Differentiation is the development of specialised cell structures and functions. 4. A leaf contains several tissues, including epidermal, mesophyll and vascular tissues, that work together.
