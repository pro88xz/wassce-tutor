## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain total, average and marginal product
- calculate them from a table
- state and explain the law of diminishing returns

## The production function

:::callout{type=definition}
A **production function** shows the relationship between the **inputs** of factors of production and the **output** they produce.
:::

In the **short run**, at least one factor is **fixed** (for example, a farm's land) while another is **variable** (for example, the number of workers). In the **long run**, all factors can be changed.

## Total, average and marginal product

When more of the variable factor is added to the fixed factor:

:::callout{type=definition}
**Total product (TP)** is the total output produced.

**Average product (AP)** is output per unit of the variable factor: $AP = \dfrac{TP}{L}$, where $L$ is the number of units of labour.

**Marginal product (MP)** is the extra output from one more unit of the variable factor: $MP = \Delta TP$ (the change in total product).
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
A farmer has **one acre of land** (fixed) and adds workers one at a time. Output is measured in bags of groundnuts.

| Workers ($L$) | Total product (TP) | Marginal product (MP) | Average product (AP) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| 2 | 24 | 14 | 12 |
| 3 | 36 | 12 | 12 |
| 4 | 44 | 8 | 11 |
| 5 | 48 | 4 | 9.6 |
| 6 | 48 | 0 | 8 |
| 7 | 45 | −3 | 6.4 |

How the columns are worked out:

- MP of the 3rd worker $= 36 - 24 = 12$
- AP with 5 workers $= \dfrac{48}{5} = 9.6$
- AP with 7 workers $= \dfrac{45}{7} \approx 6.4$
:::

## The law of diminishing returns

:::callout{type=definition}
**The law of diminishing returns** (the law of variable proportions) states that as more and more units of a variable factor are added to a fixed factor, the marginal product of the variable factor will **eventually fall**, other things being equal.
:::

In the table:

1. **Increasing returns** (workers 1 to 2): MP **rises** from 10 to 14. The second worker lets the two share tasks, so each adds more.
2. **Diminishing returns** (from the 3rd worker): MP **falls**: 12, 8, 4, 0. The acre is getting crowded, so each extra worker adds less.
3. **Negative returns** (the 7th worker): MP is **negative** (−3). Workers get in each other's way and total output actually falls.

### Why returns diminish

Because the fixed factor does not grow. One acre can only usefully employ so many people. Each extra worker has less land to work with.

### Useful relationships

- When **MP is above AP**, AP is **rising** (workers 1 and 2).
- When **MP equals AP**, AP is at its **maximum** (the 3rd worker: MP = AP = 12).
- When **MP is below AP**, AP is **falling** (from the 4th worker).
- When **MP is zero**, TP is at its **maximum** (the 6th worker: TP = 48).
- When **MP is negative**, TP **falls**.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Diminishing returns does **not** mean that total product falls. TP keeps rising (from 36 to 44 to 48) while MP is falling. TP falls only when MP becomes **negative**.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
A cassava farmer who keeps adding helpers to the same small plot will find that, after a point, each new helper adds fewer tubers than the one before. The sensible farmer stops adding labour, or gets more land.
:::

## Returns to scale (long run)

In the long run, all factors can be increased together. If all inputs are doubled:

- output **more than doubles**: **increasing returns to scale**
- output **exactly doubles**: **constant returns to scale**
- output **less than doubles**: **decreasing returns to scale**

## Key points

- TP is total output; $AP = \dfrac{TP}{L}$; MP is the change in TP from one more unit of input.
- The **law of diminishing returns**: adding a variable factor to a fixed factor eventually makes MP fall.
- AP is at its maximum where **MP = AP**; TP is at its maximum where **MP = 0**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
A very common question gives a table of labour and total product and asks you to **complete the MP and AP columns**, **identify where diminishing returns begin**, and **state the law**. Show one sample calculation for each column, state the law in full (with *other things being equal*), and quote figures from the table in your explanation.
:::

## Quick check

1. If TP rises from 30 to 38 when a fifth worker is added, what is the MP of the fifth worker?
2. In the table above, at which worker do diminishing marginal returns begin?
3. What happens to TP when MP is zero?

**Answers:** 1. $38 - 30 = 8$. 2. At the 3rd worker, when MP falls from 14 to 12. 3. TP is at its maximum (it stops rising).
