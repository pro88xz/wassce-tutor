The world's main **climate types** are the **equatorial** climate (hot and wet all year), the **tropical continental or savanna** climate (hot, with a wet and a dry season), the **tropical monsoon** climate (very heavy seasonal rain), the **hot desert** climate (very dry, with great heat by day), the **Mediterranean** climate (hot dry summers, mild wet winters) and the **temperate** climates of the middle latitudes. Each has a typical location, pattern of temperature and rainfall, and natural vegetation. This lesson describes them, with African examples and climate graphs.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the location, temperature, rainfall and vegetation of the main climate types
- explain the causes of each climate's characteristics
- read and interpret a climate graph
- identify a climate type from temperature and rainfall data

## How to read a climate graph

A climate graph shows the **average monthly rainfall** as bars (read on the left axis, in mm) and the **average monthly temperature** as a line (read on the right axis, in °C). From it you can find:

- the **wettest and driest months** and the **rainy season**
- the **total annual rainfall** (add the 12 bars)
- the **hottest and coolest months** and the **annual temperature range**

## The main climate types

| Climate | Location | Temperature | Rainfall | Vegetation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Equatorial** | about 0–5° N and S: Congo Basin, Amazon Basin, Indonesia | hot all year, about 26–27 °C; **annual range under 3 °C** | over 2,000 mm, **all year**, often with two peaks; daily convectional storms | tropical rainforest |
| **Tropical continental (savanna)** | about 5–15° N and S: West African interior, East Africa | hot; range about 5–10 °C; hottest just before the rains | 500–1,500 mm; **wet summer, dry winter** | savanna grassland with scattered trees |
| **Tropical monsoon** | coasts with seasonal wind reversals: West African coast (Sierra Leone, Guinea), India, South-East Asia | hot all year | very heavy (often over 2,500 mm) in a long **wet season**, with a marked dry season | monsoon forest |
| **Hot desert** | about 15–30° N and S, often on west coasts: Sahara, Kalahari, Namib | very hot days (over 40 °C), cool nights; **large daily range** | **under 250 mm**, unreliable | xerophytes: cacti, thorn scrub; oases |
| **Mediterranean** | about 30–40°, west sides of continents: Cape Town, North African coast, California | hot, dry summers; mild winters | 500–900 mm, mostly in **winter** | evergreen trees and shrubs (olives, citrus) |
| **Cool temperate western margin** | about 40–60°, west coasts: Britain, western Europe | mild summers and winters | rain all year, from fronts | deciduous forest |
| **Cold (polar and tundra)** | high latitudes | very cold most of the year | little precipitation, mostly snow | tundra mosses or none |

## Causes of the characteristics

- **Equatorial:** the Sun is high overhead all year, so it is always hot; strong heating causes daily **convectional rain**; nearness to the Equator keeps the temperature range small.
- **Savanna:** as the overhead Sun moves north and south, the rain belt follows it, giving a **wet season** when the Sun is overhead and a **dry season** when dry winds from the desert take over.
- **Monsoon:** seasonal reversal of winds: moist sea winds bring torrential rain; dry land winds bring the dry season.
- **Hot desert:** the regions lie under **sinking air** (high pressure) in the trade-wind belts, and many lie on west coasts beside **cold ocean currents** (Benguela, Canary), so there are no clouds and little rain; clear skies give hot days and cold nights.
- **Mediterranean:** in summer the dry high-pressure belt moves over these regions; in winter, rain-bearing westerly winds bring rain.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A station's figures show: temperature between 26 °C and 27 °C every month, and rainfall over 150 mm in every month, totalling 2,400 mm. Name the climate.**

A tiny temperature range, high temperatures and heavy rain in every month show an **equatorial** climate.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A station has 830 mm of rain, nearly all between June and September, and temperatures from 22 °C to 31 °C. Name the climate.**

A single wet season and a long dry season, moderate total rainfall and a larger temperature range show a **tropical continental (savanna)** climate, as at Kano in Figure 1.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Freetown's graph shows a **tropical monsoon** pattern: about **3,300 mm** of rain a year, most of it from May to October, with July and August each bringing around 800 mm, and almost no rain from December to March. Temperatures stay between about 25 °C and 28 °C, so the annual range is small because of the nearby sea.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not identify a climate from temperature alone. The **rainfall pattern**, whether rain falls all year or in one season, and the total, is usually the key clue.
:::

## Key points

- **Equatorial:** hot (26–27 °C), wet all year, tiny range; rainforest.
- **Savanna:** hot, one wet and one dry season, 500–1,500 mm; grassland with trees.
- **Monsoon:** very heavy seasonal rain from seasonal winds; Sierra Leone's coast.
- **Hot desert:** under 250 mm, huge daily range; sinking air and cold currents.
- **Mediterranean:** hot dry summers, wet winters.
- Read climate graphs: **bars = rainfall (mm)**, **line = temperature (°C)**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **describe the characteristics of a named climate type**, to **explain its causes**, to **draw or interpret a climate graph**, and to **identify a climate from data**. Equatorial, savanna and hot desert climates are the most frequently tested.
:::

## Quick check

1. State two characteristics of the equatorial climate.
2. Why do hot deserts have a large daily temperature range?
3. In which season does a Mediterranean climate receive most rain?
4. What climate type does Freetown have?
5. On a climate graph, what do the bars usually show?

**Answers:**

1. Any two: high temperatures all year (26–27 °C); small annual range; heavy rain (over 2,000 mm) in every month; daily convectional storms.
2. Clear skies let the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night.
3. In winter.
4. Tropical monsoon.
5. Average monthly rainfall.
