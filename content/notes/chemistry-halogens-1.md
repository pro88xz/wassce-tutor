Chlorine makes Freetown's tap water safe to drink, bleaches cloth and is the starting point for PVC pipes. It is also a poisonous gas that must be handled in a fume cupboard. This lesson covers the laboratory preparation of chlorine, how it is purified and collected, its test and its reactions, and its manufacture and uses.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the laboratory preparation, purification and collection of chlorine
- describe the test for chlorine
- state the physical properties of chlorine
- describe and explain the reactions of chlorine with metals, hydrogen, water, alkalis and other reducing agents
- explain how chlorine bleaches
- state the industrial source and uses of chlorine

## Laboratory preparation

![Manganese(IV) oxide and concentrated hydrochloric acid heated in a flask; the gas passes through water then concentrated sulphuric acid and is collected by downward delivery](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/chlorine-prep.svg)

*Figure 1. Preparing, purifying and collecting chlorine. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

Chlorine is made by **oxidising concentrated hydrochloric acid** with an oxidising agent.

**With manganese(IV) oxide** (heat needed):

$$\text{MnO}_2\text{(s)} + 4\text{HCl(aq)} \rightarrow \text{MnCl}_2\text{(aq)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} + \text{Cl}_2\text{(g)}$$

**With potassium manganate(VII)** (no heat needed):

$$2\text{KMnO}_4 + 16\text{HCl} \rightarrow 2\text{KCl} + 2\text{MnCl}_2 + 8\text{H}_2\text{O} + 5\text{Cl}_2$$

**Purifying and drying:**

1. Pass the gas through **water**, which removes **hydrogen chloride** fumes (very soluble in water; chlorine is only fairly soluble).
2. Then pass it through **concentrated sulphuric acid**, which **dries** it.

**Collection:** by **downward delivery** (upward displacement of air), because chlorine is about **2.5 times denser** than air. It is **not** collected over water, because it dissolves in water.

**Safety:** chlorine is **poisonous**; it attacks the lungs. Prepare it only in a **fume cupboard**.

## Test for chlorine

**Damp blue litmus paper** turns **red**, then is **bleached white**. The acidity comes from HCl and HOCl formed with water; the bleaching comes from HOCl.

## Physical properties

| Property | Chlorine |
|---|---|
| Colour | **Greenish-yellow** gas |
| Smell | **Choking**, irritating |
| Density | About 2.5 times denser than air |
| Solubility | Fairly soluble in water, giving **chlorine water** |
| Toxicity | **Poisonous** |
| Boiling point | −34 °C; easily liquefied under pressure |

## Chemical properties

Chlorine is a **strong oxidising agent**: it readily gains electrons, Cl₂ + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻.

**1. With metals** (heated), forming chlorides. With iron it gives **iron(III)** chloride, showing chlorine's oxidising strength:

$$2\text{Fe(s)} + 3\text{Cl}_2\text{(g)} \rightarrow 2\text{FeCl}_3\text{(s)} \qquad 2\text{Na(s)} + \text{Cl}_2\text{(g)} \rightarrow 2\text{NaCl(s)}$$

**2. With hydrogen,** forming hydrogen chloride. The reaction is slow in the dark, explosive in sunlight:

$$\text{H}_2\text{(g)} + \text{Cl}_2\text{(g)} \rightarrow 2\text{HCl(g)}$$

**3. With water,** forming chlorine water, a mixture of hydrochloric acid and **chloric(I) acid** (hypochlorous acid):

$$\text{Cl}_2\text{(g)} + \text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \rightleftharpoons \text{HCl(aq)} + \text{HOCl(aq)}$$

In sunlight, chlorine water slowly gives off **oxygen**: 2HOCl → 2HCl + O₂.

**4. With sodium hydroxide:**

- **Cold, dilute** NaOH gives sodium chloride and sodium chlorate(I), the active ingredient of **household bleach**:

$$\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{NaCl} + \text{NaOCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

- **Hot, concentrated** NaOH gives sodium chlorate(V): 3Cl₂ + 6NaOH → 5NaCl + NaClO₃ + 3H₂O.

**5. With reducing agents:**

- **Hydrogen sulphide:** H₂S + Cl₂ → 2HCl + S (yellow deposit)
- **Iron(II) salts:** 2FeCl₂ + Cl₂ → 2FeCl₃ (green → yellow-brown)
- **Ammonia:** chlorine oxidises it, giving white fumes of NH₄Cl
- **Bromides and iodides** (displacement): Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂ (orange); Cl₂ + 2KI → 2KCl + I₂ (brown)

**6. With hydrocarbons:** substitution reactions (CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl in light) and addition to alkenes (see organic chemistry). Turpentine bursts into flame in chlorine because chlorine removes its hydrogen.

## How chlorine bleaches

Chlorine bleaches **only when moist**. With water it forms **chloric(I) acid**, which gives up oxygen to the dye and **oxidises** it to a colourless compound:

$$\text{HOCl} \rightarrow \text{HCl} + [\text{O}] \qquad \text{dye} + [\text{O}] \rightarrow \text{colourless product}$$

Dry chlorine does **not** bleach dry litmus. Because chlorine bleaching is **oxidation**, it is permanent.

## Manufacture and uses

Chlorine is made industrially by the **electrolysis of brine** (concentrated sodium chloride solution), which also gives sodium hydroxide and hydrogen.

| Use | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Water treatment** | Kills bacteria in drinking water and swimming pools |
| **Bleach** | Sodium chlorate(I) bleaches cloth and paper pulp, and disinfects |
| **Plastics** | Making **PVC** (polyvinyl chloride) for pipes, cables and flooring |
| **Hydrochloric acid** | Burning hydrogen in chlorine |
| **Solvents and pesticides** | Chlorinated hydrocarbons |
| **Medicines and disinfectants** | Antiseptics such as TCP |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Household bleaches sold in Sierra Leone contain about 3–5% **sodium chlorate(I)**. A few drops per litre of water, left for 30 minutes, kill most germs, which is why health workers recommend it during cholera outbreaks. Never mix bleach with acid toilet cleaners: the acid releases **chlorine gas**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Chlorine is collected by **downward delivery**, not over water: it **dissolves** in water and is **denser** than air. Its test needs **damp** litmus paper: dry chlorine has no effect on dry litmus.
:::

## Key points

- Prepared by **oxidising concentrated HCl** with **MnO₂** (heated) or **KMnO₄**; **water** removes HCl, **concentrated H₂SO₄** dries it; collected by **downward delivery** in a fume cupboard.
- **Test:** damp blue litmus turns **red then white**.
- Greenish-yellow, choking, poisonous, denser than air.
- A strong **oxidising agent**: forms FeCl₃ with iron, displaces Br₂ and I₂, oxidises H₂S and Fe²⁺.
- **Bleaches by oxidation** via HOCl, and only when moist.
- Made by electrolysis of **brine**; used for **water treatment, bleach, PVC, HCl**.

## Quick check

1. Why is the chlorine passed through water during its preparation?
2. Why is chlorine collected by downward delivery?
3. Write the equation for chlorine and cold dilute sodium hydroxide.
4. Why does dry chlorine not bleach dry litmus paper?

**Answers:**

1. To remove hydrogen chloride fumes.
2. It is denser than air and dissolves in water.
3. Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaOCl + H₂O.
4. Bleaching needs water to form HOCl, which releases oxygen to oxidise the dye.
