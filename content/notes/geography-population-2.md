:::callout{type=definition}
**Population growth** is the change in the number of people in an area over time.
:::

It depends on **births**, **deaths** and **migration**: the population grows when births and immigration exceed deaths and emigration. The **population structure**, the numbers of males and females in each age group, is shown on a **population pyramid**, and **migration** is the movement of people from one place to another to live. This lesson explains how populations grow, how to read a pyramid, and the causes and effects of migration in West Africa.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain birth rate, death rate, natural increase and growth rate, and calculate them
- explain the causes of rapid population growth in Africa
- read population pyramids and explain the dependency ratio
- define migration and classify its types
- explain the push and pull factors of migration and its effects
- discuss the problems and benefits of a large population

## Measuring population growth

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Crude birth rate (CBR)** | live births per 1,000 people per year |
| **Crude death rate (CDR)** | deaths per 1,000 people per year |
| **Natural increase** | birth rate − death rate |
| **Net migration** | immigrants − emigrants |
| **Population growth** | natural increase + net migration |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A country has a birth rate of 35 per 1,000 and a death rate of 10 per 1,000. Find its rate of natural increase as a percentage.**

Natural increase $= 35 - 10 = 25$ per 1,000 $= 2.5\%$ per year.

At 2.5% a year, a population doubles in about 28 years.
:::

### Causes of rapid population growth in Africa

1. **High birth rates:** early marriage, the value placed on large families, children as farm labour and security in old age, limited use of family planning.
2. **Falling death rates:** better health care, vaccination, clean water and food supply.
3. **Low status and limited education of women** in some areas.
4. **Religious and cultural beliefs** that favour large families.
5. **Immigration** into some countries.

## Population structure

![Population pyramids: a developing country with a wide base and narrow top, and a developed country with a narrow base and straighter sides](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/population-pyramids.svg)

*Figure 1. Population pyramids of a developing and a developed country (illustrative). WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

A **population pyramid** shows the percentage of males (left) and females (right) in each age group.

| Developing country (e.g. Sierra Leone) | Developed country (e.g. Germany, Japan) |
|---|---|
| **wide base**: high birth rate, many children | **narrow base**: low birth rate |
| **narrow top**: few old people; shorter life expectancy | **wider top**: many old people; long life expectancy |
| about 40% of people under 15 | under 20% under 15 |
| **young, growing** population | **ageing**, slow-growing or shrinking population |

The **dependency ratio** compares dependants (children under 15 and adults over 64) with people of working age (15–64). A high dependency ratio, as in most of Africa, means each worker supports many dependants.

## Migration

:::callout{type=definition}
**Migration** is the movement of people from one place to another to live, temporarily or permanently. **Emigration** is moving out of a country; **immigration** is moving into it.
:::

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Internal** (within a country) | from rural Kailahun to Freetown |
| **International** | from Sierra Leone to the UK or the USA |
| **Rural–urban** | villages to towns: the most common type in West Africa |
| **Rural–rural** | to mining or plantation areas, such as diamond fields in Kono |
| **Seasonal** | labourers moving during the dry season for work |
| **Forced** | refugees fleeing war or disaster, as during the civil war |
| **Voluntary** | moving by choice for work, study or family |

### Push and pull factors

| Push factors (drive people away) | Pull factors (attract people) |
|---|---|
| poverty and unemployment | jobs and higher wages |
| lack of schools, hospitals, water and electricity | better social amenities |
| poor harvests, drought, land shortage | education and training |
| war, conflict and insecurity | security |
| natural disasters | bright lights and city lifestyle; relatives already there |

### Effects of migration

| On the source area (often rural) | On the destination (often towns) |
|---|---|
| loss of young, active workers; farms neglected | more labour and skills |
| fewer people to support | overcrowding, slums, unemployment |
| money sent home (remittances) | pressure on housing, water, schools, transport |
| mostly old people, women and children left behind | crime and social problems |

## A large population: problems and benefits

**Problems:** unemployment; pressure on land, food and services; slums; high dependency; environmental damage.

**Benefits:** a large labour force; a large market for goods; more people to defend and develop the country.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone has a **young population**: around four in ten people are under 15. This creates heavy demand for schools (the Free Quality School Education programme since 2018 enrolled many more children), health care and, soon, jobs. Remittances from Sierra Leoneans abroad are also an important source of income for many families.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Population growth is not natural increase alone. Natural increase counts only **births minus deaths**; total growth also includes **net migration**.
:::

## Key points

- **Natural increase** = birth rate − death rate; **growth** = natural increase + net migration.
- Rapid growth in Africa comes from **high births and falling deaths**.
- Pyramids: **wide base** (young, developing) or **narrow base** (ageing, developed); **dependency ratio**.
- Migration: **internal, international, rural–urban, seasonal, forced, voluntary**; **push** and **pull** factors.
- Effects: rural areas lose workers; towns gain labour but face overcrowding.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **calculate natural increase**, to **explain the causes and effects of rapid population growth**, to **describe and interpret population pyramids**, and to **explain the causes and effects of rural–urban migration**. Push and pull factors are a favourite essay question.
:::

## Quick check

1. Define natural increase.
2. A country's birth rate is 40 per 1,000 and death rate 12 per 1,000. Find the rate of natural increase as a percentage.
3. What does a wide-based population pyramid show?
4. Give two push factors of rural–urban migration.
5. State two effects of migration on rural areas.

**Answers:**

1. The difference between the birth rate and the death rate.
2. $28$ per 1,000 $= 2.8\%$.
3. A high birth rate and a young population.
4. Any two: poverty; unemployment; lack of amenities; poor harvests; land shortage; insecurity; disasters.
5. Any two: loss of young workers; neglected farms; remittances received; ageing population left behind.
