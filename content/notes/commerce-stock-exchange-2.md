Two people each lend money to the same company. One buys shares and becomes a part-owner, sharing in profits and losses; the other buys debentures and becomes a creditor, earning fixed interest whatever happens. This lesson explains the securities traded in the capital market, how dividends are worked out, and the investors and speculators who trade them.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe ordinary shares, preference shares and their types
- describe debentures and their types
- distinguish shares from debentures
- calculate dividends, dividend yield and debenture interest
- explain stock exchange terms: bulls, bears, stags, par, premium, rights and bonus issues

## Shares

:::callout{type=definition}
A **share** is a unit of the capital of a company. A **shareholder** is a **part-owner** of the company and receives a share of profits called a **dividend**.
:::

| | Ordinary shares (equity) | Preference shares |
|---|---|---|
| **Dividend** | Varies with profit; paid **last**, after preference shareholders | A **fixed rate** (e.g. 8%), paid **before** ordinary shareholders |
| **Voting rights** | **Yes**: one vote per share; they control the company | Usually **no** voting rights |
| **Risk** | **Highest**: may receive nothing in a bad year | Lower |
| **Repayment if wound up** | Repaid **last** | Repaid before ordinary shareholders |
| **Reward in good years** | Can be very high | Limited to the fixed rate |

**Types of preference shares:**

- **Cumulative**: unpaid dividends are carried forward and paid in later years before ordinary dividends.
- **Non-cumulative**: a missed dividend is lost.
- **Participating**: a share of extra profits in addition to the fixed dividend.
- **Redeemable**: the company may buy them back at a set date.

**Deferred (founders') shares** are held by the founders. They receive dividends only after all other shareholders have been paid.

## Debentures

:::callout{type=definition}
A **debenture** is a **long-term loan** to a company, at a **fixed rate of interest**, for a stated period. A **debenture holder is a creditor**, not an owner.
:::

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Secured (mortgage) debenture** | Backed by the company's assets, which can be sold if interest or capital is not paid |
| **Unsecured (naked) debenture** | No specific security |
| **Redeemable debenture** | Repaid on a fixed date |
| **Irredeemable debenture** | No fixed repayment date; repaid when the company is wound up |
| **Convertible debenture** | Can be exchanged for shares at a later date |

### Shares and debentures compared

| Shares | Debentures |
|---|---|
| Shareholders are **owners** | Debenture holders are **creditors** |
| Receive **dividends**, paid only from profit | Receive **interest**, which must be paid **even if there is a loss** |
| Dividend may vary | Interest is **fixed** |
| Ordinary shareholders **vote** | No voting rights |
| Repaid **last** on winding up | Repaid **before** shareholders |
| Usually not repaid while the company exists | Usually redeemed on a fixed date |
| More risk | Less risk, often secured |

### Government securities

- **Treasury bills**: short-term (91, 182 or 364 days).
- **Government bonds (stocks)**: long-term loans to the government at fixed interest. They are called **gilt-edged** securities because they are almost risk-free.

## Calculating dividends

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A company's capital:**

- 50,000 **8% preference shares** of NLe 2 each (NLe 100,000)
- 200,000 **ordinary shares** of NLe 1 each (NLe 200,000)
- NLe 60,000 of **10% debentures**

Profit **before** debenture interest is NLe 56,000. The directors keep NLe 12,000 as reserves and distribute the rest.

1. Debenture interest (paid first, whatever the profit) $= 10\% \times 60000 = $ NLe 6,000
2. Profit left $= 56000 - 6000 = $ NLe 50,000
3. Preference dividend $= 8\% \times 100000 = $ NLe 8,000
4. Left for ordinary shareholders after reserves $= 50000 - 8000 - 12000 = $ NLe 30,000
5. Ordinary dividend per share $= 30000 \div 200000 = $ **NLe 0.15** (a 15% dividend on a NLe 1 share)
:::

![A bar of NLe 56,000 profit divided in order: debenture interest NLe 6,000, preference dividend NLe 8,000, reserves NLe 12,000 and ordinary dividend NLe 30,000](https://wasscetutor.com/img/commerce/profit-distribution.svg)

*Figure 1. How the profit in the example is shared: creditors first, ordinary shareholders last. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

**Dividend yield** compares the dividend with the **market price** an investor pays:

$$\text{dividend yield} = \dfrac{\text{dividend per share}}{\text{market price per share}} \times 100$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
The ordinary shares above sell at NLe 1.50 on the exchange.

$$\text{dividend yield} = \dfrac{0.15}{1.50} \times 100 = 10\%$$

A buyer at NLe 1.50 earns 10% a year from dividends, even though the dividend is 15% of the **nominal (par) value**.
:::

## Stock exchange terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Par (nominal) value** | The face value printed on the share |
| **Market value** | The price at which the share is traded today |
| **At a premium** | Market price **above** par value |
| **At a discount** | Market price **below** par value |
| **Rights issue** | New shares offered to **existing** shareholders, usually below market price, in proportion to their holdings |
| **Bonus (scrip) issue** | **Free** shares given to existing shareholders from reserves |
| **Blue chips** | Shares of large, safe, well-established companies |
| **Cum dividend / ex dividend** | With or without the right to the next dividend |
| **Yield** | The return on an investment as a percentage of its price |

### Speculators

| Speculator | Expects | Does |
|---|---|---|
| **Bull** | Prices to **rise** | **Buys** now to sell later at a higher price |
| **Bear** | Prices to **fall** | **Sells** now (even shares not yet owned) to buy back later at a lower price |
| **Stag** | A **new issue** to rise on its first day of trading | **Applies for new shares** to sell them quickly at a profit |
| **Lame duck** | — | A speculator (usually a bear) who **cannot meet their commitments** |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Most Sierra Leonean investors hold **government treasury bills and bonds**, bought through their banks, because few company shares are listed. A rise in treasury bill rates attracts savings away from bank deposits; a fall encourages people to look for other investments.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Debenture interest must be paid even when the company makes a loss**, because debenture holders are creditors. Dividends are paid only out of profit and only when the directors declare them.
:::

## Key points

- **Ordinary shares**: owners, variable dividend, voting rights, highest risk. **Preference shares**: fixed dividend paid first, usually no vote.
- **Debentures**: long-term loans at fixed interest; holders are **creditors**, paid before shareholders.
- Order of payment: **debenture interest → preference dividend → ordinary dividend**.
- **Dividend yield** = dividend per share ÷ market price × 100.
- **Bulls** buy expecting rises; **bears** sell expecting falls; **stags** buy new issues to sell quickly.

## Quick check

1. Give two differences between ordinary and preference shares.
2. Why is a debenture holder a creditor and not an owner?
3. A share with par value NLe 1 pays a dividend of NLe 0.12 and sells at NLe 2. Find the dividend yield.
4. What is a stag?

**Answers:** 1. Any two: ordinary dividends vary while preference dividends are fixed; preference dividends are paid first; ordinary shareholders vote; ordinary shares carry more risk; preference shares are repaid first on winding up. 2. Because a debenture is a loan to the company; the holder is owed interest and repayment, but owns no part of the company. 3. $\dfrac{0.12}{2} \times 100 = 6\%$. 4. A speculator who applies for new shares, hoping to sell them at a profit as soon as dealing starts.
