**Mining** is the extraction of valuable minerals from the Earth's crust. The main **methods of mining** are **open-cast (surface) mining**, **underground (shaft) mining**, **alluvial (placer) mining** of river gravels, and **drilling** for oil and gas. West Africa is rich in minerals: oil and gas in Nigeria, gold in Ghana, bauxite in Guinea, iron ore in Liberia, Mauritania and Sierra Leone, uranium in Niger, and diamonds and rutile in Sierra Leone. This lesson explains the methods, the factors that affect mining and the main minerals of West Africa.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define minerals and classify them
- describe the main methods of mining and when each is used
- explain the factors that affect the exploitation of minerals
- name the main minerals of West Africa and where they are mined
- explain the importance of mining

## Minerals

:::callout{type=definition}
A **mineral** is a naturally occurring substance with a definite chemical composition, found in the Earth's crust. An **ore** is a rock that contains enough of a mineral to be mined profitably.
:::

| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Metallic** | iron ore, bauxite (aluminium), gold, tin, copper, manganese, rutile (titanium), uranium |
| **Non-metallic** | diamonds, limestone, phosphate, salt, clay |
| **Fuel (energy) minerals** | petroleum, natural gas, coal |

## Methods of mining

![Three methods: open-cast mining from a large open pit, underground mining through a vertical shaft and tunnels, and alluvial mining of gravels in a river bed with a sieve](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/mining-methods.svg)

*Figure 1. Open-cast, underground and alluvial mining. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Method | How it works | Used for | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open-cast (open-pit, surface) mining** | remove the soil and rock (**overburden**) above a deposit near the surface, then dig the mineral from a large pit with machines | large deposits near the surface | iron ore (Tonkolili), bauxite, coal at Enugu (Nigeria) |
| **Strip mining** | remove long strips of overburden over a shallow, flat-lying deposit | coal, phosphate | phosphate in Togo |
| **Underground (shaft) mining** | sink a vertical **shaft** and dig horizontal tunnels (**adits, galleries**) to reach deep deposits | deep veins | gold at Obuasi (Ghana); deep diamond kimberlite pipes |
| **Alluvial (placer) mining** | dig, wash and sieve sand and gravel from river beds and flood plains, by hand or with **dredges** | heavy minerals deposited by rivers | alluvial diamonds and gold in Sierra Leone; rutile |
| **Drilling** | bore wells to pump out liquids and gases | petroleum, natural gas | Niger Delta (Nigeria), offshore Ghana |

## Factors affecting mining

1. **Size and quality (grade) of the deposit:** large, rich deposits are worth mining.
2. **Accessibility:** depth, location and ease of reaching the deposit.
3. **Transport:** railways, roads and ports to carry bulky ores (the Pepel railway for iron ore).
4. **Capital:** mining needs huge investment in machines and infrastructure.
5. **Technology and skilled labour.**
6. **Power and water supply.**
7. **World demand and prices.**
8. **Government policy**, political stability and mining laws.

## Importance of mining

1. **Foreign exchange** from mineral exports.
2. **Government revenue** from royalties, taxes and licences.
3. **Employment** directly and in related services.
4. **Raw materials** for industry (bauxite for aluminium, limestone for cement).
5. **Infrastructure:** railways, roads, ports and power built for mines.
6. **Growth of towns** such as Koidu, Obuasi and Jos.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Mining provides most of Sierra Leone's **export earnings**. The country produces **diamonds** (Kono and Kenema), **iron ore** (Tonkolili and Marampa, exported through Pepel), **rutile** and **ilmenite** (Moyamba and Bonthe, exported through Nitti), **bauxite** (Moyamba District) and **gold**. The next lesson looks at each in detail and at the effects of mining on people and the environment.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Alluvial** mining recovers minerals that rivers have **already eroded and deposited** in gravels; it does not involve digging into the original rock. Diamonds mined from **kimberlite pipes** need open-pit or underground mining instead.
:::

## Key points

- Minerals: **metallic, non-metallic, fuel**. An **ore** is rock rich enough to mine.
- Methods: **open-cast, strip, underground (shaft), alluvial (placer), drilling**.
- Factors: **size and grade, accessibility, transport, capital, technology, power, prices, government**.
- West Africa: **oil (Nigeria), gold (Ghana), bauxite (Guinea), iron ore (Liberia, Mauritania, Sierra Leone), uranium (Niger), diamonds and rutile (Sierra Leone)**.
- Mining brings **foreign exchange, revenue, jobs, raw materials, infrastructure and towns**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **describe methods of mining with diagrams**, to **explain factors affecting mineral exploitation**, to **name minerals and their locations** in West Africa, and to **state the importance of mining**. A sketch map of mineral locations is common in Paper 2.
:::

## Quick check

1. What is an ore?
2. Which mining method is used for alluvial diamonds?
3. Name the country that is West Africa's main oil producer.
4. Which West African country has some of the world's largest bauxite reserves?
5. Give three benefits of mining.

**Answers:**

1. A rock containing enough of a mineral to be mined profitably.
2. Alluvial (placer) mining: digging, washing and sieving river gravels.
3. Nigeria.
4. Guinea.
5. Any three: foreign exchange; government revenue; employment; raw materials for industry; infrastructure; growth of towns.
