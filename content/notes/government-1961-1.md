At independence in 1961, Sierra Leone did not write a new constitution from scratch. Its **Independence Constitution**, agreed at Lancaster House, was modelled closely on the British **Westminster** system: a monarch as head of state, a Prime Minister and Cabinet answerable to Parliament, and judges whose final court of appeal sat in London. This lesson explains the main features of the 1961 Constitution.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain how the 1961 Constitution came into being
- describe the structure of government under the 1961 Constitution
- state its main features
- explain the role of the Governor-General and the Prime Minister

## Background

The 1961 Constitution was agreed at the **Lancaster House conference** in **1960** and brought into force by a British **Order in Council**. It came into effect on **27 April 1961**, when Sierra Leone became an independent state and a member of the **Commonwealth**.

## Features of the 1961 Constitution

1. **Parliamentary (Westminster) system** of government.
2. **Head of state**: the **British monarch** (Queen Elizabeth II), represented in Sierra Leone by a **Governor-General** with mainly ceremonial powers.
3. **Head of government**: the **Prime Minister**, leader of the majority party in Parliament, appointed by the Governor-General.
4. **Cabinet** of ministers drawn from Parliament and **collectively responsible** to it.
5. **Unicameral legislature**: the **House of Representatives**, made up of **elected members** and **Paramount Chief members** (one from each district), with a **Speaker**.
6. **Fusion of powers**: ministers were members of Parliament.
7. **Multi-party system**, with an official **opposition**.
8. **Fundamental human rights** protected in the constitution.
9. **Independent judiciary**: a **Supreme Court** and a **Court of Appeal**, with **final appeals to the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council** in London.
10. **Public Service Commission** and **Judicial Service Commission** to keep appointments free from politics.
11. An **Electoral Commission** to conduct elections.
12. **Entrenched clauses** protected by a special amendment procedure.
13. **Recognition of chieftaincy** and customary law.
14. **Citizenship** provisions for the new state.

## The Governor-General and the Prime Minister

| Governor-General | Prime Minister |
|---|---|
| Represented the British monarch | Head of government |
| Ceremonial head of state in practice | Held real executive power |
| Appointed the Prime Minister: the person most likely to command a majority in the House | Leader of the majority party in Parliament |
| Acted on the advice of the Prime Minister, except in a few matters | Chose ministers and chaired the Cabinet |
| Assented to bills in the monarch's name | Answerable to Parliament; could be removed by a vote of no confidence |

The first Governor-General was **Sir Maurice Dorman**, and the first Prime Minister was **Sir Milton Margai**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Under the 1961 Constitution the Governor-General's power to appoint the Prime Minister seemed a formality. But in **March 1967**, after a close general election, Governor-General **Sir Henry Lightfoot Boston** appointed **Siaka Stevens** of the APC as Prime Minister. The army commander, **Brigadier David Lansana**, intervened, and Sierra Leone's first period of military rule followed. The episode exposed how much depended on this "ceremonial" power.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
At independence, Sierra Leone was **not** a republic. The British monarch remained head of state until **19 April 1971**. Sierra Leone had a **parliamentary** system with a **Prime Minister**, not an executive President.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Westminster system**: the parliamentary system of government, modelled on Britain's, adopted at independence in 1961.
- **Governor-General**: the representative of the British monarch in Sierra Leone, with mainly ceremonial powers, under the 1961 Constitution.
- **Privy Council**: the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in London, Sierra Leone's final court of appeal until 1971.
- **Unicameral legislature**: a legislature with one chamber; in 1961 it was the House of Representatives.
- **Paramount Chief members**: members of Parliament who are Paramount Chiefs, one from each district.
:::

## Key points

- The **1961 Constitution** was agreed at **Lancaster House (1960)** and took effect on **27 April 1961**.
- **Westminster model**: the **British monarch** as head of state through a **Governor-General**; the **Prime Minister** and **Cabinet** responsible to Parliament.
- **House of Representatives**: elected members plus **Paramount Chief members**.
- **Multi-party**, **fundamental rights**, **independent judiciary** with final appeal to the **Privy Council**.
- First Governor-General **Sir Maurice Dorman**; first Prime Minister **Sir Milton Margai**.

## Quick check

1. Who was head of state under the 1961 Constitution?
2. What was the final court of appeal under the 1961 Constitution?
3. Name the first Prime Minister and the first Governor-General of independent Sierra Leone.
4. State three features of the 1961 Constitution.

**Answers:**

1. The British monarch (Queen Elizabeth II), represented by the Governor-General.
2. The Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in London.
3. Sir Milton Margai (Prime Minister) and Sir Maurice Dorman (Governor-General).
4. Any three: parliamentary system; British monarch as head of state through a Governor-General; Prime Minister and Cabinet responsible to Parliament; House of Representatives with Paramount Chief members; multi-party system; fundamental rights; independent judiciary with Privy Council appeals; Public Service Commission; Electoral Commission; entrenched clauses; recognition of chieftaincy.
