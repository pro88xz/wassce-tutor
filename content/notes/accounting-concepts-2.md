:::callout{type=definition}
The **qualitative characteristics of financial information** are the qualities that make accounts useful to people who rely on them: **relevance**, **faithful representation (reliability)**, **comparability**, **verifiability**, **timeliness** and **understandability**.
:::

Accounts are also prepared on an agreed **basis**: the **accrual basis**, which records income and expenses when they are **earned or incurred**, or the simpler **cash basis**, which records them only when **cash is received or paid**. This lesson explains the characteristics, compares the two bases, and introduces the accounting standards that govern financial statements.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the qualitative characteristics of useful financial information
- distinguish the fundamental from the enhancing characteristics
- distinguish the cash basis from the accrual basis of accounting
- calculate profit on both bases for a simple case
- explain the purpose of accounting standards and name the bodies that set them

## Qualitative characteristics

| Characteristic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Relevance** *(fundamental)* | the information can make a difference to users' decisions, helping them predict or confirm results |
| **Faithful representation (reliability)** *(fundamental)* | the information is **complete, neutral and free from error**; it shows what really happened |
| **Comparability** *(enhancing)* | users can compare a business over time and with other businesses |
| **Verifiability** *(enhancing)* | independent people would agree the figures from the evidence |
| **Timeliness** *(enhancing)* | the information is available in time to influence decisions |
| **Understandability** *(enhancing)* | the information is clearly classified and presented for users with reasonable knowledge |

Information that is **not relevant** or **not faithful** is of no use, however well presented. The enhancing characteristics make useful information **more** useful.

## Cash basis and accrual basis

| Cash basis | Accrual basis |
|---|---|
| income recorded when **cash is received** | income recorded when **earned** (goods delivered, services given) |
| expenses recorded when **cash is paid** | expenses recorded when **incurred**, matched with the income they help earn |
| simple; used by small traders and some clubs (receipts and payments account) | required for proper final accounts of businesses and companies |
| ignores amounts owed and owing at the period end | includes accruals, prepayments, debtors and creditors |
| can give a misleading profit | gives a **true and fair** profit |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**In 2026 a tailor sewed uniforms worth NLe 60,000. By 31 December customers had paid NLe 52,000; NLe 8,000 was still owed. Expenses incurred were NLe 35,000, of which NLe 32,000 was paid in cash; NLe 3,000 of electricity was unpaid. Find the profit on each basis.**

**Cash basis:**

Profit $= 52\,000 - 32\,000 = 20\,000$

**Accrual basis:**

Profit $= 60\,000 - 35\,000 = 25\,000$

The accrual basis shows the true profit of NLe 25,000 earned in 2026, because it includes the NLe 8,000 earned but not yet received and the NLe 3,000 expense incurred but not yet paid.
:::

## Accounting standards

**Accounting standards** are official rules on how particular items should be measured and presented, so that financial statements are prepared in the same way everywhere.

- The **International Accounting Standards Board (IASB)** issues **International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS)**; older standards are called **International Accounting Standards (IAS)**, for example **IAS 2** on inventories and **IAS 16** on property, plant and equipment.
- In Sierra Leone, the **Institute of Chartered Accountants of Sierra Leone (ICASL)** promotes standards and regulates the profession, and companies prepare their financial statements under IFRS.

**Why standards matter:** they reduce the choice of methods, improve **comparability**, increase users' **confidence**, and help auditors judge whether accounts are **true and fair**.

### Modern names for the statements

| Traditional name | Modern (IFRS) name |
|---|---|
| Trading, profit and loss account | Income statement (statement of profit or loss) |
| Balance sheet | Statement of financial position |
| Stock | Inventory |
| Debtors | Trade receivables |
| Creditors | Trade payables |
| Fixed assets | Non-current assets |

WASSCE questions use both sets of names, so learn both.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
"Reliable" does not mean "exact to the last cent". Some figures, such as depreciation and doubtful debts, are estimates. Information is faithful when it is **complete, neutral and free from material error**, using reasonable estimates.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Relevance**: financial information can make a difference to users' decisions, helping them predict or confirm results.
- **Faithful representation**: financial information is complete, neutral and free from error, showing what really happened.
- **Comparability**: users can compare a business over time and with other businesses.
- **Timeliness**: information is available in time to influence decisions.
- **Cash basis**: accounting that records only cash received and paid.
- **Accrual basis**: accounting that records income earned and expenses incurred in the period, whether or not cash has moved.
:::

## Key points

- Fundamental characteristics: **relevance** and **faithful representation**.
- Enhancing characteristics: **comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability**.
- **Cash basis** records cash received and paid; **accrual basis** records income earned and expenses incurred.
- The accrual basis gives a **true and fair** profit and is required for proper final accounts.
- **IFRS** (from the IASB) set standards; **ICASL** regulates accountants in Sierra Leone.

## Quick check

1. Name the two fundamental qualitative characteristics.
2. What makes information a faithful representation?
3. Distinguish the cash basis from the accrual basis.
4. A shop earned NLe 40,000 of income, of which NLe 36,000 was received, and incurred NLe 25,000 of expenses, of which NLe 22,000 was paid. Find the profit on the accrual basis.
5. Give the modern name for the balance sheet.

**Answers:**

1. Relevance and faithful representation.
2. It is complete, neutral and free from material error.
3. The cash basis records income and expenses when cash is received or paid; the accrual basis records them when earned or incurred.
4. $40\,000 - 25\,000 = 15\,000$: NLe 15,000.
5. The statement of financial position.
