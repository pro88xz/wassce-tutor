**Microorganisms** (microbes) are living things too small to see without a microscope: bacteria, viruses, fungi such as yeast and moulds, protozoa and some algae. They are everywhere: in soil, water, air, food, and on and inside our bodies. A few cause disease, such as cholera, malaria, Ebola and ringworm. But most are harmless or useful: they make bread rise and palm wine ferment, turn milk into yoghurt, recycle dead matter, fix nitrogen for crops and make antibiotics. This lesson describes the main groups, how microbes grow and reproduce, their useful and harmful effects, and how we control their growth.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the structure and features of bacteria, viruses, fungi and protozoa
- state the conditions microorganisms need to grow
- describe useful and harmful activities of microorganisms
- explain methods of controlling microorganisms, including food preservation
- explain how antibiotics work and why resistance develops

## The main groups

![A bacterium with cell wall, DNA loop and flagellum; a virus with a protein coat around DNA or RNA; bread mould with hyphae and spore-bearing sporangia; and an Amoeba with a nucleus, contractile vacuole and pseudopodia](https://wasscetutor.com/img/biology/microbe-types.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
| Group | Structure | Reproduction | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **bacteria** | single cells; cell wall, cytoplasm, **no true nucleus** (a loop of DNA); some have flagella | **binary fission** (one cell splits into two), as often as every 20 minutes; some form resistant **spores** | *Vibrio cholerae* (cholera), *Lactobacillus* (yoghurt), *Rhizobium* (nitrogen fixation) |
| **viruses** | **not cells**: DNA or RNA inside a protein coat; far smaller than bacteria | **only inside a living host cell**, which they take over and often destroy | HIV, Ebola, Lassa, measles, polio; cassava mosaic virus |
| **fungi** | cells with nuclei and cell walls of chitin; most made of threads (**hyphae**) forming a **mycelium**; yeast is single-celled | **spores**; yeast by **budding** | yeast, bread mould (*Rhizopus*), *Penicillium*, mushrooms |
| **protozoa** | single cells with a nucleus; animal-like | **binary fission** | *Amoeba*, *Paramecium*, *Plasmodium* (malaria), *Trypanosoma* (sleeping sickness) |
| **algae** | single cells or threads with chlorophyll | various | *Chlamydomonas*, *Spirogyra*, phytoplankton |
:::

### Shapes of bacteria

| Shape | Name | Example |
|---|---|---|
| spheres | **cocci** | *Streptococcus* (sore throat) |
| rods | **bacilli** | *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (TB) |
| comma-shaped | **vibrios** | *Vibrio cholerae* (cholera) |
| spirals | **spirilla** | the spirochaete of syphilis |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Treating viruses with antibiotics.** Antibiotics work on **bacteria** by attacking their cell walls or processes. Viruses are not cells and are **not affected** by antibiotics. Taking antibiotics for a cold or measles does nothing to the virus and helps resistant bacteria to spread.
:::

## What microorganisms need to grow

- **food** (organic matter)
- **moisture**
- a **suitable temperature** (most grow best around 25–40 °C; boiling kills most; cold slows them)
- a **suitable pH**
- **oxygen** for aerobic microbes (some are anaerobic and grow without it)

These conditions explain how we preserve food: remove one of them.

## Useful microorganisms

| Use | Microorganism |
|---|---|
| **decomposition** and recycling of nutrients | bacteria and fungi in soil |
| **nitrogen fixation** | *Rhizobium* in legume root nodules |
| bread, palm wine, beer | **yeast** (fermentation) |
| yoghurt, cheese, gari and fufu | **lactic acid bacteria** |
| **antibiotics** (penicillin, streptomycin) | *Penicillium* (a fungus), *Streptomyces* (a bacterium) |
| digestion of cellulose in cattle, goats, termites | bacteria and protozoa in the gut |
| sewage treatment, biogas (methane) | bacteria |
| vaccines, insulin by genetic engineering | bacteria and viruses |

## Harmful microorganisms

- **Diseases of humans**: cholera, typhoid and TB (bacteria); HIV, Ebola, Lassa fever, measles (viruses); ringworm, athlete's foot, thrush (fungi); malaria, sleeping sickness, amoebic dysentery (protozoa).
- **Diseases of crops and animals**: cassava mosaic (virus), rice blast and cocoa black pod (fungi), anthrax (bacteria).
- **Food spoilage**: moulds on bread and fruit, bacteria in meat and fish.
- **Toxins**: *Aspergillus* moulds on damp, badly stored **groundnuts and maize** make **aflatoxins**, which damage the liver and are linked to liver cancer.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Drying groundnuts properly.** Groundnuts and maize stored damp in Sierra Leone's humid climate easily grow *Aspergillus* mould. The aflatoxin it makes cannot be seen, smelt or cooked away. Drying the crop well on clean mats or tarpaulins (not bare ground), sorting out mouldy or damaged nuts, and storing in dry, airy conditions are the best protection.
:::

## Controlling microorganisms

### Food preservation

Each method removes one condition microbes need:

| Method | How it works | Local examples |
|---|---|---|
| **drying** | removes water | dried fish, dried cassava, dried pepper |
| **smoking** | dries and adds chemicals that kill microbes | smoked bonga and other fish at Tombo and along the coast |
| **salting** and **sugaring** | draw water out of microbes by osmosis | salted fish; jams |
| **refrigeration** and **freezing** | low temperature slows or stops growth | cold storage of fish and meat |
| **boiling**, **pasteurisation**, **canning** | heat kills microbes; sealing keeps others out | canned sardines, pasteurised milk |
| **pickling** | acid (vinegar) stops growth | pickled vegetables |

### Hygiene and medicine

- **Sterilisation** (high heat, autoclaves) kills all microbes on instruments.
- **Antiseptics** (on skin) and **disinfectants** (on surfaces) kill or slow microbes.
- **Antibiotics** kill bacteria inside the body.
- **Vaccines** prepare the immune system against specific pathogens.

### Antibiotic resistance

In any population of bacteria, a few may carry a mutation that lets them survive an antibiotic. If a patient stops treatment early or takes the wrong drug, those resistant bacteria survive and multiply (**natural selection**). To slow resistance: take antibiotics **only when prescribed**, take the **full course**, and never share or buy them without advice.

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Give two differences between bacteria and viruses."}
Any two: bacteria are cells, viruses are not; bacteria have a cell wall and cytoplasm, viruses only genetic material in a protein coat; bacteria reproduce by binary fission on their own, viruses only inside host cells; antibiotics affect bacteria, not viruses.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. State four conditions needed for the growth of microorganisms."}
Food, moisture, suitable temperature, suitable pH (and oxygen for aerobic microbes).
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Explain how salting preserves fish."}
Salt draws water out of microorganisms by **osmosis**, so they are dehydrated and cannot grow or reproduce.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Name the microorganism used in making (a) bread, (b) yoghurt, (c) penicillin."}
(a) yeast (b) lactic acid bacteria (*Lactobacillus*) (c) *Penicillium* mould.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Why should a patient complete a full course of antibiotics?"}
To kill **all** the bacteria, including the more resistant ones. Stopping early leaves resistant bacteria alive to multiply and spread.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Microorganism**: a living thing too small to see without a microscope, such as a bacterium, virus, fungus or protozoan.
- **Decomposition**: the breakdown of dead matter by bacteria and fungi, which returns nutrients to the soil.
- **Nitrogen fixation**: turning nitrogen gas into compounds plants can use, as by Rhizobium in legume root nodules.
- **Antibiotic**: a substance made by a microorganism that kills or stops the growth of bacteria, such as penicillin.
- **Pathogen**: a microorganism that causes disease.
- **Aerobic**: needing oxygen to live and grow.
- **Anaerobic**: able to live and grow without oxygen.
:::

## Key points

- Microbes: **bacteria** (no nucleus; binary fission), **viruses** (not cells; reproduce only in hosts), **fungi** (hyphae, spores; yeast buds), **protozoa** (single cells with a nucleus), **algae**.
- They need **food, moisture, warmth, suitable pH** (and oxygen if aerobic).
- **Useful**: decomposition, nitrogen fixation, fermentation, antibiotics, digestion in herbivores.
- **Harmful**: diseases, food spoilage, **aflatoxins**.
- Control: **drying, smoking, salting, cold, heat, pickling**; sterilisation, antiseptics, antibiotics, vaccines.
- Use antibiotics correctly to slow **resistance**.
