The **sources of energy** are divided into **renewable** sources, which are replaced naturally and will not run out (**solar, hydroelectric, wind, biomass, geothermal, tidal and wave**), and **non-renewable** sources, which took millions of years to form and will run out (**coal, petroleum, natural gas and nuclear fuel**). Energy powers homes, transport, farms and factories, and a country's development depends heavily on a reliable supply. This lesson compares the main sources, their advantages and disadvantages, and the energy situation in West Africa and Sierra Leone.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define energy and distinguish renewable from non-renewable sources
- describe the main sources of energy with their advantages and disadvantages
- name the main energy resources of West Africa
- describe the sources of energy used in Sierra Leone
- explain the importance of energy and the problems of energy supply

## Renewable and non-renewable energy

![Sources of energy divided into renewable (solar, hydroelectric, wind, biomass, geothermal, tides and waves) and non-renewable (coal, petroleum, natural gas, nuclear)](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/energy-sources.svg)

*Figure 1. Renewable and non-renewable sources of energy. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=definition}
**Renewable energy** comes from sources that are naturally replaced and will not run out. **Non-renewable energy** comes from sources that exist in fixed amounts and will eventually be used up. **Fossil fuels** (coal, oil and gas) formed from the remains of plants and animals buried millions of years ago.
:::

## Non-renewable sources

| Source | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Petroleum (crude oil)** | high energy content; easy to transport; refined into petrol, diesel, kerosene, jet fuel | will run out; price swings; pollution, oil spills; releases CO₂ |
| **Natural gas** | cleaner than oil and coal; used for power and cooking (LPG) | needs pipelines or ships; will run out; releases CO₂ |
| **Coal** | plentiful in some countries; reliable | the most polluting fuel; mining is dangerous |
| **Nuclear (uranium)** | huge energy from little fuel; no CO₂ while running | very expensive; radioactive waste; accident risk |

## Renewable sources

| Source | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Hydroelectric power (HEP)** | clean; cheap to run once built; reservoirs supply water and fish | expensive to build; floods land and displaces people; output falls in the dry season |
| **Solar** | abundant sunshine in West Africa; good for remote villages (solar panels, mini-grids) | needs batteries at night; panels cost money at first |
| **Wind** | clean; cheap to run | needs strong, steady winds; noisy; output varies |
| **Biomass (firewood, charcoal, crop waste)** | cheap and easily available; used by most rural households | causes deforestation; smoke harms health |
| **Geothermal** | constant supply from the Earth's heat | only in volcanic areas, such as East Africa's Rift Valley |
| **Tidal and wave** | predictable | expensive; few suitable sites |

## Energy resources of West Africa

| Resource | Where |
|---|---|
| **Petroleum and natural gas** | Nigeria (Niger Delta and offshore), Ghana (offshore Jubilee field, producing since 2010), Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal (offshore Sangomar field, producing since 2024), Niger |
| **Coal** | Enugu (Nigeria) |
| **Uranium** | Niger (Arlit), exported for nuclear power abroad |
| **Hydroelectric power** | Akosombo and Bui (Ghana), Kainji, Jebba and Shiroro (Nigeria), Manantali (Mali), Bumbuna (Sierra Leone) |
| **Solar** | sunshine everywhere, especially in the dry north; growing numbers of solar farms and home systems |
| **Biomass** | forests and savanna woodland across the region |

:::callout{type=local-example}
In **Sierra Leone**, most households cook with **firewood and charcoal**, which puts heavy pressure on forests around towns. Electricity comes mainly from **Bumbuna** and smaller hydroelectric stations, from **thermal (diesel and heavy-fuel-oil) plants**, including a floating **power ship** moored off Freetown since **2018**, and from power imported through the regional **CLSG** line linking Côte d'Ivoire, Liberia, Sierra Leone and Guinea. **Solar** home systems and mini-grids are spreading in rural towns. All petroleum products are imported.
:::

## Importance of energy

1. Powers **industries** and machines.
2. Fuels **transport**.
3. Provides **lighting, cooking and cooling** in homes.
4. Runs **hospitals, schools, water pumps** and communications.
5. Supports **agriculture** (irrigation pumps, processing, storage).
6. Earns **foreign exchange** when exported (Nigeria's oil).

## Problems of energy supply in West Africa

1. **Low electricity access**, especially in rural areas.
2. **Frequent power cuts** and low generating capacity.
3. **High cost** of imported fuel and of building power stations.
4. **Seasonal fall** in hydroelectric output in the dry season.
5. **Deforestation** from heavy use of firewood and charcoal.
6. **Poor maintenance** and losses in old power lines.
7. **Pollution** and oil spills in oil-producing areas.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Firewood and charcoal are **renewable** only if trees are replanted as fast as they are cut. Where forests are cleared faster than they regrow, as around many West African towns, biomass is being used **unsustainably**.
:::

## Key points

- **Renewable:** solar, hydroelectric, wind, biomass, geothermal, tidal. **Non-renewable:** coal, oil, gas, nuclear.
- **Fossil fuels** are convenient but will run out and release CO₂.
- West Africa: **oil and gas** (Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal), **uranium** (Niger), **HEP** (Akosombo, Kainji, Bumbuna), abundant **sunshine**.
- Sierra Leone relies on **firewood and charcoal** for cooking; electricity from **hydro, thermal plants, imports and solar**.

Expect to **classify sources of energy**, to **state the advantages and disadvantages of named sources**, and to **name energy resources in West Africa**. Questions on the problems of energy supply and the importance of energy are also common.

## Quick check

1. Distinguish renewable from non-renewable energy.
2. Give two advantages and one disadvantage of solar energy.
3. Name two West African countries that produce petroleum.
4. Where is uranium mined in West Africa?
5. Why is the use of charcoal a problem in Sierra Leone?

**Answers:**

1. Renewable sources are replaced naturally and will not run out; non-renewable sources are fixed in amount and will be used up.
2. Advantages: abundant sunshine; clean; suitable for remote areas. Disadvantage: needs batteries at night, or high initial cost.
3. Any two: Nigeria; Ghana; Côte d'Ivoire; Senegal; Niger.
4. At Arlit in Niger.
5. Cutting trees for charcoal causes deforestation, and smoke harms health.
