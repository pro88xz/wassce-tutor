## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain how shifts in demand or supply change the equilibrium
- explain maximum (ceiling) and minimum (floor) price controls
- state the effects of price controls, including black markets and surpluses

## When demand or supply shifts

A market stays at equilibrium only until something other than price changes. Then demand or supply shifts, and the market finds a **new equilibrium**.

| Change | Curve shift | New equilibrium price | New equilibrium quantity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Increase in demand** (e.g. higher incomes) | D shifts right | Rises | Rises |
| **Decrease in demand** (e.g. a cheaper substitute) | D shifts left | Falls | Falls |
| **Increase in supply** (e.g. a good harvest) | S shifts right | Falls | Rises |
| **Decrease in supply** (e.g. a poor harvest, higher costs) | S shifts left | Rises | Falls |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A bumper rice harvest.** Good rains give farmers far more rice than usual.

- Supply increases: the supply curve shifts **right**.
- At the old price there is now a surplus, so the price **falls**.
- As the price falls, buyers take more (an extension of demand).
- **New equilibrium:** a **lower price** and a **larger quantity** bought and sold.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
When supply increases, do not say "demand increases too". Demand (the curve) has not changed; only the **quantity demanded** rises as the price falls, a movement along the same demand curve.
:::

## Government price controls

Sometimes the government decides that the market price is unfair and fixes a price by law.

### Maximum price (price ceiling)

:::callout{type=definition}
A **maximum price** is a legal price set **below the equilibrium price**, above which the good must not be sold. Its aim is to **protect consumers**, usually for essential goods such as rice, fuel or house rents.
:::

Using the plantain market (equilibrium NLe 15, 60 bunches), suppose the government fixes a maximum price of **NLe 10**:

- Quantity demanded: 80 bunches; quantity supplied: 40 bunches.
- Result: a **shortage** (excess demand) of **40 bunches**.

**Effects of a maximum price:**

1. **Shortages**: more is wanted than is offered.
2. **Queues** and first-come-first-served rationing.
3. **Black markets**: goods are sold illegally above the legal price.
4. **Hoarding** by traders waiting to sell illegally.
5. **Reduced supply** over time, as producers move to more profitable goods.
6. The government may need **rationing** (coupons) or to supply the good itself.

### Minimum price (price floor)

:::callout{type=definition}
A **minimum price** is a legal price set **above the equilibrium price**, below which the good must not be sold. Its aim is to **protect producers** (for example, farmers' prices) or **workers** (a minimum wage).
:::

Suppose the government fixes a minimum price of **NLe 20** for plantain:

- Quantity demanded: 40 bunches; quantity supplied: 80 bunches.
- Result: a **surplus** (excess supply) of **40 bunches**.

**Effects of a minimum price:**

1. **Surpluses** of unsold goods.
2. The government may have to **buy and store** the surplus, which is costly.
3. Producers may **sell illegally below** the minimum price.
4. Consumers **pay more** than the market price.
5. For a **minimum wage**, a wage above equilibrium may reduce the number of workers employed.

:::callout{type=local-example}
A minimum wage is a minimum price for labour. It is meant to give workers a fairer income; economists debate how far a high minimum wage reduces jobs, especially in small businesses.
:::

## Key points

- Shifts in demand or supply create a **new equilibrium price and quantity**.
- **Maximum price**: below equilibrium, protects consumers, causes **shortages** and **black markets**.
- **Minimum price**: above equilibrium, protects producers or workers, causes **surpluses**.

Draw the original equilibrium, show the new line or the controlled price clearly, label the shortage or surplus on the quantity axis, and explain at least three effects.

## Quick check

1. What happens to equilibrium price and quantity when demand increases?
2. A maximum price is set below the equilibrium. What problem appears?
3. Why might a government set a minimum price for a farm product?

**Answers:** 1. Both rise. 2. A shortage (excess demand), often with queues and black markets. 3. To protect farmers' incomes by guaranteeing them a price above the market price.
