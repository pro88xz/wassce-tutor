The periodic table is chemistry's most useful map. Knowing just an element's position tells you whether it is a metal, how many electrons it uses in bonding, the formula of its oxide and how it is likely to react. This lesson shows how the table was built and how it is organised.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the contributions of Döbereiner, Newlands, Mendeleev and Moseley
- state the periodic law
- describe groups, periods and blocks, and name the main families of elements
- locate metals, non-metals and metalloids
- find an element's position from its atomic number or electron configuration

## Development of the periodic table

| Scientist | Idea | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Döbereiner** (1829) | **Triads**: groups of three similar elements in which the middle element's atomic mass is about the **average** of the other two | Worked for only a few groups |
| **Newlands** (1864) | **Law of octaves**: arranged by atomic mass, every **eighth** element had similar properties, like notes in music | Broke down after calcium |
| **Mendeleev** (1869) | Arranged elements by **atomic mass** in groups of similar properties; **left gaps** for undiscovered elements and **predicted their properties** | A few pairs (such as Ar and K) were out of order by mass |
| **Moseley** (1913) | Showed that **atomic number** (number of protons), not mass, is the true basis | (Gave the modern table) |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A Döbereiner triad.** Calcium (40), strontium (88) and barium (137) are similar metals.

$$\text{average of Ca and Ba} = \dfrac{40 + 137}{2} = 88.5$$

This is close to strontium's relative atomic mass of 87.6.
:::

Mendeleev's boldest prediction was "eka-silicon", a missing element below silicon. When **germanium** was found in 1886, its mass, density and oxide formula matched his predictions closely, which convinced chemists his table was right.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Modern periodic law:** the properties of elements are a **periodic function of their atomic numbers**.
:::

## Structure of the modern table

![Outline of the periodic table showing groups 1 to 0, periods 1 to 4, and the s-block, d-block and p-block, with the first 20 elements labelled](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/periodic-table-blocks.svg)

*Figure 1. Groups, periods and blocks. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

- **Groups** are **vertical columns**. Elements in a group have the **same number of outer electrons**, so they have similar chemical properties.
- **Periods** are **horizontal rows**. Elements in a period have the **same number of electron shells**.
- **Blocks** are named after the sub-shell being filled: **s-block** (groups 1, 2), **p-block** (groups 3 to 0), **d-block** (transition metals) and **f-block** (lanthanides and actinides).

### Families of elements

| Group | Name | Outer electrons | Examples | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Alkali metals** | 1 | Li, Na, K | Very reactive metals; form M⁺ ions |
| 2 | **Alkaline earth metals** | 2 | Be, Mg, Ca | Reactive metals; form M²⁺ ions |
| 3 | Boron group | 3 | B, Al | Al forms Al³⁺ |
| 4 | Carbon group | 4 | C, Si | Mainly covalent bonding |
| 5 | Nitrogen group | 5 | N, P | Non-metals at the top |
| 6 | Oxygen group (chalcogens) | 6 | O, S | Form X²⁻ ions |
| 7 | **Halogens** | 7 | F, Cl, Br, I | Very reactive non-metals; form X⁻ ions |
| 0 (18) | **Noble gases** | 8 (He: 2) | He, Ne, Ar | Very unreactive |
| d-block | **Transition metals** | — | Fe, Cu, Zn | Coloured compounds; variable valency; catalysts |

### Metals, non-metals and metalloids

- **Metals** are on the **left and centre** of the table, about three-quarters of all elements.
- **Non-metals** are at the **top right**, plus hydrogen.
- **Metalloids** (B, Si, Ge, As) lie along the **staircase** between them and have properties of both. Silicon is used in solar cells and computer chips.

## Finding an element's position

| You know | Period | Group |
|---|---|---|
| Shell arrangement 2, 8, 6 | 3 shells → **period 3** | 6 outer electrons → **group 6** |
| Configuration [Ar] 4s¹ | n = 4 → **period 4** | 1 outer electron → **group 1** |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Element X has atomic number 17.**

- Arrangement: 2, 8, 7.
- Three shells, so **period 3**; seven outer electrons, so **group 7**.
- X is a **halogen** (chlorine). It forms the ion X⁻ and the compound NaX with sodium.
:::

**Uses of the periodic table:**

- predicting the **properties** of an element from its group
- predicting **formulas** of compounds (group 2 + group 7 gives MX₂)
- classifying elements as **metals or non-metals**
- understanding **trends** in reactivity (next lesson)

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many of Sierra Leone's mineral resources sit in particular parts of the table. **Iron** (Tonkolili, Marampa) and **titanium** (rutile) are d-block transition metals. **Aluminium** (bauxite) is in group 3. **Gold** is a d-block metal, and **diamond** is pure carbon from group 4.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Elements in the same **group** have similar properties, not elements in the same **period**. Across a period, properties change steadily from metal to non-metal.
:::

## Key points

- **Döbereiner** (triads), **Newlands** (octaves), **Mendeleev** (gaps and predictions) and **Moseley** (atomic number) built the table.
- **Modern periodic law:** properties are a periodic function of **atomic number**.
- **Groups** share outer electrons and properties; **periods** share the number of shells; **blocks** are s, p, d and f.
- Main families: **alkali metals, alkaline earth metals, halogens, noble gases, transition metals**.
- Find **period** from the number of shells and **group** from the number of outer electrons.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **describe Mendeleev's contribution** (gaps and predictions), to **state the periodic law**, to **locate elements** from atomic number or configuration, and to **name groups and their characteristics**. Questions often give an unknown letter, such as "element Y with Z = 12", and ask for its group, period and the formula of a compound.
:::

## Quick check

1. State the modern periodic law.
2. What did Mendeleev do that convinced chemists his table was right?
3. Element Q has atomic number 12. Give its period, group and the formula of its chloride.
4. Where are the non-metals found in the periodic table?

**Answers:**

1. The properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers.
2. He left gaps for undiscovered elements and correctly predicted their properties, e.g. germanium.
3. 2, 8, 2: period 3, group 2; chloride QCl₂.
4. At the top right of the table (plus hydrogen).
