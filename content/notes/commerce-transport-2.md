A trader in Bo has ten tonnes of palm oil for Freetown, and an exporter has fifty tonnes of cocoa for Europe. Should they use a lorry or a train, a ship or a plane? This lesson explains how businesses choose a mode of transport, how containerisation changed world trade, and the documents and charges that go with moving goods.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the factors that influence the choice of transport
- explain containerisation and its advantages and disadvantages
- describe the main transport documents and the parties to them
- calculate freight charges
- explain the role of government in transport

## Factors that influence the choice of transport

| Factor | What the trader asks |
|---|---|
| **Cost** | Which mode gives the lowest total cost, including packing, handling and insurance? |
| **Speed and urgency** | How quickly must the goods arrive? Urgent goods go by air or road. |
| **Nature of the goods** | Are they perishable, fragile, bulky, heavy, dangerous or valuable? |
| **Distance** | Road for short trips; rail, sea or air for long ones |
| **Weight and volume** | Bulky goods go by sea or rail; small light parcels by air or courier |
| **Value of the goods** | High-value goods (gold, medicines) justify the cost of air and reduce theft risk |
| **Safety and security** | Which mode has fewer accidents, losses and theft? |
| **Reliability** | Will the goods arrive on time? |
| **Availability** | Is there a railway, airport or port nearby? |
| **Door-to-door service** | Can the goods be delivered right to the buyer's premises? |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Choosing a mode.**

- **Fresh fish from Tombo to Freetown market**: road, because it is a short distance, the fish is perishable and the lorry delivers to the market.
- **Iron ore from the mines to Europe**: rail to the port, then ocean ship, because the cargo is heavy, bulky and of low value per tonne.
- **Diamonds or gold to a buyer abroad**: air, because they are light, very valuable and need security.
- **Fuel from a coastal depot to a nearby storage tank**: pipeline, because the flow is continuous.
:::

## Containerisation

:::callout{type=definition}
**Containerisation** is the carrying of goods in large, standard-sized steel boxes (**containers**) that are packed and sealed at the start of the journey and moved between lorry, train and ship **without unpacking** until they reach the buyer.
:::

![The journey of a container: packed and sealed at the factory, taken by lorry or train to the port, loaded onto a ship, unloaded and cleared at Freetown port, and taken by lorry to the importer, where the seal is opened](https://wasscetutor.com/img/commerce/container-journey.svg)

*Figure 1. A container travels door to door without being opened. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

Standard containers are 20 feet or 40 feet long. Special ports have **container terminals** with large cranes, and ships are built to carry thousands of them.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| **Less handling**, so less **damage and theft** (the container is sealed) | Ports need costly **cranes and terminals** |
| **Faster loading and unloading**: ships spend less time in port | Not suitable for every cargo (very large machinery, liquids in bulk) |
| **Lower costs** of packing, labour and insurance | Fewer dock jobs, as machines replace workers |
| **Door-to-door** service using several modes (**intermodal** transport) | Empty containers must be returned, which costs money |
| Goods are protected from weather | Roads and bridges must carry heavy container lorries |
| Easier customs checks at inland depots | Small traders may not fill a whole container (they share one: **groupage**) |

## Transport documents

| Document | Used in | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Consignment note** | Road and rail | Gives details of the goods, sender and receiver; signed on delivery |
| **Waybill** | Road and rail | A list of goods carried, travelling with the vehicle |
| **Air waybill** | Air | Receipt and evidence of the contract of carriage by air (not a document of title) |
| **Bill of lading** | Sea | Receipt, evidence of the contract of carriage and **document of title** |
| **Charter party** | Sea (and air) | Contract to **hire a whole ship** (or aircraft) |
| **Freight note** | Sea | The shipping company's bill for freight charges |
| **Ship's manifest** | Sea | A list of all cargo on board, for customs |
| **Shipping note** | Sea | Details of goods to be loaded, sent to the port |
| **Dock warrant** | Sea | Proof that goods are stored at the docks |

**Types of charter party:**

- a **voyage charter**: the ship is hired for a single voyage
- a **time charter**: the ship is hired for a fixed period of time

## Freight charges

**Freight** is the charge for carrying goods. It is usually based on **weight**, **volume** or **distance**, and some carriers quote a rate **per tonne-kilometre**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A trader sends 20 tonnes of rice from Freetown to Kenema, a road distance of 300 km.** The haulage company charges NLe 2 per tonne-kilometre, plus a loading charge of NLe 500.

1. Tonne-kilometres $= 20 \times 300 = 6000$
2. Haulage charge $= 6000 \times 2 = $ NLe 12,000
3. Total freight $= 12000 + 500 = $ **NLe 12,500**
4. Freight per bag (400 bags of 50 kg) $= 12500 \div 400 = $ **NLe 31.25**

The trader must add this to the cost of each bag when fixing the selling price.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Comparing modes.** Goods worth NLe 50,000 must reach a buyer.

| | Road | Air |
|---|---|---|
| Freight | NLe 3,000 | NLe 9,000 |
| Insurance | NLe 1,000 | NLe 300 |
| Packing | NLe 800 | NLe 400 |
| **Total cost** | **NLe 4,800** | **NLe 9,700** |
| Time | 5 days | 1 day |

Road is cheaper by $9700 - 4800 = $ NLe 4,900, so it is chosen unless the goods are urgent or perishable.
:::

## Government and transport

The government:

- builds and maintains **roads, bridges, ports and airports**
- **licenses** vehicles and drivers and enforces **road safety**
- regulates **fares and freight rates**
- runs or supervises transport bodies: the **Sierra Leone Road Transport Corporation**, the **Sierra Leone Ports and Harbours Authority** and the **Sierra Leone Road Safety Authority**
- signs agreements with other countries for **ECOWAS road and transit routes**

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A **waybill** and an **air waybill** are not documents of title. Only the **bill of lading** is a document of title, so only it can be used to transfer ownership of goods at sea.
:::

## Key points

- Choice of transport depends on **cost, speed, nature of goods, distance, weight, value, safety, reliability and availability**.
- **Containerisation** moves sealed standard boxes door to door; it cuts handling, theft and port time but needs costly terminals.
- Transport documents: **consignment note, waybill, air waybill, bill of lading, charter party, manifest**.
- **Freight** = rate × weight (or volume) × distance, plus handling charges.

:::

3. Give two advantages of containerisation.
4. Distinguish a voyage charter from a time charter.

**Answers:** 1. Any four: cost, speed, nature of the goods, distance, weight and volume, value, safety, reliability, availability, door-to-door service. 2. Carrying goods in standard sealed steel boxes moved between lorry, train and ship without unpacking. 3. Any two: less handling, damage and theft; faster loading; lower costs; door-to-door service; protection from weather. 4. A voyage charter hires a ship for one voyage; a time charter hires it for a fixed period.
