On the night of 26 April 1961, the Union Jack was lowered in Freetown and the green, white and blue flag of Sierra Leone was raised. The road to that moment ran through elections, strikes, riots, party splits and a constitutional conference in London. This lesson traces the road to independence from 1951 to 1961 and the roles of the main leaders.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- outline the main events on the road to independence, 1951 to 1961
- explain the roles of Sir Milton Margai, Albert Margai and Siaka Stevens
- describe the Lancaster House conference of 1960
- explain how the APC was formed
- explain why Sierra Leone's road to independence was peaceful

## The road to independence

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1951** | The **SLPP** is formed and wins the elections under the new constitution; **Milton Margai** becomes Leader of Government Business |
| **1953** | Ministerial system introduced; Sierra Leoneans head government departments |
| **1955** | A **general strike and riots** in Freetown over wages and prices |
| **1955–1956** | **Riots in the north** against heavy taxes and chiefs' abuses |
| **1957** | General election under the 1956 constitution; the **SLPP** wins most seats |
| **1958** | **Albert Margai** and **Siaka Stevens** leave the SLPP and form the **People's National Party (PNP)**; Milton Margai becomes **Premier** |
| **1960** | Parties form the **United National Front** and attend the **Lancaster House conference** in London; independence is agreed |
| **1960** | **Siaka Stevens** refuses to sign the agreement and forms the **Elections Before Independence Movement**, which becomes the **All People's Congress (APC)** |
| **27 April 1961** | **Independence**; Sir Milton Margai becomes the first **Prime Minister** |

## The Lancaster House conference, 1960

In **April and May 1960**, Sierra Leonean leaders met British officials at **Lancaster House** in London. To speak with one voice, most parties had joined in a **United National Front** under Milton Margai. The conference agreed:

- **Independence on 27 April 1961**, within the Commonwealth.
- A **parliamentary constitution** with the British monarch as head of state, represented by a **Governor-General**.
- A **defence agreement** with Britain.

**Siaka Stevens** refused to sign. He objected to the defence agreement and demanded a **general election before independence**, arguing that the people should choose who would lead the new state. He formed the **Elections Before Independence Movement**, which became the **APC** later in 1960.

## The main leaders

| Leader | Role |
|---|---|
| **Sir Milton Margai** (1895–1964) | A medical doctor from the Protectorate; leader of the SLPP; Chief Minister, Premier and first Prime Minister; known for patient, moderate leadership that united Colony and Protectorate |
| **Albert Margai** (1910–1980) | Milton's younger brother, a lawyer; co-founder of the PNP; later rejoined the SLPP and succeeded his brother as Prime Minister in 1964 |
| **Siaka Stevens** (1905–1988) | Trade unionist and politician; minister under the SLPP, co-founder of the PNP, founder of the APC; later Prime Minister and President |
| **I. T. A. Wallace-Johnson** | Radical nationalist who mobilised workers and youth from 1938 |
| **H. C. Bankole-Bright** | Leader of the NCSL; champion of Colony interests |

## Why was the road peaceful?

- Britain was willing to **negotiate**, after Ghana's independence in 1957 showed the way.
- **Moderate leadership** under Milton Margai, who preferred negotiation to confrontation.
- **No large European settler population** demanding to keep power, unlike Kenya.
- **Constitutional reforms** gradually transferred power, reducing pressure for violence.
- **Unity** of the parties in the United National Front at the key moment.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Independence was celebrated on **27 April 1961**, which is still marked each year as **Independence Day**. The new flag's **green** stands for agriculture and natural resources, **white** for unity and justice, and **blue** for the natural harbour of Freetown and hopes for peace. Just before independence, the government declared a state of emergency and briefly detained APC leaders, including Stevens, a sign of the political tensions that followed.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The APC was not formed before the Lancaster House conference. It grew out of **Siaka Stevens' refusal to sign** the independence agreement in **1960**, through the **Elections Before Independence Movement**.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Leader of Government Business**: the post Milton Margai held after the SLPP won the 1951 elections.
- **People's National Party (PNP)**: the party formed in 1958 by Albert Margai and Siaka Stevens after they left the SLPP.
- **United National Front**: the alliance of parties formed in 1960 to attend the Lancaster House conference.
- **Lancaster House conference** (1960): the conference in London at which Sierra Leone's independence was agreed.
- **Elections Before Independence Movement**: the movement Siaka Stevens formed in 1960 after refusing to sign the independence agreement; it became the All People's Congress.
:::

## Key points

- **1951** SLPP wins; **1953** ministerial system; **1957** election; **1958** PNP formed and Margai becomes **Premier**.
- **1960 Lancaster House conference**: United National Front; independence agreed; **Stevens refused to sign** and formed the **APC**.
- **27 April 1961**: independence; **Sir Milton Margai**, first **Prime Minister**.
- The road was **peaceful** because of negotiation, moderate leaders and gradual reforms.

## Quick check

1. When did Sierra Leone become independent?
2. Who was Sierra Leone's first Prime Minister?
3. Why did Siaka Stevens refuse to sign the Lancaster House agreement?
4. What was the United National Front?

**Answers:**

1. 27 April 1961.
2. Sir Milton Margai.
3. He objected to the defence agreement with Britain and demanded a general election before independence.
4. A coalition of most political parties, formed in 1960 under Milton Margai, to negotiate independence with Britain with one voice.
