Volume measures the **space a solid takes up**: how much water a tank holds, how much concrete a pillar needs, how much rice a sack contains. Surface area measures the **total area of its outside**: how much metal makes a tin, how much cloth covers a box. WASSCE sets both, usually with cylinders, cones, spheres, prisms and pyramids, and often with a practical question about capacity in litres. This lesson gives every formula, the reasoning behind it, and worked examples of each type.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- find the volume of prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones and spheres
- find the surface area of cuboids, cylinders, cones and spheres
- convert between cm³, m³ and litres
- solve practical problems: tanks, containers, melting and recasting

## Units of volume and capacity

- $1$ cm³ $= 1$ ml
- $1000$ cm³ $= 1$ litre
- $1$ m³ $= 1\,000\,000$ cm³
- $1$ m³ $= 1000$ litres

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Cubing conversions.** $1$ m $= 100$ cm, so $1$ m³ $= 100^3 = 1\,000\,000$ cm³, not $100$ or $10\,000$.
:::

## Volumes

![Five solids with their volume formulas: cuboid V = lbh, cylinder V = πr²h, cone V = ⅓πr²h, sphere V = ⁴⁄₃πr³, pyramid V = ⅓ × base area × h](https://wasscetutor.com/img/mathematics/solids-formulas.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
| Solid | Volume |
|---|---|
| **prism** (any constant cross-section) | $V = \text{area of cross-section} \times \text{length}$ |
| cuboid | $V = lbh$ |
| cylinder | $V = \pi r^2 h$ |
| **pyramid** | $V = \tfrac13 \times \text{base area} \times h$ |
| cone | $V = \tfrac13 \pi r^2 h$ |
| sphere | $V = \tfrac43 \pi r^3$ |
| hemisphere | $V = \tfrac23 \pi r^3$ |
:::

:::callout{type=tip}
A **cylinder is a prism** with a circular cross-section, so $V = \pi r^2 \times h$. A **cone is a pyramid** with a circular base, so it is $\tfrac13$ of the cylinder with the same base and height. Remembering these links means fewer formulas to memorise.
:::

## Surface areas

:::callout{type=definition}
| Solid | Total surface area |
|---|---|
| cuboid | $2(lb + bh + lh)$ |
| cylinder (closed) | $2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh$ (two circles + curved surface) |
| cylinder (open at one end) | $\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh$ |
| cone | $\pi r^2 + \pi r l$ ($l$ = slant height) |
| sphere | $4\pi r^2$ |
:::

For a cone, the **slant height** $l$ comes from Pythagoras: $l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$.

## Worked examples

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A cylindrical water tank has radius 70 cm and height 1.5 m. How many litres does it hold when full? ($\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$)**

Use one unit: $h = 150$ cm.

$$V = \pi r^2 h = \frac{22}{7} \times 70^2 \times 150 = \frac{22}{7} \times 4900 \times 150 = 2\,310\,000 \text{ cm}^3$$

$2\,310\,000 \div 1000 = $ **2 310 litres**.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A cone has base radius 6 cm and height 8 cm. Find (a) its volume, (b) its total surface area. Give answers in terms of $\pi$.**

**(a)** $V = \tfrac13 \pi (6)^2 (8) = 96\pi$ cm³.

**(b)** Slant height $l = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = 10$ cm.

Total surface area $= \pi r^2 + \pi r l = 36\pi + 60\pi = 96\pi$ cm².
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A solid metal sphere of radius 3 cm is melted and recast into a cylinder of radius 2 cm. Find the height of the cylinder.**

The **volume stays the same**:

$$\pi (2)^2 h = \tfrac43 \pi (3)^3 \;\Rightarrow\; 4\pi h = 36\pi \;\Rightarrow\; h = 9 \text{ cm}$$
:::

:::callout{type=tip}
In **melting and recasting** problems, the volume before equals the volume after. Write the two volume formulas equal to each other, and $\pi$ often cancels.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A triangular prism has a cross-section that is a right-angled triangle with legs 6 cm and 8 cm. The prism is 20 cm long. Find its volume and total surface area.**

Cross-section area $= \tfrac12 \times 6 \times 8 = 24$ cm². Volume $= 24 \times 20 = 480$ cm³.

Hypotenuse $= 10$ cm. Surface area $= 2 \times 24 + (6 + 8 + 10) \times 20 = 48 + 480 = 528$ cm².
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
**A family in Makeni stores drinking water in a cylindrical drum of diameter 56 cm and height 90 cm. If the family uses 20 litres a day, how many days does a full drum last? ($\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$)**

$r = 28$ cm. $V = \tfrac{22}{7} \times 28^2 \times 90 = \tfrac{22}{7} \times 784 \times 90 = 221\,760$ cm³ $\approx 221.8$ litres.

$221.76 \div 20 \approx 11.1$, so a full drum lasts **11 full days**.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Find the volume of a cuboid 12 cm × 5 cm × 4 cm, and its capacity in litres."}
$V = 240$ cm³ $= 0.24$ litres.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Find the volume of a sphere of radius 21 cm (π = 22/7)."}
$\tfrac43 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 21^3 = \tfrac43 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 9261 = 38\,808$ cm³.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. A closed cylinder has radius 7 cm and height 10 cm. Find its total surface area (π = 22/7)."}
$2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh = 2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 49 + 2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 10 = 308 + 440 = 748$ cm².
:::

:::reveal{title="4. A cone has volume 154 cm³ and radius 3.5 cm. Find its height (π = 22/7)."}
$\tfrac13 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 12.25 \times h = 154 \Rightarrow \tfrac{38.5}{3}h = 154 \Rightarrow h = 12$ cm.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. How many cubes of edge 2 cm can be cut from a cuboid 10 cm × 8 cm × 6 cm?"}
$\tfrac{10}{2} \times \tfrac82 \times \tfrac62 = 5 \times 4 \times 3 = 60$ cubes.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Prism**: a solid with the same cross-section all along its length; its volume is the cross-section times the length.
- **Cross-section**: the shape of the cut face when a solid is sliced at right angles to its length.
- **Pyramid**: a solid with a flat base and sloping faces that meet at a point; its volume is one third of base times height.
- **Slant height**: the distance from the tip of a cone to the edge of its base along the sloping side: $l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$.
- **Surface area**: the total area of all the faces of a solid.
- **Volume**: the amount of space a solid takes up.
- **Cylinder**: a prism with a circular cross-section; its volume is $\pi r^2 h$.
- **Melting and recasting**: reshaping a solid into another shape; the volume stays the same.
:::

## Key points

- **Prism**: cross-section × length; **pyramid/cone**: $\tfrac13 \times$ base $\times$ height; **sphere**: $\tfrac43\pi r^3$.
- **Surface area**: add the areas of all faces; cone uses the slant height $l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$.
- $1000$ cm³ $= 1$ litre; $1$ m³ $= 1000$ litres. **Cube** the conversion factor.
- **Melting and recasting**: volume before = volume after.
- Use **one unit** throughout, and give answers in the unit asked.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Volume and surface area questions appear in both papers: cylinders and cones most often, with spheres, prisms and pyramids, and practical contexts (tanks in litres, recasting, cost of material). Examiners award marks for the formula, correct substitution and units; mixing cm and m is the commonest error.
:::
