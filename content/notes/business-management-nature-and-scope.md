# Nature and scope of business management: functions and resources

## Learning objectives
You should be able to explain business management; distinguish effectiveness from efficiency; describe core management functions; identify organisational resources; and explain why coordination and control matter.

## Meaning and purpose
Business management is the process of planning, organising, directing and controlling people and other resources to achieve organisational objectives. A business may seek profit, growth, survival, customer satisfaction or social value. Management determines how limited resources are combined to deliver products or services reliably.

Effectiveness means achieving the intended objectives. Efficiency means using resources with minimum avoidable waste while maintaining the required standard. A business can be effective but inefficient if it reaches its sales target using excessive labour or materials. It can be efficient at producing an item that customers do not want, which is not effective.

## Main management functions
Planning involves setting objectives, assessing the current position, considering alternatives and deciding actions. A plan should specify responsibilities, resources, timing and measures of success. Organising arranges tasks, people, authority and resources so the plan can be carried out. It includes grouping activities into departments and defining reporting relationships.

Staffing ensures that the organisation has people with appropriate skills and provides recruitment, selection, training, appraisal and development. Directing or leading involves communicating expectations, motivating employees, resolving problems and guiding work. Controlling compares actual performance with standards, identifies deviations and takes corrective action. Control is not simply punishment; it provides feedback that helps the organisation improve.

These functions overlap and operate continuously. A manager may revise a plan after reviewing sales results, reorganise duties after a staff change or arrange training after identifying a performance gap.

## Resources and business environment
Managers coordinate human resources, finance, physical assets, information, technology and time. They must also respond to external influences such as customers, competitors, suppliers, government regulation, economic conditions and technological change. A decision to expand production, for example, should consider demand, working capital, skilled labour, equipment capacity, reliable electricity and distribution.

Management levels often include top, middle and supervisory management. Top management sets broad direction and policy; middle managers translate strategy into departmental plans; supervisors coordinate day-to-day operations. The exact titles and structure vary with the size of the organisation.

## Skills and responsibilities
Useful management skills include technical knowledge, communication, decision-making, problem-solving, delegation, negotiation and ethical judgement. Managers are accountable for how resources are used and how decisions affect employees, customers, owners and the wider community. Good management requires accurate records and honest reporting.

## WASSCE examination focus
When asked to state management functions, name each function and explain its purpose. Do not treat leadership as identical to planning or controlling. Use practical examples to show how the functions work together, and distinguish efficiency from effectiveness.

## Check your understanding
1. Define business management.
2. Distinguish efficiency from effectiveness.
3. State and explain four management functions.
4. Identify five resources managed by a business.
5. Explain the role of control in management.

1. Coordinating and directing organisational resources through management functions to achieve objectives.
2. Effectiveness is achieving objectives; efficiency is using resources with minimal avoidable waste.
3. Planning, organising, staffing, directing/leading and controlling are core functions.
4. People, finance, physical assets, information, technology and time.
5. It compares results with standards, identifies gaps and supports corrective action.
