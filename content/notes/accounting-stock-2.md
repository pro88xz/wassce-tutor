The basic rule for valuing inventory is that it must be shown at the **lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV)**. **Cost** includes the purchase price plus costs of bringing the goods to their present place and condition; **net realisable value** is the expected **selling price less the costs** of completing and selling the goods. The rule applies **prudence**: losses are recognised as soon as they are expected. This lesson explains the rule, **stocktaking**, how to adjust a stock count taken on a date other than the year end, and the effect of inventory errors on profit.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- apply the rule of the lower of cost and net realisable value
- explain what is included in the cost of inventory
- describe stocktaking and its purposes
- adjust a stock count taken after (or before) the year end
- explain the effect of errors in closing inventory on profit

## Lower of cost and net realisable value

$$\text{NRV} = \text{Expected selling price} - \text{Costs to complete and sell}$$

**Inventory value = the lower of cost and NRV**, item by item (or group by group).

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Value the following items of closing inventory (NLe).**

| Item | Cost | Selling price | Costs to sell | NRV | Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: bags of cement | 6,000 | 9,000 | 300 | 8,700 | **6,000** |
| B: damaged fridge | 4,000 | 3,200 | 500 | 2,700 | **2,700** |
| C: roofing sheets | 10,000 | 12,500 | 1,000 | 11,500 | **10,000** |
| **Total** | 20,000 | | | | **18,700** |

Only the damaged fridge is written down, from 4,000 to 2,700: a loss of 1,300 recognised now, as **prudence** requires.
:::

**Cost** includes the purchase price, import duties and **carriage inwards**, but **not** selling costs, storage after purchase or general administration.

## Stocktaking

**Stocktaking** is the physical counting, listing and valuing of all goods held at a date, usually the last day of the financial year.

**Purposes:**

1. To find the **closing inventory** figure for the final accounts.
2. To **check** the stock records against the goods actually held.
3. To find **losses** from theft, damage or deterioration.
4. To identify **slow-moving or obsolete** goods.

## Adjusting a stocktake to the year end

If stock is counted **after** the year end, adjust the count back to the year-end date:

| Movement between year end and count | Adjustment to the count |
|---|---|
| goods **sold** (at cost) after the year end | **add** back: they were in stock at the year end |
| goods **bought** after the year end | **deduct**: they were not in stock at the year end |
| goods **returned by customers** after the year end | **deduct** |
| goods **returned to suppliers** after the year end | **add** back |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Stock was counted on 7 January 2027 at NLe 26,000 (cost). Between 31 December 2026 and 7 January, sales were NLe 5,000 at selling price (cost plus a mark-up of 25%) and goods costing NLe 3,000 were received. Find the inventory at 31 December 2026.**

Cost of sales $= 5\,000 \times \frac{100}{125} = 4\,000$

Inventory at 31 December $= 26\,000 + 4\,000 - 3\,000 = 27\,000$
:::

## Effect of inventory errors

| Error | Effect in that year | Effect next year |
|---|---|---|
| closing inventory **overstated** | gross and net profit **overstated** | opening inventory overstated, so profit **understated** |
| closing inventory **understated** | profit **understated** | profit **overstated** |

An inventory error corrects itself over two years, but both years' profits are wrong.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Shops selling perishable goods, such as fresh fish or bread in Freetown's markets, or medicines near their expiry date, must watch net realisable value closely. Goods that will only sell at a reduced price must be **written down** at the year end, not left at cost.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
When converting sales after the year end to cost, use the **mark-up correctly**. With a mark-up of 25% on cost, cost is $\frac{100}{125}$ of selling price, **not** 75% of selling price.
:::

## Key points

- Inventory is valued at the **lower of cost and net realisable value**, item by item.
- **NRV = selling price − costs to complete and sell.**
- **Stocktaking** counts and values goods; counts on other dates are **adjusted** to the year end.
- Overstated closing inventory **overstates** profit this year and **understates** it next year.

## Quick check

1. State the rule for valuing inventory.
2. Goods cost NLe 2,000 and can be sold for NLe 2,400 after spending NLe 600 on repairs. What is their value?
3. Name two purposes of stocktaking.
4. A count after the year end includes goods bought after the year end. What adjustment is made?
5. What is the effect on gross profit if closing inventory is understated by NLe 1,000?

**Answers:**

1. The lower of cost and net realisable value.
2. NRV $= 2\,400 - 600 = 1\,800$, lower than cost, so NLe 1,800.
3. Any two: find closing inventory; check stock records; find losses; identify slow-moving or obsolete goods.
4. Deduct them, because they were not in stock at the year end.
5. Gross profit is understated by NLe 1,000.
