**Pollution** is the release into the environment of substances or energy that harm living things. In Sierra Leone you can see it in the plastic bottles that block Freetown's drains before the rains, the brown water downstream of a mine, the smoke from generators and charcoal fires, and the rubbish on the beaches. Some pollution affects only a street; some, like the carbon dioxide that drives global warming, affects the whole planet. This lesson describes the main types of pollution, their sources and effects, how they can be controlled, and the wider environmental problems of deforestation, erosion, flooding and climate change.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define pollution and pollutant, and distinguish biodegradable and non-biodegradable pollutants
- describe the sources, effects and control of air, water, land and noise pollution
- explain eutrophication and bioaccumulation
- explain the greenhouse effect, global warming and ozone depletion
- discuss environmental issues in Sierra Leone: deforestation, erosion, flooding and waste

## Pollution and pollutants

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Pollution**: the addition of harmful substances or energy to the environment, faster than it can absorb or remove them.
- **Pollutant**: the substance or energy that causes pollution (smoke, sewage, noise, heat, plastic).
- **Biodegradable** pollutants are broken down by decomposers (sewage, food waste, paper).
- **Non-biodegradable** pollutants are not broken down, or only very slowly (plastics, metals such as mercury and lead, DDT, glass).
:::

## Air pollution

| Pollutant | Main sources | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| **smoke and soot** (particles) | charcoal and wood fires, bush burning, generators, vehicles | coughs, bronchitis, asthma; blackens buildings; reduces light for plants |
| **carbon monoxide** (CO) | vehicle exhausts, generators and charcoal stoves in closed rooms | combines with haemoglobin and stops it carrying oxygen; can kill |
| **sulfur dioxide** (SO₂) | burning fuels containing sulfur, some industry | **acid rain**, which damages leaves, buildings and lakes; breathing problems |
| **carbon dioxide** (CO₂) | burning fossil fuels, wood and charcoal; deforestation | **global warming** |
| **CFCs** | old refrigerators, air conditioners, some aerosols | **destroy the ozone layer** |
| **dust** | quarries, mining, unpaved roads | lung disease |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Running a generator indoors.** Every year people die from carbon monoxide by running a generator, or burning charcoal, inside a closed room. CO has **no colour and no smell**, so victims do not notice. Generators belong **outside**, away from windows.
:::

### The greenhouse effect and global warming

![Sunlight passes through the atmosphere and warms the Earth's surface; the surface gives off heat; greenhouse gases such as CO₂ and methane trap some heat and send it back down](https://wasscetutor.com/img/biology/greenhouse-effect.svg)

Greenhouse gases (**carbon dioxide, methane, water vapour, nitrous oxide**) let sunlight through but trap some of the heat given off by the Earth. This natural **greenhouse effect** keeps the planet warm enough for life. Burning fuels and cutting forests have raised the amount of CO₂, so more heat is trapped: **global warming**.

**Effects:** rising sea levels (threatening coastal areas such as parts of Freetown and the islands), heavier and more irregular rainfall, droughts, coral bleaching, and changes to where crops grow and diseases spread.

### Ozone depletion

The **ozone layer** high in the atmosphere absorbs harmful **ultraviolet (UV)** radiation from the sun. **CFCs** break ozone down, letting more UV reach the ground, which increases skin cancer and eye cataracts and damages crops. CFCs are now banned in most countries.

## Water pollution

| Pollutant | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **sewage** | open defecation, overflowing pit latrines, untreated sewers | spreads **cholera, typhoid, dysentery**; causes deoxygenation |
| **fertilisers** | run-off from farms | **eutrophication** |
| **oil** | spills, boats, garages | coats birds and fish, blocks oxygen entering the water |
| **mining waste** | silt, chemicals and mercury from mines | muddy water blocks light; poisons fish and people |
| **industrial waste and heavy metals** (lead, mercury) | factories, mining | poisoning; bioaccumulation |
| **plastics** | litter washed into drains, rivers and the sea | block drains; kill turtles, fish and seabirds |

:::callout{type=definition}
**Eutrophication** step by step:

1. Fertilisers or sewage add **nitrates and phosphates** to water.
2. **Algae** grow rapidly (an **algal bloom**), turning the water green.
3. The algae block light, so plants below **die**.
4. **Decomposers** (bacteria) feed on the dead plants and algae and multiply.
5. The bacteria use up the **dissolved oxygen** in respiration.
6. **Fish and other animals die** from lack of oxygen.
:::

### Bioaccumulation

Some non-biodegradable poisons, such as **DDT** and **mercury**, are not broken down or excreted. They build up in an organism's body (**bioaccumulation**) and become **more concentrated at each step** up the food chain (**biomagnification**). Top predators, including people who eat large fish, receive the highest dose.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Water contains 0.00005 ppm of a pesticide. Plankton contain 0.04 ppm, small fish 0.5 ppm, large fish 2 ppm and fish-eating birds 25 ppm. Explain these figures.**

The pesticide is **not broken down or excreted**. Each animal eats many organisms from the level below and keeps the pesticide from all of them, so the concentration **increases at each trophic level**. The birds at the top have the highest concentration, enough to harm them (for example, thin eggshells).
:::

## Land pollution

- **Refuse and plastics** dumped in streets, drains and open dumps: breed flies, mosquitoes and rats; block drains; look and smell bad.
- **Pesticides and herbicides**: kill useful soil organisms, pollinators and predators of pests.
- **Mining**: open pits and spoil heaps destroy farmland; abandoned pits fill with water and breed mosquitoes.
- **Oil and chemical spills** at workshops and depots.

## Noise pollution

Loud music, generators, vehicles, aircraft and machinery cause **hearing loss**, stress, headaches and poor sleep, and make it hard to concentrate in school. Control: limit hours, keep generators away from homes and schools, use silencers, wear ear protection.

## Environmental issues in Sierra Leone

- **Deforestation** for farming, charcoal, timber, mining and building: loss of habitat, soil erosion, flooding, and less rainfall.
- **Soil erosion**: rain washes bare soil from slopes and farms, silting rivers and dams.
- **Flooding in Freetown**: plastics and rubbish block drains; building on riverbanks and hills increases run-off.
- **Landslides**: the **August 2017 mudslide at Regent**, after very heavy rain on a deforested, built-up hillside, killed hundreds of people and left many more missing.
- **Coastal erosion**: removing sand from beaches for building leaves the coast open to the sea.
- **Mining**: damaged land and polluted rivers in mining areas.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Plastic bottles and the rainy season.** Water sold in plastic sachets and bottles is everywhere in Sierra Leone. When the empties are thrown into gutters, the first heavy rains wash them into the drains, which block, and streets flood. Collecting and recycling plastic, community clean-ups before the rains, and fines for littering are simple ways to break the cycle.
:::

## Controlling pollution

- **Reduce, reuse, recycle**; proper refuse collection and sanitary landfills.
- **Treat sewage**; build and use proper latrines and toilets.
- Use fertilisers and pesticides **correctly** and only as needed; use biological control.
- Keep generators outdoors and well maintained; fit vehicles with catalytic converters.
- Reclaim mined land: fill pits, replace topsoil and replant.
- **Laws and education**, enforced by the Environment Protection Agency.

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Distinguish between biodegradable and non-biodegradable pollutants, with one example of each."}
Biodegradable pollutants can be broken down by decomposers (sewage, food waste). Non-biodegradable ones cannot, or only very slowly (plastics, DDT, mercury).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Explain why carbon monoxide is dangerous."}
It combines with **haemoglobin** more strongly than oxygen does, so blood carries less oxygen; it has no colour or smell, so poisoning is not noticed.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Outline the steps of eutrophication."}
Nitrates and phosphates enter water → algal bloom → plants below die from lack of light → bacteria decompose them and multiply → oxygen is used up → fish die.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Give two effects of global warming."}
Any two: rising sea level; more extreme rainfall and flooding; drought; changes in crop growing areas; coral bleaching; spread of some diseases.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Suggest three ways to reduce flooding in Freetown."}
Any three: keep drains clear of rubbish; collect and recycle plastics; stop building on riverbanks and steep slopes; protect and replant forests on the hills; build bigger drains.
:::

## Key points

- **Pollution** harms living things; pollutants may be **biodegradable** or **non-biodegradable**.
- **Air**: smoke, CO, SO₂ (acid rain), CO₂ (global warming), CFCs (ozone depletion).
- **Water**: sewage (disease), fertiliser (**eutrophication**), oil, mining waste, plastics.
- **Bioaccumulation**: persistent poisons concentrate up the food chain.
- Sierra Leone issues: **deforestation, erosion, flooding, landslides, plastics, mining damage**.
- Control: reduce/reuse/recycle, treat sewage, careful chemical use, laws and education.
