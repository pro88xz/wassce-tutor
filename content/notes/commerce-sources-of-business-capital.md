# Sources of business capital: internal and external finance

## Learning objectives
Distinguish capital from revenue, identify internal and external finance and select finance suitable for a business need.

## Meaning of business capital
Business capital is the finance and other resources committed to establishing, operating or expanding an enterprise. It pays for assets, stock, premises, wages, technology and operating needs. Capital is not the same as sales revenue: revenue is income earned from trading, while capital refers to funds or resources invested in the business.

## Internal sources
Internal finance is generated within the business or contributed by its owners. **Owner's savings** are personal funds invested by the proprietor. **Retained profit** is profit kept in the business instead of being distributed. **Sale of surplus assets** provides proceeds from assets no longer required. Improving working-capital management can release cash by reducing excess stock or collecting customer debts faster.

Internal finance may avoid interest and external lender conditions, but it may be insufficient for large projects. Selling an asset can also reduce productive capacity if the asset is still needed.

## External sources
External finance comes from outside the business. A **bank loan** is repaid over an agreed period, normally with interest and possible fees. An **overdraft** allows a current account to go below zero up to an approved limit and is generally for short-term cash-flow needs. **Trade credit** allows a buyer to receive goods now and pay the supplier later. **Equity finance** involves investors providing funds in exchange for an ownership interest. **Leasing** gives a business use of an asset under a lease agreement. **Hire purchase** normally transfers ownership only after the required instalments and conditions are completed. Debentures and bonds are debt instruments with stated terms.

## Choosing finance
A business should compare the amount required, purpose, total cost, repayment period, security, cash-flow capacity, effect on ownership and risk. Long-term equipment is usually better matched with longer-term finance than a short-term overdraft. Seasonal stock may be financed with short-term working capital if expected sales can repay it.

A small retailer in Makeni might use retained profit to increase stock or negotiate trade credit. A larger firm purchasing machinery may compare a loan, lease and equity investment. The lowest advertised interest rate is not necessarily the cheapest total option once fees and conditions are included.

## WASSCE examination focus
Match the term of finance to the life of the asset or need. Loans and overdrafts create repayment obligations; equity finance may dilute existing ownership but does not usually require fixed loan repayments.
