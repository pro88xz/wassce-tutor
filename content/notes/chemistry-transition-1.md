The block of elements in the middle of the periodic table includes iron, copper, chromium, nickel and titanium: the metals of buildings, wires, machines and coloured paints. These **transition metals** share a set of properties that set them apart from the metals of groups 1 and 2: many colours, several oxidation states, and the ability to act as catalysts.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a transition element and write the configurations of the first transition series
- describe the general physical properties of transition metals
- explain variable oxidation states, coloured compounds, catalytic activity and complex-ion formation
- give examples of coloured ions and catalysts
- compare transition metals with group 1 and 2 metals

## What is a transition element?

The **first transition series** runs from **scandium (Sc, 21)** to **zinc (Zn, 30)** in period 4. In these elements the **3d sub-shell** is being filled.

:::callout{type=definition}
A **transition element** is a d-block element that forms **at least one stable ion with a partially filled d sub-shell**.
:::

| Element | Configuration | Common ions |
|---|---|---|
| Sc | [Ar] 3d¹ 4s² | Sc³⁺ (3d⁰) |
| Ti | [Ar] 3d² 4s² | Ti⁴⁺ |
| V | [Ar] 3d³ 4s² | V³⁺, VO²⁺ |
| Cr | [Ar] **3d⁵ 4s¹** | Cr³⁺ |
| Mn | [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s² | Mn²⁺, MnO₄⁻ |
| Fe | [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s² | Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺ |
| Co | [Ar] 3d⁷ 4s² | Co²⁺ |
| Ni | [Ar] 3d⁸ 4s² | Ni²⁺ |
| Cu | [Ar] **3d¹⁰ 4s¹** | Cu⁺, Cu²⁺ |
| Zn | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² | Zn²⁺ (3d¹⁰) |

By the strict definition, **scandium** (Sc³⁺ is d⁰) and **zinc** (Zn²⁺ is d¹⁰) are **not** true transition elements, though they are d-block elements. Zinc shows few typical transition properties: its compounds are white and it has only one oxidation state.

## General physical properties

- **Hard and strong**, with **high melting points** and boiling points (many delocalised electrons, from both 4s and 3d)
- **High densities**
- Good **conductors** of heat and electricity
- **Malleable and ductile**, and form useful **alloys**
- Iron, cobalt and nickel are strongly **magnetic** (ferromagnetic)

## Characteristic chemical properties

### 1. Variable oxidation states

The 4s and 3d electrons have similar energies, so different numbers can be lost.

| Element | Common oxidation states | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Iron | +2, +3 | FeSO₄ (green), FeCl₃ (yellow-brown) |
| Copper | +1, +2 | Cu₂O (red), CuSO₄ (blue) |
| Manganese | +2, +4, +7 | MnSO₄, MnO₂, KMnO₄ |
| Chromium | +3, +6 | Cr₂O₃ (green), K₂Cr₂O₇ (orange) |

Group 1 and 2 metals have only **one** oxidation state each (+1 or +2).

### 2. Coloured compounds

Most transition-metal ions are **coloured** in solution, because of their partly filled d sub-shells.

| Ion | Colour in solution |
|---|---|
| Cu²⁺ (aq) | **Blue** |
| Fe²⁺ (aq) | **Pale green** |
| Fe³⁺ (aq) | **Yellow-brown** |
| Ni²⁺ (aq) | Green |
| Co²⁺ (aq) | Pink |
| Mn²⁺ (aq) | Very pale pink |
| MnO₄⁻ | **Purple** |
| Cr₂O₇²⁻ | **Orange** |
| CrO₄²⁻ | **Yellow** |
| Zn²⁺, Sc³⁺ | **Colourless** (d¹⁰ and d⁰) |

Compounds of group 1 and 2 metals are usually **white**, or colourless in solution.

### 3. Catalytic activity

Transition metals and their compounds are important **catalysts**, because they can change oxidation state and provide surfaces where reactions happen.

| Catalyst | Process |
|---|---|
| **Iron** | Haber process (ammonia) |
| **Vanadium(V) oxide** | Contact process (sulphuric acid) |
| **Nickel** | Hydrogenation of vegetable oils (margarine) |
| **Platinum / rhodium** | Ostwald process (nitric acid); catalytic converters in cars |
| **Manganese(IV) oxide** | Decomposition of hydrogen peroxide |

### 4. Complex ions

Transition-metal ions form **complex ions**, in which molecules or ions (**ligands**) such as water, ammonia or cyanide donate lone pairs to the metal ion through **dative bonds**.

| Complex ion | Colour |
|---|---|
| [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | Blue (copper(II) sulphate solution) |
| [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ | **Deep blue** (Cu²⁺ with excess ammonia) |
| [Fe(SCN)]²⁺ | **Blood red** (test for Fe³⁺ with thiocyanate) |
| [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ | Gives a dark-blue precipitate with Fe²⁺ (a test for iron(II)) |

## Transition metals compared with group 1 and 2 metals

| Property | Group 1 and 2 metals | Transition metals |
|---|---|---|
| Hardness, melting point | Soft, low to moderate | **Hard, high** |
| Density | Low | **High** |
| Reactivity with water | Reactive | **Much less reactive** (iron rusts slowly) |
| Oxidation states | One (+1 or +2) | **Several** |
| Colour of compounds | White or colourless | **Coloured** |
| Catalysts | Rarely | **Often** |
| Complex ions | Rarely | **Readily** |

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Rutile**, mined in southern Sierra Leone, is titanium(IV) oxide, TiO₂. Titanium is a transition metal. Its oxide is the brilliant white pigment in house paints, toothpaste and plastics. Titanium **metal** is light, strong and corrosion-resistant, and is used in aircraft and in artificial hip joints.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Zinc is in the d-block but is **not** a typical transition metal: its only ion, Zn²⁺, has a **full** d sub-shell (3d¹⁰), so its compounds are white and it has one oxidation state.
:::

## Key points

- The first transition series (Sc to Zn) fills the **3d** sub-shell; a **transition element** forms a stable ion with a **partly filled d sub-shell**.
- Transition metals are **hard, dense and high-melting**, conduct well and form alloys.
- Characteristic properties: **variable oxidation states**, **coloured ions** (Cu²⁺ blue, Fe²⁺ green, Fe³⁺ yellow-brown, MnO₄⁻ purple, Cr₂O₇²⁻ orange), **catalytic activity** (Fe, V₂O₅, Ni, Pt, MnO₂) and **complex ions**.
- **Zn and Sc** are d-block elements but not true transition elements.

## Quick check

1. Define a transition element.
2. Give two oxidation states of iron and the colours of their ions in solution.
3. Name the transition-metal catalyst used in the Haber process.
4. Why are zinc compounds white?

**Answers:**

1. A d-block element that forms at least one stable ion with a partially filled d sub-shell.
2. +2 (Fe²⁺, pale green) and +3 (Fe³⁺, yellow-brown).
3. Iron.
4. Zn²⁺ has a completely filled d sub-shell (3d¹⁰), so it does not absorb visible light.
