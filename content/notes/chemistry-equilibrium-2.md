If a reaction reaches equilibrium with only 15% conversion, can a factory push it further? Le Chatelier's principle tells chemical engineers which way an equilibrium will move when conditions change, and the equilibrium constant tells them by how much. This lesson applies both to the industrial manufacture of ammonia and sulphuric acid.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state Le Chatelier's principle
- predict the effect of changing concentration, pressure and temperature on the position of equilibrium
- explain why a catalyst does not affect the position of equilibrium
- explain the choice of conditions in the Haber and Contact processes
- write expressions for the equilibrium constant Kc and use them in calculations

## Le Chatelier's principle

:::callout{type=definition}
**Le Chatelier's principle:** if a system at equilibrium is subjected to a **change in conditions**, the position of equilibrium **shifts so as to oppose** (partly cancel) that change.
:::

| Change | Shift in equilibrium position |
|---|---|
| **Add a reactant** (raise its concentration) | To the **right**, to use it up; more product |
| **Remove a product** | To the **right**, to replace it |
| **Add a product** | To the **left** |
| **Increase pressure** (gases) | Towards the side with **fewer moles of gas** |
| **Decrease pressure** | Towards the side with **more moles of gas** |
| **Increase temperature** | In the **endothermic** direction, to absorb heat |
| **Decrease temperature** | In the **exothermic** direction, to release heat |
| **Add a catalyst** | **No shift**: equilibrium is reached **faster** |

A **catalyst** speeds up the forward and backward reactions **equally**, so it does not change the position of equilibrium or the yield. It only shortens the time needed to reach equilibrium.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g); ΔH = −92 kJ/mol.** Predict the effect on the yield of ammonia.

| Change | Effect | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Increase pressure | **More NH₃** | 4 moles of gas on the left, 2 on the right; the system moves to the side with fewer moles |
| Increase temperature | **Less NH₃** | The forward reaction is exothermic; heating favours the endothermic (backward) reaction |
| Remove NH₃ (liquefy it) | **More NH₃** | The equilibrium shifts right to replace it |
| Add iron catalyst | **No change in yield** | Equilibrium is reached faster |
:::

If both sides have the **same number of moles of gas**, as in H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), pressure has **no effect** on the position.

## Industrial compromises

### The Haber process (ammonia)

$$\text{N}_2\text{(g)} + 3\text{H}_2\text{(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3\text{(g)} \qquad \Delta H = -92 \text{ kJ/mol}$$

| Condition | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Temperature** | About **450 °C** | Low temperature gives a better yield but is **too slow**; 450 °C is a **compromise** between yield and rate |
| **Pressure** | About **200 atm** | High pressure increases yield and rate; higher pressures are too **costly and dangerous** |
| **Catalyst** | **Iron** | Speeds up attainment of equilibrium |
| **Removing product** | Ammonia is cooled and **liquefied**; unreacted gases are **recycled** | Only about 15% converts in each pass, but recycling gives a high overall yield |

### The Contact process (sulphuric acid)

$$2\text{SO}_2\text{(g)} + \text{O}_2\text{(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{SO}_3\text{(g)} \qquad \Delta H = -197 \text{ kJ/mol}$$

| Condition | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Temperature** | About **450 °C** | Compromise between yield (favoured by low T) and rate |
| **Pressure** | **1–2 atm** | Conversion is already about 99% at low pressure, so high pressure is not worth its cost |
| **Catalyst** | **Vanadium(V) oxide**, V₂O₅ | Speeds up the reaction |
| **Excess oxygen** (air) | Shifts the equilibrium to the right |

## The equilibrium constant, Kc

For a general reaction $a\text{A} + b\text{B} \rightleftharpoons c\text{C} + d\text{D}$:

$$K_c = \dfrac{[\text{C}]^c\,[\text{D}]^d}{[\text{A}]^a\,[\text{B}]^b}$$

where square brackets mean concentration in mol/dm³ **at equilibrium**. Products go on top; each concentration is raised to the power of its coefficient.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**1. H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g).** At equilibrium, [H₂] = 0.10, [I₂] = 0.10 and [HI] = 0.70 mol/dm³. Calculate Kc.

$$K_c = \dfrac{[\text{HI}]^2}{[\text{H}_2][\text{I}_2]} = \dfrac{(0.70)^2}{0.10 \times 0.10} = \dfrac{0.49}{0.010} = 49$$

(Kc has no units here, because the units cancel.)

**2. At the same temperature, an equilibrium mixture contains [H₂] = [I₂] = 0.20 mol/dm³. Find [HI].**

$$[\text{HI}]^2 = 49 \times 0.20 \times 0.20 = 1.96 \quad\Rightarrow\quad [\text{HI}] = 1.4 \text{ mol/dm}^3$$

**3. Write Kc for the Haber process.**

$$K_c = \dfrac{[\text{NH}_3]^2}{[\text{N}_2][\text{H}_2]^3} \quad (\text{units: dm}^6\,\text{mol}^{-2})$$
:::

**Meaning of Kc:**

- **Large Kc** (much greater than 1): the equilibrium lies to the **right**, with mostly products.
- **Small Kc** (much less than 1): the equilibrium lies to the **left**, with mostly reactants.
- Kc is changed **only by temperature**. Changing concentration or pressure shifts the position but leaves Kc unchanged; a catalyst does not change it either.
- For an **exothermic** reaction, **raising** the temperature **decreases** Kc; for an endothermic reaction it increases Kc.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Nitrogen fertilisers bought by Sierra Leonean farmers, such as urea and ammonium nitrate, start as **ammonia made by the Haber process** in factories abroad. Its compromise conditions, balancing yield, rate and cost, are why fertiliser prices depend so much on the cost of energy and natural gas.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Raising the temperature makes an equilibrium reaction **reach equilibrium faster**, but for an **exothermic** reaction it gives a **lower yield**. Rate and yield are different questions: do not say "higher temperature gives more ammonia".
:::

## Key points

- **Le Chatelier:** an equilibrium shifts to **oppose** any change in concentration, pressure or temperature.
- Higher **pressure** favours the side with **fewer gas moles**; higher **temperature** favours the **endothermic** direction.
- A **catalyst** does **not** shift the equilibrium; it only speeds its attainment.
- **Haber:** 450 °C, 200 atm, iron catalyst, NH₃ removed. **Contact:** 450 °C, 1–2 atm, V₂O₅, excess air.
- $K_c = \dfrac{[\text{products}]^{\text{coefficients}}}{[\text{reactants}]^{\text{coefficients}}}$; it changes **only with temperature**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **state Le Chatelier's principle**, to **predict and explain** the effect of changing conditions on a given equilibrium (often the Haber or Contact process), to **explain the compromise conditions** used in industry, and to **write and calculate Kc**. Always give the reason (moles of gas, exothermic or endothermic).
:::

## Quick check

1. State Le Chatelier's principle.
2. For 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ (ΔH negative), what is the effect of raising the temperature on the yield of SO₃?
3. Why does a catalyst not change the yield of an equilibrium reaction?
4. Write the Kc expression for 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g).

**Answers:**

1. If a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change in conditions, the equilibrium shifts to oppose the change.
2. The yield decreases, because the forward reaction is exothermic and higher temperature favours the endothermic backward reaction.
3. It speeds up the forward and backward reactions equally, so the position of equilibrium is unchanged.
4. $K_c = \dfrac{[\text{SO}_3]^2}{[\text{SO}_2]^2[\text{O}_2]}$.
