*"You **must** wear a uniform."* *"You **should** wear a uniform."* *"You **may** wear a uniform."* One small word changes a rule into advice, and advice into permission. Modal verbs express **ability, permission, possibility, obligation, advice and certainty**, and choosing the right one is tested often in the WASSCE objective paper. This lesson explains each modal by its meaning, how to use them in the past, and the distinctions that examiners test most.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- list the modal verbs and state their grammar rules
- choose the right modal for ability, permission, possibility, obligation, advice and deduction
- distinguish *must / have to / mustn't / needn't* and *may / might / can / could*
- use modals to talk about the past (*should have*, *must have*, *could have*)

## What makes a verb modal

:::callout{type=definition}
The **modal verbs** are: *can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would, ought to*. (*Need, dare* and *have to* behave like modals in some uses.)

Their grammar is special:

| Rule | Correct | Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| followed by the **bare infinitive** (no *to*) | *She **can swim**.* | *She can **to** swim.* |
| **no -s** with *he/she/it* | *He **must** go.* | *He **musts** go.* |
| questions by **inversion** | ***Can** you help?* | *Do you can help?* |
| negative with **not** | *You **should not** (shouldn't) go.* | *You don't should go.* |

*Ought to* is the exception: it keeps *to* (*You **ought to** apologise*).
:::

## Modals by meaning

### Ability

| Modal | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **can** | present ability | *She **can** speak three languages.* |
| **could** | past ability (general) | *When I was ten, I **could** climb any tree.* |
| **was able to** | past ability on **one occasion** | *The road was flooded, but we **were able to** cross.* |

### Permission

| Modal | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **can** | informal | ***Can** I use your phone?* |
| **could** | polite | ***Could** I leave early today?* |
| **may** | formal, most polite | ***May** I come in, sir?* |

:::callout{type=tip}
In formal writing and when addressing someone senior, use **may** for permission: *"**May** I see you after the lesson, sir?"* Examiners sometimes test *can* (ability) against *may* (permission).
:::

### Possibility

| Modal | How sure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **may** | fairly possible | *It **may** rain later.* |
| **might** | less certain | *He **might** come, but I doubt it.* |
| **could** | possible | *That **could** be the answer.* |

### Obligation and necessity

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **must** | strong obligation, often from the speaker | *I **must** finish this tonight.* |
| **have to** | obligation from outside (rules, circumstances) | *Students **have to** wear uniforms.* |
| **mustn't** | it is **forbidden** | *You **mustn't** smoke here.* |
| **don't have to / needn't** | it is **not necessary** | *You **don't have to** come; it's optional.* |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Mustn't ≠ don't have to.** *"You **mustn't** pay"* means paying is **forbidden**. *"You **don't have to** pay"* means paying is **not necessary** (you may if you want). They are very different, and this difference is a favourite test question.
:::

### Advice

| Modal | Example |
|---|---|
| **should** | *You **should** revise every day.* |
| **ought to** | *You **ought to** see a doctor.* |
| **had better** (strong, with a warning) | *You **had better** leave now, or you'll miss the bus.* |

### Deduction: how certain you are

| Modal | Certainty | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **must** | sure it is true | *The lights are off. They **must** be asleep.* |
| **can't** | sure it is **not** true | *That **can't** be Musa; he's in Bo today.* |
| **may / might / could** | possible | *She **might** be at the market.* |

## Modals in the past: modal + have + past participle

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **should have** done | it was the right thing, but you didn't | *You **should have** studied harder.* (you didn't) |
| **could have** done | it was possible, but it didn't happen | *We **could have** won.* (we didn't) |
| **must have** done | I'm sure it happened | *The ground is wet. It **must have** rained.* |
| **can't have** done | I'm sure it didn't happen | *She **can't have** seen us; she was asleep.* |
| **might have** done | perhaps it happened | *He **might have** missed the bus.* |
| **needn't have** done | it happened but wasn't necessary | *You **needn't have** brought food; we had plenty.* |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
*"The road is wet and the gutters are full. It ____ rained last night."*

A. should have B. must have C. needn't have D. ought to have

The speaker is **sure** from the evidence: **B. must have.**
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Some modal patterns in everyday Sierra Leonean English differ from the standard:

| Often heard | Standard English |
|---|---|
| *I can be able to do it.* | *I **can** do it.* / *I **will be able to** do it.* |
| *You suppose to go.* | *You **are supposed to** go.* / *You **should** go.* |
| *He would come yesterday.* | *He **was supposed to** come yesterday.* / *He **should have** come yesterday.* |
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. 'Visitors ____ smoke inside the hospital.' A. needn't B. mustn't C. don't have to D. couldn't"}
**B. mustn't**: smoking is forbidden.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. 'You ____ bring a pen; we have plenty.' A. mustn't B. needn't C. can't D. shouldn't"}
**B. needn't**: it is not necessary.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. 'That ____ be Fatu's car; she sold it last month.' A. must B. can't C. may D. should"}
**B. can't**: the speaker is sure it is **not** hers.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Correct: 'He can to speak French.'"}
*He **can speak** French.* (No *to* after a modal.)
:::

:::reveal{title="5. 'I failed the test. I ____ more.' A. should study B. should have studied C. must study D. could study"}
**B. should have studied**: advice about the past that wasn't followed.
:::

:::reveal{title="6. '____ I borrow your dictionary, sir?' (most polite) A. Can B. May C. Will D. Must"}
**B. May.**
:::

## Key points

- Modals take the **bare infinitive**, have **no -s**, and form questions by **inversion**.
- **can/could** (ability), **may/can/could** (permission), **may/might/could** (possibility).
- **must/have to** (obligation); **mustn't** = forbidden; **don't have to / needn't** = not necessary.
- **should / ought to / had better** (advice); **must / can't** (sure deductions).
- Past: **should have, could have, must have, can't have, might have, needn't have** + past participle.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Structure questions in the objective paper regularly test modal meaning: **mustn't vs don't have to**, **must have vs should have**, **can vs may**, and deduction (*must / can't*). Read the whole sentence: the context always shows whether the meaning is obligation, advice, possibility or certainty.
:::
