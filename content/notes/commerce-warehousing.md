# Warehousing: functions, types and stock management

## Learning objectives
Define warehousing, explain time utility, identify common warehouse types and describe basic stock-control principles.

## Meaning
Warehousing is the organised storage of goods until they are required for sale, production or use. A warehouse is a facility used to receive, protect, record and dispatch stock. Warehousing creates time utility: it makes goods available at a later time than when they were produced or received.

Agricultural products may be harvested seasonally while consumers need food throughout the year. Imported goods may arrive in large shipments but be sold gradually. Storage helps match the timing of supply with demand.

## Functions
Warehouses receive and inspect goods, store them safely, protect them against theft and deterioration, maintain stock records, assemble orders and prepare goods for dispatch. They may also perform grading, packaging or labelling. Good storage requires suitable layout, ventilation, temperature and humidity control where needed, fire protection, pest control, security and safe handling. Perishable goods need appropriate cold-chain arrangements.

## Types of warehouse
- **Private warehouse:** owned or leased by a business mainly for its own goods.
- **Public warehouse:** provides storage services to customers for a fee.
- **Bonded warehouse:** stores imported goods under customs control before applicable duties or taxes are paid or the goods are released under customs procedures.
- **Cold-storage warehouse:** maintains low temperatures for products such as fish, meat and some medicines.
- **Cooperative warehouse:** owned or used collectively by members, often to store and market produce.

## Stock management
Stock records help a business know what it has, where it is stored and when to reorder. A reorder point should reflect expected demand during supplier lead time plus an appropriate safety buffer. Too much stock ties up cash and increases storage and deterioration costs; too little stock can cause lost sales or production stoppages.

First-in, first-out (FIFO) is useful where older stock should be issued first. For goods with expiry dates, first-expiry, first-out (FEFO) prioritises the earliest expiry date. Accurate receiving records, physical counts and secure access reduce losses.

## WASSCE examination focus
A warehouse is the facility; warehousing is the activity. When asked for functions, include protection, storage, stock recording and orderly distribution.

## Quick check
1. Define warehousing.
2. Explain how warehousing creates time utility.
3. Distinguish a private warehouse from a public warehouse.
4. Why is FEFO suitable for goods with expiry dates?

**Answers:** 1. Organised storage of goods until they are needed. 2. It stores goods produced or received earlier so they can be supplied when demand occurs. 3. A private warehouse is used by a business for its own goods; a public warehouse stores goods for customers for a fee. 4. It ensures goods with the nearest expiry are issued first, reducing expiry-related waste.
