Two of the most common questions in the WASSCE objective paper look simple: *"Choose the option **nearest in meaning** to the underlined word"* and *"Choose the option **opposite in meaning** to the underlined word."* They are harder than they look, because the right answer depends on the word's meaning **in that sentence**, and because the wrong options are chosen to tempt you. This lesson teaches you how synonyms and antonyms really work, a method for these questions, and a bank of practice.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain what synonyms and antonyms are, and why few words are exact synonyms
- choose the meaning that fits the context of the sentence
- recognise and use the main kinds of antonym
- build antonyms with prefixes
- answer nearest-in-meaning and opposite-in-meaning questions with a reliable method

## Synonyms: close, not identical

:::callout{type=definition}
**Synonyms** are words with the same or nearly the same meaning: *big / large*, *begin / start*, *brave / courageous*.

**Antonyms** are words with opposite meanings: *big / small*, *begin / end*, *brave / cowardly*.
:::

Very few words are **exact** synonyms. Most differ in one of three ways:

| Difference | Example |
|---|---|
| **Strength** | *annoyed* → *angry* → *furious*; *like* → *love* → *adore* |
| **Formality** | *kids* (informal), *children* (neutral), *offspring* (formal) |
| **Feeling** (connotation) | *slim* (positive), *thin* (neutral), *skinny* (negative) |

:::callout{type=tip}
When two options both look right, choose the one that matches the original word's **strength**, **formality** and **feeling**. *"The crowd was **jubilant**"*: *happy* is too weak; *overjoyed* or *elated* is right.
:::

## Meaning in context

Many words have several meanings. The question always wants the meaning **in the sentence given**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
| Sentence | Meaning in context | Nearest in meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *The judge was **fair** to both sides.* | just, not favouring anyone | **impartial** |
| *We visited the trade **fair** in Freetown.* | an exhibition | **exhibition** |
| *She has **fair** skin.* | light in colour | **light** |
| *The chances of rain are **fair**.* | moderate, reasonable | **reasonable** |

The same word, four different synonyms. Read the **whole sentence** before you look at the options.
:::

## Kinds of antonym

| Kind | How it works | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Complementary** | one or the other, nothing in between | *alive / dead*, *pass / fail*, *true / false* |
| **Gradable** | two ends of a scale, with degrees between | *hot / cold* (warm, cool between), *rich / poor*, *old / young* |
| **Relational** | two sides of one relationship | *buy / sell*, *teacher / student*, *lend / borrow*, *give / receive* |

### Antonyms made with prefixes

| Prefix | Examples |
|---|---|
| **un-** | *happy → unhappy, kind → unkind, usual → unusual* |
| **in- / im- / il- / ir-** | *correct → incorrect, possible → impossible, legal → illegal, regular → irregular* |
| **dis-** | *agree → disagree, honest → dishonest, appear → disappear* |
| **mis-** | *understand → misunderstand, behave → misbehave* |
| **non-** | *violent → non-violent, existent → non-existent* |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Choosing a word that is merely different, not opposite.** The opposite of *generous* is *mean* or *stingy*, not *poor*: a poor person can be generous. The opposite of *ancient* is *modern*, not *young*. Check that your choice is the **other end** of the same idea.
:::

## A method for the objective questions

1. **Read the whole sentence** and decide what the underlined word means **here**.
2. **Put your own word** in its place before looking at the options.
3. **Test each option** in the sentence: does it keep (or, for antonyms, reverse) the meaning?
4. **Check the grammar**: the same word class and form (a verb for a verb, past tense for past tense).
5. **Eliminate the traps**: words that look or sound like the original, or that are related but not equivalent.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
*"The principal's speech was so **verbose** that many students fell asleep."*

**Nearest in meaning:** A. loud B. wordy C. angry D. brief

1. The students fell asleep, so the speech was long and full of words.
2. My own word: *long-winded*.
3. Test: *so **wordy** that many students fell asleep* — it works.
4. **Answer: B. wordy.** (*Brief* is the **opposite**; *loud* and *angry* don't explain falling asleep.)

**Opposite in meaning:** A. concise B. clear C. polite D. boring → **A. concise.**
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Test-setters often use a sentence about everyday West African life, so the context helps you: *"The farmer was **despondent** after the floods destroyed his rice."* Whatever *despondent* means, a farmer who has lost his crop is unhappy, so look for *hopeless*, *dejected* or *downcast*, and for the opposite, *hopeful* or *cheerful*.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Nearest in meaning: 'The new road will ALLEVIATE the suffering of the farmers.' A. increase B. ease C. end D. cause"}
**B. ease.** *Alleviate* means to make less severe. *End* is too strong (the suffering is reduced, not finished); *increase* and *cause* are opposite in direction.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Opposite in meaning: 'The witness gave a CANDID account of the accident.' A. honest B. detailed C. evasive D. brief"}
**C. evasive.** *Candid* means frank and open; its opposite is avoiding the truth.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Nearest in meaning: 'Fuel became SCARCE during the strike.' A. expensive B. hidden C. rare D. useless"}
**C. rare** (in short supply). It may also have become *expensive*, but that is a result, not the meaning of *scarce*.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Give the antonym with a prefix: legal, patient, obey, regular, understand"}
*illegal, impatient, disobey, irregular, misunderstand.*
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Opposite in meaning: 'The trader was GENEROUS to the beggars.' A. poor B. mean C. rude D. busy"}
**B. mean** (stingy). *Poor* is a trap: a poor person can still be generous.
:::

## Key points

- **Synonyms** are close in meaning but may differ in **strength, formality and feeling**.
- The right answer is the meaning **in context**: read the whole sentence first.
- Antonyms may be **complementary**, **gradable** or **relational**, and many are formed with **prefixes**.
- Method: **understand the sentence, supply your own word, test each option, check the grammar, eliminate traps**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
The objective paper contains sets of **nearest in meaning** and **opposite in meaning** questions on words and phrases in sentences. The traps are options that are related but not equivalent, too strong or too weak, or opposite when you need similar (and the reverse). Reading the whole sentence and testing each option in it is the surest method.
:::
