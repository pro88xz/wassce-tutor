When head office sends goods to a branch **at selling price (invoice price)** instead of at cost, it keeps a **branch stock account** at selling price and a **branch adjustment (mark-up) account** to hold the profit element, called the **loading**. This method gives tight **control**: because every item at the branch is recorded at the price it should be sold for, any **shortage** of goods or cash shows up immediately. This lesson explains the method, calculates the loading from a mark-up, and finds the branch gross profit.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain why goods may be sent to a branch at selling price
- calculate cost and loading from a mark-up
- write up the branch stock account at selling price
- write up the branch adjustment (mark-up) account and find gross profit
- detect a shortage of inventory at the branch

## Mark-up and loading

If goods are invoiced at **cost plus a mark-up**, the **loading** is the mark-up part of the selling price:

$$\text{Loading} = \text{Selling price} \times \frac{\text{mark-up}}{100 + \text{mark-up}}$$

With a mark-up of **25% on cost**: cost = 100, selling price = 125, so loading $= \frac{25}{125} = \frac{1}{5}$ of selling price.

## The two accounts

| Account | Kept at | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Branch stock account** | **selling price** | controls goods: what came in, what went out (sales), what should remain |
| **Branch adjustment (mark-up) account** | the **loading** only | removes the unearned profit in inventory and shows the **gross profit** earned |

## Worked example

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Goods are sent to the Kenema branch at selling price, which is cost plus 25%. For 2026 (NLe):**

- Branch inventory 1 January at selling price 10,000 (cost 8,000)
- Goods sent to branch at selling price 75,000 (cost 60,000)
- Branch sales (all cash, remitted) 70,000
- Branch inventory 31 December at selling price 15,000 (cost 12,000)
:::

**Branch stock account (at selling price)**

| Debit | NLe |
|---|---|
| Inventory 1 January | 10,000 |
| Goods sent to branch | 75,000 |
| **Total** | **85,000** |

| Credit | NLe |
|---|---|
| Sales (cash remitted) | 70,000 |
| Inventory 31 December c/d | 15,000 |
| **Total** | **85,000** |

The account **balances exactly**, so there is **no shortage**: every item sent was either sold or is still in stock.

**Branch adjustment (mark-up) account**

| Debit | NLe |
|---|---|
| Loading on closing inventory c/d (15,000 − 12,000) | 3,000 |
| **Gross profit to branch income statement** | **14,000** |
| **Total** | **17,000** |

| Credit | NLe |
|---|---|
| Loading on opening inventory (10,000 − 8,000) | 2,000 |
| Loading on goods sent (75,000 − 60,000) | 15,000 |
| **Total** | **17,000** |

**Check at cost:** sales 70,000 − cost of sales $(8\,000 + 60\,000 - 12\,000 = 56\,000)$ = gross profit **14,000** ✓

## Detecting shortages

If the branch stock account does **not** balance, the difference is a **shortage at selling price**: goods have been lost, stolen or sold without the cash being sent.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Suppose closing inventory at the Kenema branch were only NLe 13,000 at selling price. Find the shortage.**

Expected closing inventory $= 85\,000 - 70\,000 = 15\,000$. Shortage at selling price $= 15\,000 - 13\,000 = 2\,000$.

At cost, the loss is $2\,000 \times \frac{100}{125} = 1\,600$, charged as a loss in the branch income statement; the loading of 400 is removed in the branch adjustment account.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The loading is a fraction of the **selling price**, not of cost. With a 25% mark-up on cost, the loading is $\frac{25}{125} = 20\%$ of selling price. Taking 25% of the selling price overstates the loading.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Branch stock account**: an account kept at selling price, which shows any shortage of goods at the branch.
- **Branch adjustment account** (mark-up account): the account that records only the loading on goods; its balance is the branch gross profit.
- **Loading**: the mark-up added to cost when goods are sent to a branch at selling price.
:::

## Key points

- Goods at **selling price** give strong **control** over branch inventory and cash.
- **Loading = selling price × mark-up ÷ (100 + mark-up).**
- **Branch stock account** (at selling price) reveals **shortages**.
- **Branch adjustment account**: loading on goods sent and opening inventory, less loading on closing inventory, = **gross profit**.

## Quick check

1. Why are goods sometimes sent to branches at selling price?
2. Goods costing NLe 40,000 are sent at cost plus 50%. Find the selling price and the loading.
3. What does a difference on the branch stock account mean?
4. With a 25% mark-up, what fraction of selling price is the loading?
5. Opening loading 3,000, loading on goods sent 20,000, closing loading 4,000. Find gross profit.

**Answers:**

1. To control branch inventory and cash: shortages show up at once.
2. Selling price $= 60\,000$; loading $= 20\,000$.
3. A shortage (or surplus) of goods or cash at the branch, valued at selling price.
4. $\frac{25}{125} = \frac{1}{5}$.
5. $3\,000 + 20\,000 - 4\,000 = 19\,000$: NLe 19,000.
