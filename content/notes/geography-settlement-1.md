:::callout{type=definition}
A **settlement** is any place where people live, from a single farmhouse to a great city.
:::

Settlements are classified as **rural** (villages and hamlets, where most people farm, fish or mine) or **urban** (towns and cities, where most people work in industry, trade and services). By the arrangement of their buildings, settlements form three main **patterns**: **nucleated** (clustered), **dispersed** (scattered) and **linear** (in a line). This lesson explains the types and patterns of settlement and the factors that decide where settlements grow.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define settlement and distinguish rural from urban settlement
- describe nucleated, dispersed and linear settlement patterns and their causes
- distinguish the site and the situation of a settlement
- explain the factors that influence the site of settlements
- describe the site of Freetown

## Rural and urban settlements

| Rural settlement | Urban settlement |
|---|---|
| small: hamlets and villages | large: towns, cities, conurbations |
| low population density | high population density |
| most people in **primary** activities: farming, fishing, mining, lumbering | most people in **secondary and tertiary** activities: industry, trade, services, administration |
| few social amenities | many amenities: hospitals, banks, universities |
| simple buildings, scattered or clustered | dense buildings, roads, utilities |
| close community ties | more diverse, impersonal society |

## Settlement patterns

![Three patterns: nucleated houses clustered around a central point, dispersed houses scattered over farmland, and linear houses built along a road](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/settlement-patterns.svg)

*Figure 1. Nucleated, dispersed and linear settlement patterns. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Pattern | Description | Reasons |
|---|---|---|
| **Nucleated (clustered)** | buildings grouped closely around a centre: a market, chief's compound, crossroads, well or church/mosque | defence; a limited water supply; communal farming; social ties; a central resource |
| **Dispersed (scattered)** | buildings spread out, each family on its own farm | plentiful water everywhere; large individual farms; security; flat, fertile land |
| **Linear (ribbon)** | buildings stretched in a line | along a **road**, **railway**, **river**, **coast** or **valley floor**, for access to transport and trade |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many villages in Sierra Leone are **nucleated**, clustered around the chief's compound, a court barray and a water point, with farms in the surrounding bush. **Linear** settlement is clear along the main highways, such as the roads between Freetown, Masiaka, Bo and Makeni, where houses, shops and markets line the road to catch passing trade.
:::

## Site and situation

:::callout{type=definition}
The **site** of a settlement is the **actual land on which it is built**: its relief, soil, water and drainage. The **situation** is its **position in relation to the surrounding area**: routes, rivers, resources and other settlements.
:::

### Factors influencing the site of settlements

1. **Water supply:** a reliable source of water from a river, spring or well (a **wet-point site**).
2. **Dry point:** raised land safe from flooding in swampy areas.
3. **Defence:** hilltops, river bends or islands that are easy to defend.
4. **Fertile soil** for farming.
5. **Relief:** flat or gently sloping land that is easy to build on.
6. **Route centre:** crossroads, river crossings (**bridging points**) and gaps between hills.
7. **Natural resources:** minerals, timber, fishing grounds.
8. **Shelter** from strong winds or storms.
9. **Building materials** nearby: stone, clay, timber.
10. **Government decisions:** new capitals or planned towns.

## The site of Freetown

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Freetown** was founded in **1792** on the north side of the **Freetown Peninsula**. Its site offered:

- a **deep, sheltered natural harbour** in the **Sierra Leone River estuary**, one of the largest natural harbours in Africa
- **fresh water** from streams flowing down from the mountains behind
- **higher ground** above the shore, healthier than the swampy coast
- a **defensible** position between the sea and the hills

Today the same site limits the city: squeezed between the sea and steep mountains, Freetown has spread along the coast and up the hillsides, causing congestion, floods and landslides.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not mix up **site** and **situation**. Site is the ground the settlement stands on; situation is where it is relative to other places. "Freetown has a deep harbour" describes its site; "Freetown is at the coastal end of the main highway to Bo and Kenema" describes its situation.
:::

## Key points

- **Rural** settlements: small, primary activities; **urban**: large, secondary and tertiary activities.
- Patterns: **nucleated** (clustered), **dispersed** (scattered), **linear** (along roads, rivers, coasts).
- **Site:** the land the settlement is built on. **Situation:** its position relative to surroundings.
- Site factors: **water, dry point, defence, fertile soil, flat land, route centres, resources, shelter, building materials**.
- **Freetown:** deep natural harbour, water from the hills, defensible, but cramped between sea and mountains.

## Quick check

1. State two differences between rural and urban settlements.
2. What is a nucleated settlement?
3. Why do linear settlements develop?
4. Distinguish site from situation.
5. Give two advantages of Freetown's site.

**Answers:**

1. Any two: size; population density; main occupations (primary vs secondary and tertiary); amenities; buildings.
2. A settlement whose buildings are clustered closely around a centre.
3. To be near a road, railway, river or coast for transport, trade or water.
4. Site is the actual land a settlement is built on; situation is its position in relation to the surrounding area.
5. Any two: a deep, sheltered natural harbour; fresh water from the hills; higher, healthier ground; a defensible position.
