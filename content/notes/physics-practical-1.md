The WASSCE Physics practical paper is worth a large share of your mark, and it rewards habits more than brilliance: careful readings, a neat table, a well-drawn graph and precise precautions. This lesson covers the habits that earn those marks.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- record readings correctly in a table with units and sensible precision
- choose scales and plot graphs accurately
- draw a line of best fit and find its gradient and intercept
- state specific precautions and sources of error

## Before you start

1. **Read the whole question** first: note what to measure, how many readings, and what graph to plot.
2. **Check the apparatus**: the zero of meters and scales, that the metre rule is not worn at the end, that connections are tight.
3. **Plan your readings**: spread them **evenly over the full range** (for example, five or six values of length from 20 cm to 90 cm).

## Recording readings

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Put **quantity and unit** in each column heading, separated by a slash | $l$ / cm, $t$ / s, $T^2$ / s² |
| Record readings to the **precision of the instrument** | metre rule: 45.0 cm (to 0.1 cm); stopwatch: 18.4 s |
| Keep the **same number of decimal places** in a column | 20.0, 30.0, 40.0 — not 20, 30.5, 40 |
| Record **raw readings** before calculated values | $t$ for 20 oscillations, then $T$, then $T^2$ |
| Give calculated values to **3 significant figures** (usually) | $T^2 = 1.85$ s² |
| **Repeat** readings and record the average | $t_1$, $t_2$, mean $t$ |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A well-recorded pendulum table:**

| $l$ / cm | $t_1$ / s (20 osc.) | $t_2$ / s | mean $t$ / s | $T$ / s | $T^2$ / s² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30.0 | 22.0 | 22.2 | 22.1 | 1.11 | 1.22 |
| 50.0 | 28.4 | 28.2 | 28.3 | 1.42 | 2.00 |
| 70.0 | 33.6 | 33.4 | 33.5 | 1.68 | 2.81 |
:::

## Plotting graphs

![Plotted points scattered around a straight line of best fit, with a large right-angled triangle drawn on the line to find the gradient as delta y over delta x](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/best-fit-graph.svg)

*Figure 1. A line of best fit and a large gradient triangle. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

1. Plot the quantity asked for on each axis: "plot $T^2$ against $l$" means $T^2$ on the **vertical (y)** axis, $l$ on the **horizontal (x)** axis.
2. **Label both axes** with quantity and unit.
3. Choose **sensible scales** (1, 2 or 5 units per large square — never 3 or 7) so the points fill **at least half** of the graph paper in each direction.
4. Start from the **origin** if you need the intercept; otherwise a "false origin" is allowed.
5. Plot points **precisely** with small, neat crosses or dots in circles.
6. Draw **one thin, straight line of best fit** with roughly equal numbers of points on each side. Do **not** join the dots, and do not force the line through the origin unless the theory says it must pass through it.

## Finding the gradient

1. Draw a **large right-angled triangle** on the line — at least half the length of the line.
2. Read off $\Delta y$ and $\Delta x$ from the triangle's sides, **with units**.
3. Gradient $= \dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$, given with its unit.

Use points **on the line**, not data points from the table (unless they lie exactly on it).

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A graph of $T^2$ (s²) against $l$ (m) is a straight line through the origin. A triangle drawn on it has Δ(T²) = 3.20 s² and Δl = 0.80 m. Find g.**

Gradient $= \dfrac{3.20}{0.80} = 4.00$ s² m⁻¹. Since $T^2 = \dfrac{4\pi^2}{g}l$, gradient $= \dfrac{4\pi^2}{g}$:

$$g = \dfrac{4\pi^2}{4.00} = 9.87\ \text{m s}^{-2}$$
:::

## Precautions and sources of error

Precautions must be **specific to the experiment**. Avoid vague statements such as "I was careful".

| Weak (no mark) | Strong (earns the mark) |
|---|---|
| "I took the readings carefully" | "I read the metre rule with my eye directly above the mark to avoid parallax" |
| "I avoided errors" | "I timed 20 oscillations instead of one to reduce the effect of reaction time" |
| "I checked the meters" | "I checked and corrected the zero error of the ammeter before starting" |
| "The connections were good" | "I switched off between readings to prevent the wire from heating up" |

**Common sources of error:** parallax, zero error, reaction time in timing, heat losses (in heat experiments), friction (in pulleys), heating of resistance wires, draughts (pendulums), and non-uniform apparatus.

## Key points

- Column headings: quantity / unit; consistent decimal places; repeat and average.
- y-axis is the first quantity named ("plot y against x"); sensible scales filling the paper.
- One thin line of best fit; large gradient triangle; gradient with units.
- Precautions must name the specific action and the error it prevents.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
In the practical paper, marks are given for **tabulation** (headings and units, precision), the **graph** (axes labelled, suitable scale, points correctly plotted, good line), the **gradient** (large triangle, correct reading), the **deduction** (using the gradient to find the quantity asked) and **two precautions**. Lose none of these routine marks.
:::

## Quick check

1. In "plot $V$ against $I$", which quantity goes on the vertical axis?
2. Why should the gradient triangle be large?
3. Give a precaution when timing a pendulum.

**Answers:** 1. $V$. 2. To reduce the percentage error in reading Δy and Δx. 3. For example: time 20 oscillations and find the average period, or start timing as the bob passes the lowest point.
