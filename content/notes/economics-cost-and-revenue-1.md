## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- distinguish fixed costs from variable costs
- calculate total, average and marginal cost from a table
- explain the U shape of the average cost curve

## Fixed and variable costs

A firm's costs of production are what it pays for the factors and materials it uses. In the short run they fall into two groups.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Fixed costs (FC)** do **not** change with the level of output. They must be paid even if nothing is produced. Examples: rent, insurance, a watchman's salary, repayments on a loan for machinery.

**Variable costs (VC)** **change** with the level of output. They are zero when nothing is produced. Examples: raw materials, fuel, electricity used by machines, wages of casual workers paid per day.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
For a baker in Makeni, the rent on the bakery is a **fixed cost**: it is the same whether she bakes 100 loaves or 1,000. Flour, sugar, yeast and the firewood for the oven are **variable costs**: the more she bakes, the more she spends on them.
:::

## The cost formulas

| Cost | Formula |
|---|---|
| Total cost | $TC = FC + VC$ |
| Average fixed cost | $AFC = \dfrac{FC}{Q}$ |
| Average variable cost | $AVC = \dfrac{VC}{Q}$ |
| Average (total) cost | $ATC = \dfrac{TC}{Q}$, or $AFC + AVC$ |
| Marginal cost | $MC = \Delta TC$ (the extra cost of one more unit) |

Here $Q$ is the quantity of output.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
A small factory has fixed costs of **NLe 100** a day. Its variable costs at each level of output are given; the rest is calculated.

**Total costs**

| Output ($Q$) | FC | VC | TC |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 100 | 0 | 100 |
| 1 | 100 | 40 | 140 |
| 2 | 100 | 70 | 170 |
| 3 | 100 | 90 | 190 |
| 4 | 100 | 120 | 220 |
| 5 | 100 | 170 | 270 |
| 6 | 100 | 240 | 340 |

**Average and marginal costs**

| Output ($Q$) | AFC | AVC | ATC | MC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100 | 40 | 140 | 40 |
| 2 | 50 | 35 | 85 | 30 |
| 3 | 33.3 | 30 | 63.3 | 20 |
| 4 | 25 | 30 | 55 | 30 |
| 5 | 20 | 34 | 54 | 50 |
| 6 | 16.7 | 40 | 56.7 | 70 |

Sample calculations:

- TC at $Q = 3$: $100 + 90 = 190$
- ATC at $Q = 4$: $\dfrac{220}{4} = 55$
- MC of the 5th unit: $270 - 220 = 50$
:::

## What the table shows

1. **AFC falls continuously** as output rises: the same NLe 100 is spread over more units.
2. **MC first falls** (40, 30, 20) and then **rises** (30, 50, 70), because of diminishing returns: as more variable factors are added to the fixed factory, each extra unit eventually costs more to make.
3. **ATC falls** while MC is below it (ATC drops from 140 to 54), and **rises** once MC is above it (the 6th unit's MC of 70 pulls ATC up to 56.7).

:::callout{type=definition}
The **average cost curve is U-shaped** in the short run. It falls at first because fixed costs are spread over more output and because of increasing returns, then rises because of diminishing returns. The **marginal cost curve cuts the average cost curve at its lowest point**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Marginal cost is the change in **total** cost, not the change in average cost. Always subtract one TC figure from the next: $MC_5 = TC_5 - TC_4$.
:::

## Short run and long run

- In the **short run**, at least one factor (and so some cost) is fixed.
- In the **long run**, all factors can be changed, so **all costs are variable**. A firm can build a bigger factory or move to a new site.

## Key points

- **Fixed costs** do not vary with output; **variable costs** do. $TC = FC + VC$.
- $ATC = \dfrac{TC}{Q}$; MC is the extra total cost of one more unit.
- AFC always falls; MC and ATC are U-shaped; **MC cuts ATC at its minimum**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
You will often get a cost table to **complete** and questions such as "at what output is average cost lowest?" or "explain why the average cost curve is U-shaped". Show sample calculations, keep columns neat, and use the words *fixed costs spread over more output* and *diminishing returns* in your explanation.
:::

## Quick check

1. Is the rent on a shop a fixed or a variable cost?
2. If TC is NLe 300 at 6 units and NLe 360 at 7 units, what is the marginal cost of the 7th unit?
3. In the table above, at what output is ATC lowest?

**Answers:** 1. A fixed cost. 2. $360 - 300 = $ NLe 60. 3. At 5 units, where ATC is NLe 54.
