# Advanced scope of commerce: distribution channels and commercial efficiency

## Learning objectives
Explain distribution channels, analyse factors that influence channel choice, and assess how commercial services improve market efficiency.

## Distribution channels
A distribution channel is the route through which a product passes from its producer to the final consumer. Common channels include:

- **Direct:** Producer → Consumer. A farmer sells vegetables directly at a roadside stall.
- **One intermediary:** Producer → Retailer → Consumer. A bakery supplies a supermarket.
- **Two intermediaries:** Producer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer. A manufacturer supplies shops through wholesalers.
- **Agent-assisted:** Producer → Agent → Wholesaler/Retailer → Consumer. An agent connects a producer to buyers without necessarily taking ownership of the goods.

The best channel depends on product perishability, market size, order size, customer location, cost, control and the availability of intermediaries. Fresh fish may need a short, rapid channel; durable packaged goods can pass through several distributors.

## Commercial efficiency
An efficient commercial system makes goods available in the required quantity, place, time and condition at a reasonable total cost. Efficiency is not simply the lowest transport charge. Delays, spoilage, damaged goods, stock-outs and unreliable payment can make a cheap channel expensive overall.

For example, a rice importer may use a clearing agent, port handling service, warehouse, wholesaler and retailers. Each stage adds a service and may add cost. Good coordination reduces unnecessary handling and delays.

## Factors that influence channel choice
1. **Nature of the product:** perishables need speed; fragile goods need careful handling.
2. **Market coverage:** widely dispersed customers may require wholesalers and regional distributors.
3. **Financial resources:** small producers may lack vehicles, storage or sales staff.
4. **Control:** direct selling gives the producer more control over price and customer contact.
5. **Intermediary competence:** reliable distributors provide reach, market knowledge and stock management.

## WASSCE examination focus
When comparing channels, draw each route with arrows and explain the circumstances in which it is suitable. Do not assume every product must pass through a wholesaler.

## Quick check
1. What is a distribution channel?
2. Give one advantage of direct distribution.
3. Why might a producer use a wholesaler?
4. State three factors affecting channel choice.

**Answers:** 1. The route goods follow from producer to final consumer. 2. Greater control and direct customer feedback, with fewer intermediary margins. 3. To reach many retailers, break bulk and reduce the producer's distribution burden. 4. Product type, market size, cost, location, control and available resources.
