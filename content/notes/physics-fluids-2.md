A steel ship weighing thousands of tonnes floats, yet a small steel nail sinks. A loaded canoe sits lower in the water than an empty one. Archimedes' principle and the law of flotation explain why.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state Archimedes' principle and use it to find upthrust and relative density
- state the principle (law) of flotation and explain why objects float or sink
- explain how a hydrometer works
- solve problems involving floating and submerged bodies

## Upthrust

When a body is placed in a fluid, the fluid pushes up on it. This upward force is the **upthrust**. It arises because the pressure on the bottom of the body (deeper) is greater than the pressure on its top.

The **apparent weight** of a body in a liquid is less than its true weight:

$$\text{upthrust} = \text{weight in air} - \text{apparent weight in liquid}$$

## Archimedes' principle

:::callout{type=definition}
**Archimedes' principle:** when a body is **wholly or partially immersed** in a fluid, it experiences an **upthrust equal to the weight of the fluid displaced**.
:::

![A stone on a spring balance reads 5 newtons in air and 3 newtons when submerged in a displacement can; the 2 newtons of water pushed out equals the upthrust](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/archimedes.svg)

*Figure 1. Verifying Archimedes' principle. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

**Verification:** weigh a stone in air on a spring balance ($W_1$). Lower it fully into a displacement (eureka) can filled with water to the spout, collecting the water that overflows. Read the balance again ($W_2$). Weigh the displaced water ($W_3$). The loss in weight $W_1 - W_2$ equals $W_3$.

## Finding relative density by Archimedes' principle

For a solid that sinks in water:

$$\text{RD} = \dfrac{\text{weight in air}}{\text{upthrust in water}} = \dfrac{W_1}{W_1 - W_2}$$

because the upthrust equals the weight of an equal volume of water.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A stone weighs 5.0 N in air and 3.0 N when fully immersed in water. Find (a) the upthrust, (b) its relative density, (c) its apparent weight in a liquid of relative density 0.8.**

(a) Upthrust $= 5.0 - 3.0 = 2.0$ N.

(b) $\text{RD} = \dfrac{5.0}{2.0} = 2.5$.

(c) The upthrust in the liquid $= 0.8 \times 2.0 = 1.6$ N (it is 0.8 times as dense as water, and the same volume is displaced). Apparent weight $= 5.0 - 1.6 = 3.4$ N.
:::

For a **liquid**, weigh a sinker in air ($W_1$), in water ($W_2$) and in the liquid ($W_3$):

$$\text{RD of liquid} = \dfrac{\text{upthrust in liquid}}{\text{upthrust in water}} = \dfrac{W_1 - W_3}{W_1 - W_2}$$

## Flotation

:::callout{type=definition}
**Principle (law) of flotation:** a floating body **displaces its own weight** of the fluid in which it floats.
:::

A body **floats** if its **average density is less** than the fluid's density, and **sinks** if it is greater. A steel ship floats because its hollow shape gives it a large volume, making its **average** density (steel plus the air inside) less than that of water; it sinks into the water until the water it displaces weighs as much as the whole ship.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A block of wood of density 600 kg m⁻³ floats in water (1000 kg m⁻³). What fraction of its volume is under water?**

Weight of block = weight of water displaced:

$$600 \times V \times g = 1000 \times V_{sub} \times g \Rightarrow \dfrac{V_{sub}}{V} = \dfrac{600}{1000} = 0.6$$

Three-fifths of the block is submerged.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
A fishing canoe at Tombo sits deeper in the water when it returns full of fish: the extra weight must be balanced by displacing more water. Ships float slightly **higher in salt water** than in fresh river water, because sea water is denser, so less of it needs to be displaced. Ships carry **Plimsoll lines** marking safe loading depths for different waters.
:::

### Submarines, balloons and fish

- A **submarine** sinks by letting water into its ballast tanks (increasing its average density) and rises by blowing the water out with compressed air.
- A **hot-air balloon** rises because hot air is less dense than the cooler air around it; the upthrust from the displaced air exceeds the balloon's weight.
- **Fish** adjust their depth with a gas-filled swim bladder.

## The hydrometer

A **hydrometer** measures the relative density of liquids directly. It is a weighted glass float with a long, narrow, graduated stem. It floats **higher** in **denser** liquids (it needs to displace less liquid to balance its weight), so the scale reads **higher values lower down** the stem.

Uses: testing the acid in car batteries (state of charge), the density of milk, and the strength of alcoholic drinks.

## Key points

- Upthrust = weight in air − apparent weight.
- Archimedes: upthrust = weight of fluid displaced.
- RD of a solid $= \dfrac{W_1}{W_1 - W_2}$; RD of a liquid $= \dfrac{W_1 - W_3}{W_1 - W_2}$.
- Flotation: a floating body displaces its own weight of fluid.
- A body floats if its average density is less than the fluid's.

## Quick check

1. A body weighs 12 N in air and 8 N in water. Find its relative density.
2. State the principle of flotation.
3. Why does a ship float higher in sea water than in river water?

**Answers:** 1. Upthrust = 4 N, so RD $= 12 \div 4 = 3$. 2. A floating body displaces its own weight of the fluid in which it floats. 3. Sea water is denser, so a smaller volume of it has to be displaced to equal the ship's weight.
