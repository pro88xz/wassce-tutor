A violin and a flute playing the same note still sound different. A whisper and a shout of the same word differ in loudness. Pitch, loudness and quality describe every sound you hear, and each is tied to one property of the wave.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- relate pitch, loudness and quality of sound to frequency, amplitude and overtones
- state the range of human hearing and define ultrasound and infrasound
- describe applications of echoes and ultrasound
- describe the Doppler effect qualitatively and discuss noise pollution

## Characteristics of a musical note

| Characteristic | Depends on | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **Pitch** | **frequency** | high frequency → high pitch (a whistle); low frequency → low pitch (a big drum) |
| **Loudness** (intensity) | **amplitude** | larger amplitude → more energy → louder sound |
| **Quality** (timbre) | **overtones** present | different instruments add different higher frequencies (overtones) to the fundamental |

:::callout{type=definition}
- The **fundamental frequency** is the lowest frequency at which a body vibrates.
- **Overtones** are higher frequencies produced together with the fundamental. When they are whole-number multiples of the fundamental they are called **harmonics**.
:::

A **tuning fork** produces an almost pure note (fundamental only); a guitar and a flute playing the same note produce different overtones, so their **waveforms** differ in shape even though their frequency (pitch) is the same.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Loudness is not pitch.** Turning up the volume of a radio increases the amplitude (loudness) but not the frequency (pitch). A soft high note and a loud low note are both possible.
:::

## The range of hearing

- Humans hear frequencies from about **20 Hz to 20 000 Hz** (20 kHz). The upper limit falls with age.
- **Infrasound**: below 20 Hz (elephants and whales communicate with it; earthquakes produce it).
- **Ultrasound**: above 20 kHz (bats and dolphins use it; dogs can hear up to about 45 kHz).

## Applications of echoes and ultrasound

1. **Echo-sounding (sonar)**: a ship sends a pulse of sound down and times the echo from the sea bed to find the depth: $d = \dfrac{vt}{2}$. Fishing boats use it to find shoals of fish.
2. **Medical ultrasound scanning**: echoes from tissue boundaries build up an image of a baby in the womb — safer than X-rays.
3. **Detecting flaws** in metal castings and welds.
4. **Cleaning** delicate jewellery and instruments in an ultrasonic bath.
5. **Bats and dolphins** navigate by echolocation.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A ship sends an ultrasonic pulse to the sea bed and receives the echo 0.8 s later. If sound travels at 1500 m s⁻¹ in sea water, how deep is the sea?**

$$d = \dfrac{vt}{2} = \dfrac{1500 \times 0.8}{2} = 600\ \text{m}$$
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Antenatal clinics in district hospitals across Sierra Leone use ultrasound scanners to check a baby's position and heartbeat. The probe sends pulses of about 2–10 MHz into the body and times the echoes.
:::

### Reverberation

When sound is reflected many times inside a large hall, the echoes merge and the sound seems to linger: **reverberation**. Too much makes speech unclear. Halls and studios reduce it with **soft materials** — curtains, carpets, padded seats and acoustic tiles — that absorb sound.

## The Doppler effect

:::callout{type=definition}
The **Doppler effect** is the apparent **change in frequency (pitch)** of a wave when the **source and the observer move relative to each other**.
:::

- As an ambulance siren **approaches**, its pitch sounds **higher**; as it **moves away**, the pitch sounds **lower**.
- The waves are crowded together in front of a moving source (shorter wavelength, higher frequency) and spread out behind it.
- Uses: police radar speed guns, weather radar, measuring blood flow, and showing that distant galaxies are moving away (red shift).

## Noise pollution

**Noise** is unwanted or harmful sound. Long exposure to loud noise (over about 85 dB) can damage hearing permanently.

Sources include generators, loud music from speakers at shops and events, vehicle horns and factory machines.

Reducing it: ear protection for workers, silencers on generators and exhausts, double glazing, planting trees as sound barriers, and sensible local by-laws on loudspeakers.

## Key points

- Pitch ↔ frequency; loudness ↔ amplitude; quality ↔ overtones.
- Human hearing: 20 Hz to 20 kHz; above is ultrasound, below is infrasound.
- Echo-sounding and medical scans use $d = \dfrac{vt}{2}$.
- Doppler effect: pitch rises as a source approaches, falls as it recedes.

## Quick check

1. Which property of a sound wave determines its pitch?
2. Why does a guitar note sound different from a flute note of the same pitch?
3. A depth sounder receives an echo after 0.4 s. If sound travels at 1500 m s⁻¹ in water, find the depth.

**Answers:** 1. Its frequency. 2. They produce different overtones (different quality/timbre). 3. $\dfrac{1500 \times 0.4}{2} = 300$ m.
