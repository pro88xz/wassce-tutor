A recipe needs $2\tfrac{1}{3}$ cups of rice for one family and $1\tfrac{3}{4}$ cups for another. A builder needs a length correct to the nearest centimetre. A news report says Sierra Leone's population is "about 8.9 million". Each of these uses the skills in this lesson: **operating with fractions**, **converting between fractions and decimals**, and **approximating** sensibly.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- add, subtract, multiply and divide fractions and mixed numbers
- apply the order of operations to fractions
- convert between fractions, terminating decimals and recurring decimals
- round to a given number of decimal places or significant figures
- write numbers in standard form and estimate answers

## Adding and subtracting fractions

Write each fraction with the **lowest common denominator** (LCM of the denominators), then add or subtract the numerators. Change mixed numbers to improper fractions first.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**(a) $2\tfrac{1}{3} + 1\tfrac{3}{4}$ (b) $3\tfrac{1}{2} - 1\tfrac{5}{6}$**

**(a)** $\dfrac{7}{3} + \dfrac{7}{4} = \dfrac{28}{12} + \dfrac{21}{12} = \dfrac{49}{12} = 4\tfrac{1}{12}$

**(b)** $\dfrac{7}{2} - \dfrac{11}{6} = \dfrac{21}{6} - \dfrac{11}{6} = \dfrac{10}{6} = \dfrac{5}{3} = 1\tfrac{2}{3}$
:::

## Multiplying and dividing fractions

- **Multiply:** numerator × numerator, denominator × denominator. Cancel first where you can.
- **Divide:** multiply by the **reciprocal** of the second fraction ("keep, change, flip").

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**(a) $2\tfrac{2}{5} \times 1\tfrac{1}{4}$ (b) $3\tfrac{3}{4} \div 1\tfrac{1}{2}$**

**(a)** $\dfrac{12}{5} \times \dfrac{5}{4} = \dfrac{12 \times 5}{5 \times 4} = \dfrac{60}{20} = 3$

**(b)** $\dfrac{15}{4} \div \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{15}{4} \times \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{30}{12} = \dfrac{5}{2} = 2\tfrac{1}{2}$
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
You cannot multiply mixed numbers by multiplying the whole numbers and the fractions separately: $2\tfrac{1}{2} \times 2\tfrac{1}{2}$ is **not** $4\tfrac{1}{4}$. Convert first: $\tfrac{5}{2} \times \tfrac{5}{2} = \tfrac{25}{4} = 6\tfrac{1}{4}$.
:::

## Order of operations (BODMAS)

Brackets, Of, Division and Multiplication (left to right), Addition and Subtraction (left to right). "Of" means multiply.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Simplify $\left(\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{3}\right) \div \tfrac{5}{6} - \tfrac{1}{4} \text{ of } 2$.**

Brackets: $\tfrac{3}{6} + \tfrac{2}{6} = \tfrac{5}{6}$. Of: $\tfrac{1}{4} \times 2 = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

Then $\tfrac{5}{6} \div \tfrac{5}{6} - \tfrac{1}{2} = 1 - \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{1}{2}$.
:::

## A word problem

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A farmer near Bo plants $\tfrac{2}{5}$ of her land with rice and $\tfrac{1}{3}$ with cassava. The remaining 12 hectares are groundnuts. How large is the farm?**

Fraction left: $1 - \tfrac{2}{5} - \tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{15}{15} - \tfrac{6}{15} - \tfrac{5}{15} = \tfrac{4}{15}$.

$\tfrac{4}{15}$ of the farm is 12 hectares, so the farm is $12 \div \tfrac{4}{15} = 12 \times \tfrac{15}{4} = 45$ hectares.
:::

## Fractions and decimals

**Fraction to decimal:** divide the numerator by the denominator.

- $\tfrac{3}{8} = 0.375$ (a **terminating** decimal)
- $\tfrac{2}{3} = 0.666\ldots = 0.\dot{6}$ (a **recurring** decimal)
- $\tfrac{5}{11} = 0.4545\ldots = 0.\dot{4}\dot{5}$

A fraction in its lowest terms gives a terminating decimal only when its denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5.

**Decimal to fraction:**

- Terminating: $0.125 = \tfrac{125}{1000} = \tfrac{1}{8}$.
- Recurring: put the repeating block over as many 9s as it has digits. $0.\dot{4}\dot{5} = \tfrac{45}{99} = \tfrac{5}{11}$, and $0.\dot{3} = \tfrac{3}{9} = \tfrac{1}{3}$.

## Approximation

### Decimal places

Count digits **after the decimal point**. Look at the next digit: if it is 5 or more, round up; otherwise leave the digit as it is.

![A number line from 3.4 to 3.5 showing 3.47 past the halfway mark 3.45, so it rounds to 3.5](https://wasscetutor.com/img/mathematics/rounding-number-line.svg)

*Figure 1. Rounding means choosing the nearer mark. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

### Significant figures

Count from the **first non-zero digit**. Zeros between non-zero digits count; leading zeros do not.

| Number | 3 s.f. | 2 s.f. |
|---|---|---|
| 2.0496 | 2.05 | 2.0 |
| 0.004 562 | 0.004 56 | 0.004 6 |
| 45 678 | 45 700 | 46 000 |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Rounding 45 678 to 2 significant figures gives **46 000**, not 46. The zeros keep the size of the number.
:::

### Standard form

A number in standard form is written $A \times 10^n$, where $1 \le A < 10$ and $n$ is an integer.

- $340\,000 = 3.4 \times 10^5$
- $0.000\,52 = 5.2 \times 10^{-4}$

### Estimation

Round each number to one significant figure to check whether an answer is sensible: $48.7 \times 0.51 \approx 50 \times 0.5 = 25$. (The exact answer is 24.837.)

## Key points

- **Add or subtract:** common denominator. **Multiply:** across. **Divide:** multiply by the reciprocal.
- Convert **mixed numbers to improper fractions** first; follow **BODMAS**.
- Fractions give **terminating** decimals only if the denominator's prime factors are 2 and 5; recurring blocks go over 9s.
- **Decimal places** count after the point; **significant figures** count from the first non-zero digit.
- **Standard form:** $A \times 10^n$ with $1 \le A < 10$.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect **mixed-number arithmetic with BODMAS**, **fraction word problems** (sharing land, money or time), **converting recurring decimals to fractions**, and **rounding to decimal places and significant figures**, often as the final step of a longer calculation. Read carefully whether the question asks for d.p. or s.f.
:::
