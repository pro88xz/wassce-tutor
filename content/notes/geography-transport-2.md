**Transport in Sierra Leone** relies mainly on **roads**, with the main highways linking Freetown to Bo, Kenema, Makeni and the borders; a heavy-haul **railway** carries iron ore from Tonkolili to **Pepel**; the **Queen Elizabeth II Quay** in Freetown handles most sea trade; and **Lungi International Airport** connects the country to the world. Across **West Africa**, coastal ports, trunk roads, a few railways, the River Niger and Lake Volta, and the West African Gas Pipeline link countries, but transport is still held back by **poor roads**, **few railways**, **border delays** and **high costs**. This lesson describes the transport networks of Sierra Leone and West Africa, their problems and solutions.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the road, rail, water and air transport of Sierra Leone
- describe the history of railways in Sierra Leone
- describe major transport routes and ports in West Africa
- explain the problems of transport in West Africa and Sierra Leone
- suggest solutions to transport problems

## Transport in Sierra Leone

### Roads

Roads carry most of Sierra Leone's passengers and goods. Major routes include:

- **Freetown – Waterloo – Masiaka**, the gateway from the capital to the provinces
- **Masiaka – Bo – Kenema**, to the south and east, continuing to Kailahun and the Liberian border
- **Masiaka – Makeni – Kabala**, to the north
- **Makeni – Koidu (Kono)**, to the diamond fields
- routes to the **Guinea** border and to the **Liberia** border at the Mano River bridge

Many rural **feeder roads** are untarred and become difficult to use in the rainy season.

### Railways

:::callout{type=local-example}
The **Sierra Leone Government Railway** was a narrow-gauge line built from **Freetown** from **1896**, reaching **Pendembu** in the east and **Kamakwie** in the north. It carried palm kernels, passengers and goods, but it ran at a loss and was **closed by 1974**, when road transport took over. Separate **mineral railways** were built to carry iron ore: from **Marampa** to **Pepel** (opened in the 1930s), and from **Tonkolili** to **Pepel**, completed in **2011** for heavy iron-ore trains.
:::

### Water transport

- The **Queen Elizabeth II Quay** at **Cline Town**, Freetown, is the main port for imports and exports; Freetown's natural harbour is one of the largest in Africa.
- **Pepel** exports iron ore; **Nitti** exports rutile.
- **Ferries** cross the Sierra Leone River estuary between Freetown (Kissy) and **Tagrin**, near Lungi, and **water taxis** link Freetown to Lungi.
- Boats serve coastal and river communities such as **Bonthe** and the **Banana Islands**.

### Air transport

**Lungi International Airport** is the country's international airport, on the north side of the estuary, reached from Freetown by ferry, water taxi or a long road journey around the estuary. A **new terminal** opened in **2023**. Small airstrips serve some provincial towns.

## Transport in West Africa

| Mode | Main features |
|---|---|
| **Roads** | the **Trans-West African Coastal Highway** (Lagos to Dakar and Nouakchott along the coast) and the **Trans-Sahara Highway** (Lagos to Algiers) are planned regional trunk roads, completed in parts |
| **Railways** | mostly built in colonial times to carry minerals and cash crops from the interior to the ports: Abidjan to Ouagadougou; Dakar to Bamako; Lagos to Kano; newer standard-gauge lines in Nigeria (Lagos to Ibadan, 2021) |
| **Seaports** | Lagos (Apapa, Tin Can, and the deep-sea port at **Lekki**, opened in **2023**), Tema, Takoradi, Abidjan, Dakar, Lomé, Cotonou, Freetown, Monrovia, Conakry |
| **Inland waterways** | River **Niger** and **Benue**, **Lake Volta**, River **Gambia**, River **Senegal**: navigable in parts and seasons |
| **Air** | international airports at Lagos, Abuja, Accra, Abidjan, Dakar and other capitals |
| **Pipelines** | oil pipelines in the Niger Delta; the **West African Gas Pipeline** (Nigeria to Ghana) |

## Problems of transport in West Africa

1. **Poor roads:** potholes, untarred feeder roads, damage by heavy rain.
2. **Few railways**, many old, with **different gauges** that cannot connect.
3. **Rivers** with rapids, waterfalls, sandbars and low water in the dry season.
4. **High cost** of building and maintaining routes.
5. **Road accidents** from bad roads, overloading, poorly maintained vehicles and reckless driving.
6. **Congestion** in cities and ports.
7. **Border delays**, checkpoints and illegal charges that slow trade.
8. **Relief and dense forest** in some areas.
9. **High fuel and transport costs.**
10. **Routes that run from interior to port**, built for exports, with few links between neighbouring countries.

## Solutions

1. **Build and maintain roads**, including feeder roads and regional highways.
2. **Rehabilitate and connect railways**, using a common gauge.
3. **Dredge rivers** and improve river ports.
4. **Modernise seaports** and airports.
5. **Enforce road safety**: vehicle checks, driver training, load limits.
6. **Remove border barriers** under ECOWAS rules on free movement of goods and people.
7. **Attract investment** through public–private partnerships.
8. **Improve urban public transport** to reduce congestion.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
West Africa's railways mostly run **inland to the coast**, not between countries. They were built to export minerals and cash crops, which is one reason trade between neighbouring West African countries is so small.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Trunk road**: a main road linking major towns, such as Freetown – Waterloo – Masiaka.
- **Feeder road**: a minor road linking villages and farms to main roads and markets.
- **West African Gas Pipeline**: the pipeline carrying natural gas from Nigeria to Ghana.
- **Inland waterway**: a navigable river or lake, such as the Niger, Benue or Lake Volta.
:::

## Key points

- Sierra Leone: **roads** carry most traffic; **Freetown–Masiaka–Bo–Kenema** and **Masiaka–Makeni–Kabala** are key routes.
- The government railway (from **1896**) **closed by 1974**; iron ore moves by rail from **Tonkolili** and **Marampa** to **Pepel**.
- Ports: **Queen Elizabeth II Quay** (Freetown), **Pepel**, **Nitti**; airport: **Lungi** (new terminal 2023).
- West Africa: coastal ports, colonial railways to the coast, the **Niger**, **Lake Volta**, the **West African Gas Pipeline**.
- Problems: **poor roads, few railways, river obstacles, cost, accidents, congestion, border delays**.

## Quick check

1. Name the main seaport of Sierra Leone.
2. When was the Sierra Leone Government Railway closed?
3. Which railway carries iron ore to Pepel?
4. Give three problems of road transport in West Africa.
5. Why is Lungi Airport difficult to reach from Freetown?

**Answers:**

1. The Queen Elizabeth II Quay, Freetown.
2. By 1974.
3. The railway from Tonkolili (and earlier from Marampa) to Pepel.
4. Any three: potholes and poor maintenance; untarred feeder roads; damage by rain; accidents; congestion; checkpoints and border delays; high costs.
5. It is across the Sierra Leone River estuary, so travellers must use a ferry, water taxi or a long road journey around the estuary.
