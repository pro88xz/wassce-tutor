# Choosing a form of business: liability, control and continuity

## Learning objectives
Compare forms of business ownership and apply liability, control, finance and continuity to business decisions.

## Key decision criteria
A person choosing a business structure should consider:
- **Ownership and control:** who makes decisions and appoints managers?
- **Liability:** can owners' personal assets be used to meet business debts?
- **Capital:** how much finance can the structure raise?
- **Continuity:** does the business continue if an owner dies or withdraws?
- **Profit distribution:** who receives the returns?
- **Regulation and reporting:** what registration, filing and governance duties apply?

## Comparison
| Form | Control | Liability and continuity |
|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | Owner controls operations | Owner commonly bears personal liability; continuity may depend on owner |
| Partnership | Partners share management under agreement | Liability depends on partnership type and law; disputes can affect continuity |
| Private company | Directors manage under company rules; shareholders own shares | Separate legal personality and limited liability generally apply, subject to law |
| Public company | Board and management operate under formal governance | Can raise capital from the public subject to regulatory requirements |
| Cooperative | Members participate under cooperative principles | Member control and liability depend on law and the society's rules |
| Public enterprise | Government ownership or control | Operates under its enabling law and public accountability arrangements |

## Applying the criteria
A small tailor with limited capital may begin as a sole proprietor because formation and control are straightforward. A group of professionals may prefer a partnership with a clear written agreement. A business seeking substantial investment and continuity may consider incorporation, accepting greater reporting and governance duties.

Limited liability does not mean owners can never be personally responsible. Personal guarantees, fraud, wrongful conduct and statutory obligations may create personal exposure.

## Partnership agreements
A sound agreement should address capital contributions, profit and loss sharing, duties, decision-making, drawings, admission or retirement of partners, dispute resolution and dissolution. Written terms reduce uncertainty.

## WASSCE examination focus
Do not state that every company is automatically large or that every partner has identical duties. Compare structures using the same criteria and qualify legal statements where the law may differ.
