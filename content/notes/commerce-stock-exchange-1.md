A large company wants to build a factory costing millions. A teacher in Kenema has NLe 5,000 of savings and wants it to grow. The stock exchange brings them together: the company sells shares, the teacher buys some, and later the teacher can sell them to someone else. This lesson explains the capital market and how a stock exchange works.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- distinguish the capital market from the money market
- distinguish the primary market from the secondary market
- define a stock exchange and explain its functions
- describe the members of the stock exchange and how shares are bought and sold
- describe stock exchanges in West Africa and their problems

## The capital market and the money market

| Capital market | Money market |
|---|---|
| Market for **long-term** funds (more than one year) | Market for **short-term** funds (up to one year) |
| Instruments: **shares, debentures, long-term government bonds**, mortgages | Instruments: **treasury bills**, bills of exchange, call money, certificates of deposit |
| Institutions: **stock exchange**, issuing houses, development banks, insurance companies, pension funds | Institutions: **central bank**, commercial banks, discount houses |
| Finances factories, buildings and infrastructure | Finances working capital and short-term government needs |

### Primary and secondary markets

- **Primary market**: where **new** securities are sold for the first time, so the company or government **receives the money**. Examples are a public issue of shares, an offer for sale and a rights issue.
- **Secondary market**: where **existing** securities are bought and sold between investors; the company receives nothing. The **stock exchange** is the main secondary market.

## The stock exchange

:::callout{type=definition}
A **stock exchange** is an organised market where **existing securities** (shares, debentures and government bonds) of **listed (quoted)** companies and the government are bought and sold through licensed **stockbrokers**, under strict rules.
:::

![How the stock market works: companies and government issue securities to investors in the primary market; investors then buy and sell existing securities through stockbrokers on the stock exchange in the secondary market](https://wasscetutor.com/img/commerce/stock-market.svg)

*Figure 1. The primary market raises new capital; the stock exchange lets investors trade existing securities. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

## Functions of the stock exchange

1. **A market for existing securities**: investors can sell shares at any time, making them **liquid**. People are more willing to buy shares they can easily sell.
2. **Helps companies and government raise capital**: because shares can be resold, people buy new issues more readily.
3. **Mobilises savings** for productive investment.
4. **Fixes the prices** of securities by demand and supply, and publishes them daily.
5. **Protects investors**: companies must meet **listing requirements** and publish accounts; dealing follows strict rules.
6. **An economic barometer**: share prices reflect confidence in companies and the economy.
7. **Encourages wider share ownership** and a savings culture.
8. **Attracts foreign investment**.
9. **Helps government** borrow through bonds and manage the economy.
10. **Facilitates mergers and takeovers** through the buying of shares.

## Members and procedure

| Member | Role |
|---|---|
| **Stockbroker** | An agent who buys and sells securities **on behalf of the public**, for a commission (brokerage) |
| **Dealer (jobber or market maker)** | Buys and sells securities **on their own account**, making a profit from the difference between buying and selling prices |
| **Issuing house** | Arranges new issues of shares for companies (primary market) |
| **Registrar** | Keeps the register of shareholders and issues certificates |
| **Central securities depository** | Holds shares electronically and records transfers |

**How an investor buys shares:**

1. The investor instructs a **stockbroker**, stating the company, the number of shares and the price limit.
2. The broker buys the shares on the exchange.
3. The investor receives a **contract note** showing the price, number of shares, brokerage and other charges.
4. The investor pays, and the shares are **registered** in their name: a **share certificate** is issued, or the shares are held electronically.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Contract note.** An investor buys 2,000 shares at NLe 1.50 each. Brokerage is 1.5% and other charges are NLe 25.

1. Cost of shares $= 2000 \times 1.50 = $ NLe 3,000
2. Brokerage $= 1.5\% \times 3000 = $ NLe 45
3. Total cost $= 3000 + 45 + 25 = $ **NLe 3,070**

If the shares are later sold at NLe 1.80 each, the proceeds before charges are $2000 \times 1.80 = $ NLe 3,600, a **capital gain** of NLe 600 on the share price.
:::

**Listing (quotation) requirements** usually include: being a public limited company, a minimum capital and number of shareholders, audited accounts for several years, and agreeing to publish information regularly.

## Stock exchanges in West Africa

| Exchange | Location |
|---|---|
| **Sierra Leone Stock Exchange** | Freetown; launched in **2009** |
| **Nigerian Exchange** | Lagos |
| **Ghana Stock Exchange** | Accra |
| **BRVM** (regional exchange for French-speaking West Africa) | Abidjan, Côte d'Ivoire |

:::callout{type=local-example}
The **Sierra Leone Stock Exchange** opened in Freetown in 2009, and **Rokel Commercial Bank** was the first company listed. Very few companies are listed, so most Sierra Leoneans who invest in securities buy **government treasury bills and bonds** through banks. More listings would give local firms a new source of long-term capital.
:::

**Problems of stock exchanges in West Africa:**

1. **Few listed companies**: most businesses are small or family-owned.
2. **Low public awareness** of shares.
3. **Low incomes and savings**.
4. **Illiquidity**: few buyers and sellers, so shares are hard to sell.
5. **Lack of confidence** after company failures or fraud.
6. **Inflation and exchange-rate instability**, which discourage long-term investment.
7. **Weak regulation and information** in some markets.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A company does **not** receive money when its existing shares are sold on the stock exchange; the money goes to the **selling shareholder**. The company receives money only from **new** issues in the **primary** market.
:::

## Key points

- **Capital market**: long-term funds (shares, debentures, bonds); **money market**: short-term funds (treasury bills, bills of exchange).
- **Primary market** sells new securities; the **stock exchange** is a **secondary market** for existing securities.
- Functions: **liquidity, raising capital, mobilising savings, pricing securities, protecting investors, economic barometer**.
- **Stockbrokers** act for the public; **dealers (jobbers)** trade on their own account.
