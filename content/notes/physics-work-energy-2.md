Not all energy goes where we want it. A generator turns only part of its fuel into electricity; a car loses energy to friction and air. This lesson deals with efficiency, energy losses, vehicle power, and the energy sources that power Sierra Leone.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- solve energy problems where friction or air resistance is present
- calculate efficiency and the power needed to keep a vehicle moving
- distinguish renewable and non-renewable energy sources
- discuss the advantages and disadvantages of different energy sources

## Energy problems with friction

When friction acts, some energy is converted into heat:

$$E_{\text{start}} = E_{\text{end}} + \text{work against friction}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A 40 kg child slides from rest down a slide 3 m high and reaches the bottom at 6 m s⁻¹. How much energy is lost to friction? ($g = 10$ m s⁻²)**

PE at the top $= 40 \times 10 \times 3 = 1200$ J.

KE at the bottom $= \tfrac{1}{2} \times 40 \times 6^2 = 720$ J.

Energy lost to friction $= 1200 - 720 = 480$ J (as heat in the slide and the child's clothes).
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A bullet of mass 20 g moving at 300 m s⁻¹ enters a tree trunk and stops after 15 cm. Find the average resistive force.**

KE lost $= \tfrac{1}{2}(0.02)(300)^2 = 900$ J. This equals the work done against the resistance:

$$F \times 0.15 = 900 \Rightarrow F = 6000\ \text{N}$$
:::

## Efficiency

:::callout{type=definition}
The **efficiency** of a device is the ratio of the **useful energy (or power) output** to the **total energy (or power) input**, usually given as a percentage:

$$\text{efficiency} = \dfrac{\text{useful output}}{\text{input}} \times 100\%$$
:::

No real machine is 100% efficient: some energy is always lost, mostly as heat.

| Device | Typical efficiency |
|---|---|
| Large electric motor | 90% |
| Hydroelectric turbine and generator | 85–90% |
| Diesel generator (gen-set) | 30–40% |
| Petrol car engine | 20–30% |
| Solar panel | 15–22% |
| Filament lamp (as a light source) | about 5% |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**An electric pump rated 2 kW lifts 1500 kg of water through 20 m in 3 minutes. Find its efficiency. ($g = 10$ m s⁻²)**

Useful work $= mgh = 1500 \times 10 \times 20 = 300\,000$ J.

Energy input $= Pt = 2000 \times 180 = 360\,000$ J.

$$\text{efficiency} = \dfrac{300\,000}{360\,000} \times 100\% = 83.3\%$$
:::

## Power of a vehicle

A vehicle at constant speed has zero resultant force: the engine's driving force equals the total resistance. Its power output is

$$P = Fv$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A minibus moves at a steady 20 m s⁻¹ against a total resistance of 1500 N. Find the useful power of the engine. If it then climbs a slope while keeping the same power, and the resistance plus weight component rise to 2500 N, what speed can it keep?**

$$P = 1500 \times 20 = 30\,000\ \text{W} = 30\ \text{kW}$$

On the slope: $v = \dfrac{P}{F} = \dfrac{30\,000}{2500} = 12$ m s⁻¹. This is why vehicles slow down on hills unless the driver gives more power.
:::

## Sources of energy

:::callout{type=definition}
**Renewable** energy sources are replaced naturally and will not run out. **Non-renewable** sources are used up faster than they are formed and will eventually run out.
:::

| Renewable | Non-renewable |
|---|---|
| Solar energy | Coal |
| Hydroelectric (falling water) | Crude oil (petrol, diesel, kerosene) |
| Wind | Natural gas |
| Biomass (firewood, crop waste, biogas) | Nuclear fuels (uranium) |
| Tides and waves | |
| Geothermal | |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's electricity comes mainly from **hydroelectric** stations (Bumbuna, Dodo, Goma), **thermal** generators that burn diesel and heavy fuel oil, power bought from neighbouring countries, and a growing number of **solar** installations, such as solar street lights and solar mini-grids in district towns. Most homes still cook with **charcoal and firewood**, a renewable source only if trees are replanted faster than they are cut.
:::

### Comparing sources

| Source | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Hydroelectric | no fuel cost; no smoke; reliable in the rainy season | expensive dams; flooding of land; output falls in the dry season |
| Solar | free and abundant in the tropics; no pollution; good for remote villages | no output at night; needs batteries; high initial cost |
| Diesel generators | can be placed anywhere; quick to start | fuel is costly; noise; smoke and CO₂ |
| Firewood and charcoal | cheap and available | deforestation; smoke harms health |
| Wind | free; no pollution | needs steady strong winds; noisy |

## Key points

- With friction: initial energy = final energy + work done against friction.
- Efficiency = useful output ÷ input × 100%; always less than 100%.
- Vehicle at steady speed: driving force = resistance, power $P = Fv$.
- Renewable: solar, hydro, wind, biomass, tidal, geothermal. Non-renewable: coal, oil, gas, nuclear.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect **efficiency calculations** (pumps, cranes, motors), **energy lost to friction**, **power = force × velocity** for vehicles, and questions asking you to **classify energy sources** and give **advantages and disadvantages** of solar or hydroelectric power. In energy calculations, write the energy at the start and at the end on separate lines before subtracting.
:::

## Quick check

1. Give two renewable sources of energy.
2. A motor takes in 500 J and does 400 J of useful work. Find its efficiency.
3. A car's engine supplies 40 kW at a steady 25 m s⁻¹. Find the resistance on the car.

**Answers:** 1. For example: solar and hydroelectric. 2. $\dfrac{400}{500} \times 100\% = 80\%$. 3. $F = \dfrac{40\,000}{25} = 1600$ N.
