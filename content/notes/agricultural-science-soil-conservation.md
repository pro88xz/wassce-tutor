After a heavy rainstorm, look at a bare upland farm on a slope: muddy water runs downhill in sheets and small channels, and at the bottom of the slope there is a fan of washed-down soil. That moving soil is the farm's most fertile **topsoil**, leaving for good. **Soil erosion** is one of the most serious threats to farming in Sierra Leone, where rainfall is heavy and much farming is on slopes. This lesson explains the agents, types, causes and effects of erosion, and the methods farmers use to conserve soil and its fertility.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define soil erosion and soil conservation
- describe the agents and types of erosion
- explain the causes and effects of soil erosion
- describe cultural, mechanical and biological methods of soil conservation
- explain why slopes must be farmed across the contour

## Erosion and conservation

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Soil erosion** is the **removal of topsoil** from one place to another by water, wind or other agents, faster than new soil can form.
- **Soil conservation** is the **protection of soil** against erosion and loss of fertility, so that it remains productive.
:::

## Agents of erosion

- **Water** (rain and run-off): the main agent in Sierra Leone, with annual rainfall of over 2500 mm in many areas and intense storms.
- **Wind**: important in dry areas and on bare, dry soil in the Harmattan season.
- **Human activities and animals**: digging, mining, overgrazing and trampling loosen soil and expose it.

## Types of water erosion

| Type | What happens |
|---|---|
| **splash erosion** | raindrops hit bare soil and **splash** particles out of place, breaking crumbs and sealing the surface |
| **sheet erosion** | a thin, even **layer** of topsoil is washed from a wide area; hard to notice until the soil is poor |
| **rill erosion** | water collects into many **small channels** (rills) a few centimetres deep; can be removed by ploughing |
| **gully erosion** | rills join into **deep, wide channels** (gullies) that cannot be crossed by machines and cut farmland apart |
| **stream bank erosion** | rivers cut into and wash away their banks |

:::callout{type=tip}
The types are **stages** of increasing damage: **splash → sheet → rill → gully**. Stopping erosion early, at the splash and sheet stages, is far easier than repairing gullies.
:::

## Causes of erosion

- **Deforestation**: clearing forest for farms, charcoal, timber, mining and building.
- **Bush burning**: removes cover and organic matter.
- **Overgrazing**: animals remove grass and trample the soil.
- **Ploughing and ridging up and down slopes**: channels water downhill.
- **Continuous cropping** and short fallows: soil loses organic matter and structure.
- **Mining and sand mining**: leave bare, loose spoil.
- **Steep slopes** and **heavy rainfall**.
- **Bare soil** left between crops.

## Effects of erosion

- Loss of the **fertile topsoil**, nutrients and organic matter: **lower yields**.
- Exposed **roots**, and plants washed away.
- **Gullies** destroy farmland, roads and buildings.
- **Silting** of rivers, swamps, dams and reservoirs; more **flooding** downstream.
- Muddy water harms fish and drinking water supplies.
- Farmers must use more fertiliser or abandon land.

## Methods of soil conservation

![Ridges made up and down a slope let water rush down carrying soil; contour ridges across the slope hold water so it soaks in; terraces on steep slopes hold soil](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/erosion-control.svg)

### Cultural (agronomic) methods

- **Cover crops**: low-growing crops such as **cowpea, groundnut, *Mucuna*, *Pueraria*** protect the soil from rain splash; legumes also add nitrogen.
- **Mulching**: dry grass, leaves or crop residues on the soil surface.
- **Crop rotation** and **mixed cropping**: keep the soil covered for longer and maintain organic matter.
- **Strip cropping**: alternate strips of a close-growing crop (grass, legumes) and a row crop (maize, cassava) across the slope; the close crop slows the water.
- **Minimum or zero tillage**: disturb the soil as little as possible.
- **Avoid bush burning**; keep fallow periods long enough.
- **Organic manure** to keep a good crumb structure.

### Mechanical methods

- **Contour ploughing and ridging**: make ridges **across** the slope, following the contour, so each ridge holds water and lets it soak in.
- **Terracing**: on steep slopes, cut level steps so water cannot gather speed.
- **Bunds** and **contour bunds**: low earth or stone walls across the slope.
- **Drainage and diversion channels**: lead excess water safely off the farm.
- **Gully control**: block gullies with **check dams** of stones, sticks or sandbags, then plant grass.

### Biological methods

- **Afforestation** and **reforestation**: planting trees on hillsides and catchments.
- **Windbreaks** and **shelterbelts**: rows of trees against wind.
- **Grass strips** (e.g. vetiver) along the contour; grass on waterways.
- **Agroforestry**: growing crops among trees.
- **Controlled grazing**: rotational grazing and stocking at the right number of animals.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Making ridges up and down the slope "so water drains away".** This is exactly what causes rill and gully erosion: each furrow becomes a channel that carries water and soil straight downhill. Ridges must go **across** the slope, along the contour.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Swamp and slope together.** In many valleys, the slopes around an inland valley swamp are farmed for upland rice and cassava. When the slopes are cleared and burnt, soil washes down into the swamp, silts the channels and covers the rice. Farmers who keep **grass strips and trees on the slopes**, ridge **across** them and avoid burning protect both the upland farm and the swamp below.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Define soil erosion."}
The removal of topsoil from one place to another by agents such as water, wind and human activities, faster than new soil forms.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Name the four stages of water erosion in order of increasing damage."}
Splash, sheet, rill and gully erosion.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. State four causes of soil erosion."}
Any four: deforestation, bush burning, overgrazing, ploughing up and down slopes, continuous cropping, mining, bare soil, steep slopes with heavy rain.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. How does a cover crop reduce erosion?"}
It covers the soil, so raindrops hit the leaves instead of bare soil (preventing splash erosion); its roots bind the soil; and it slows run-off. Legume cover crops also add nitrogen.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Give two mechanical and two biological methods of soil conservation."}
Mechanical: any two of contour ridging, terracing, bunds, drainage channels, check dams. Biological: any two of afforestation, windbreaks, grass strips, agroforestry, controlled grazing.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Splash erosion**: raindrops hitting bare soil and splashing particles out of place, breaking crumbs and sealing the surface.
- **Sheet erosion**: the washing away of a thin, even layer of topsoil from a wide area.
- **Rill erosion**: erosion by water collecting into many small channels a few centimetres deep.
- **Gully erosion**: erosion in which rills join into deep, wide channels that cut farmland apart.
- **Contour ploughing**: ploughing across the slope, along the contours, so that the ridges slow run-off.
- **Terracing**: cutting a slope into a series of level steps to reduce run-off and erosion.
:::

## Key points

- **Erosion** removes topsoil; **conservation** protects it.
- Agents: **water** (main in Sierra Leone), **wind**, humans and animals.
- Types: **splash → sheet → rill → gully**; also stream bank erosion.
- Causes: **deforestation, burning, overgrazing, up-and-down ridging, continuous cropping, mining**.
- Methods: **cultural** (cover crops, mulching, rotation, strip cropping), **mechanical** (contour ridges, terraces, bunds, check dams), **biological** (trees, windbreaks, grass strips).
