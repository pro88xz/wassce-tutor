:::callout{type=definition}
**Weather** is the condition of the atmosphere at a particular place and time, such as hot and sunny this morning or heavy rain this afternoon.
:::

:::callout{type=definition}
**Climate** is the average weather of a large area over a long period, usually 30 years or more: Sierra Leone, for example, has a hot, humid tropical climate with a long rainy season.
:::

Weather and climate are both described by the same **elements of weather**: temperature, rainfall, humidity, air pressure, wind, sunshine and cloud. This lesson explains the elements and the **factors that affect climate**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- distinguish weather from climate
- describe the elements of weather and climate
- explain the factors that affect the climate of a place
- explain the three types of rainfall
- describe the air masses and winds that control West Africa's climate

## Weather and climate compared

| Weather | Climate |
|---|---|
| condition of the atmosphere at a **particular time** | **average** weather conditions over a **long period** (30–35 years) |
| for a **small area** | for a **large area** (a region or country) |
| changes from hour to hour and day to day | changes very slowly |
| studied by **meteorology** | studied by **climatology** |
| e.g. "It rained heavily in Bo this afternoon." | e.g. "Bo has a tropical climate with a long rainy season." |

## The elements of weather and climate

| Element | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| **Temperature** | degree of hotness or coldness of the air | degrees Celsius (°C) |
| **Precipitation** | water falling from clouds: rain, snow, hail, sleet | millimetres (mm) |
| **Humidity** | amount of water vapour in the air; **relative humidity** compares it with the most the air could hold | percent (%) |
| **Air pressure** | weight of the atmosphere pressing on the Earth's surface | millibars (mb) |
| **Wind** | moving air, described by its **direction** and **speed** | direction from which it blows; km/h or knots |
| **Sunshine** | number of hours of bright sunshine | hours per day |
| **Cloud cover** | fraction of the sky covered by cloud | oktas (eighths) |

## Factors affecting climate

1. **Latitude:** places near the Equator receive the Sun's rays almost vertically all year, so they are hot; towards the poles the rays are slanting and spread over a larger area, so it is cooler.
2. **Altitude:** temperature falls by about **6.5 °C for every 1,000 m** of height, so highlands are cooler than lowlands. Jos, on the Jos Plateau in Nigeria at about 1,200 m, is noticeably cooler than Lagos.
3. **Distance from the sea (continentality):** the sea heats and cools slowly, so coastal places have **small temperature ranges** and more rain; places far inland have **large ranges** and less rain.
4. **Prevailing winds:** winds from the sea bring moisture and rain; winds from over land are dry (like the Harmattan).
5. **Ocean currents:** warm currents warm and moisten coasts; cold currents cool them and make them dry. The cold **Benguela Current** helps explain the **Namib Desert**; the cold **Canary Current** contributes to dryness on the north-west African coast.
6. **Relief and aspect:** mountains force moist air to rise and rain on the **windward** side, leaving a dry **rain shadow** on the **leeward** side.
7. **Vegetation:** forests increase humidity and may add to rainfall.
8. **Human activities:** deforestation, cities (urban heat islands) and greenhouse gases change local and global climate.

## Types of rainfall

![Three types of rainfall: convectional (strong heating makes moist air rise into storm clouds), relief or orographic (moist wind forced over mountains, with a dry rain shadow on the leeward side) and frontal (warm air rising over cold air)](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/rainfall-types.svg)

*Figure 1. Air must rise and cool for rain to form. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

In every case, **moist air rises, cools, its water vapour condenses into clouds, and the droplets grow until they fall as rain**.

| Type | How the air rises | Where common |
|---|---|---|
| **Convectional** | intense heating of the ground makes warm, moist air rise rapidly | equatorial regions; heavy afternoon storms with thunder and lightning |
| **Relief (orographic)** | moist wind is forced to rise over mountains or highlands | windward slopes, such as the slopes facing the Atlantic in West Africa |
| **Frontal (cyclonic)** | warm, light air rises over a wedge of cold, dense air along a **front** | temperate latitudes, such as Western Europe |

## Air masses and winds over West Africa

West Africa's climate is controlled by two **air masses**:

| Tropical maritime (mT) air | Tropical continental (cT) air |
|---|---|
| from the **Atlantic Ocean**, to the south-west | from the **Sahara**, to the north-east |
| warm and **moist** | hot by day, cool at night, and **dry and dusty** |
| arrives as the **south-west monsoon** wind | arrives as the **north-east trade wind**, the **Harmattan** |
| brings the **rainy season** | brings the **dry season** |

The boundary between them is the **Inter-Tropical Discontinuity (ITD)**, part of the Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone. It moves **north** with the overhead Sun in the northern summer, bringing rain inland, and **south** in the northern winter, letting the dry Harmattan spread to the coast.

:::callout{type=local-example}
In Sierra Leone the **south-west monsoon** brings the rainy season from about **May to October**, while the **Harmattan** brings dry, hazy, dusty weather, with cool nights, between about **December and February**. The **Freetown Peninsula mountains** force the moist monsoon air to rise, which is one reason Freetown is among the wettest capital cities in West Africa.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not describe a single day's weather as "climate". "It was cloudy in Makeni yesterday" is **weather**; "Makeni has a rainy season from May to October" is **climate**.
:::

## Key points

- **Weather:** short-term state of the atmosphere at a place; **climate:** average weather over 30+ years for a large area.
- Elements: **temperature, precipitation, humidity, pressure, wind, sunshine, cloud**.
- Factors: **latitude, altitude, distance from the sea, winds, ocean currents, relief, vegetation, human activity**.
- Rainfall types: **convectional, relief, frontal**: air rises, cools, condenses.
- West Africa: moist **south-west monsoon** (rain) and dry **Harmattan** (dry season), separated by the **ITD**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **distinguish weather from climate**, to **name and explain the elements**, to **explain the factors that influence climate** (especially latitude, altitude, distance from the sea and ocean currents), and to **describe with diagrams the types of rainfall**. The air masses and the Harmattan are frequently tested for West Africa.
:::

## Quick check

1. Define climate.
2. By roughly how much does temperature fall for every 1,000 m of height?
3. Why do coastal places have a smaller temperature range than inland places?
4. What is a rain shadow?
5. Which wind brings the dry season to Sierra Leone?

**Answers:**

1. The average weather conditions of a large area over a long period, usually 30 years or more.
2. About 6.5 °C.
3. The sea heats up and cools down slowly, so it moderates the temperature of nearby land.
4. A dry area on the leeward side of mountains, where descending air warms and gives little rain.
5. The Harmattan (the north-east trade wind from the Sahara).
