Ask any farmer in Sierra Leone what takes most of the work on the farm, and the answer is often **weeding**. Weeds grow fast, compete with crops for light, water and nutrients, and in the first weeks after planting can cut yields by half or more if left alone. **Spear grass** on upland farms and many grasses and sedges in rice swamps are among the hardest to control. This lesson defines weeds, explains their harmful and useful effects, classifies them, and describes preventive, cultural, mechanical, chemical and biological methods of weed control, including the safe use of herbicides.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a weed and state the characteristics that make weeds successful
- state the harmful effects and possible uses of weeds
- classify weeds and name common examples
- describe the methods of weed control, with their advantages and disadvantages
- classify herbicides and state precautions in their use
- explain the critical period of weed competition

## What is a weed?

:::callout{type=definition}
A **weed** is any plant growing **where it is not wanted**. Even a crop can be a weed: maize growing in a rice field from last season's spilled grain is a weed.
:::

### Why weeds succeed

- They **grow fast** and are adapted to local conditions.
- They produce **huge numbers of seeds**, many of which stay **dormant** in the soil for years.
- Many spread **vegetatively** by rhizomes, stolons, tubers or bulbs (e.g. spear grass, nutgrass).
- Their seeds are spread by wind, water, animals, people and machinery, or in impure crop seed.
- They tolerate poor conditions and resist many control methods.

## Harmful effects of weeds

- **Compete** with crops for **light, water, nutrients and space**, reducing yield.
- **Harbour pests and diseases** (e.g. grasses host stem borers; some weeds carry viruses).
- **Reduce quality**: weed seeds in grain lower its price.
- **Parasitic weeds** such as **witchweed** (*Striga*) attack the roots of cereals and suck their food and water.
- Some are **poisonous** to animals; some have spines.
- **Increase costs** of labour and herbicides; block irrigation channels.
- Some **reduce the value of land** (spear grass is very hard to clear).

### Possible uses of weeds

Weeds can **cover and protect the soil** from erosion, add **organic matter** when slashed or composted, provide **fodder** for animals, and some are used as **medicine** or **vegetables**.

## Classifying weeds

| By | Groups | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **life cycle** | **annual** (one season); **biennial**; **perennial** (many years, often from rhizomes) | annual: goat weed (*Ageratum*); perennial: **spear grass** (*Imperata cylindrica*), nutgrass |
| **leaf type** | **grasses** (narrow leaves); **broad-leaved weeds**; **sedges** (triangular stems) | grass: spear grass; broadleaf: *Chromolaena*, *Mimosa pudica*; sedge: nutgrass (*Cyperus*) |
| **habitat** | upland and arable weeds; **aquatic weeds** | water hyacinth, water lettuce |
| **mode of life** | free-living; **parasitic** | witchweed (*Striga*) |

:::callout{type=tip}
Classifying weeds as **grasses, sedges or broad-leaved** matters because many herbicides are **selective**: a herbicide that kills broad-leaved weeds may leave grasses (and grass crops such as rice and maize) unharmed.
:::

## The critical period

Weeds do the most damage in the **first 4–6 weeks** after a crop is planted, when crop seedlings are small. This is the **critical period of weed competition**. Keeping the crop clean during this period protects most of the yield; later weeds do less harm because the crop canopy shades them out.

## Methods of weed control

![Integrated weed management linked to preventive, cultural, mechanical, chemical and biological methods of control](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/weed-control-methods.svg)

### Preventive

- Plant **clean, certified seed** free of weed seeds.
- **Clean tools and machines** before moving between fields.
- Use **well-composted manure** (composting heat kills many weed seeds).
- Weed field edges and channels before weeds set seed.

### Cultural

- **Correct spacing** and plant population, so the crop canopy closes quickly and shades weeds.
- **Cover crops** and **mulching** smother weeds.
- **Crop rotation** breaks the cycle of weeds associated with one crop.
- **Timely planting** and fertiliser so the crop gets ahead.
- **Flooding** in swamp rice drowns many weeds.

### Mechanical (physical)

- **Hand pulling** (rogueing), **hoeing**, **slashing** with a cutlass.
- **Tillage**: ploughing and harrowing bury weeds and expose rhizomes to sun.
- **Mowing** and **burning** (burning is not recommended: it destroys organic matter).

### Chemical: herbicides

:::callout{type=definition}
| Classification | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **selective** | kills certain weeds without harming the crop | 2,4-D (broad-leaved weeds in rice and maize); propanil (in rice) |
| **non-selective** | kills all green plants it touches | **glyphosate**, paraquat |
| **contact** | kills only the parts it touches | paraquat |
| **systemic (translocated)** | absorbed and moved through the plant, killing roots and rhizomes | glyphosate (effective on spear grass) |
| **pre-emergence** | applied **before** crop or weeds emerge | |
| **post-emergence** | applied **after** they emerge | |
:::

**Advantages:** fast, saves labour, controls weeds in the crop row and perennial weeds. **Disadvantages:** cost; danger to people, animals and water; damage to crops if misused; weeds may become resistant.

**Safe use:** read the label; use the right dose and nozzle; wear protective clothing; spray on calm, dry days; keep away from wells, streams and food; wash the sprayer and yourself afterwards; store safely and never reuse containers.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Slashing spear grass and expecting it to die.** Spear grass regrows quickly from its underground **rhizomes**. Slashing or burning only removes the leaves. It is controlled by repeated ploughing and harrowing to bring rhizomes to the surface to dry, by shading with cover crops or trees, or by a **systemic** herbicide such as glyphosate that reaches the rhizomes.
:::

### Biological

Using living organisms: **insects** that feed on particular weeds (used against water hyacinth and some invasive weeds), **grazing animals** in plantations and orchards, and fungal diseases of weeds.

### Integrated weed management

Combining several methods, for example clean seed + correct spacing + one early hoeing + a cover crop, gives better and cheaper control than relying on one method.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Weeding the swamp rice.** In inland valley swamps, farmers who **transplant in straight rows** can weed quickly with a simple push-type **rotary weeder** between the rows, and keeping **5 cm of water** in the field stops many weeds from germinating. Weeds that come in the first month cost the most yield, so one well-timed weeding at 2–3 weeks after transplanting pays back many times over.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Define a weed and give three characteristics of weeds."}
A plant growing where it is not wanted. Characteristics: fast growth; many seeds; long seed dormancy; vegetative spread; easy dispersal; tolerance of poor conditions (any three).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. State four harmful effects of weeds on crops."}
Any four: competition for light, water, nutrients and space; harbouring pests and diseases; reducing quality; parasitism (Striga); poisoning animals; increasing costs.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Distinguish selective from non-selective herbicides, with an example of each."}
A **selective** herbicide kills certain weeds but not the crop (2,4-D, propanil). A **non-selective** herbicide kills all green plants (glyphosate, paraquat).
:::

:::reveal{title="4. What is the critical period of weed competition?"}
The first **4–6 weeks** after planting, when weeds cause most yield loss; keeping the crop weed-free then protects most of the yield.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Give three cultural methods of weed control."}
Any three: correct spacing; cover crops; mulching; crop rotation; timely planting and fertiliser use; flooding (rice).
:::

## Key points

- A **weed** is a plant growing where it is not wanted; weeds grow fast, seed heavily and spread vegetatively.
- Weeds **compete**, **harbour pests**, reduce **quality**, and some are **parasitic** (Striga) or **poisonous**.
- Classify by **life cycle**, **leaf type** (grass, sedge, broadleaf) and habitat.
- Keep crops weed-free in the **first 4–6 weeks**.
- Methods: **preventive, cultural, mechanical, chemical (selective/non-selective, contact/systemic), biological**; combine them.
