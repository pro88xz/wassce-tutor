Algebra is arithmetic with **letters standing for numbers**. Instead of saying *"a number, doubled, plus three"*, you write $2x + 3$, shorter, clearer, and ready to work with. Every algebra topic in the WASSCE syllabus (equations, factorisation, graphs, inequalities, change of subject) is built on the skills in this lesson: reading expressions, collecting like terms, using brackets and substituting values. Get these automatic, and the rest of algebra becomes much easier.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name the parts of an algebraic expression: terms, variables, coefficients and constants
- write expressions from words
- simplify expressions by collecting like terms
- remove single brackets
- substitute numbers, including negative numbers and fractions, into expressions

## The language of algebra

:::callout{type=definition}
In the expression $5x^2 - 3xy + 7$:

| Word | Meaning | Here |
|---|---|---|
| **variable** | a letter that stands for a number | $x$, $y$ |
| **term** | one part of the expression, separated by $+$ or $-$ | $5x^2$, $-3xy$, $7$ |
| **coefficient** | the number in front of a variable | $5$ (of $x^2$), $-3$ (of $xy$) |
| **constant** | a term with no variable | $7$ |
| **expression** | terms joined by $+$ or $-$ (no equals sign) | $5x^2 - 3xy + 7$ |
:::

### Conventions

| We write | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $3x$ | $3 \times x$ (number first, no $\times$ sign) |
| $xy$ | $x \times y$ |
| $x^2$ | $x \times x$ |
| $\dfrac{x}{4}$ or $\tfrac14 x$ | $x \div 4$ |
| $x$ | $1x$ (the 1 is not written) |
| $-x$ | $-1x$ |

## Writing expressions from words

| Words | Expression |
|---|---|
| a number increased by 7 | $n + 7$ |
| 5 less than a number | $n - 5$ (**not** $5 - n$) |
| twice a number, plus 3 | $2n + 3$ |
| the square of a number, minus 4 | $n^2 - 4$ |
| the product of two numbers | $xy$ |
| a third of the sum of $a$ and $b$ | $\dfrac{a + b}{3}$ |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A trader buys $x$ bags of rice at NLe 650 each and $y$ tins of oil at NLe 120 each. Write an expression for the total cost.**

Cost of rice $= 650x$; cost of oil $= 120y$.

**Total cost $= 650x + 120y$ leones.**
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**"5 less than $n$" is $n - 5$, not $5 - n$.** "Less than" reverses the order of the words. Check with a number: 5 less than 12 is 7, and $12 - 5 = 7$.
:::

## Collecting like terms

:::callout{type=definition}
**Like terms** have exactly the same variables raised to the same powers: $3x$ and $-7x$ are like terms; $3x$ and $3x^2$ are **not**; $2ab$ and $5ba$ **are** (since $ab = ba$).
:::

Add or subtract the **coefficients** of like terms; keep the variable part unchanged.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Simplify $7a + 3b - 2a + 5b - b$.**

Group the like terms:

$$7a - 2a + 3b + 5b - b = 5a + 7b$$

**Simplify $4x^2 + 3x - x^2 + 6 - 5x - 2$.**

$$(4x^2 - x^2) + (3x - 5x) + (6 - 2) = 3x^2 - 2x + 4$$
:::

## Removing brackets

Multiply **every** term inside the bracket by the term outside:

$$a(b + c) = ab + ac$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**1.** $3(2x - 5) = 6x - 15$

**2.** $-4(a - 3b) = -4a + 12b$ (a negative times a negative is positive)

**3.** $x(x + 7) = x^2 + 7x$

**4. Simplify $5(2y + 3) - 3(y - 4)$.**

$$= 10y + 15 - 3y + 12 = 7y + 27$$
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**The minus sign outside a bracket.** $-3(y - 4)$ is $-3y + 12$, not $-3y - 12$. Multiply the $-3$ by **both** terms: $(-3)\times(-4) = +12$.
:::

## Substitution

Replace each letter with its value, **in brackets**, then follow the order of operations (BODMAS: Brackets, Orders/powers, Division and Multiplication, Addition and Subtraction).

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**If $a = 3$, $b = -2$ and $c = \tfrac12$, evaluate:**

**(a)** $2a - 3b = 2(3) - 3(-2) = 6 + 6 = 12$

**(b)** $a^2 + b^2 = (3)^2 + (-2)^2 = 9 + 4 = 13$

**(c)** $\dfrac{a - b}{4c} = \dfrac{3 - (-2)}{4 \times \tfrac12} = \dfrac{5}{2} = 2\tfrac12$

**(d)** $ab^2 = 3 \times (-2)^2 = 3 \times 4 = 12$ (the square applies only to $b$)
:::

:::callout{type=tip}
**Always put negative numbers in brackets** when you substitute. $-2^2 = -4$, but $(-2)^2 = 4$. The brackets make the difference.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Formulas are expressions waiting for numbers. An okada rider charges NLe 15 to start plus NLe 8 per kilometre: the fare is $15 + 8k$. For a 6 km ride: $15 + 8(6) = 15 + 48 = $ **NLe 63**.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Simplify: 6p − 4q + 2p + 9q − p"}
$$(6p + 2p - p) + (-4q + 9q) = 7p + 5q$$
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Expand and simplify: 4(3m − 2) − 2(m + 5)"}
$$12m - 8 - 2m - 10 = 10m - 18$$
:::

:::reveal{title="3. If x = −3 and y = 4, find the value of x² − 2xy + y²."}
$$(-3)^2 - 2(-3)(4) + (4)^2 = 9 + 24 + 16 = 49$$

(Check: this is $(x - y)^2 = (-7)^2 = 49$.)
:::

:::reveal{title="4. A school buys n exercise books at NLe 12 each and pays NLe 50 for delivery. Write the total cost, then find it when n = 40."}
Total cost $= 12n + 50$.

When $n = 40$: $12(40) + 50 = 480 + 50 = $ **NLe 530**.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Simplify: 3a(2a − b) − a(a + 4b)"}
$$6a^2 - 3ab - a^2 - 4ab = 5a^2 - 7ab$$
:::

## Key points

- **Terms** are separated by $+$ and $-$; the **coefficient** is the number in front of the variable.
- Only **like terms** (same letters, same powers) can be added or subtracted.
- When removing brackets, multiply **every** term inside, and watch the **signs**.
- When substituting, put values **in brackets**, then follow **BODMAS**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect objective questions that ask you to **simplify**, **expand** or **evaluate** an expression for given values, often with negative numbers or fractions, and theory questions that ask you to **form an expression** from a word problem. Sign errors when expanding with a negative multiplier and when squaring negatives are the commonest causes of lost marks.
:::
