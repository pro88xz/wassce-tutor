Since independence, almost every elected government in Sierra Leone has been formed by one of two parties: the **Sierra Leone People's Party (SLPP)** or the **All People's Congress (APC)**. Their rivalry has shaped elections, the constitution and even the country's experience of military rule. This lesson explains how the two parties were formed, what they stood for, where their support came from, and their records in government.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the formation and early leadership of the SLPP and the APC
- state the aims and symbols of each party
- explain the bases of support of the two parties
- outline each party's periods in government and their main achievements and failures

## The Sierra Leone People's Party (SLPP)

- **Formed:** **1951**, by the merger of the **Sierra Leone Organisation Society**, the **People's Party** and members of the **Protectorate Educational Progressive Union**.
- **Founding leader:** **Dr (later Sir) Milton Margai**.
- **Symbol and colour:** the **palm tree**; **green**.
- **Early aims:** to unite the Colony and the Protectorate, to win self-government and independence by peaceful means, and to develop the Protectorate.
- **Traditional base:** strongest in the **south and east**, among Mende-speaking areas, with early support from **Paramount Chiefs** and the Protectorate educated elite.

**SLPP in government:**

| Period | Leader | Highlights |
|---|---|---|
| **1951–1964** | Sir Milton Margai | Led the country to **independence (1961)**; moderate, consensus-building style |
| **1964–1967** | Sir Albert Margai | Expanded development projects; proposed a **one-party state** and republic; lost the **1967** election |
| **1996–2007** | Ahmad Tejan Kabbah | Elected during the war; overthrown by the AFRC in 1997 and restored in 1998; **ended the civil war (2002)**; post-war reconstruction |
| **2018 onward** | Julius Maada Bio | **Free Quality School Education**; abolition of the death penalty (2021); repeal of criminal libel (2020); move to PR elections (2023) |

## The All People's Congress (APC)

- **Formed:** **1960**, from the **Elections Before Independence Movement**, after **Siaka Stevens** refused to sign the Lancaster House agreement.
- **Founding leader:** **Siaka Stevens**.
- **Symbol and colour:** the **rising sun**; **red**.
- **Early aims:** to represent ordinary people, workers and youth; to oppose the SLPP's close links with chiefs and the elite; to make Sierra Leone a **republic**.
- **Traditional base:** strongest in the **north** and among urban workers in the **Western Area**.

**APC in government:**

| Period | Leader | Highlights |
|---|---|---|
| **1968–1985** | Siaka Stevens | Made Sierra Leone a **republic (1971)**; introduced the **one-party state (1978)**; hosted the OAU summit (1980); economic decline and corruption |
| **1985–1992** | Joseph Saidu Momoh | Severe economic crisis; restored multi-party politics in the **1991 Constitution**; overthrown by the **NPRC** in **1992** |
| **2007–2018** | Ernest Bai Koroma | Won the **2007** election, a peaceful change of party; road building and energy projects; the **Ebola** epidemic (2014–2016) |

## Comparing the two parties

| | SLPP | APC |
|---|---|---|
| **Founded** | 1951 | 1960 |
| **Founder** | Milton Margai | Siaka Stevens |
| **Symbol / colour** | Palm tree / green | Rising sun / red |
| **Traditional strongholds** | South and east | North and Western Area |
| **Early image** | Party of chiefs and the Protectorate elite | Party of workers, youth and "the common man" |
| **Notable record** | Independence; ending the civil war | Republic; one-party state; post-war infrastructure |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone has had **two peaceful changes of ruling party through elections** since the civil war: **2007**, when the APC's Ernest Bai Koroma defeated the SLPP's Solomon Berewa in a run-off, and **2018**, when the SLPP's Julius Maada Bio defeated the APC's Samura Kamara in a run-off. Both times the losing party accepted the result, strengthening democracy.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not describe either party as purely ethnic. Both have **national** support, and both have won votes and seats outside their traditional strongholds. However, voting patterns have often followed **regional** lines, which is a major challenge for national unity.
:::

## Key points

- **SLPP**: founded **1951**, **Milton Margai**, **palm tree, green**; strongest in the **south and east**; led the country to **independence** and **ended the war**.
- **APC**: founded **1960**, **Siaka Stevens**, **rising sun, red**; strongest in the **north and Western Area**; created the **republic** and the **one-party state**.
- Power has alternated: **SLPP 1961–67, APC 1968–92, SLPP 1996–2007, APC 2007–18, SLPP from 2018**.

## Quick check

1. When was the SLPP formed, and by whom?
2. What is the symbol of the APC?
3. Which party was in power when the civil war ended?
4. In which years did power pass peacefully between the parties through elections after the war?

**Answers:**

1. In 1951, under the leadership of Dr Milton Margai.
2. The rising sun.
3. The SLPP, under President Ahmad Tejan Kabbah.
4. 2007 (SLPP to APC) and 2018 (APC to SLPP).
