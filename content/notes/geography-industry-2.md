**Manufacturing industries in West Africa** are mostly **light industries** that process local raw materials or produce consumer goods for the home market: food and drink, textiles, cement, soap, plastics and oil refining. The main industrial centres are **Lagos**, **Tema and Accra**, **Abidjan**, **Dakar** and **Kano**. Industrial growth is held back by **unreliable power**, **shortage of capital**, **small markets**, **poor transport** and **competition from imports**. This lesson describes the main industries and centres, the importance of manufacturing, its problems and the solutions.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name the main types of manufacturing industry in West Africa
- describe the major industrial centres of West Africa
- describe manufacturing in Sierra Leone
- explain the importance of manufacturing industries
- explain the problems of industrial development in West Africa and suggest solutions

## Main types of manufacturing

| Industry | Examples in West Africa |
|---|---|
| **Food, drink and tobacco** | flour milling, breweries, soft drinks, sugar refining, cocoa processing, palm-oil mills |
| **Textiles and clothing** | cotton spinning and weaving in Kano and Kaduna; printed cloth |
| **Building materials** | cement at Ewekoro and Obajana (Nigeria) and Tema (Ghana); bricks, roofing sheets |
| **Petroleum and petrochemicals** | oil refineries in Nigeria, including the large **Dangote refinery** at Lekki, near Lagos, commissioned in **2023**; fertilisers, plastics |
| **Metals** | aluminium smelting at **Tema** (VALCO), using power from Akosombo; steel rolling |
| **Chemicals** | soap, detergents, paints, pharmaceuticals |
| **Assembly** | vehicles, electrical goods, furniture |
| **Cottage industries** | weaving, dyeing, pottery, carving, blacksmithing, leatherwork in Kano |

## Industrial centres of West Africa

| Centre | Why it developed | Main industries |
|---|---|---|
| **Lagos (Nigeria)** | largest market, main ports (Apapa, Tin Can, Lekki), capital and labour, power | food, drink, textiles, vehicle assembly, chemicals, oil refining |
| **Tema (Ghana)** | purpose-built port and industrial town; cheap power from **Akosombo** | aluminium smelting, oil refining, cement, food processing |
| **Abidjan (Côte d'Ivoire)** | deep-water port, stable growth, cocoa and coffee | cocoa processing, food, oil refining, textiles |
| **Dakar (Senegal)** | port and capital; groundnuts and phosphate | groundnut oil, fish canning, fertilisers |
| **Kano (Nigeria)** | old trade centre, labour, local raw materials | textiles, leather, groundnut processing |

## Manufacturing in Sierra Leone

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's manufacturing sector is **small** and concentrated in **Freetown**, especially at the **Wellington Industrial Estate**. Products include **beer and soft drinks**, **soap**, **paint**, **plastics**, **foam mattresses**, **cement** and **flour**. Smaller industries elsewhere include rice milling, palm-oil processing, sawmilling and fish smoking. Cottage industries such as **gara (tie-dye)** cloth, **country cloth** weaving, carving and blacksmithing are found across the country.
:::

## Importance of manufacturing

1. **Employment** for many people.
2. **Adds value** to raw materials, so that countries earn more than from exporting them raw.
3. **Saves foreign exchange** by producing goods that were imported (**import substitution**).
4. **Earns foreign exchange** from manufactured exports.
5. **Government revenue** from taxes.
6. **Develops skills** and technology.
7. **Market** for agricultural and mineral raw materials.
8. **Growth of towns** and infrastructure.
9. **Diversifies the economy** away from dependence on a few primary exports.

## Problems of manufacturing in West Africa

1. **Unreliable and expensive power** supply; many factories run their own generators.
2. **Shortage of capital** for building and running factories.
3. **Shortage of skilled labour** and managers.
4. **Small home markets** because of low incomes.
5. **Poor transport** and communication.
6. **Competition from cheap imported goods.**
7. **Dependence on imported machinery, spare parts and raw materials.**
8. **Political instability** and corruption that discourage investors.
9. **High production costs** and interest rates.
10. **Poor maintenance** of equipment.

## Solutions

1. Improve **power supply** with hydroelectric, solar and regional power links.
2. Provide **credit**, attract **foreign investment** and give **tax incentives**.
3. **Technical education** and training.
4. **Regional integration** through ECOWAS and the African Continental Free Trade Area to enlarge markets.
5. **Tariffs** to protect young industries.
6. **Better roads, railways and ports.**
7. **Use local raw materials** and develop local spare-parts industries.
8. **Political stability** and fighting corruption.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not confuse **import substitution** with exporting. Import substitution means making at home goods that used to be **imported**, to save foreign exchange.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Import substitution**: making goods at home that were formerly imported.
- **Industrial estate**: an area set aside and served with roads, power and water for factories.
- **Assembly industry**: industry that puts together imported parts, such as vehicles and electrical goods.
:::

## Key points

- West African manufacturing is mostly **light industry**: food and drink, textiles, cement, soap, plastics, oil refining.
- Main centres: **Lagos, Tema, Abidjan, Dakar, Kano**; Sierra Leone's industry is mainly at **Wellington, Freetown**.
- Importance: **jobs, added value, import substitution, exports, revenue, skills, diversification**.
- Problems: **power, capital, skills, small markets, transport, cheap imports, imported inputs, instability**.
- Solutions: **power, credit and investment, training, regional markets, protection, infrastructure, local inputs**.

## WASSCE examination focus

Expect to **describe a named industrial centre** and explain why it developed, to **explain the importance of manufacturing**, and to **discuss the problems of industrialisation in West Africa**. Linking Tema's aluminium smelter to Akosombo power is a classic example.
:::

## Quick check

1. Name two industrial centres in West Africa.
2. Why did aluminium smelting develop at Tema?
3. Where are most of Sierra Leone's factories located?
4. What is import substitution?
5. State three problems facing manufacturing industries in West Africa.

**Answers:**

1. Any two: Lagos; Tema; Abidjan; Dakar; Kano.
2. Because of cheap hydroelectric power from the Akosombo dam and Tema's port.
3. In Freetown, especially the Wellington Industrial Estate.
4. Producing at home goods that were previously imported.
5. Any three: unreliable power; shortage of capital; shortage of skilled labour; small markets; poor transport; cheap imports; imported inputs; instability; high costs.
