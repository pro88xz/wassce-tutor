Every sentence is built from smaller units: **phrases** and **clauses**. Understanding them is the key to three things in WASSCE: the *"grammatical name and function"* question under every comprehension passage, correct punctuation, and writing varied, mature sentences in your essays. This lesson gives you the full picture: every kind of phrase and clause, how to identify each, what job each does, and plenty of practice.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- tell a phrase from a clause, and a main clause from a subordinate clause
- identify noun, adjectival, adverbial, prepositional, verb, infinitive, participial and gerund phrases
- identify noun, adjectival (relative) and adverbial clauses, and the types of adverbial clause
- state the function of a phrase or clause in its sentence

## Phrase or clause?

:::callout{type=definition}
- A **phrase** is a group of words that works as a unit but has **no subject + finite verb**: *the old fisherman*, *in the morning*, *to pass the examination*, *running for the bus*.
- A **clause** is a group of words **with a subject and a finite verb**: *the fisherman **sailed***, *when **the rain stopped***, *that **she was** tired*.

A **finite verb** shows tense and agrees with its subject (*sailed, is, has gone*). Infinitives (*to sail*) and participles (*sailing, sailed* without an auxiliary) are **non-finite**.
:::

## Kinds of phrase

| Phrase | Built around | Example | Usual function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Noun phrase** | a noun | ***The tired market women*** went home. | subject, object, complement |
| **Verb phrase** | a main verb with its auxiliaries | She ***has been studying*** all night. | the predicate's verb |
| **Adjectival phrase** | an adjective | The soup was ***very hot indeed***. | describes a noun |
| **Adverbial phrase** | an adverb | He ran ***very quickly***. | modifies a verb |
| **Prepositional phrase** | a preposition + object | The house ***on the hill*** is ours. / He swam ***across the river***. | adjectival or adverbial |
| **Infinitive phrase** | *to* + verb | ***To pass the WASSCE*** is my aim. / She came ***to see the doctor***. | noun, or adverbial of purpose |
| **Participial phrase** | a present or past participle | ***Running for the bus***, he dropped his phone. / ***Tired from the journey***, she slept. | adjectival (describes the subject) |
| **Gerund phrase** | an *-ing* form used as a noun | ***Swimming in the river*** is dangerous. | noun (subject, object) |

:::callout{type=tip}
**Participle or gerund?** Both end in *-ing*. If it **describes** a noun (*the **running** water*; ***Running** for the bus, he…*), it is a **participle** (adjectival). If it **is** a noun (*Swimming is fun*; *I enjoy **reading***), it is a **gerund**.
:::

## Main and subordinate clauses

:::callout{type=definition}
- A **main (independent) clause** makes complete sense on its own: *The bus left.*
- A **subordinate (dependent) clause** does not make complete sense alone; it depends on a main clause: *…because it was full.*

Subordinate clauses usually begin with a **subordinating conjunction** (*because, when, although, if…*) or a **relative pronoun** (*who, which, that…*).
:::

## Kinds of subordinate clause

### Noun clauses

Do the work of a **noun**. Test: replace with *"something"* or *"it"*.

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| **subject** of a verb | ***What she said*** surprised everyone. |
| **object** of a verb | I know ***that you are right***. |
| **complement** | The problem is ***that we have no money***. |
| **object of a preposition** | It depends on ***what the principal decides***. |
| **in apposition** to a noun | The news ***that the school would close*** upset parents. |

### Adjectival (relative) clauses

Describe (**qualify**) a noun or pronoun. They come **right after** it and usually begin with *who, whom, whose, which, that, where, when*.

> *The doctor **who treated my mother** is from Bo.* (qualifies *doctor*)
> *This is the village **where I was born**.* (qualifies *village*)

| Defining | Non-defining |
|---|---|
| tells us **which one**; no commas | adds **extra** information; set off by **commas** |
| *The students **who passed** were praised.* (only those who passed) | *My brother, **who lives in Makeni**, is a teacher.* (I have one brother) |

### Adverbial clauses

**Modify** the verb in the main clause, telling **when, why, where, how, on what condition**…

| Type | Conjunctions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **time** | when, while, before, after, until, as soon as | *We waited **until the rain stopped**.* |
| **place** | where, wherever | *Sit **where you can see the board**.* |
| **reason** | because, since, as | *She stayed home **because she was ill**.* |
| **purpose** | so that, in order that | *He saved **so that he could buy a bicycle**.* |
| **result** | so…that, such…that | *It was so hot **that the tar melted**.* |
| **condition** | if, unless, provided that | ***If you study**, you will pass.* |
| **concession** | although, though, even if, while | ***Although it rained**, the match continued.* |
| **manner** | as, as if, as though | *He spoke **as if he were the principal**.* |
| **comparison** | than, as…as | *She is taller **than her brother is**.* |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Sentence:** *"When the generator stopped, the students who were studying in the library could not see what they were writing."*

| Clause | Name | Function |
|---|---|---|
| *the students… could not see* | main clause | — |
| *When the generator stopped* | adverbial clause of time | modifies the verb *could (not) see* |
| *who were studying in the library* | adjectival clause | qualifies the noun *students* |
| *what they were writing* | noun clause | object of the verb *see* |

One sentence, three different subordinate clauses, each with its own job.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Naming by the first word.** *"That"* can begin a **noun** clause (*I know **that** he left*) or an **adjectival** clause (*the book **that** I bought*). *"When"* can begin an **adverbial** clause (***When** he arrived, we ate*) or a **noun** clause (*I don't know **when** he arrived*). Always decide by the **job** the clause does, not by its first word.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many students in Sierra Leone write long sentences joined only by *and*: *"The rains came and the roads flooded and the lorries stopped and the market was empty."* Using subordinate clauses shows the connections and earns **Expression** marks: *"**When** the rains came, the roads flooded so badly **that** the lorries stopped, and the market, **which** is usually crowded, was empty."*
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Phrase or clause? (a) after the meeting (b) after the meeting ended (c) to win the race"}
(a) **phrase** (prepositional) (b) **clause** (*the meeting ended*: subject + finite verb) (c) **phrase** (infinitive)
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Name and function: 'Whoever wins the election will face many problems.'"}
**Noun clause**; **subject** of the verb *will face*.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Name and function: 'The girl whose bag was stolen reported it to the police.'"}
**Adjectival (relative) clause**; qualifies the noun *girl*.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Name and function: 'Although he was tired, Abu finished his homework.'"}
**Adverbial clause of concession**; modifies the verb *finished*.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Participle or gerund? (a) 'Smoking is forbidden.' (b) 'The smoking chimney.'"}
(a) **gerund** (a noun, the subject) (b) **participle** (an adjective describing *chimney*)
:::

:::reveal{title="6. Defining or non-defining? Punctuate: 'Freetown which is the capital is on the coast.'"}
**Non-defining**: there is only one Freetown, so the clause adds extra information: *Freetown, **which is the capital**, is on the coast.*
:::

## Key points

- A **phrase** has no subject + finite verb; a **clause** has both.
- **Main** clauses stand alone; **subordinate** clauses depend on them.
- Phrases: **noun, verb, adjectival, adverbial, prepositional, infinitive, participial, gerund**.
- Clauses: **noun** (subject, object, complement…), **adjectival** (qualifies a noun; defining or non-defining), **adverbial** (time, place, reason, purpose, result, condition, concession, manner, comparison).
- Name a structure by its **job**, not by its first word.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Every comprehension passage has a **grammatical name and function** question, and the objective paper tests clause types and relative pronouns. Answer with the **exact name** (including the type of adverbial clause) and the **exact function** (naming the word it qualifies, modifies, or is the subject or object of).
:::
