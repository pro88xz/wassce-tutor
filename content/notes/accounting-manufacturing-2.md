A full **manufacturing, trading and profit and loss account** question gives you a trial balance of a manufacturer and asks you to find, in order, the **raw materials consumed**, **prime cost**, **production cost of goods completed**, **gross profit** and **net profit**. The production cost is transferred to the **trading account**, where it takes the place of purchases in the cost of sales. This lesson works through a complete example step by step, including apportioned expenses and work in progress, and calculates the **cost per unit**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- prepare a manufacturing account from given information
- transfer the production cost to the trading account
- prepare the trading and profit and loss sections of a manufacturer
- calculate the production cost per unit
- show the three closing inventories in the statement of financial position

## Worked example

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Kanu Furniture Makers, year ended 31 December 2026 (NLe):**

- Raw materials: opening 12,000; purchases 80,000; carriage inwards on raw materials 2,000; closing 15,000
- Direct wages 45,000; royalties (direct expense) 3,000
- Indirect (factory) wages 9,000; power 6,000; depreciation of machinery 8,000
- Rent 20,000, shared factory 60%, office 40%
- Work in progress: opening 7,000; closing 9,000
- Finished goods: opening 20,000; closing 25,000
- Sales 260,000
- Office salaries 30,000; advertising 6,000; delivery van expenses 5,000
- Units completed in the year: 4,000
:::

**Workings:**

- Raw materials consumed $= 12\,000 + 80\,000 + 2\,000 - 15\,000 = 79\,000$
- Prime cost $= 79\,000 + 45\,000 + 3\,000 = 127\,000$
- Production cost $= 127\,000 + 35\,000 + 7\,000 - 9\,000 = 160\,000$
- Gross profit $= 260\,000 - 155\,000 = 105\,000$
- Net profit $= 105\,000 - 49\,000 = 56\,000$

## Cost per unit

Production cost per unit = production cost of goods completed ÷ units completed:

$$\frac{160\,000}{4\,000} = 40$$

Each item of furniture costs NLe 40 to produce. The owner can compare this with the selling price, plan prices and control costs.

## Factory profit (transfer price)

Some manufacturers transfer goods to the trading account at **market value** (what it would cost to buy the goods from outside) instead of at cost, to show whether the factory is efficient. The difference is a **factory profit**, added to the gross profit. It is an internal figure, not extra profit for the business as a whole, and any unrealised factory profit in closing finished goods must be removed by a **provision for unrealised profit**.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not deduct **closing finished goods** in the manufacturing account. **Raw materials** and **WIP** are adjusted in the manufacturing account; **finished goods** are adjusted in the **trading account**.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Cost per unit**: production cost divided by the number of units completed.
:::

## Key points

- Order: **materials consumed → prime cost → factory overheads → WIP adjustment → production cost**.
- The **production cost** replaces purchases in the **trading account**.
- **Office, selling and distribution expenses** go to the profit and loss section.
- **Cost per unit = production cost ÷ units completed.**
- Three closing inventories are **current assets**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
A full **manufacturing, trading and profit and loss account** is a standard Paper 2 question. Label **prime cost** and **production cost** clearly, show the apportionment of shared expenses, and remember which inventory is adjusted in which account.
:::

## Quick check

1. To which account is the production cost transferred?
2. Prime cost NLe 90,000, factory overheads NLe 25,000, opening WIP NLe 4,000, closing WIP NLe 6,000. Find the production cost.
3. Where is closing work in progress shown in the statement of financial position?
4. Production cost is NLe 72,000 for 1,800 units. Find the cost per unit.
5. Where do advertising costs appear?

**Answers:**

1. The trading account, in place of purchases.
2. $90\,000 + 25\,000 + 4\,000 - 6\,000 = 113\,000$: NLe 113,000.
3. Among the current assets (inventories).
4. $72\,000 \div 1\,800 = 40$: NLe 40.
5. In the profit and loss section of the income statement, as a selling expense.
