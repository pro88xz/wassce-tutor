Feed is usually the **largest single cost** in livestock farming, often well over half of all costs on a poultry or pig farm. Feeding animals the right nutrients in the right amounts decides how fast they grow, how many eggs or how much milk they give, and how well they resist disease. Underfed animals grow slowly; overfed or badly balanced rations waste money. This lesson covers the nutrients animals need, the classes of feedstuffs, balanced rations and how to mix one with the **Pearson square**, and pasture and forage for ruminants, including feed conservation for the dry season.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the nutrients required by farm animals, their functions and deficiency signs
- classify feedstuffs into roughages, concentrates and supplements
- define a balanced ration and distinguish maintenance and production rations
- formulate a simple ration using the Pearson square
- describe pasture establishment, grazing systems and forage conservation (hay and silage)

## Nutrients and their functions

| Nutrient | Functions | Sources | Deficiency signs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **carbohydrates** | energy | maize, sorghum, cassava, rice bran, grass | poor growth, weight loss |
| **fats and oils** | concentrated energy; carry fat-soluble vitamins | palm oil, oilseed cakes | dry skin, poor coat |
| **proteins** | growth, repair, milk, eggs, enzymes | **fish meal, groundnut cake, soya bean meal**, blood meal, legume forages | slow growth, low egg and milk output, poor fertility |
| **minerals** | bones and eggshells (calcium, phosphorus); blood (iron); many body processes | bone meal, oyster shell, limestone, salt licks | rickets, weak bones, thin-shelled eggs, anaemia |
| **vitamins** | regulate body processes | green forage, premixes, fish oil | night blindness (A), rickets (D), poor fertility (E) |
| **water** | makes up most of the body; transports nutrients; cools the body | clean drinking water | dehydration; rapid fall in feeding and production |

:::callout{type=tip}
**Water is the cheapest and most neglected nutrient.** A laying hen drinks about twice as much water as the feed she eats, more in hot weather. If water runs out for even a day, egg production drops sharply. Clean water must be available **at all times**.
:::

## Classes of feedstuffs

| Class | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **roughages** | bulky, high in **fibre**, low in energy per kg; mainly for ruminants | fresh grass, legume forages, **hay**, **silage**, straw, crop residues (groundnut haulms, rice straw), browse leaves |
| **concentrates** | low in fibre, **high in energy or protein** | **energy**: maize, sorghum, cassava chips, rice bran, palm kernel cake; **protein**: fish meal, groundnut cake, soya bean meal, blood meal |
| **supplements and additives** | added in small amounts | mineral and vitamin premixes, salt, bone meal, oyster shell; additives such as drugs to prevent coccidiosis |

## Rations

:::callout{type=definition}
- A **ration** is the amount of feed given to an animal in 24 hours.
- A **balanced ration** supplies **all** the nutrients the animal needs, in the **right proportions** and amounts, for its age and purpose.
- A **maintenance ration** keeps an animal alive and healthy without gain or production.
- A **production ration** is extra feed for growth, pregnancy, milk, eggs, fattening or work.
:::

Feeds for poultry are made for each stage: **chick (starter) mash**, **grower mash**, **broiler finisher**, and **layers' mash** (with extra calcium).

## Mixing a feed: the Pearson square

The Pearson square finds the proportions of **two** feeds needed to reach a target nutrient level (usually crude protein). The target must lie **between** the two feeds' values.

![Pearson square for an 18% protein ration from maize (9%) and groundnut cake (45%): subtracting diagonally gives 27 parts maize and 9 parts groundnut cake, which is 75% maize and 25% groundnut cake](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/pearson-square.svg)

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A farmer wants 100 kg of feed with 18% crude protein (CP), using maize (9% CP) and groundnut cake (45% CP). How much of each?**

1. Write 18 in the centre, 9 (maize) and 45 (groundnut cake) at the left corners.
2. Subtract **diagonally**, ignoring signs: $45 - 18 = 27$ parts **maize**; $18 - 9 = 9$ parts **groundnut cake**.
3. Total parts $= 27 + 9 = 36$.
4. Maize $= \dfrac{27}{36} \times 100 = 75$ kg; groundnut cake $= \dfrac{9}{36} \times 100 = 25$ kg.

**Check:** $0.75 \times 9 + 0.25 \times 45 = 6.75 + 11.25 = 18\%$ ✓
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Putting the answer on the wrong feed.** The number on each row of the square belongs to the feed **on that row** (top left with top right). A quick test: the **larger share** always goes to the feed whose value is **closer to the target**. Maize (9%) is 9 away from 18%, but groundnut cake (45%) is 27 away, so maize gets the larger share (27 parts). Always check the final mix with a calculation.
:::

## Pasture and forage for ruminants

**Pasture** is land covered with grasses and legumes grazed by animals.

- **Natural pasture**: whatever grows naturally; often low in quality.
- **Improved (sown) pasture**: planted grasses such as **guinea grass** (*Panicum maximum*), **elephant grass** (*Pennisetum purpureum*) and *Brachiaria*, mixed with **legumes** such as *Stylosanthes*, *Centrosema* and *Pueraria*, which add protein and fix nitrogen.

### Grazing systems

| System | Description |
|---|---|
| **continuous grazing** | animals graze the same area all the time; simple but leads to overgrazing |
| **rotational grazing** | pasture divided into **paddocks** grazed in turn, letting each recover |
| **strip grazing** | a movable fence gives animals a fresh strip each day |
| **zero grazing (cut and carry)** | forage is cut and brought to housed animals |
| **tethering** | animals tied on a rope and moved during the day (common for village goats and sheep) |

**Carrying capacity** is the number of animals a pasture can support without being damaged.

### Conserving forage for the dry season

- **Hay**: grass or legumes cut at a young, leafy stage, **dried** to about 15% moisture, and stored under cover.
- **Silage**: green forage chopped and packed tightly in an **airtight** pit, trench or bag, where bacteria ferment it to acid that preserves it.
- Storing **crop residues**: groundnut haulms, cowpea vines, maize stover, rice straw.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Feeding broilers is mostly a feed bill.** A small broiler farm near Bo buys commercial starter and finisher feed. Its owner records that each bird eats about **4 kg** of feed to reach about **2.2 kg** live weight, so feed is the biggest cost. Buying feed in bulk with neighbours, keeping it **dry and away from rats**, and preventing spillage from feeders all raise the profit directly.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. State the functions of protein and calcium in animal feed."}
Protein: growth, repair, milk and egg production, enzymes. Calcium: bones and eggshells (and muscle and nerve function).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Distinguish between roughages and concentrates, with two examples of each."}
**Roughages** are bulky and high in fibre (grass, hay, silage, straw). **Concentrates** are low in fibre and high in energy or protein (maize, groundnut cake, fish meal).
:::

:::reveal{title="3. What is a balanced ration?"}
A ration that supplies all the nutrients an animal needs in the right proportions and amounts for its age and purpose.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Using the Pearson square, mix a 16% protein feed from cassava (2% CP) and soya bean meal (44% CP). Find the percentage of each."}
Cassava: $44 - 16 = 28$ parts; soya: $16 - 2 = 14$ parts; total 42. Cassava $= \dfrac{28}{42} = 66.7\%$; soya $= \dfrac{14}{42} = 33.3\%$. Check: $0.667 \times 2 + 0.333 \times 44 \approx 16\%$ ✓
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Distinguish between hay and silage."}
**Hay** is forage dried to about 15% moisture and stored dry. **Silage** is green forage packed airtight and preserved by fermentation (acid).
:::

## Key points

- Nutrients: **carbohydrates, fats, proteins, minerals, vitamins, water**; water must always be available.
- Feedstuffs: **roughages** (fibre), **concentrates** (energy or protein), **supplements**.
- A **balanced ration** meets all needs; **maintenance** plus **production** rations.
- **Pearson square**: subtract diagonally; each result belongs to the feed on its row; check the answer.
- Pasture: improved grasses and legumes; **rotational grazing**; conserve forage as **hay** and **silage**.
