Water alone cannot wash palm-oil stains out of a school shirt: oil and water do not mix. Soap works because each soap particle has **one end that dissolves in water and another that dissolves in grease**. This lesson explains how soaps and detergents clean, why soap forms scum in hard water, and how detergents differ from soap.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the structure of a soap anion and explain how soap removes grease
- explain why soap forms scum in hard water, with an equation
- describe soapless detergents and how they are made
- compare soaps and detergents
- explain the environmental effects of detergents

## Structure of a soap

Soap is the sodium (or potassium) salt of a long-chain fatty acid, such as **sodium stearate**, C₁₇H₃₅COO⁻Na⁺. In water it gives sodium ions and **soap anions**. Each anion has two parts:

| Part | Structure | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **Tail** | Long hydrocarbon chain, C₁₇H₃₅– | **Hydrophobic** (water-hating); dissolves in grease and oil |
| **Head** | Ionic carboxylate group, –COO⁻ | **Hydrophilic** (water-loving); dissolves in water |

## How soap cleans

![A soap anion with a hydrophobic tail and hydrophilic head; tails enter a grease spot on cloth; the grease is lifted off as a droplet surrounded by negative heads](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/soap-action.svg)

*Figure 1. The cleansing action of soap. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

1. Soap **lowers the surface tension** of water, so the water **wets** the cloth and spreads into the fabric.
2. The **hydrophobic tails** of the soap anions **dissolve in the grease**; the **ionic heads** stay in the water.
3. **Rubbing, scrubbing or stirring** breaks the grease into small droplets, each surrounded by soap anions with their negative heads outwards (a **micelle**).
4. The droplets **repel each other** (all carry negative charges), so they stay suspended as an **emulsion** and do not settle back on the cloth.
5. **Rinsing** washes the droplets, and the dirt held in them, away.

## Soap and hard water

**Hard water** contains dissolved **calcium and magnesium ions** (see the Water topic). These react with soap anions to form an insoluble, greyish **scum** (calcium or magnesium stearate):

$$2\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COO}^-\text{(aq)} + \text{Ca}^{2+}\text{(aq)} \rightarrow (\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COO})_2\text{Ca(s)}$$

- Soap is **wasted**: no lather forms until all the Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions have been precipitated.
- Scum sticks to clothes, baths and hair.

## Soapless detergents

:::callout{type=definition}
A **soapless (synthetic) detergent** is a cleaning agent made from **petroleum**, with a long hydrocarbon tail and an ionic head, usually a **sulphonate (–SO₃⁻)** or **sulphate (–OSO₃⁻)** group.
:::

A typical detergent is **sodium alkylbenzene sulphonate**, R–C₆H₄–SO₃⁻Na⁺. It is made by:

1. reacting a long-chain alkylbenzene (from petroleum) with **concentrated sulphuric acid** (sulphonation)
2. **neutralising** the sulphonic acid with **sodium hydroxide**

Detergents clean in the **same way** as soap (hydrophobic tail, hydrophilic head). Their calcium and magnesium salts are **soluble**, so they **do not form scum** in hard water.

Washing powders also contain **builders** (phosphates or zeolites, to soften water), **bleaches**, **optical brighteners**, **enzymes** (to digest protein stains such as blood and egg) and **perfumes**.

## Soaps compared with detergents

| Feature | Soap | Soapless detergent |
|---|---|---|
| **Raw materials** | Fats and oils (renewable) + alkali | Petroleum (non-renewable) + conc. H₂SO₄ + NaOH |
| **Head group** | Carboxylate, –COO⁻ | Sulphonate, –SO₃⁻, or sulphate |
| **In hard water** | Forms **scum**; lathers poorly | **No scum**; lathers well |
| **In acidic water** | Precipitates the fatty acid; works poorly | Works well |
| **Biodegradability** | Readily **biodegradable** | Older types not biodegradable; modern ones mostly are |
| **Effect on skin** | Mild | Can be harsh; may remove skin oils |

## Detergents and the environment

- **Foam:** early detergents with **branched** hydrocarbon chains were **not biodegradable** and caused thick foam on rivers. Modern detergents use **straight chains**, which bacteria break down.
- **Eutrophication:** **phosphates** from washing powders fertilise rivers and lakes, causing rapid growth of **algae**. When the algae die and decay, bacteria use up the dissolved **oxygen**, and fish die.
- Detergents are toxic to some aquatic life even at low concentrations.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Explaining an observation.** A student shakes soap solution with water from two sources. Sample A lathers at once; sample B forms a grey scum and lathers only after much more soap is added. A detergent lathers well with both. Explain.

- Sample B is **hard water**: its Ca²⁺ or Mg²⁺ ions precipitate soap anions as insoluble **scum**, using up soap before a lather can form.
- Sample A is **soft water**.
- The **detergent's** calcium and magnesium salts are **soluble**, so it does not form scum and lathers in both.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many people in Sierra Leone wash clothes in **streams and rivers**. Washing powder and detergent foam flowing straight into the water adds **phosphates** and surfactants close to where fish breed and where others draw drinking water. Washing on the bank and pouring used water onto soil, away from the stream, lets the soil and its bacteria break the detergent down.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
It is not the sodium ion that cleans: the **anion** does the work. Its **tail** goes into the **grease**, not into the water.
:::

## Key points

- A soap anion has a **hydrophobic hydrocarbon tail** (dissolves in grease) and a **hydrophilic –COO⁻ head** (dissolves in water).
- Soap **lowers surface tension**, surrounds grease droplets (**micelles**), and the **repelling negative heads** keep them suspended for rinsing.
- In **hard water**, Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ form insoluble **scum**, wasting soap.
- **Soapless detergents** (alkylbenzene sulphonates, from petroleum) work the same way but **do not form scum**.
- Detergent problems: **foam** (non-biodegradable) and **eutrophication** (phosphates).

## Quick check

1. Which part of a soap anion dissolves in grease?
2. Write an equation for the formation of scum from stearate ions and magnesium ions.
3. Give one advantage of soapless detergents over soap.
4. How do phosphates in detergents harm rivers?

**Answers:**

1. The long hydrocarbon tail (hydrophobic part).
2. 2C₁₇H₃₅COO⁻ + Mg²⁺ → (C₁₇H₃₅COO)₂Mg.
3. They do not form scum in hard water (they also work in acidic water).
4. They cause eutrophication: algae grow rapidly, then die and decay; bacteria use up the dissolved oxygen, killing fish.
