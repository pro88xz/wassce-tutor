A poultry farmer in Waterloo buys two acres outright, fences them, builds pens and a borehole, and plans to leave the farm to her children. Because the land is hers for good, every leone she invests stays with her family. This is **freehold** ownership, the strongest form of land tenure a farmer can have.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define freehold land ownership
- describe how freehold land is obtained and proved
- state the advantages and disadvantages of freehold ownership for farming
- explain where freehold applies in Sierra Leone

## What is freehold?

:::callout{type=definition}
**Freehold** is a land tenure system in which a person or organisation owns land **outright and permanently**, with the right to use, sell, lease, mortgage or pass it on.
:::

:::callout{type=definition}
A **title deed** is the legal document that proves who owns a piece of land and describes its size and boundaries.
:::

A freehold owner does not pay rent to anyone for the land, and the ownership does not end after a number of years. The owner must still obey the law: planning rules, taxes on land, and respect for neighbours' boundaries.

## How freehold land is obtained

1. **Outright purchase**: buying land from its owner and registering the sale.
2. **Inheritance**: receiving freehold land from parents under a will or by law.
3. **Gift**: an owner gives the land freely, and the transfer is registered.
4. **Grant** by the government in some cases.

A buyer should check the seller's title deed, have the land **surveyed** so the boundaries are clear, and register the **conveyance** (the document that transfers ownership) at the land registry.

## Advantages of freehold ownership

1. **Security of tenure**: the land cannot be taken back, so the owner is willing to make long-term improvements.
2. **Investment**: the owner can plant tree crops, build stores and pens, and install irrigation.
3. **Collateral**: the land can be **mortgaged** to a bank to obtain a loan.
4. **Freedom of use**: the owner decides what to grow or rear and how.
5. **Transfer**: the land can be sold, leased out or left to children.
6. **Fewer disputes**, when the land is properly surveyed and registered.

## Disadvantages of freehold ownership

1. **Cost**: buying land needs a lot of money, which most young and small-scale farmers do not have.
2. **Unequal access**: wealthy people can buy up large areas while the poor have none.
3. **Land speculation**: some buyers hold land idle, waiting for prices to rise, instead of farming it.
4. **Fragmentation**: when an owner dies, the land may be divided among many heirs into small plots.
5. **Fraud**: the same land may be sold to more than one buyer where records are poor.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Freehold does not mean the owner can do anything at all. Owners must still obey the law on planning, the environment and taxes. What makes freehold different is that ownership is **permanent** and does not depend on paying rent to a landlord.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
In Sierra Leone, freehold applies mainly in the **Western Area**, where land around Freetown, Waterloo and the peninsula can be bought and sold and transfers are recorded by the Ministry of Lands. Disputes over plots sold more than once are a common problem there, which is why buyers are advised to check the records and have the land surveyed. In the provinces, land is held under customary tenure and outsiders obtain it by lease, not freehold purchase.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Why does freehold encourage tree crops?** A farmer plants cashew trees that will not bear fruit for three years and will keep bearing for decades. Under freehold, the farmer is sure to keep the land and enjoy every future harvest, and can even borrow against the land to pay for seedlings. A tenant on a one-year rent would not plant them, because the benefit would go to the landlord.
:::

## Key points

- **Freehold** is permanent, outright ownership of land with rights to use, sell, lease, mortgage and bequeath.
- It is obtained by **purchase**, **inheritance**, **gift** or **grant**, and proved by a **title deed**.
- Advantages: **security**, long-term **investment**, **collateral** for loans, freedom of use and transfer.
- Disadvantages: **high cost**, unequal access, speculation, fragmentation and fraud.
- In Sierra Leone, freehold applies mainly in the **Western Area**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **define freehold** and to **state its advantages and disadvantages**, often by comparing it with **leasehold** or **communal tenure**. Link each advantage to its effect on farming, for example "security of tenure → owner invests in tree crops and soil conservation".
:::
