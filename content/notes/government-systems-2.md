Britain has a queen or king but is governed by an elected Parliament. Saudi Arabia has a king who rules. Sierra Leone has no monarch at all: it is a **republic** with an elected President. This lesson classifies the many forms of rule, starting with Aristotle's famous scheme, then explains monarchy, republic, aristocracy, oligarchy, theocracy, dictatorship and military rule.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain Aristotle's classification of governments
- distinguish absolute and constitutional monarchy, and monarchy and republic
- explain aristocracy, oligarchy, plutocracy, gerontocracy and theocracy
- describe dictatorship and military rule, with their features

## Aristotle's classification

![A grid: rule by one (monarchy or tyranny), by a few (aristocracy or oligarchy) and by the many (polity or democracy), for the good of all or for the rulers' own good](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/aristotle-classification.svg)

*Figure 1. Aristotle classified governments by who rules and in whose interest. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

The Greek philosopher **Aristotle** (4th century BC) classified governments by **number of rulers** and by **whose interest they serve**. Each good form has a perverted (corrupt) form when rulers serve themselves.

## Monarchy

:::callout{type=definition}
A **monarchy** is a government headed by a king, queen or emperor who usually inherits the throne and rules for life.
:::

| Absolute monarchy | Constitutional (limited) monarchy |
|---|---|
| The monarch holds real power: makes laws, governs and judges | The monarch is a ceremonial head of state; real power lies with elected officials |
| No constitution limits the monarch | A constitution and parliament limit the monarch |
| Examples: Saudi Arabia, Eswatini | Examples: Britain, Japan, Sweden |

**Merits of constitutional monarchy:** a symbol of national unity above party politics; continuity and stability; tradition. **Demerits of monarchy:** heredity may produce an incapable ruler; absolute monarchy can become tyranny; it is costly and undemocratic.

## Republic

:::callout{type=definition}
A **republic** is a state in which the head of state is **elected** (directly or indirectly) for a fixed term, rather than inheriting the office.
:::

A republic may be presidential (Sierra Leone, USA) or parliamentary (India, Germany, where an elected president is mainly ceremonial). Sierra Leone became a republic on **19 April 1971**, replacing the British monarch as head of state.

## Other forms of rule

| Form | Rule by | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Aristocracy** | A few of the best, wisest or noblest, ruling for the common good | Often hereditary nobility |
| **Oligarchy** | A small group ruling in its own interest | Aristotle's corrupt form of aristocracy |
| **Plutocracy** | The wealthy | Power follows money |
| **Gerontocracy** | The elders | Common in many traditional African societies, where councils of elders advised chiefs |
| **Theocracy** | Religious leaders, in the name of God or religious law | Iran since 1979; the Vatican |
| **Dictatorship (autocracy)** | One person or a small group with unlimited power, not accountable to the people | Often comes from a coup or the abuse of elected office |
| **Military rule** | The armed forces, after a coup d'état | Governs by decree; suspends the constitution and parliament |

## Dictatorship and military rule

**Features of dictatorship:** concentration of power in one person or party; no free elections or rigged elections; suppression of opposition and the press; abuse of human rights; no rule of law; often a personality cult.

**Features of military rule:**

1. Comes to power through a **coup d'état**.
2. **Suspends** the constitution (or parts of it) and **dissolves** the legislature.
3. Rules by **decrees** issued by a ruling council.
4. **Bans** political parties and political activity.
5. Uses military tribunals and limits fundamental rights.
6. Usually promises to **hand over** to a civilian government after a transition.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone has experienced several of these forms: **constitutional monarchy** (1961–1971, with the British monarch as head of state), a **republic** from 1971, a **one-party state** under the 1978 Constitution, and **military rule** under the **NRC (1967–1968)**, the **NPRC (1992–1996)** and the **AFRC (1997–1998)**. In traditional chiefdoms, councils of elders reflect elements of **gerontocracy**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A country with a king is not always a monarchy in which the king rules. In a **constitutional monarchy** such as Britain, the monarch "reigns but does not rule": elected politicians govern.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Aristocracy**: rule by a few of the best, wisest or noblest, for the common good.
- **Oligarchy**: rule by a small group in its own interest.
- **Theocracy**: rule by religious leaders, in the name of God or religious law.
- **Gerontocracy**: rule by the elders.
- **Dictatorship** (autocracy): rule by one person or a small group with unlimited power, not accountable to the people.
- **Constitutional monarchy**: a monarchy in which the monarch is ceremonial and real power lies with elected leaders.
:::

## Key points

- Aristotle: rule by **one, few or many**, for the **common good** (monarchy, aristocracy, polity) or **self-interest** (tyranny, oligarchy, democracy as mob rule).
- **Absolute monarchy** gives real power to the monarch; **constitutional monarchy** keeps the monarch ceremonial.
- A **republic** has an **elected head of state**; Sierra Leone became one on **19 April 1971**.
- Other forms: **oligarchy, plutocracy, gerontocracy, theocracy, dictatorship and military rule**.
- Military regimes **suspend the constitution, rule by decree and ban parties**.

## Quick check

1. What is Aristotle's perverted form of aristocracy?
2. Distinguish a monarchy from a republic.
3. What is a theocracy?
4. State two features of military rule.

**Answers:**

1. Oligarchy.
2. In a monarchy the head of state inherits the office, usually for life; in a republic the head of state is elected for a fixed term.
3. Government by religious leaders in the name of God or according to religious law.
4. Any two: it comes to power by a coup; it suspends the constitution and dissolves the legislature; it rules by decree; it bans political parties; it restricts fundamental rights; it usually promises a return to civilian rule.
