A report gives a clear, factual account of an event, a situation or an investigation to someone who needs the information: a principal, a council, a club, a newspaper. Unlike a story, a report is **objective**: it states what happened or what was found, in a logical order, and usually ends with **recommendations**. WASSCE questions often read *"As the secretary of…, write a report to… on…"*. This lesson shows you the format, the language and a complete model report.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- set out a report with a title, introduction, findings, recommendations, conclusion and signature block
- write in a clear, impersonal, factual style
- organise findings logically, using sub-headings where helpful
- write practical recommendations
- tell a report from a story and from an article

## When the question asks for a report

| You are asked to… | For example |
|---|---|
| report on an **event** | a fire, an accident, an excursion, an inter-house sports day |
| report on a **situation** | the condition of school toilets, the problem of lateness, the state of a road |
| report on an **investigation** or a **visit** | a survey of students' reading habits, a visit to a health centre |

## The format

:::callout{type=definition}
| Part | What it contains |
|---|---|
| **Title** | a clear heading saying what the report is about |
| **Introduction** | who asked for the report, its purpose, and how the information was gathered |
| **Findings** (the body) | what happened or what was found, in logical order; sub-headings may be used |
| **Recommendations** | practical steps to solve the problem or improve the situation |
| **Conclusion** | a brief closing statement |
| **Signature block** | the writer's name, position (and signature), and the date |
:::

:::callout{type=tip}
Read the question for **who** the report is for and **who** you are. Both appear in your report: *"This report was requested by the Principal…"* and, at the end, *"Mariama Kamara, Secretary, Health Club"*.
:::

## The language of reports

| Feature | Example |
|---|---|
| **Objective**: facts, not feelings | *Four of the six toilets were out of use.* (not *"The toilets were disgusting!"*) |
| **Past tense** for what was found | *The committee **visited** the toilets on 3rd March.* |
| **Present tense** for the current situation | *The water tank **is** empty for most of the day.* |
| **Formal vocabulary** | *inspected, observed, it was noted that, insufficient, as a result* |
| **Passive voice** where the doer doesn't matter | *The toilets **were inspected** on Monday.* |
| **Recommendations** with *should* | *The tank **should** be repaired as a matter of urgency.* |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Writing a report like a story.** *"It was a bright sunny morning and we were all excited as we set out…"* belongs in a narrative. A report opens with its **purpose** and gives facts in a logical order.
:::

## A complete model report

**Question:** *As the secretary of your school's Health Club, write a report to the Principal on the state of sanitation in the school, with recommendations for improvement.*

::::model{title="Sanitation in the school" label="Model report"}
**REPORT ON THE STATE OF SANITATION AT GOVERNMENT SECONDARY SCHOOL, BO**

**Introduction**

This report was requested by the Principal at the staff and prefects' meeting of 24th February, 2026, following complaints from students and parents about the cleanliness of the school compound. Its purpose is to describe the present state of sanitation in the school and to recommend improvements. Members of the Health Club inspected the toilets, the water supply, the compound and the canteen area between 2nd and 6th March, 2026, and spoke to students, cleaners and the canteen operators.

**Findings**

*Toilets.* The school has six toilets for over twelve hundred students. At the time of the inspection, four of them were out of use: two had broken doors and two were blocked. The remaining two were in constant use and were not cleaned between the morning and the end of the school day. Many students, especially girls, reported that they avoid using the toilets altogether.

*Water supply.* The water tank that serves the toilets and the hand-washing station has a leaking valve and is empty for most of the day. The hand-washing station by the canteen had no soap on any of the five days of the inspection.

*The compound.* There are only three rubbish bins for the whole compound, and they are emptied irregularly. As a result, plastic bags and food wrappers collect behind the classroom blocks and block the gutter beside the football field, which floods after heavy rain.

*The canteen.* Food is sold from open tables without covers, and flies were observed on cooked food on three of the five days.

*Students' habits.* Many students drop wrappers and water sachets on the ground even where bins are available, and few wash their hands after using the toilets. Several students told the committee that they had never been taught why hand-washing matters.

**Recommendations**

The Health Club recommends that:

1. the four broken toilets should be repaired as a matter of urgency, and a cleaning rota for every break should be agreed with the cleaners;
2. the leaking valve on the water tank should be replaced, and soap should be supplied daily to the hand-washing stations;
3. at least ten additional bins should be placed around the compound and emptied every afternoon, and a "Clean Compound" competition between classes should be introduced;
4. the canteen operators should be required to cover all food, and a short food-hygiene talk should be organised for them by the Health Club and the school nurse;
5. a health talk on hand-washing and litter should be given at assembly once a month, led by members of the Health Club.

**Conclusion**

The present state of sanitation endangers the health of students and staff and affects their concentration and attendance. Most of the improvements recommended are inexpensive, and the members of the Health Club are ready to help carry them out.

:::right
*(signature)*\
**Mariama Kamara**\
Secretary, Health Club\
9th March, 2026.
:::
::::

### Why it scores

- **Format**: title, labelled sections (*Introduction, Findings, Recommendations, Conclusion*), signature block with name, position and date.
- **Introduction**: who requested it, its purpose, and how the information was gathered.
- **Findings**: organised by area under sub-headings, factual and specific (*six toilets, four out of use*), with no emotional language.
- **Recommendations**: numbered, practical, each linked to a finding.
- **Language**: past tense for the inspection, present tense for current conditions, formal and impersonal.
- **Length**: about 490 words, with no padding.

## Report, article or story?

| | Report | Article | Story |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose | inform someone who needs the facts | inform and persuade the public | entertain |
| Tone | objective, impersonal | lively, personal opinion allowed | personal, emotional |
| Structure | introduction, findings, recommendations, conclusion | title, introduction, points, conclusion | beginning, climax, ending |
| Ending | name, position, date | writer's name | the story's resolution |

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Rewrite objectively: 'The canteen was absolutely filthy and the food was horrible!'"}
*The canteen tables were not covered, and flies were observed on the cooked food.*
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Write the introduction of a report, to the Principal, on an excursion your class made to the Bumbuna dam."}
*This report was requested by the Principal following the Form Five Geography excursion to the Bumbuna hydroelectric dam on 12th April, 2026. Its purpose is to describe the visit and what the students learnt, and to make recommendations for future excursions. Information was gathered from the students' notes, the guide's talk and the two teachers who accompanied the class.*
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Turn this finding into a recommendation: 'Many students arrive late because the school bus leaves Kissy at 7:30.'"}
*The school bus **should** leave Kissy at 7:00 so that students arrive before the morning assembly.*
:::

## Key points

- A report is **factual and objective**, written for someone who needs the information.
- Format: **title, introduction, findings, recommendations, conclusion, signature block** (name, position, date).
- Use **past tense** for what was found, **present** for the current situation, **should** for recommendations.
- Organise findings **logically**, with **sub-headings** where they help.
- Make recommendations **practical** and linked to the findings.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Examiners check the **report format** under Organisation, reward **clear, specific, objective content**, and expect **formal, impersonal expression**. The commonest faults are writing the report as a story, omitting recommendations when the question asks for them, and ending without a name, position and date.
:::
