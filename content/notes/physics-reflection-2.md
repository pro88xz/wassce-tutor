Curve a mirror inwards and it can magnify your face or focus sunlight to a point hot enough to burn paper. Curve it outwards and it shows a wide view of the road behind. These are concave and convex mirrors.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define the terms used with curved mirrors
- draw ray diagrams for concave and convex mirrors
- describe the images for different object positions
- use the mirror formula and magnification, with the "real is positive" sign convention

## Terms used with curved mirrors

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Pole (P)** | the centre of the mirror's surface |
| **Centre of curvature (C)** | the centre of the sphere of which the mirror is a part |
| **Radius of curvature (r)** | the distance from P to C |
| **Principal axis** | the line through C and P |
| **Principal focus (F)** | for a concave mirror, the point where rays parallel to the axis **meet** after reflection; for a convex mirror, the point they **appear to come from** |
| **Focal length (f)** | the distance PF; $f = \dfrac{r}{2}$ |

A **concave** (converging) mirror has a **real** focus in front of it. A **convex** (diverging) mirror has a **virtual** focus behind it.

## Rules for ray diagrams

1. A ray **parallel to the axis** reflects **through F** (concave) or as if from F (convex).
2. A ray **through F** (or heading towards F for a convex mirror) reflects **parallel to the axis**.
3. A ray **through C** reflects **back along itself**.

Two rays are enough to locate an image.

## Images formed by a concave mirror

| Object position | Image position | Nature of image | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| At infinity | at F | real, inverted, point-sized | solar furnace, telescope |
| Beyond C | between F and C | real, inverted, **diminished** | |
| At C | at C | real, inverted, **same size** | |
| Between C and F | beyond C | real, inverted, **magnified** | |
| At F | at infinity | — (parallel reflected beam) | torch, car headlamp reflector |
| Between F and P | behind the mirror | **virtual, upright, magnified** | shaving or make-up mirror, dentist's mirror |

### Convex mirror

For **any** object position, the image in a convex mirror is **virtual, upright and diminished**, between P and F behind the mirror. It gives a **wide field of view**, so convex mirrors are used as **driving (side) mirrors** and at shop corners and dangerous road bends.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Vehicle side mirrors in Sierra Leone often carry the warning "objects in mirror are closer than they appear". The convex mirror shrinks the image so it can show a wider stretch of road.
:::

## The mirror formula

$$\dfrac{1}{u} + \dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f} \qquad m = \dfrac{v}{u} = \dfrac{\text{image height}}{\text{object height}}$$

**Sign convention ("real is positive"):** distances to **real** objects and images are **positive**; distances to **virtual** images are **negative**. A **concave** mirror has a **positive** focal length; a **convex** mirror a **negative** one.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**An object is 30 cm from a concave mirror of focal length 10 cm. Find the image position, its nature and the magnification.**

$$\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{10} - \dfrac{1}{30} = \dfrac{2}{30} \Rightarrow v = 15\ \text{cm}$$

$v$ is positive, so the image is **real** (and inverted), 15 cm in front of the mirror. $m = \dfrac{15}{30} = 0.5$: half the size of the object.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A man holds a shaving mirror (concave, f = 25 cm) 15 cm from his face. Find the position of the image and the magnification.**

$$\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{25} - \dfrac{1}{15} = \dfrac{3 - 5}{75} = -\dfrac{2}{75} \Rightarrow v = -37.5\ \text{cm}$$

The negative sign shows a **virtual** image, 37.5 cm **behind** the mirror. $m = \dfrac{37.5}{15} = 2.5$: upright and 2.5 times larger.
:::

## Key points

- $f = r/2$; concave mirrors converge (real focus), convex mirrors diverge (virtual focus).
- Ray rules: parallel → through F; through F → parallel; through C → back on itself.
- Concave: real images when the object is beyond F; virtual magnified image when inside F.
- Convex: always virtual, upright, diminished — wide field of view.
- $\dfrac{1}{u} + \dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f}$, $m = v/u$; virtual distances negative.

## Quick check

1. What kind of mirror is used as a driving mirror, and why?
2. Where must an object be placed in front of a concave mirror to get a virtual, magnified image?
3. A concave mirror has radius of curvature 40 cm. What is its focal length?

**Answers:** 1. Convex, because it gives an upright image with a wide field of view. 2. Between the focus F and the mirror. 3. 20 cm.
