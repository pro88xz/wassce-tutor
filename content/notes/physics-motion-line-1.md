You sit still in a poda-poda while it races along Wilkinson Road. Are you moving? Relative to the vehicle, no; relative to the road, yes. Physics describes motion carefully, with five quantities you must never mix up: distance, displacement, speed, velocity and acceleration.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe motion relative to a reference point, using a sign for direction
- distinguish distance from displacement and speed from velocity
- calculate average speed, average velocity and acceleration
- convert between km h⁻¹ and m s⁻¹

## Describing motion

A body is in **motion** when its position changes with time relative to a chosen **reference point**. For motion along a straight line, choose one direction as **positive** (say east). Then:

- $+12$ m means 12 m east of the reference point;
- $-5$ m means 5 m west of it.

The **sign** tells you the direction; the **number** tells you the size.

## Distance and displacement

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Distance** is the total length of the path travelled. It is a **scalar**: it has size only.
- **Displacement** is the change in position, measured in a straight line from start to finish **in a stated direction**. It is a **vector**.
:::

![A straight east-west road: a student walks 300 m east from the start to a shop, then 100 m west to the finish. The distance is 400 m; the displacement is 200 m east](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/displacement-line.svg)

*Figure 1. Distance and displacement on a straight road. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A student walks 300 m east to a shop, then 100 m west. Find the distance travelled and the displacement.**

Distance $= 300 + 100 = 400$ m.

Taking east as positive: displacement $= +300 - 100 = +200$ m, that is **200 m east** of the start.
:::

Distance can never be negative. Displacement can be positive, negative or zero, and its size can never be larger than the distance.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A runner completes one 400 m lap of the National Stadium track and stops where she started. Find the distance and the displacement.**

Distance $= 400$ m. She ends where she began, so displacement $= 0$.

Do not say she "travelled 0 m": zero is the displacement, not the distance.
:::

## Speed and velocity

$$\text{average speed} = \dfrac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}} \qquad \text{average velocity} = \dfrac{\text{displacement}}{\text{time taken}}$$

Speed is a scalar; velocity is a vector, so it carries a direction (or a sign). Both are measured in **m s⁻¹**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A cyclist rides 600 m east in 120 s, then 200 m west in 40 s. Find her average speed and average velocity for the whole journey.**

Total distance $= 600 + 200 = 800$ m; total time $= 120 + 40 = 160$ s.

$$\text{average speed} = \dfrac{800}{160} = 5.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}$$

Displacement $= +600 - 200 = +400$ m (east).

$$\text{average velocity} = \dfrac{400}{160} = 2.5\ \text{m s}^{-1}\ \text{east}$$

The two answers differ because she turned back.
:::

### Converting km h⁻¹ and m s⁻¹

$$1\ \text{km h}^{-1} = \dfrac{1000\ \text{m}}{3600\ \text{s}} = \dfrac{5}{18}\ \text{m s}^{-1}$$

- km h⁻¹ → m s⁻¹: multiply by $\dfrac{5}{18}$. So $72\ \text{km h}^{-1} = 72 \times \dfrac{5}{18} = 20\ \text{m s}^{-1}$.
- m s⁻¹ → km h⁻¹: multiply by $\dfrac{18}{5}$. So $15\ \text{m s}^{-1} = 54\ \text{km h}^{-1}$.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A vehicle covers 1.8 km in 3 minutes. Find its average speed in m s⁻¹ and in km h⁻¹.**

$$v = \dfrac{1800\ \text{m}}{180\ \text{s}} = 10\ \text{m s}^{-1} = 10 \times \dfrac{18}{5} = 36\ \text{km h}^{-1}$$
:::

### Instantaneous speed

Average speed describes a whole journey. **Instantaneous speed** is the speed at one moment: the number on a car's speedometer. On a displacement–time graph it is the gradient of the tangent at that instant (see the next lesson).

## Acceleration

:::callout{type=definition}
**Acceleration** is the rate of change of velocity:

$$a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$$

where $u$ is the initial velocity, $v$ the final velocity and $t$ the time taken. Its unit is **m s⁻²**. Acceleration is a vector.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A bus speeds up from 5 m s⁻¹ to 17 m s⁻¹ in 6 s. Find its acceleration.**

$$a = \dfrac{17 - 5}{6} = 2.0\ \text{m s}^{-2}$$
:::

When a body slows down, its acceleration is opposite to its velocity. This is called **deceleration** or **retardation**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A car moving east at 20 m s⁻¹ stops uniformly in 5 s. Taking east as positive, find its acceleration.**

$$a = \dfrac{0 - 20}{5} = -4.0\ \text{m s}^{-2}$$

The minus sign means the acceleration points west, against the motion. The car has a **deceleration** of 4.0 m s⁻².
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
"Negative acceleration" does not always mean slowing down. A body moving **west** (negative) that speeds up also has a negative acceleration. Decide the sign from your chosen positive direction, and say in words whether the body is speeding up or slowing down.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Okada riders in Bo and Makeni know acceleration by feel. Pulling away from a junction, a bike might reach 12 m s⁻¹ (about 43 km h⁻¹) in 6 s, an acceleration of 2 m s⁻². Braking hard from the same speed to rest in 2 s is a deceleration of 6 m s⁻², which is why passengers lurch forward.
:::

## Key points

- Choose a positive direction; signs show direction along a line.
- Distance (scalar) is the path length; displacement (vector) is the straight-line change in position, with direction.
- Average speed = total distance ÷ time; average velocity = displacement ÷ time.
- km h⁻¹ × 5/18 = m s⁻¹; m s⁻¹ × 18/5 = km h⁻¹.
- $a = (v - u)/t$, in m s⁻²; deceleration is acceleration opposite to the motion.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
WASSCE asks you to **define** distance, displacement, speed, velocity and acceleration and to **classify them as scalars or vectors**. Calculations involve **journeys with a change of direction** (where average speed and average velocity differ), **unit conversion** between km h⁻¹ and m s⁻¹, and **acceleration or deceleration** from $a = (v - u)/t$. Always state the direction of a vector answer.
:::

## Quick check

1. A student walks 250 m north and then 90 m south. Find the distance and the displacement.
2. Convert 72 km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹.
3. A cyclist travels 500 m east in 100 s, then 200 m west in 50 s. Find the average speed and the average velocity.
4. A car's velocity rises from 8 m s⁻¹ to 26 m s⁻¹ in 6 s. Find its acceleration.
5. Is speed a scalar or a vector? Is velocity?

**Answers:** 1. Distance 340 m; displacement 160 m north. 2. 20 m s⁻¹. 3. Speed $= 700 \div 150 = 4.67$ m s⁻¹; velocity $= 300 \div 150 = 2.0$ m s⁻¹ east. 4. $(26 - 8) \div 6 = 3.0$ m s⁻². 5. Speed is a scalar; velocity is a vector.
