Pass a current through salt water and bubbles of gas appear at the wires. Pass it through copper sulphate and copper coats the negative plate. Electrolysis — chemical change caused by electric current — is used to plate cutlery, refine metals and charge car batteries.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define electrolysis, electrolyte, electrodes, anode and cathode
- explain conduction in electrolytes by the movement of ions
- describe the electrolysis of copper(II) sulphate and of acidified water
- distinguish conductors, electrolytes and non-electrolytes

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Electrolysis** is the **chemical decomposition** of a substance (in solution or molten) by the passage of an electric current.
- An **electrolyte** is a substance that **conducts electricity when molten or in solution** and is **decomposed** by it (acids, bases, salts in water).
- **Electrodes** are the conductors through which current enters and leaves the electrolyte. The **anode** is connected to the **positive** terminal; the **cathode** to the **negative** terminal.
- A **voltameter** is the vessel in which electrolysis takes place.
:::

Substances that do not conduct when dissolved or molten (sugar solution, pure water, paraffin) are **non-electrolytes**.

## How electrolytes conduct

In an electrolyte the current is carried by **ions** (charged atoms or groups of atoms), not by electrons:

- **positive ions (cations)** — such as H⁺, Cu²⁺, Na⁺ — move to the **cathode** (−)
- **negative ions (anions)** — such as SO₄²⁻, OH⁻, Cl⁻ — move to the **anode** (+)

At the electrodes the ions gain or lose electrons and are **discharged** as atoms or molecules.

| Conduction in metals | Conduction in electrolytes |
|---|---|
| by free **electrons** | by **ions** (positive and negative) |
| no chemical change | chemical decomposition |
| resistance increases with temperature | resistance decreases with temperature |

## Electrolysis of copper(II) sulphate with copper electrodes

![Two copper electrodes in copper sulphate solution connected to a battery; copper ions move to the cathode and are deposited, while copper dissolves from the anode](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/electrolysis-cell.svg)

*Figure 1. Electrolysis of copper sulphate with copper electrodes. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

- At the **cathode**: Cu²⁺ ions gain electrons and are deposited as copper. $\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu}$. The cathode **gains mass**.
- At the **anode**: copper atoms lose electrons and go into solution. $\text{Cu} \rightarrow \text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^-$. The anode **loses mass**.
- The **concentration** (and blue colour) of the solution **stays the same**: copper is simply transferred from anode to cathode.

This is the principle of **copper refining** and **electroplating**.

## Electrolysis of acidified water (Hofmann voltameter)

Pure water barely conducts, so a little dilute sulphuric acid is added. With inert **platinum** electrodes:

- at the **cathode**: hydrogen ions are discharged — **hydrogen** gas is released
- at the **anode**: **oxygen** gas is released
- the volume of hydrogen is **twice** the volume of oxygen (water is H₂O)

## Applications of electrolysis

1. **Electroplating**: coating a cheap metal with a thin layer of a better-looking or corrosion-resistant one (chromium on bicycle handlebars, silver on cutlery, gold on jewellery).
2. **Refining** of metals such as copper to high purity.
3. **Extraction** of reactive metals such as aluminium and sodium from their molten ores.
4. **Charging** of secondary cells (lead–acid car batteries).
5. **Electrotyping** and anodising aluminium.

### Electroplating a spoon with silver

- **Cathode**: the spoon to be plated (cleaned first)
- **Anode**: a bar of pure silver
- **Electrolyte**: a solution of a silver salt (such as silver nitrate)
- Use a **small current** for a long time, so the coating is even and firmly attached.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Jewellers in Freetown's markets electroplate brass and copper jewellery with a thin layer of gold or silver. The object is made the cathode, so the precious-metal ions are deposited on it.
:::

## Key points

- Electrolysis: chemical decomposition by an electric current; current carried by ions.
- Cations (+) go to the cathode (−); anions (−) go to the anode (+).
- CuSO₄ with copper electrodes: copper moves from anode to cathode; concentration unchanged.
- Acidified water: hydrogen at the cathode, oxygen at the anode, volume ratio 2 : 1.
- Uses: electroplating, refining, extraction, charging batteries.

## Quick check

1. To which electrode do positive ions move?
2. In electroplating a spoon with silver, what is the spoon connected to?
3. What happens to the colour of copper sulphate solution during electrolysis with copper electrodes?

**Answers:** 1. The cathode (negative electrode). 2. The negative terminal: it is the cathode. 3. It stays the same, because copper dissolves from the anode as fast as it is deposited on the cathode.
