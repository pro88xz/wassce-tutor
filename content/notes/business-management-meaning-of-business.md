A woman sells fried fish at a lorry park every evening. Her neighbour cooks the same fish for her family. A third person teaches at a government school. Only one of the three is running a business. Telling them apart is the first step in business management: a business is defined by **what** it does and **why** it does it.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define business in your own words and give examples
- distinguish business from non-business activities
- describe the three broad kinds of business activity: industry, commerce and services
- explain why businesses exist

## Meaning of business

:::callout{type=definition}
**Business** is any lawful activity in which goods or services are produced, bought or sold regularly in order to earn a **profit** by satisfying people's wants.
:::

Three ideas sit inside that definition:

1. **Goods and services**: a business provides something people want, such as rice, a haircut or a bus ride.
2. **Exchange**: the goods or services are sold, not given away or used by the producer.
3. **Profit**: the aim is to earn more than it costs to run the business.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Profit** is the amount by which a business's **revenue** (money received from sales) is greater than its **costs** (money spent to produce and sell).
:::

## Business and non-business activities

| Activity | Business? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Selling fried fish at a lorry park | Yes | Goods are sold regularly for profit |
| Cooking fish for your own family | No | Nothing is sold; it is for personal use |
| A government school teaching pupils | No (mainly) | The aim is a public service, not profit |
| A private school charging fees to make a profit | Yes | A service is sold for profit |
| Selling your old phone once | No | It is not a regular activity |
| A mobile money agent charging commission | Yes | A service is sold regularly for profit |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A single sale does not make a business. Selling your old phone once is not a business, but buying and selling phones every week is. A business is carried on **regularly**.
:::

## Kinds of business activity

Business activities fall into three broad groups:

- **Industry**: extracting raw materials and making goods. Examples: mining iron ore at Tonkolili, fishing at Tombo, rice milling, block making.
- **Commerce**: distributing goods through **trade** (buying and selling) and the **aids to trade** (transport, banking, insurance, warehousing, communication and advertising).
- **Services**: selling a service directly to customers, such as hairdressing, tailoring, private clinics and phone repair.

Later lessons look at each group in detail.

## Why businesses exist

Businesses exist because people have **wants** that they cannot satisfy by themselves. A family in Freetown cannot grow its own rice, make its own soap and generate its own electricity. Businesses specialise in producing these things and sell them; in return the owners earn profit and workers earn wages. Businesses therefore:

- satisfy human wants by supplying goods and services
- provide **employment** and **incomes**
- pay **taxes** that fund schools, roads and hospitals
- make use of the country's resources, such as land, minerals and labour

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Is it a business?** Decide, giving a reason.

1. A farmer grows cassava and sells it at Bo market every week. **Yes**: goods sold regularly for profit.
2. A church organises a free health talk. **No**: no sale, and the aim is not profit.
3. A tailor sews school uniforms for customers each term. **Yes**: a service sold regularly for profit.
4. A student lends a friend a textbook for free. **No**: nothing is sold.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Most businesses in Sierra Leone are small: market traders, okada (motorbike taxi) riders, tailors, mobile money agents and provision shops. Larger businesses include banks, mobile phone companies, breweries and mining companies. All of them, large or small, meet the definition of business: they sell goods or services regularly to make a profit.
:::

## Key points

- **Business** is lawful, regular production or exchange of goods and services for **profit**.
- **Profit** = revenue minus costs.
- Non-business activities are done for personal use, as a one-off, or for a non-profit purpose.
- The three kinds of business activity are **industry**, **commerce** and **services**.
- Businesses exist to satisfy wants; they also create jobs, pay taxes and use the country's resources.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Questions ask you to **define business**, to **distinguish business from non-business activities** with examples, and to **give reasons why businesses exist**. In a definition, include goods or services, exchange and profit; in a comparison, give a clear example of each side.
:::

## Quick check

1. Define business.
2. What is profit?
3. Is a government hospital a business? Give a reason.
4. Name the three broad kinds of business activity.

**Answers:** 1. Lawful, regular production or exchange of goods and services in order to make a profit. 2. Revenue minus costs. 3. Mainly no: its aim is a public service, not profit. 4. Industry, commerce and services.
