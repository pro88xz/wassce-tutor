Ask a market trader why she is in business and she will probably say "to make money". Ask the manager of a large company and you will hear more: to grow, to keep customers, to look after workers, to protect the environment. Both are right. Every business has **objectives**, the goals it works towards, and profit is only the first of them.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define business objectives
- explain the economic, social, human and national objectives of business
- explain why objectives matter to a business
- describe what makes an objective a good one (SMART)

## What are business objectives?

:::callout{type=definition}
**Business objectives** are the goals or targets that a business sets out to achieve, which guide its plans and decisions.
:::

Objectives are set by the owners and managers. They tell everyone in the business what they are working towards and give a standard against which results can be measured.

## Kinds of business objectives

### 1. Economic objectives

| Objective | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Profit** | Earning more than the business spends; the main reward to owners |
| **Survival** | Staying in business, especially in the first years or in hard times |
| **Growth** | Increasing sales, opening branches, or entering new areas |
| **Market share** | Selling a bigger portion of all the goods of that kind sold |
| **Innovation** | Developing new products or better ways of working |

### 2. Social objectives

A business lives in a community and has duties to it:

- supplying **good-quality goods** at **fair prices**
- avoiding **pollution** and protecting the environment
- supporting the community, for example by sponsoring schools or clean-up campaigns
- being honest in advertising and in dealings with customers

:::callout{type=definition}
**Social responsibility** is the duty of a business to act in ways that benefit society and the environment, not only its owners.
:::

### 3. Human objectives

These concern the people who work in the business:

- paying **fair wages** on time
- providing **safe working conditions**
- **training** workers and giving them chances for promotion
- treating workers with respect and listening to their views

### 4. National objectives

A business also serves the country by:

- creating **employment**
- paying **taxes** honestly
- producing goods locally instead of importing them, which saves foreign exchange
- training citizens and developing skills

## Why objectives are important

1. They give the business **direction**: everyone knows what they are working for.
2. They help **planning**: plans are made to reach the objectives.
3. They allow **control**: results can be compared with the objectives.
4. They **motivate** workers, especially when targets are rewarded.
5. They help **decision-making**: choices are judged by whether they help meet the objectives.

## Good objectives are SMART

:::callout{type=definition}
A **SMART objective** is **S**pecific, **M**easurable, **A**chievable, **R**ealistic (relevant) and **T**ime-bound.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Making an objective SMART.** A tailor in Kenema says, "I want to sell more uniforms."

- Not specific or measurable: how many more?
- No deadline.

A SMART version: "Sell **300 school uniforms** between **August and September** this year, **50 more** than last year." It is specific, can be measured by counting sales, is achievable with one extra machine, is relevant to her business, and has a deadline.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not say a business has only one objective. Profit is the **main** economic objective, but survival, growth, social, human and national objectives also guide what businesses do, especially large ones.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Mobile phone companies in Sierra Leone aim at growth and market share by extending network coverage to new towns, while also pursuing social objectives such as sponsoring education and health programmes. A small cookshop may focus mainly on survival and profit, but still meets social objectives by keeping its food clean and its prices fair.
:::

## Key points

- **Business objectives** are the goals a business works towards.
- **Economic**: profit, survival, growth, market share, innovation.
- **Social**: fair prices, good quality, environment, community support (**social responsibility**).
- **Human**: fair pay, safety, training, respect for workers.
- **National**: jobs, taxes, local production, skills.
- Objectives give direction, guide planning and control, and motivate workers; good objectives are **SMART**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
WASSCE asks you to **state and explain the objectives of a business**, sometimes divided into economic and social objectives, and to **explain the importance of objectives**. Group your answer under headings and give an example for each objective.
:::

## Quick check

1. Define business objectives.
2. Give two economic objectives other than profit.
3. What is social responsibility?
4. What does the "T" in SMART stand for?

**Answers:** 1. The goals or targets a business sets out to achieve. 2. Any two of survival, growth, market share, innovation. 3. The duty of a business to act in ways that benefit society and the environment. 4. Time-bound.
