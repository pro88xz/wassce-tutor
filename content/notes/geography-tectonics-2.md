:::callout{type=definition}
An **earthquake** is a sudden shaking of the ground caused by the release of energy when rocks break or slip along a fault, usually at a plate boundary.
:::

A **volcano** is an opening in the Earth's crust through which magma, gases and ash erupt onto the surface. Both are caused mainly by plate movements, both can be deadly, and both also bring benefits. This lesson explains their causes, features, effects and how people reduce the risks.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define an earthquake, its focus and epicentre, and explain its causes
- describe how earthquakes are measured
- state the effects of earthquakes and ways of reducing their damage
- describe the parts of a volcano and the materials it erupts
- classify volcanoes and distinguish extrusive and intrusive features
- state the advantages and disadvantages of volcanic activity

## Earthquakes

:::callout{type=definition}
The **focus** (hypocentre) is the point underground where the rock breaks and the earthquake starts. The **epicentre** is the point on the surface directly above the focus, where shaking is usually strongest. Energy spreads out as **seismic waves**.
:::

**Causes of earthquakes:**

1. **Plate movements** at boundaries: plates lock, stress builds up, then they suddenly slip. This causes most earthquakes.
2. **Volcanic activity:** magma forcing its way upwards cracks the surrounding rock.
3. **Human activities:** filling large reservoirs behind dams, mining, and underground nuclear tests can trigger small earthquakes.
4. **Collapse of underground caves** in limestone areas (minor tremors).

### Measuring earthquakes

- A **seismograph** records the shaking as a trace called a seismogram.
- The **magnitude** (energy released) is given on the **Richter scale** or the modern **moment magnitude scale**. Each whole number is about **32 times** more energy than the one below.
- The **intensity** (the damage felt at a place) is described on the **Mercalli scale** (I to XII).

### Effects of earthquakes

| Primary effects (immediate) | Secondary effects (later) |
|---|---|
| buildings, bridges and roads collapse | fires from broken gas pipes and power lines |
| people killed and injured | landslides and avalanches |
| ground cracks open | **tsunamis** when the sea floor moves |
| water and power supplies cut | disease, homelessness and economic loss |

**Recent examples:** the **Indian Ocean tsunami (2004)** killed about 230,000 people in 14 countries; the **Turkey–Syria earthquakes (February 2023)**, magnitude 7.8, killed more than 50,000 people. In West Africa, the **Accra earthquake of 1939** killed 17 people and damaged much of the city.

### Reducing earthquake damage

- **Earthquake-resistant buildings** with steel frames, deep foundations and flexible joints.
- **Land-use planning:** no building on fault lines or loose soils.
- **Monitoring and early-warning systems**, and tsunami warnings.
- **Education and drills:** "drop, cover and hold on".
- **Emergency services** and supplies ready for rescue and relief.

## Volcanoes

![Cross-section of a volcano: magma chamber, pipe, vent, crater and cone of lava and ash, with intrusive features: a dyke, a sill and a laccolith](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/volcano-section.svg)

*Figure 1. Extrusive and intrusive volcanic features. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

**Parts of a volcano:** the **magma chamber** (a store of molten rock below), the **pipe** (vent) up which it rises, the **crater** at the top, and the **cone** built of layers of lava and ash. **Magma** is molten rock underground; once it reaches the surface it is called **lava**.

**Materials erupted:**

- **Lava:** **basic** (basaltic) lava is runny and flows far, building gentle slopes; **acid** lava is thick and sticky, giving steep cones and violent eruptions.
- **Pyroclasts:** ash, cinders, lapilli and volcanic **bombs** thrown into the air.
- **Gases:** steam, carbon dioxide, sulphur dioxide.

### Types of volcano

| By activity | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Active** | has erupted recently and may erupt again | Mount Cameroon; Nyiragongo (DR Congo) |
| **Dormant** | "sleeping": no eruption for a long time but could erupt | Kilimanjaro |
| **Extinct** | will not erupt again | Edinburgh's Arthur's Seat |

By shape, volcanoes may be **shield volcanoes** (broad, gentle slopes of basic lava), **composite cones** (steep, layered lava and ash; Mount Cameroon is one), **ash and cinder cones**, or **calderas** (large basins formed when the top collapses).

### Intrusive features

When magma cools **underground** it forms intrusive features, which may be exposed later by erosion:

- **Dyke:** a vertical sheet cutting **across** rock layers.
- **Sill:** a horizontal sheet **between** rock layers.
- **Laccolith:** a dome-shaped mass that bulges the layers above it.
- **Batholith:** a huge mass of granite deep below, often forming the core of mountains.

### Advantages and disadvantages of volcanoes

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| **fertile volcanic soils** for farming (coffee, bananas and cocoa around Mount Cameroon) | loss of life from lava, ash and gas |
| **geothermal energy** (Olkaria, Kenya) | destruction of farms, homes and roads |
| **minerals** brought near the surface (diamonds in kimberlite) | ash clouds disrupt air travel and harm health |
| **tourism** (Kilimanjaro, hot springs) | poisonous gases, such as the carbon dioxide released by **Lake Nyos**, Cameroon, in **1986**, which killed about 1,700 people |
| building stone (basalt, pumice) | lava flows can cut off cities: Nyiragongo threatened **Goma** in **2021** |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone has no active volcanoes, but it carries the marks of ancient volcanic activity. The **kimberlite** pipes and dykes of **Kono** are intrusive features that brought diamonds up from the mantle, and the gabbro of the **Freetown Peninsula** is a huge intrusion of magma that cooled underground.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The **epicentre** is on the **surface**, directly above the **focus**, which is underground. Do not mix them up.
:::

## Key points

- An earthquake starts at the **focus**; the **epicentre** is directly above it on the surface.
- Causes: mainly **plate movements**; also volcanoes and human activities. Measured by **seismograph**; **Richter/moment magnitude** and **Mercalli** scales.
- Effects: collapse, death, fires, landslides, **tsunamis**. Reduce damage with resistant buildings, planning, warnings and drills.
- Volcano parts: **magma chamber, pipe, crater, cone**. Volcanoes are **active, dormant or extinct**.
- Intrusive features: **dyke, sill, laccolith, batholith**.
- Volcanoes bring **fertile soils, geothermal energy, minerals and tourism**, but also loss of life and property.

## Quick check

1. What is the difference between the focus and the epicentre of an earthquake?
2. Name the instrument that records earthquakes.
3. Give two secondary effects of an earthquake.
4. Distinguish a dyke from a sill.
5. State two advantages of volcanic activity.

**Answers:**

1. The focus is where the earthquake starts underground; the epicentre is the point on the surface directly above it.
2. The seismograph.
3. Any two: fires; landslides; tsunamis; disease; homelessness; economic loss.
4. A dyke cuts vertically across rock layers; a sill lies horizontally between them.
5. Any two: fertile soils; geothermal energy; minerals; tourism; building materials.
