Most of Sierra Leone's rice is harvested in a few months at the end of the year, yet families buy rice every week. Imported goods arrive by the shipload, but shops sell them a few cartons at a time. Something must hold goods between the time they are produced and the time they are wanted. That is the work of **warehousing**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define warehousing and explain how it removes the hindrance of time
- explain the functions of a warehouse
- explain the importance of warehousing to producers, traders, consumers and government
- state the features of a good warehouse
- work out whether storing goods is profitable

## Meaning of warehousing

:::callout{type=definition}
A **warehouse** is a building where goods are **stored** until they are needed. **Warehousing** is the storing of goods, and their care, from the time they are produced or imported until the time they are sold or used. It removes the **hindrance of time** and creates **time utility**.
:::

![Bar chart: rice production is concentrated in September to December, while consumption is steady all year; the surplus at harvest is stored and released in the other months](https://wasscetutor.com/img/commerce/warehouse-seasons.svg)

*Figure 1. Warehousing bridges the gap between seasonal production and steady consumption. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

Production and consumption rarely happen at the same time:

- **Seasonal production, all-year demand**: rice, cocoa, coffee, groundnuts.
- **All-year production, seasonal demand**: umbrellas and raincoats (rainy season), school uniforms (September), Christmas goods.
- **Production in anticipation of demand**: factories produce continuously and store until orders come.

## Functions of a warehouse

1. **Storage**: keeps goods safely until they are needed.
2. **Protection**: shields goods from rain, heat, pests, theft and fire.
3. **Price stabilisation**: goods stored when plentiful are released when scarce, so prices do not swing so sharply.
4. **Continuous supply**: consumers get goods all year, not only at harvest.
5. **Continuous production**: factories can keep producing even when sales are slow, and keep stocks of raw materials so work never stops.
6. **Breaking bulk**: large consignments are divided into smaller lots for retailers.
7. **Preparing goods for sale**: grading, sorting, blending, packing, labelling and branding.
8. **Seasoning and ripening**: some goods improve with storage (timber dries, some fruits ripen, rice ages).
9. **Collateral for loans**: a **warehouse warrant** can be used as security for a bank loan.
10. **Customs control**: **bonded warehouses** hold imported goods until duty is paid.
11. **Display**: some warehouses act as showrooms where buyers inspect goods.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Is storage worth it?** At harvest a trader buys 200 bags of rice at NLe 400 a bag. Storage and insurance cost NLe 15 per bag per month. Four months later the price is NLe 500 a bag.

1. Cost of the rice $= 200 \times 400 = $ NLe 80,000
2. Storage and insurance $= 200 \times 15 \times 4 = $ NLe 12,000
3. Total cost $= 80000 + 12000 = $ NLe 92,000
4. Sales revenue $= 200 \times 500 = $ NLe 100,000
5. Profit from storing $= 100000 - 92000 = $ **NLe 8,000**

Storage pays only if the **price rise is greater than the cost of storing**. Here the price rose by NLe 100 a bag and storage cost NLe 60 a bag. The trader also bears the **risk** that prices fall or rats damage the stock. That risk is why the reward exists.
:::

## Importance of warehousing

| To | Importance |
|---|---|
| **Producers and manufacturers** | Continuous production; store raw materials and finished goods; produce in anticipation of demand |
| **Wholesalers and retailers** | Hold stock to meet orders promptly; buy in bulk when prices are low; prepare goods for sale |
| **Consumers** | Goods available all year; steadier prices; fresher seasonal goods from cold stores |
| **Importers and exporters** | Store goods at ports while documents and transport are arranged; delay duty in bonded warehouses |
| **Government** | Food security reserves; customs control of imports; jobs and taxes |
| **Farmers** | Avoid selling everything at low harvest prices; reduce post-harvest losses |

## Features of a good warehouse

1. **Good location**: near roads, railways or ports, and near markets or factories.
2. **Adequate space** and the right **design** for the goods (shelves, racks, cold rooms, tanks).
3. **Security**: strong doors, fences, guards, alarms and CCTV.
4. **Protection from fire**: extinguishers, sprinklers and fire exits.
5. **Ventilation, dryness and temperature control**, so goods do not spoil.
6. **Pest control** against rats and insects.
7. **Handling equipment**: forklifts, pallets, conveyors and trolleys.
8. **Loading bays** for lorries.
9. **Proper records**: stock cards and computer systems showing goods received and issued.
10. **Insurance** of the building and its contents.

:::callout{type=local-example}
In Sierra Leone, cocoa and coffee are stored and graded before export, and rice is stored by importers and wholesalers in Freetown and the provincial towns. **Cold stores** keep fish landed at Tombo and Goderich fresh. At the Queen Elizabeth II Quay, **port warehouses** and **bonded warehouses** hold imported goods until customs duties are paid. Poor storage on farms means some harvest is lost to rot and pests every year: a strong case for more community stores.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Warehousing removes the hindrance of **time**, not of place. Transport removes the hindrance of **place**. WASSCE answers often confuse the two.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Seasonal production**: goods produced at one time of year but demanded all year, such as rice and cocoa, which must be stored.
- **Production in anticipation of demand**: making goods before they are ordered and storing them until they are wanted.
:::

## Key points

- **Warehousing** stores goods from production until use; it removes the hindrance of **time** and creates time utility.
- Functions: **storage, protection, price stabilisation, continuous supply and production, breaking bulk, preparing goods, collateral, customs control**.
- Storing is profitable only if the **price rise exceeds the cost of storage**, and the trader bears the risk.
- A good warehouse is **well located, secure, fire-protected, ventilated, equipped and well recorded**.

## Quick check

1. Which hindrance to trade does warehousing remove?
2. Give three functions of a warehouse.
3. A trader buys 50 bags at NLe 300, stores them for 3 months at NLe 10 per bag per month and sells at NLe 360. Find the profit.
4. Give three features of a good warehouse.

**Answers:** 1. The hindrance of time. 2. Any three: storage, protection, price stabilisation, continuous supply, continuous production, breaking bulk, preparing goods for sale, seasoning, collateral, customs control, display. 3. Cost $= 50 \times 300 = 15000$; storage $= 50 \times 10 \times 3 = 1500$; revenue $= 50 \times 360 = 18000$; profit $= 18000 - 16500 = $ NLe 1,500. 4. Any three: good location, adequate space, security, fire protection, ventilation and temperature control, pest control, handling equipment, loading bays, proper records, insurance.
