The **analytical (columnar) petty cash book** has a **receipts** column, a **total payments** column and several **analysis columns**, one for each type of expense, such as postage, travelling, cleaning and stationery, plus a **ledger** column for payments to creditors. Each payment is entered twice: once in the **total** column and once in the right **analysis** column. At the end of the period the columns are totalled, the book is balanced, the imprest is restored, and only the **column totals** are posted to the expense accounts. This lesson works through a complete analytical petty cash book.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the layout of an analytical petty cash book
- record petty cash payments in the total and analysis columns
- total, balance and restore the imprest
- post the analysis totals to the ledger
- check that the analysis totals agree with the total payments

## Layout

| Column | Use |
|---|---|
| **Receipts** | money received: the opening float and the reimbursement |
| **Date, details, voucher no.** | each payment |
| **Total** | every payment, in full |
| **Analysis columns** | each payment again, under its type: postage, travelling, cleaning, stationery and so on |
| **Ledger** | payments to creditors, posted to their personal accounts |

## Worked example

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**The petty cashier of Kanu Enterprises has an imprest of NLe 5,000. Record the March 2026 payments in an analytical petty cash book, balance it and restore the imprest (NLe).**

- 1 Mar: received the imprest from the main cashier, 5,000.
- 2 Mar: postage stamps, 300 (voucher 1).
- 4 Mar: bus fares, 450 (voucher 2).
- 7 Mar: office cleaning, 600 (voucher 3).
- 10 Mar: envelopes and pens, 700 (voucher 4).
- 15 Mar: paid J. Conteh, a creditor, 1,200 (voucher 5).
- 22 Mar: taxi fare, 550 (voucher 6).
- 28 Mar: cleaning materials, 500 (voucher 7).
:::

**Checking the totals:**

- Total payments $= 300 + 450 + 600 + 700 + 1\,200 + 550 + 500 = 4\,300$
- Analysis totals $= 300 + 1\,000 + 1\,100 + 700 + 1\,200 = 4\,300$ ✓
- Balance c/d $= 5\,000 - 4\,300 = 700$
- Reimbursement on 1 April $= 4\,300$, restoring the imprest to $700 + 4\,300 = 5\,000$.

## Posting to the ledger

| Account | Entry |
|---|---|
| **Postage** | debit 300 |
| **Travelling expenses** | debit 1,000 |
| **Cleaning** | debit 1,100 |
| **Stationery** | debit 700 |
| **J. Conteh** | debit 1,200 (reduces the amount owed to him) |
| **Petty cash** | the petty cash book itself is the credit side of these entries |

When the petty cashier is reimbursed, the main **cash book** is **credited** (cash or bank, 4,300) and the **petty cash book** is **debited** (receipts column, 4,300).

## In the final accounts

- Petty cash **expenses** (postage, travelling, cleaning, stationery) go to the **income statement**.
- The **cash in hand** in the petty cash box at the year end (after reimbursement, usually the full imprest) is a **current asset**, often added to cash in hand.

:::callout{type=local-example}
In many offices in Sierra Leone, the petty cash box pays for okada and keke fares to the bank, credit for staff phones, drinking water and photocopying. Keeping the vouchers and receipts, and analysing them each month, shows managers exactly where small sums go.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not forget to enter each payment in **both** the total column **and** one analysis column. The sum of the analysis totals must equal the total of the total column; if it does not, an item has been missed or entered in two analysis columns.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Analysis columns**: columns of the petty cash book in which each payment is entered again under its type, so that only totals are posted to the ledger.
- **Ledger column**: the petty cash column for small amounts paid to creditors, posted to their personal accounts.
:::

## Key points

- Analytical petty cash book: **receipts**, **total**, **analysis columns** and a **ledger** column.
- Each payment goes in the **total** and in **one analysis** column.
- **Analysis totals = total payments**; **balance c/d = imprest − total payments**.
- Only **analysis totals** are posted to expense accounts; ledger items go to **personal accounts**.
- Reimbursement: **credit cash book, debit petty cash book**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **prepare an analytical petty cash book from a list of payments**, **balance it**, **show the reimbursement** and **post the totals to the ledger**. Choose sensible analysis columns from the question, rule off neatly, and show the balance brought down and the reimbursement on the first day of the next month.
:::

## Quick check

1. Why is each payment entered twice in an analytical petty cash book?
2. Payments are postage 400, cleaning 900, travelling 650 and stationery 550, with an imprest of 3,000. Find the balance c/d.
3. How much is reimbursed in question 2?
4. To which account is the total of the travelling column posted, and on which side?
5. What entries record the reimbursement of the petty cashier?

**Answers:**

1. Once in the total column, to find total spending, and once in an analysis column, to group the expense by type.
2. Total $= 2\,500$; balance c/d $= 3\,000 - 2\,500 = 500$: NLe 500.
3. NLe 2,500 (the amount spent).
4. The travelling expenses account, on the debit side.
5. Credit the cash book (cash or bank column) and debit the petty cash book (receipts column).
