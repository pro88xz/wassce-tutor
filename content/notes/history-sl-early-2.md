**Pre-colonial Sierra Leone** was made up of many independent **chiefdoms and small kingdoms**, each ruled by a **chief or king** chosen from **ruling houses** and advised by **councils of elders and sub-chiefs**. Power was balanced by **secret societies**, above all the men's **Poro** and the women's **Sande (Bondo)**, which educated the young, made laws and checked rulers. The people lived mainly by **rice farming**, fishing, crafts such as **weaving and iron working**, and **trade** in kola, salt, cloth and cattle with the coast and the north. This lesson explains the **political, social, economic and religious organisation of pre-colonial Sierra Leone**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the political organisation of pre-colonial chiefdoms
- explain the roles of the Poro and Sande (Bondo) societies
- describe the social organisation of pre-colonial societies
- describe the economic activities and trade of the people
- describe traditional religion and the spread of Islam before colonial rule

## Political organisation

![A pre-colonial chiefdom: the chief or king chosen from ruling houses; a council of elders and sub-chiefs with a speaker; section and town chiefs; village and family heads; the people. Beside them, the Poro society makes laws, controls trade, checks the chief and initiates boys, and the Sande (Bondo) society initiates and trains girls](https://wasscetutor.com/img/history/sl-chiefdom-structure.svg)

*Figure 1. How a pre-colonial chiefdom was organised. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

1. **Many small states:** there was no single kingdom of Sierra Leone. Each people was divided into many **chiefdoms**, some of which joined in **confederacies** for war or trade.
2. **The chief or king:** ruled the chiefdom, settled disputes, led in war, controlled land on behalf of the people and received **tribute and gifts**. Among the Temne a king was called **Bai**, as in **Bai Bureh** and **Bai Farma**.
3. **Ruling houses:** chiefs were chosen from particular families, usually by the elders and the secret societies, not simply by birth order.
4. **Council of elders and sub-chiefs:** advised the chief; no important decision was made without them. A **speaker** spoke for the chief and acted as his deputy.
5. **Section, town and village chiefs:** governed smaller units under the chief.
6. **Checks on power:** a chief who ruled badly could be warned, fined or removed by the council and the **Poro**.

## Secret societies

:::callout{type=definition}
A **secret society** is an organisation whose members are initiated and bound by secret knowledge and rules. In Sierra Leone the main societies, especially **Poro** for men and **Sande (Bondo)** for women, were central to **education, law, religion and government**.
:::

| Society | Members | Roles |
|---|---|---|
| **Poro** | men; found among the **Mende, Temne, Sherbro, Kissi, Gola, Vai, Krim, Loko** and others | **initiated boys** in the "bush school", teaching history, skills, law and duty; **made and enforced laws**; **controlled trade** and could ban harvesting of crops; settled disputes; helped to **choose and check chiefs**; could declare **war or peace** |
| **Sande (Bondo)** | women | **initiated girls**, teaching home management, childcare, farming, medicine, songs and good conduct; protected **women's interests**; known for the **sowei masks**, among the few masks in Africa worn by women |
| **Others** | various | the Mende **Humoi** (rules on marriage and sexual conduct), **Njayei** (healing and farming), **Wunde** (war); the Temne **Ragbenle** (healing and fertility of the land) |

## Social organisation

1. **Family and lineage:** the **extended family** was the basic unit; lineages traced descent from a common ancestor.
2. **Land:** land belonged to the **lineage or community**; the chief held it **in trust** and gave strangers the right to use it, which later caused misunderstandings with Europeans.
3. **Marriage:** marriage joined families; the groom's family paid **bride-wealth**; many men had more than one wife.
4. **Strangers:** visiting traders and settlers were placed under a **landlord** who protected them and spoke for them.
5. **Social ranks:** ruling families, free commoners, and **domestic slaves**, who were often captives or debtors and could sometimes rise through marriage or service.

## Economic life

| Activity | Description |
|---|---|
| **Farming** | **upland rice** by shifting cultivation, and swamp rice; also cassava, millet, groundnuts, yams and **oil palm** |
| **Fishing and salt-making** | along the coast, rivers and Sherbro |
| **Hunting** | for meat, ivory and skins |
| **Crafts** | **weaving country cloth**, dyeing (**gara**), **iron smelting and smithing**, pottery, basketry, carving |
| **Local trade** | weekly markets exchanged food, cloth, salt and tools |
| **Long-distance trade** | **kola nuts** went north to the Futa Jallon and the Western Sudan in exchange for **cattle, cloth, gold and horses**; **salt** and fish went inland; Mandinka and Fula traders led caravans |

## Religion

1. **Traditional religion:** belief in a **Supreme God** (for example **Ngewo** among the Mende and **Kuru** among the Temne), in **ancestral spirits** and in spirits of the land, rivers and forest. Diviners, herbalists and the societies performed rituals.
2. **Islam:** spread from the north through **Fula, Mandinka and Susu** traders and clerics from the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, especially among the **Susu, Temne, Fula, Mandinka** and later others. It brought **Arabic literacy** and Qur'anic schools.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Today's **paramount chiefs**, chiefdom councils and the continuing importance of **Poro and Sande** in many communities show that pre-colonial institutions still shape life in Sierra Leone. During the **2014–2015 Ebola outbreak**, chiefs and society leaders were important in persuading communities to change traditional burial practices.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not describe pre-colonial chiefs as absolute rulers. In most Sierra Leone societies the chief's power was **limited** by the **council of elders**, by **custom**, and by the **Poro society**, which could discipline or even remove a bad ruler.
:::

## Key points

- Pre-colonial Sierra Leone had many **chiefdoms**; chiefs (e.g. the Temne **Bai**) were chosen from **ruling houses** and ruled with **councils of elders**.
- **Poro** (men) and **Sande/Bondo** (women) educated the young, made laws and **checked chiefs**.
- Society was built on the **extended family and lineage**; land belonged to the **community**.
- Economy: **rice farming**, fishing, salt, crafts (country cloth, iron), and trade in **kola**, salt, cattle and cloth.
- Religion: belief in a **Supreme God**, ancestors and spirits; **Islam** spread from the north.

## Quick check

1. How were chiefs chosen in pre-colonial Sierra Leone?
2. Give three functions of the Poro society.
3. What did girls learn in the Sande (Bondo) society?
4. Who owned land in pre-colonial Sierra Leone?
5. Name two goods traded north from Sierra Leone and two received in return.
6. How did Islam spread into Sierra Leone?

**Answers:**

1. From ruling houses, usually selected by elders and the secret societies.
2. Any three: initiating and educating boys; making and enforcing laws; controlling trade; settling disputes; choosing and checking chiefs; declaring war or peace.
3. Home management, childcare, farming, medicine, songs and good conduct.
4. The lineage or community; the chief held it in trust.
5. Kola nuts and salt (or fish) were sent north; cattle, cloth, gold or horses came in return.
6. Through Fula, Mandinka and Susu traders and clerics from the north.
