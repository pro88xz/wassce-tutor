Light trapped inside glass can bounce along a hair-thin fibre for kilometres. The internet cables under the Atlantic, the sparkle of a diamond and the shimmering "water" on a hot road all come from total internal reflection.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define the critical angle and relate it to refractive index, $n = \dfrac{1}{\sin c}$
- state the conditions for total internal reflection
- explain applications: optical fibres, prism periscopes, diamonds, mirages
- calculate the critical angle for glass and water

## The critical angle

When light travels from a **denser** medium (glass, water) to a **less dense** one (air), it bends **away** from the normal. As the angle of incidence increases, the refracted ray bends further until it travels **along the surface** (angle of refraction 90°).

:::callout{type=definition}
The **critical angle** $c$ is the angle of incidence **in the denser medium** for which the angle of refraction **in the less dense medium is 90°**.
:::

From Snell's law (light going from the medium into air):

$$n = \dfrac{1}{\sin c} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \sin c = \dfrac{1}{n}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Find the critical angle for (a) glass, n = 1.5, (b) water, n = 1.33.**

(a) $\sin c = \dfrac{1}{1.5} = 0.667 \Rightarrow c = 41.8°$.

(b) $\sin c = \dfrac{1}{1.33} = 0.752 \Rightarrow c = 48.8°$.
:::

## Total internal reflection

*Figure 1. Refraction, the critical angle and total internal reflection. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

If the angle of incidence **exceeds** the critical angle, no light escapes: **all** of it is reflected back into the denser medium. This is **total internal reflection** (TIR).

:::callout{type=definition}
**Conditions for total internal reflection:**

1. Light must travel from an **optically denser** medium to a **less dense** medium.
2. The angle of incidence must be **greater than the critical angle**.
:::

TIR reflects **100%** of the light, better than any silvered mirror (which absorbs a little), and the reflecting surface cannot tarnish.

## Applications

### Optical fibres

An optical fibre is a thin strand of very pure glass with a **core** surrounded by a **cladding** of lower refractive index. Light entering one end strikes the core–cladding boundary at angles greater than the critical angle and is totally internally reflected again and again, travelling along even a bent fibre with very little loss.

Uses:

- **telecommunications**: fibres carry telephone, TV and internet signals as pulses of light, with huge capacity and little interference
- **endoscopes**: doctors view the inside of the stomach or knee without major surgery
- decorative lamps

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone is connected to the global internet by the ACE submarine fibre-optic cable, which lands near Freetown. Pulses of infrared light carry data along glass fibres for thousands of kilometres under the Atlantic, trapped inside by total internal reflection.
:::

### Totally reflecting prisms

A right-angled isosceles glass prism (angles 45°, 45°, 90°) has a critical angle of about 42°. Light striking the long face at 45° (> 42°) is totally reflected, so the prism can:

- turn a ray through **90°** — used in **prism periscopes** (better than mirrors: brighter, no multiple images, no tarnishing)
- turn a ray through **180°** — used in **binoculars** and bicycle reflectors (cat's eyes)

### Diamonds

Diamond has a very high refractive index (2.42), so its critical angle is only about **24°**. Light entering a cut diamond is totally reflected many times inside before leaving, which makes it **sparkle**.

### Mirages

On a hot day, the air just above a tarred road is hotter and **less dense** than the air above it. Light from the sky bends gradually as it passes into the hotter layers until it is totally internally reflected upwards into the eye. You see an image of the sky on the road, which looks like a pool of water.

:::callout{type=local-example}
On the long straight stretches of the Freetown–Bo highway at midday in the dry season, drivers see "water" shimmering on the road ahead that vanishes as they approach — a mirage.
:::

## Key points

- Critical angle: angle of incidence in the denser medium giving 90° refraction; $\sin c = 1/n$.
- TIR needs: denser → less dense medium, and $i > c$.
- Glass ($n = 1.5$): $c \approx 42°$; water ($n = 1.33$): $c \approx 49°$; diamond: $c \approx 24°$.
- Applications: optical fibres, prism periscopes and binoculars, diamonds, mirages.

## Quick check

1. State the two conditions for total internal reflection.
2. The critical angle of a liquid is 45°. Find its refractive index.
3. Give one advantage of a prism over a plane mirror in a periscope.

**Answers:** 1. Light travels from a denser to a less dense medium, and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle. 2. $n = \dfrac{1}{\sin 45°} = 1.41$. 3. For example: it reflects all the light (brighter image) and does not form multiple images or tarnish.
