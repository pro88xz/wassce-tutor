Most of the diseases that kill people in Sierra Leone, such as malaria, diarrhoea, cholera, typhoid, measles and worms, can be **prevented**. Prevention depends less on hospitals than on clean water, safe toilets, proper disposal of refuse, control of mosquitoes and rats, vaccination and good hygiene. That is **public health**: protecting the health of the whole community. This lesson explains personal and community hygiene, how water is made safe, how refuse and sewage are disposed of, how vectors are controlled, how vaccination and immunity work, and the roles of health organisations.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define public health and distinguish personal and community health
- describe methods of making water safe to drink
- describe safe disposal of refuse and sewage, including the VIP latrine
- describe the control of mosquitoes, houseflies and rats
- explain types of immunity and the importance of vaccination
- describe the roles of health organisations

## Personal hygiene

- **Wash hands with soap** after using the toilet, before preparing or eating food, and after caring for the sick: the single most effective habit against diarrhoea, cholera and many other infections.
- Bathe daily; keep nails short and clean; wash clothes and bedding.
- Brush teeth at least twice a day.
- Wear shoes (against hookworm and injuries).
- Do not share razors, toothbrushes or needles.
- Eat a balanced diet, rest and exercise; avoid smoking, drug abuse and excess alcohol.

## Safe water

Water from rivers, unprotected wells and streams may carry the pathogens of cholera, typhoid, dysentery and worms.

![Water treatment for a town: screening, sedimentation with alum, filtration through sand and gravel, chlorination, and covered storage before piping](https://wasscetutor.com/img/biology/water-treatment.svg)

**At home:**

- **Boil** water for at least one minute (a rolling boil), cool and store covered.
- Treat with **chlorine** tablets or a few drops of household bleach as directed, and wait 30 minutes.
- Filter cloudy water through clean cloth or a ceramic filter first.
- Store in **clean, covered** containers and use a cup with a handle, so hands do not touch the water.

**Protecting sources:** wells with a **raised wall, cover and pump**; latrines at least **30 m** from wells and downhill; no washing or defecating near springs and streams.

## Refuse disposal

**Refuse** (solid waste: food remains, plastics, paper, ash) left in heaps breeds **flies, mosquitoes and rats**, blocks drains and causes floods.

| Method | Notes |
|---|---|
| **collection** in covered bins, taken to a sanitary landfill | the best for towns |
| **composting** | turns food and plant waste into manure for farms and gardens |
| **burying** | in a pit, covered with soil |
| **burning** (incineration) | reduces volume; but smoke pollutes, and plastics give off toxic fumes |
| **recycling** and **reuse** | plastics, metals, glass, paper |

## Sewage and excreta

Faeces carry the pathogens of cholera, typhoid, dysentery and worms, so **excreta must never contaminate water or food**. Open defecation spreads these diseases.

![A VIP latrine: a deep pit under a concrete slab with a squat hole, a dark shelter, and a vent pipe with a fly screen; air flows down the hole and up the pipe, carrying smells away, and flies attracted to light at the top of the pipe are trapped by the screen](https://wasscetutor.com/img/biology/vip-latrine.svg)

- **Pit latrine**: simple and cheap; must be deep, with a slab, and away from wells.
- **Ventilated improved pit (VIP) latrine**: a **vent pipe** with a **fly screen** removes smells, and flies that enter the pit fly towards the light at the top of the pipe and are trapped. The shelter is kept **dark**.
- **Water closet and septic tank**: sewage flows into a sealed tank where bacteria break it down.
- **Sewage treatment works** in cities: screening, settling, then bacteria break down the organic matter before the water is released.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Covering the squat hole of a VIP latrine.** The hole is left **open** so that air can flow down it and up the vent pipe; this airflow carries the smell away. Flies are controlled by keeping the shelter **dark** and by the **fly screen** on the pipe, not by a lid.
:::

## Controlling vectors and pests

| Vector or pest | Diseases | Control |
|---|---|---|
| **mosquitoes** | malaria, yellow fever, dengue, filariasis | sleep under **insecticide-treated nets**; drain stagnant water; clear blocked gutters, old tyres and tins; indoor residual spraying; larvicides; screens on windows |
| **houseflies** | cholera, typhoid, dysentery (carry germs on legs and mouthparts onto food) | cover food; dispose of refuse and excreta properly; keep kitchens clean; fly screens |
| **rats** | Lassa fever, plague, leptospirosis | store food in rat-proof containers; keep compounds clean; block holes; traps |
| **blackflies** | river blindness | treatment of breeding sites in rivers; ivermectin for people |
| **water snails** | bilharzia | avoid infested water; latrines; clear vegetation at water edges |

## Immunity and vaccination

:::callout{type=definition}
**Immunity** is the body's ability to resist a disease. When a pathogen enters, **lymphocytes** make **antibodies** that recognise and destroy it, and **memory cells** remain, so a second infection is destroyed quickly.

| Type | How it is gained | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **natural active** | having the disease and recovering | immunity after measles |
| **artificial active** | **vaccination**: a harmless (dead or weakened) form of the pathogen or its parts makes the body produce its own antibodies and memory cells | measles, polio, BCG, yellow fever vaccines |
| **natural passive** | antibodies passed from mother to baby across the placenta and in **breast milk** | newborn babies |
| **artificial passive** | ready-made antibodies injected | anti-tetanus and anti-snake-venom serum |

**Active** immunity lasts a long time (memory cells); **passive** immunity is quick but **short-lived**.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Under-five vaccinations in Sierra Leone.** Babies receive vaccines free at government health centres, starting at birth with **BCG** (against TB) and polio drops, then a series of vaccines in the first year and beyond against diseases including polio, diphtheria, whooping cough, tetanus, hepatitis B, pneumonia, rotavirus diarrhoea, measles and yellow fever. Keeping the child's health card and completing every visit protects not only the child but the whole community, by stopping outbreaks.
:::

## Health organisations

- **Ministry of Health** (and local health centres): health services, vaccination, disease surveillance, health education.
- **Primary health care**: community health workers bring basic care, vaccination, malaria testing and treatment, and health education to villages.
- **World Health Organization (WHO)**: international guidance, emergency response (as during Ebola), eradication campaigns.
- **UNICEF**: child health, nutrition, vaccines, water and sanitation.
- **Red Cross**: first aid, emergencies, blood donation, safe burials during Ebola.

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Give three ways of making water safe to drink at home."}
Boiling; adding chlorine tablets or bleach as directed; filtering; storing in clean covered containers (any three).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Explain how a VIP latrine reduces flies and smells."}
Air flows down the squat hole and up the **vent pipe**, carrying smells out above the roof. Flies in the pit fly towards the **light** at the top of the pipe and are trapped by the **fly screen**; the dark shelter does not attract them.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. State three methods of controlling mosquitoes."}
Any three: insecticide-treated nets; draining stagnant water and clearing containers; indoor spraying; larvicides; window screens.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Distinguish between active and passive immunity, with one example of each."}
**Active**: the body makes its own antibodies, e.g. after vaccination or infection; long-lasting. **Passive**: ready-made antibodies are received, e.g. from breast milk or anti-tetanus serum; short-lived.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Why are houseflies dangerous to health?"}
They breed in refuse and faeces, then land on food, carrying pathogens of cholera, typhoid and dysentery on their legs and mouthparts.
:::

## Key points

- **Public health** protects the whole community through prevention.
- **Hand washing with soap** is the most effective single habit.
- Safe water: **boil, chlorinate, filter, store covered**; protect wells and keep latrines 30 m away.
- Dispose of **refuse** (collection, composting, landfill, recycling) and **excreta** (latrines, VIP latrines, septic tanks).
- Control **mosquitoes, flies, rats, snails** to stop disease.
- **Vaccination** gives artificial active immunity; breast milk gives natural passive immunity.
