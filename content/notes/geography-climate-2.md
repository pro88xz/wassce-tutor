The **climate of West Africa** is controlled by two air masses: moist **tropical maritime air** from the Atlantic, carried by the **south-west monsoon**, and dry **tropical continental air** from the Sahara, carried by the **Harmattan**. As the boundary between them moves north and south with the overhead Sun, it brings a **rainy season** and a **dry season**, with rainfall decreasing steadily from more than 3,000 mm on parts of the coast to less than 250 mm on the edge of the Sahara. This lesson explains the pattern and describes the climate of Sierra Leone.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the air masses and winds that control West Africa's climate
- explain the movement of the Inter-Tropical Discontinuity and the seasons it brings
- describe the distribution of rainfall and temperature across West Africa
- describe the Harmattan and its effects
- describe the climate of Sierra Leone and its effects on people

## Air masses, winds and the ITD

| | Tropical maritime (mT) | Tropical continental (cT) |
|---|---|---|
| **Source** | South Atlantic Ocean | Sahara Desert |
| **Wind** | **south-west monsoon** | **north-east trades (Harmattan)** |
| **Character** | warm, moist, unstable | hot by day, dry, dusty, cool at night |
| **Brings** | clouds and rain | dry, hazy weather |

The zone where the two meet is the **Inter-Tropical Discontinuity (ITD)**:

- **January:** the overhead Sun is south of the Equator; the ITD lies near the coast (about 5–7° N). Dry Harmattan air covers almost all of West Africa.
- **July–August:** the overhead Sun is over the Tropic of Cancer; the ITD moves north to about 20° N. Moist monsoon air covers nearly all of West Africa, bringing the rains far inland.

Rain does not fall right at the ITD but in a belt **south** of it, where the moist air is deep enough to form tall clouds.

## Rainfall across West Africa

1. **Rainfall decreases northwards**, from over 2,000–3,000 mm on parts of the coast to under 250 mm in the Sahara.
2. **The rainy season gets shorter northwards**: up to 7–8 months on the coast, 3–4 months in the Sahel.
3. **Rainfall becomes less reliable northwards**, so droughts are more common in the Sahel.
4. Highlands facing the monsoon receive **extra relief rain**, as on the Freetown Peninsula, the Guinea Highlands and Mount Cameroon.
5. Parts of the coast, such as southern Ghana and Nigeria, have **two rainfall peaks** with a short drier spell (the "little dry season") in August; further north and west there is a single rainy season.

## Temperature

- Temperatures are **high all year**, usually above 21 °C.
- The **annual range** is small on the coast (about 3–5 °C) and larger inland (up to 10–15 °C in the Sahel).
- The **daily range** is greatest in the dry, cloudless north, especially during the Harmattan.
- The hottest months are usually **just before the rains**; cloud and rain lower temperatures during the wet season.

## The Harmattan

:::callout{type=definition}
The **Harmattan** is the dry, dusty north-east trade wind that blows from the Sahara over West Africa, mainly from **November to March**, reaching the coast around **December to February**.
:::

| Effects | |
|---|---|
| **Weather** | low humidity, hazy skies full of dust, poor visibility, cold nights and warm days |
| **Health** | dry skin, cracked lips, sore eyes, respiratory infections; meningitis outbreaks in the Sahel are linked to the dry season |
| **Daily life** | flights delayed by poor visibility; dust everywhere; bush fires spread easily |
| **Benefits** | drying of crops, fish and cocoa; few mosquitoes; low humidity eases evaporation for salt making |

## The climate of Sierra Leone

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone has a **tropical monsoon climate**, hot and humid all year:

- **Rainy season:** about **May to October**, brought by the south-west monsoon. Rain is heaviest in **July and August**, with Freetown receiving roughly 3,000–3,500 mm a year. Rainfall is highest on the coast and on the Freetown Peninsula mountains, and decreases towards the north-east, where it is still around 2,000 mm or more.
- **Dry season:** about **November to April**. The **Harmattan** reaches Sierra Leone mainly between December and February, bringing dust, haze and cooler nights.
- **Temperatures:** about **24–31 °C**, with a small annual range, especially on the coast.
- **Humidity:** high, often above 80% in the rainy season.
:::

**Effects of the climate on people in Sierra Leone:**

1. **Farming** follows the seasons: upland rice is planted as the rains begin; swamp rice is grown in the wet season; the dry season is used for harvesting and drying.
2. **Heavy rains** cause flooding in Freetown, landslides on steep slopes, and damage to unpaved roads, making travel difficult.
3. **Abundant water** feeds rivers for hydroelectric power (Bumbuna) and water supply.
4. **Warm, wet conditions** favour malaria-carrying mosquitoes and waterborne diseases in the rains.
5. **The dry season** is the main time for construction, travel, trading and festivals.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The rainy season is not caused by "the rain wind" alone. It is the **movement of the ITD** with the overhead Sun that lets moist monsoon air spread inland. Explain the cause, not just the wind's name.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **South-west monsoon**: the warm, moist, unstable wind from the South Atlantic Ocean that brings clouds and rain to West Africa.
- **Inter-Tropical Discontinuity** (ITD): the meeting line of the moist monsoon air and the dry Harmattan air, which moves north and south with the overhead Sun.
- **Rainy season**: the months when monsoon air covers the country; in Sierra Leone, May to October.
:::

## Key points

- Two air masses: **tropical maritime** (moist, south-west monsoon) and **tropical continental** (dry, Harmattan).
- The **ITD** moves north in July–August (rains inland) and south in January (dry season everywhere).
- Rainfall **decreases northwards**; the rainy season **shortens** and becomes less reliable.
- **Harmattan:** dry, dusty wind from the Sahara, December–February at the coast.
- **Sierra Leone:** tropical monsoon; rains May–October (peak July–August); dry season November–April; 24–31 °C.

## Quick check

1. Name the wind that brings rain to West Africa.
2. Where is the ITD in January?
3. How does rainfall change from the coast to the Sahara?
4. State two effects of the Harmattan.
5. During which months is rain heaviest in Sierra Leone?

**Answers:**

1. The south-west monsoon.
2. Near the coast, at about 5–7° N.
3. It decreases, the rainy season shortens, and rain becomes less reliable.
4. Any two: dry air and low humidity; dust haze and poor visibility; cold nights; health problems; bush fires; drying of crops.
5. July and August.
