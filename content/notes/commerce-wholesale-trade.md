# Wholesale trade: functions, types and distribution efficiency

## Learning objectives
Define wholesale trade, explain wholesaler functions and distinguish merchant wholesalers from agents and brokers.

## Meaning
Wholesale trade is the buying of goods in relatively large quantities from producers or suppliers and selling them mainly to retailers, institutions or other business buyers for resale or business use. A wholesaler is an intermediary between producers and downstream buyers. Unlike a retailer, the wholesaler does not mainly sell small quantities to final household consumers.

A common channel is **Producer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer**. Some manufacturers sell directly to large retailers, so the wholesaler is not essential in every distribution channel.

## Functions
Wholesalers buy in bulk, hold stock and break bulk into quantities that retailers can afford and store. They may transport goods, provide market information, extend trade credit to approved customers, assemble products from different manufacturers and absorb some inventory risks. By purchasing large quantities, they help producers distribute output without dealing separately with many small shops.

Wholesalers may stabilise supply by holding stock when production is seasonal or deliveries are irregular. Their value depends on whether the services they provide justify the costs and margins added to goods.

## Types
**Merchant wholesalers** buy and take ownership of goods before reselling them. **Cash-and-carry wholesalers** sell mainly to business customers who pay at purchase and arrange collection. **Full-service wholesalers** may provide storage, delivery, credit and product advice. **Limited-service wholesalers** provide fewer services. **Agents and brokers** bring buyers and sellers together but generally do not take ownership of the goods; they earn commission or fees under their agreements.

## Why wholesalers may be bypassed
Large retailers may buy directly from manufacturers when they have enough scale, storage and logistics capacity. Digital platforms can also connect producers with buyers. However, removing a wholesaler does not remove the functions of transport, storage, finance and stock management; another party must perform them.

A small shop in Kenema may rely on a wholesaler because buying a few cartons from one supplier is easier than arranging separate purchases from many manufacturers. A national chain may have enough purchasing power to import directly.
