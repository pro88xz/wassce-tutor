Should the government own the mines, the electricity company and the banks, or should private businesses run them? Should everyone receive free health care paid for by taxes, or should people pay for what they use? Answers to these questions come from **political ideologies**: organised sets of ideas about how society and the economy should be run. This lesson covers capitalism, socialism, communism, fascism and totalitarianism, the welfare state, the mixed economy and feudalism.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define political ideology
- explain the features, merits and demerits of capitalism, socialism and communism
- explain fascism, Nazism and totalitarianism
- describe the welfare state, the mixed economy and feudalism
- compare the main ideologies

## What is a political ideology?

:::callout{type=definition}
A **political ideology** is a set of ideas, beliefs and values about how society should be organised, how power should be used and how the economy should be run. It guides the policies of governments and political parties.
:::

## Capitalism

:::callout{type=definition}
**Capitalism** is a system in which the means of production (land, factories, mines and businesses) are privately owned and run for profit, and prices are set mainly by market forces of demand and supply.
:::

**Features:** private ownership of property; the profit motive; free enterprise and competition; the price mechanism; limited government intervention (*laissez-faire*); consumer sovereignty.

**Merits:** encourages hard work, initiative and innovation; efficient use of resources; wide choice of goods; individual freedom. **Demerits:** wide inequality between rich and poor; exploitation of workers; neglect of unprofitable but essential services; monopolies; booms and slumps.

The Scottish economist **Adam Smith** (*The Wealth of Nations*, 1776) is regarded as the father of capitalism.

## Socialism

:::callout{type=definition}
**Socialism** is a system in which the major means of production are owned or controlled by the state (or the community) and run for the benefit of all, with wealth distributed more equally.
:::

**Features:** public ownership of key industries; central planning; emphasis on equality and social welfare; limited private enterprise.

**Merits:** reduces inequality; essential services are provided for all; less exploitation; planning can direct resources to national priorities. **Demerits:** inefficiency and waste in state enterprises; weak incentives; heavy bureaucracy; less individual freedom and choice.

## Communism

:::callout{type=definition}
**Communism** is the ideology of **Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels** (*The Communist Manifesto*, 1848): a classless society in which all property is owned in common and goods are distributed "from each according to his ability, to each according to his needs".
:::

Marx saw history as a **class struggle** between owners (the bourgeoisie) and workers (the proletariat). He predicted a workers' revolution, a period of socialism under the "dictatorship of the proletariat", and finally communism, when the state would "wither away".

**Features in practice (for example, the Soviet Union, 1917–1991):** one-party rule by the Communist Party; total state ownership; central planning; suppression of opposition. **Examples today:** China, Cuba, Vietnam, North Korea (China and Vietnam now allow much private enterprise).

## Fascism, Nazism and totalitarianism

- **Fascism:** founded in Italy by **Benito Mussolini** (in power 1922–1943). Features: extreme nationalism, glorification of the state and of war, one-party rule, a single all-powerful leader, suppression of opposition, private property kept but controlled by the state.
- **Nazism:** the German form of fascism under **Adolf Hitler** (1933–1945), adding racial ideology and the persecution and murder of Jews and other groups (the Holocaust).
- **Totalitarianism:** a system in which the state seeks to control every part of public and private life: politics, economy, media, education, religion and culture. Fascist and communist regimes have both been totalitarian.

## Other systems

| System | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Welfare state** | Government provides social security for all citizens: free or cheap health care, education, pensions and unemployment benefits, funded by taxes (for example, Britain after 1945, the Scandinavian countries) |
| **Mixed economy** | Both the public sector and the private sector own and run businesses; government regulates the market and provides essential services. Most modern states, including Sierra Leone, have mixed economies |
| **Feudalism** | A system in medieval Europe in which kings granted land to lords in return for military service, and peasants (serfs) worked the land in return for protection |

## Comparing the main ideologies

| Feature | Capitalism | Socialism | Communism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership of production | Private | Mainly state or community | Common (state in practice) |
| Main motive | Profit | Welfare of all | Equality and need |
| Economic coordination | Market forces | Planning plus some markets | Central planning |
| Party system (in practice) | Usually multi-party | Varies | One party |
| Inequality | High | Lower | Officially none |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone runs a **mixed economy**. Private firms run most shops, farms, mines and banks, while the state owns or controls public corporations such as the **Guma Valley Water Company** and the **Sierra Leone Broadcasting Corporation**, and provides services such as Free Quality School Education. After independence, several West African leaders were drawn to forms of "African socialism", but most countries now combine markets with public services.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Socialism and communism are not identical. In Marx's theory, **socialism** is a stage on the way to **communism**. Many democratic countries have socialist or social-democratic parties that win elections and keep multi-party politics and private property.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Mixed economy**: an economy in which both the public and private sectors own and run businesses, and the government regulates the market and provides essential services.
- **Feudalism**: a system in medieval Europe in which kings granted land to lords for military service, and peasants (serfs) worked the land for protection.
- **Fascism**: an ideology founded in Italy by Benito Mussolini, marked by extreme nationalism, glorification of the state and war, one-party rule and a single all-powerful leader.
- **Totalitarianism**: a system in which the state seeks to control every part of public and private life.
:::

## Key points

- An **ideology** is a set of ideas about how society, power and the economy should be organised.
- **Capitalism:** private ownership, profit and markets (**Adam Smith**); **socialism:** public ownership and equality; **communism:** classless common ownership (**Marx and Engels, 1848**).
- **Fascism** (**Mussolini**) and **Nazism** (**Hitler**) are nationalist, one-party, totalitarian systems.
- **Totalitarianism** controls every part of life; the **welfare state** provides social security; most states, including **Sierra Leone**, have **mixed economies**.
