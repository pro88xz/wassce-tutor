In a listening test you cannot reread. The passage or dialogue is read aloud, often only once or twice, and then it is gone. Students who are excellent at reading comprehension can still lose marks here, because listening is a **different skill**: it needs preparation before the recording starts, focus while it plays, and quick, careful decisions afterwards. This lesson gives you a method for the WASSCE listening test, the kinds of questions to expect, and practice you can do with a friend.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- prepare in the seconds before a passage is read
- listen for the main idea, key details and the speakers' attitudes
- recognise the common question types in listening comprehension
- avoid the traps built into the answer options
- practise listening skills effectively before the examination

## What is tested

In the listening section, a passage, a dialogue or a short talk is read aloud, and you answer questions about it, usually by choosing from options. The questions test whether you can:

| Skill | Example question |
|---|---|
| **Main idea** | *What is the talk mainly about?* |
| **Specific details** | *At what time does the meeting start?* *How many people…?* |
| **Inference** | *Where does the conversation probably take place?* *What is the relationship between the speakers?* |
| **Attitude and tone** | *How does the woman feel about the decision?* |
| **Purpose** | *Why does the speaker mention the bridge?* |
| **Sound-based** | words or stresses heard in the passage (in some tests) |

## The three-stage method

### Before: prepare (the seconds before the reading)

1. **Read the questions** (and the options, if printed) before the reading starts.
2. **Underline the key word** in each: *when, who, why, how many, where*.
3. **Predict**: if a question asks *"At what time…?"*, you know to listen for a time.

### During: listen with purpose

1. Listen first for the **main idea**: who is speaking, where, about what.
2. Then listen for the **details** your questions need. When you hear one, note it **briefly**: a number, a name, one key word.
3. Listen for **signal words** that warn you something important is coming:

| Signal | What follows |
|---|---|
| *but, however, actually, in fact* | a **change** or correction: often the real answer |
| *the most important thing…, above all…* | the **main point** |
| *first, then, finally* | a **sequence** |
| *because, so, as a result* | a **reason** or **result** |

### After: decide carefully

1. Answer the easy questions first, while the passage is fresh.
2. Eliminate options you **know** are wrong.
3. Do not leave a question blank: choose the most likely remaining option.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Choosing the option with words you heard.** Test-setters often put words from the passage into a **wrong** option. If the speaker says *"I thought the meeting was at nine, but actually it starts at ten,"* an option saying *"nine o'clock"* is a trap: you heard *nine*, but the answer is **ten**. Listen for corrections after *but* and *actually*.
:::

## Listening for inference

Many questions ask what the speakers **imply**: where they are, who they are, how they feel. Listen for clues:

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Dialogue:**

*Man:* Next, please. Where are you travelling to?\
*Woman:* Kenema. How much is it now?\
*Man:* A hundred and fifty leones. The vehicle leaves when it is full. You can sit at the front if you pay twenty more.\
*Woman:* At the front, then. My back won't survive the back seat on that road.

**Questions and reasoning:**

| Question | Answer | Clue |
|---|---|---|
| Where does the conversation probably take place? | at a motor park / a vehicle loading point | *"travelling to"*, *"the vehicle leaves when it is full"* |
| What is the relationship between the speakers? | a ticket seller (or driver's agent) and a passenger | *"Next, please"*, the price, the seating |
| How much does the woman pay? | one hundred and seventy leones | 150 + 20 more for the front |
| Why does she choose the front seat? | for comfort on a rough road | *"My back won't survive the back seat on that road"* |

Notice the third answer: the number **170** is never spoken. You had to **combine** two details.
:::

## Attitude from the voice

In listening, **tone of voice** carries attitude, just as word choice does in reading. Listen for:

- **pace**: fast can mean excitement or anger; slow can mean sadness or care
- **pitch**: high can mean surprise or excitement; low can mean seriousness
- **emphasis** on particular words (emphatic stress)
- **what is said**: *"Well… I suppose so"* sounds reluctant even before you think about it

## How to practise before the examination

:::callout{type=local-example}
Listening is a skill you can train every day without any special materials:

- Listen to **radio news** in English (SLBC, BBC World Service on FM) and afterwards write down the three main stories and one detail from each.
- With a **friend**, take turns reading a short passage from a textbook or newspaper aloud **once**, while the other answers five questions written beforehand.
- Listen to a **sermon, a speech or a football commentary** and identify the speaker's attitude and one fact.
- Practise taking **brief notes**: numbers, names and key words only.
:::

## Practice with a partner

Ask a friend to read this passage aloud **once**, at normal speed, while you answer the questions without looking.

::::model{label="Passage to be read aloud" title="The community clean-up"}
Good morning, everyone. As you know, the monthly community clean-up was planned for Saturday the fourteenth. However, because the council's lorry will not be available that day, we have moved it to Saturday the twenty-first. We will meet at the mosque junction at seven o'clock, not eight as we said last month, so that we can finish before the sun gets too hot. Please bring a cutlass, a hoe or a broom if you have one; the council will provide gloves and rubbish sacks. The street that collects the most rubbish will receive a football for its youth team. Last month, only thirty people came. This time, I hope to see at least twice that number.
::::

:::reveal{title="1. On what date will the clean-up now take place?"}
**Saturday the twenty-first.** (*Fourteenth* is the trap: it was the old date.)
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Why was the date changed?"}
Because the **council's lorry** would not be available on the fourteenth.
:::

:::reveal{title="3. At what time will they meet, and why that time?"}
At **seven o'clock**, so they can finish **before it gets too hot**. (*Eight* is the trap.)
:::

:::reveal{title="4. What will the council provide?"}
**Gloves and rubbish sacks.**
:::

:::reveal{title="5. How many people does the speaker hope will come?"}
**At least sixty**: twice last month's thirty. (Sixty is never said; you had to work it out.)
:::

:::reveal{title="6. What is the speaker's purpose?"}
To **inform** residents of the new date and time, and to **encourage** more people to take part (the football prize, the hope for twice as many people).
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Main idea**: what a talk or passage is mainly about.
- **Inference**: working out what is not said directly, such as where a conversation takes place.
- **Attitude**: how a speaker feels about something, shown by what is said and by pace, pitch and emphasis.
- **Pitch**: how high or low the voice is; high can mean surprise or excitement, low can mean seriousness.
- **Key word**: the word in a question, such as when, who, why or how many, that shows what to listen for.
- **Specific details**: exact facts in a passage, such as times, numbers and names.
:::

## Key points

- **Before**: read the questions, underline the key words, predict what to listen for.
- **During**: get the main idea, then the details; note numbers, names and key words briefly; listen for **signal words** like *but* and *actually*.
- **After**: eliminate wrong options; never leave a blank.
- Beware options that **repeat words you heard** but give the wrong information.
- Some answers must be **worked out** from two details (prices, numbers, times).

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Listening questions test **main ideas, details, inference, attitude and purpose** from a passage or dialogue heard aloud. The commonest traps are corrected details (*"not eight, but seven"*), words from the passage placed in wrong options, and answers that need two details combined. Regular listening practice with radio and a partner is the most effective preparation.
:::
