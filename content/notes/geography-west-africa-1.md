**West Africa** is the western part of Africa south of the Sahara, made up of **sixteen countries**: Benin, Burkina Faso, Cabo Verde, Côte d'Ivoire, The Gambia, Ghana, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Liberia, Mali, Mauritania, Niger, Nigeria, Senegal, Sierra Leone and Togo. It stretches from the **Atlantic coast and the Gulf of Guinea** in the south and west to the **Sahara** in the north. Its **relief** is mostly low **plateau**, with highlands such as the **Fouta Djallon**, **Jos Plateau** and **Aïr Mountains**, and its great rivers are the **Niger**, **Senegal**, **Gambia** and **Volta**. This lesson covers the countries, location, relief and drainage of West Africa.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name the countries of West Africa and their capitals
- describe the location and extent of West Africa
- describe the main relief features of West Africa
- describe the main rivers and lakes and their importance
- draw a sketch map of West Africa

- West Africa covers about **6 million km²**, roughly a fifth of Africa.
- It lies between about **4°N** (the coast of Liberia and Côte d'Ivoire) and about **27°N** (northern Mauritania), and between about **17°W** (Dakar) and **16°E** (eastern Niger) on the mainland; the Cabo Verde islands lie further west.
- It is home to **over 400 million people**, about half of them in **Nigeria**, Africa's most populous country.
- **Niger** and **Mali** are the largest countries by area; **The Gambia** is the smallest on the mainland.
- Three countries are **landlocked**, with no coast at all: **Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger**. They depend on neighbours' ports such as Dakar, Abidjan, Tema, Lomé and Cotonou.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not confuse **West Africa** with **ECOWAS**. West Africa is a **geographical region** of sixteen countries. ECOWAS is an **economic organisation** whose membership has changed: Mauritania left in 2000, and Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger left in 2025.
:::

## Relief

West Africa is mostly a **plateau** of old, hard rocks between about **300 and 600 m** high, with coastal lowlands and a few highlands.

| Relief feature | Location and notes |
|---|---|
| **Coastal plains** | a narrow lowland along the coast, with lagoons (Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Benin, Nigeria), mangrove swamps and sand bars |
| **Fouta Djallon** | highlands in **Guinea**; the source of the **Niger, Senegal and Gambia**, so called the "water tower of West Africa" |
| **Guinea Highlands** | south-eastern Guinea and neighbouring countries, including **Mount Nimba** on the Guinea–Côte d'Ivoire–Liberia border and the **Loma Mountains** in Sierra Leone |
| **Jos Plateau** | central **Nigeria**, about 1,200 m; tin mining, cool climate |
| **Aïr Mountains** | northern **Niger**, rising from the Sahara |
| **Atakora Mountains** | northern **Togo and Benin** |
| **Adamawa and Cameroon Highlands** | on the Nigeria–Cameroon border |
| **Basins and lowlands** | the **Inland Niger Delta** in Mali, the **Sokoto** and **Chad** basins, the **Niger Delta** in southern Nigeria |
| **Sahara** | sand seas (ergs), rocky plateaus and mountains in the north |

## Drainage

| River or lake | Notes |
|---|---|
| **River Niger** | about **4,180 km** long, the longest river in West Africa and third longest in Africa; rises in the **Guinea Highlands** near the Sierra Leone border, flows **north-east** to the **Inland Delta** and **Timbuktu** in Mali, then **south-east** through Niger and Nigeria; joins the **Benue** at **Lokoja** and enters the Gulf of Guinea through the **Niger Delta**; **Kainji**, **Jebba** and **Shiroro** dams |
| **River Benue** | main tributary of the Niger, rising in Cameroon |
| **River Senegal** | rises in the Fouta Djallon, flows north-west to the Atlantic at **Saint-Louis**; forms the Senegal–Mauritania border; **Manantali** dam in Mali |
| **River Gambia** | rises in the Fouta Djallon; navigable far inland; The Gambia is built along it |
| **River Volta** | formed from the **Black, White and Red Volta**; dammed at **Akosombo** to form **Lake Volta** |
| **Lake Chad** | a shallow lake shared by **Nigeria, Niger, Chad and Cameroon**; has **shrunk greatly since the 1960s** because of drought and water use |

**Importance of the rivers:** water for irrigation and homes, **fishing**, **transport** on navigable stretches, **hydroelectric power**, fertile **floodplains** and deltas for farming, and **tourism**.

**Problems:** rapids and waterfalls, seasonal low water, sandbars at the mouths, waterborne diseases and floods.

:::callout{type=local-example}
The **River Niger** rises in the **Guinea Highlands** very close to Sierra Leone's north-eastern border, yet it flows away from the sea, north-east into the Sahel, before turning south to reach the ocean in Nigeria, over 4,000 km later.
:::

## Key points

- West Africa: **16 countries**, about **6 million km²**, **over 400 million people** (half in Nigeria).
- Landlocked: **Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger**.
- Relief: low **plateau** with **Fouta Djallon**, **Guinea Highlands** (Nimba, Loma), **Jos Plateau**, **Aïr**, **Atakora**; coastal plains, inland basins, deltas.
- Rivers: **Niger** (4,180 km, with the **Benue**), **Senegal**, **Gambia**, **Volta**; **Lake Chad**, **Lake Volta**.

## Quick check

1. Name the three landlocked countries of West Africa.
2. What is the capital of Nigeria?
3. Why is the Fouta Djallon called the "water tower of West Africa"?
4. Where do the Rivers Niger and Benue meet?
5. Give three uses of West African rivers.

**Answers:**

1. Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger.
2. Abuja.
3. Because the Niger, Senegal and Gambia rivers rise there.
4. At Lokoja, in Nigeria.
5. Any three: irrigation; domestic water; fishing; transport; hydroelectric power; fertile floodplains; tourism.
