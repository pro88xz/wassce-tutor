# Earth movements: rotation, revolution, seasons and time

## Learning objectives
You should be able to distinguish rotation from revolution, explain day and night, account for seasons, and calculate local time using longitude.

## Rotation and its effects
Rotation is the spinning of the Earth on its axis from west to east. One complete rotation relative to the Sun takes approximately 24 hours. Because the Earth rotates eastward, the Sun appears to move across the sky from east to west.

The half of the Earth facing the Sun experiences daylight; the opposite half experiences night. The boundary between them is the circle of illumination. Rotation also causes the apparent daily movement of the Sun and stars and produces differences in local time between longitudes.

The Earth's axis is tilted about 23.5° from the perpendicular to the plane of its orbit. This tilt is central to the seasonal pattern.

## Revolution and the seasons
Revolution is the Earth's annual movement around the Sun. One orbit takes approximately 365¼ days. The extra quarter-day accumulates, so an additional day is included in February in a leap year under the Gregorian calendar's century rules.

Seasons are caused mainly by the axial tilt, not by large changes in the Earth's distance from the Sun. As the Earth revolves, each hemisphere alternately tilts towards and away from the Sun. A hemisphere tilted towards the Sun receives more direct rays and has longer daylight hours; it experiences summer. The hemisphere tilted away receives less direct energy and has shorter days; it experiences winter.

At the equinoxes, around March and September, the Sun is overhead at the Equator and day and night are approximately equal worldwide. At the solstices, around June and December, the Sun is overhead at the Tropic of Cancer and Tropic of Capricorn respectively. The dates and exact solar positions vary slightly from year to year.

In tropical West Africa, seasonal rainfall patterns are also strongly influenced by the movement of the Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone and regional atmospheric circulation. The four-season temperate model should not be applied uncritically to Sierra Leone.

## Longitude and local time
The Earth rotates through 360° in 24 hours:
- 15° of longitude corresponds to 1 hour.
- 1° corresponds to 4 minutes.
- Places east are ahead in local solar time; places west are behind.

**Worked example:** Place A is at 0° longitude and local solar time is 12:00 noon. Place B is at 30°E. Time difference = 30 × 4 = 120 minutes = 2 hours. B is east, so its local solar time is 14:00 (2:00 p.m.).

If B were at 30°W, the local solar time would be 10:00 a.m. Always establish east or west before adding or subtracting.

## Standard time and time zones
Countries adopt standard time for administration, transport and communication. A time zone may cover a broad longitude range and does not always follow exact meridians because political boundaries and practical needs influence its shape. Greenwich Mean Time is based on the Prime Meridian at Greenwich; Coordinated Universal Time is the modern international reference.

## WASSCE examination focus
A common error is to attribute seasons to the Earth being nearer the Sun. Explain axial tilt and changing solar angle and day length. For time calculations, show the longitude difference, convert degrees to time at four minutes per degree, and state whether the destination is east or west.

## Quick check
1. Distinguish rotation from revolution.
2. Why do seasons occur?
3. What is the time difference across 45° longitude?
4. If it is 09:00 at 15°E, what is local solar time at 15°W?

**Answers:** 1. Rotation is spinning on the axis; revolution is orbital movement around the Sun. 2. The tilted axis changes solar angle and daylight duration through the year. 3. 45 × 4 = 180 minutes = 3 hours. 4. The difference is 30° = 120 minutes; 15°W is west and therefore behind: 07:00.
