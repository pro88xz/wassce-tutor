Should Sierra Leone let cheap imported rice, cement and clothing flow in freely, or tax them to protect local farmers and factories? Every government weighs the gains of free trade against the case for protection. This lesson covers both sides, the tools of protection, and the pattern of Sierra Leone's own trade.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the advantages and disadvantages of international trade
- explain the arguments for and against protection
- describe the methods of protection
- describe the structure of Sierra Leone's exports and imports and the problems of a primary-producing country

## Advantages and disadvantages of international trade

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Countries obtain goods they cannot produce (fuel, machinery) | Dependence on other countries for essentials such as food and fuel |
| Specialisation raises world output and lowers costs | Local industries may collapse in the face of cheap imports |
| Larger markets allow economies of scale | Over-specialisation in a few exports makes earnings unstable |
| Earns foreign exchange through exports | Imported inflation and recessions spread from abroad |
| Competition improves quality and efficiency | Dumping of cheap or low-quality goods |
| Spreads technology and ideas | Balance of payments deficits if imports exceed exports |
| Promotes friendly relations between countries | Exhaustion of natural resources for export |

## Free trade and protection

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Free trade** is international trade with **no restrictions** such as tariffs or quotas.
- **Protection** is the use of **restrictions on imports** (and sometimes help to exporters) to shield home industries from foreign competition.
:::

### Arguments for protection

1. **Infant industry**: new local industries need temporary protection until they are big and efficient enough to compete.
2. **Employment**: protecting home industries saves local jobs.
3. **Balance of payments**: cutting imports reduces a deficit.
4. **Government revenue**: import duties are an important source of revenue.
5. **Anti-dumping**: preventing foreign firms from selling below cost to destroy local producers.
6. **Strategic industries**: food, defence and energy should not depend entirely on foreign supply.
7. **Diversification**: protection can help a country develop new industries instead of depending on a few exports.
8. **Discouraging harmful goods**: high duties on tobacco or alcohol.

### Arguments against protection (for free trade)

1. It **raises prices** and reduces choice for consumers.
2. It shelters **inefficient** firms, which may never "grow up".
3. Other countries may **retaliate**, hurting exporters.
4. It **reduces the gains** from specialisation and comparative advantage.
5. It encourages **smuggling** and corruption.

## Methods of protection

| Method | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Tariffs (customs duties)** | A tax on imports raises their price, making local goods more competitive |
| **Quotas** | A limit on the **quantity** of a good that may be imported |
| **Embargo** | A complete **ban** on imports of a good (or from a country) |
| **Exchange control** | Limiting the foreign currency available to pay for imports |
| **Subsidies to local producers** | Lower their costs so they can compete with imports |
| **Import licensing** | Importers must obtain government permission |
| **Administrative barriers** | Strict standards, health rules and slow customs procedures |
| **Devaluation** | Makes all imports dearer and exports cheaper (see *Exchange Rates*) |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A tariff of 20% on imported rice.** Imported rice costs NLe 600 a bag landed at the port. With the tariff it sells for at least $600 \times 1.2 = $ NLe 720. Local rice selling at NLe 700 can now compete. Consumers pay more, local farmers gain, and the government earns NLe 120 on each imported bag.
:::

## Sierra Leone's foreign trade

| Main exports | Main imports |
|---|---|
| **Minerals**: iron ore, rutile, bauxite, diamonds, gold | **Food**, especially rice |
| **Agricultural products**: cocoa, coffee, palm products, cashew | **Petroleum products** (fuel) |
| Fish and timber | **Machinery, vehicles and equipment** |
| | Manufactured goods: cement, building materials, textiles, medicines |

**Main features:** exports are mostly **primary products**, often exported raw; imports are mostly **manufactured goods, fuel and food**; trade is often in **deficit**; and earnings depend heavily on **world mineral prices**.

### Problems of a primary-producing country

1. **Unstable export earnings**: world prices of minerals and cash crops swing widely.
2. **Worsening terms of trade**: export prices may fall relative to the prices of imported manufactures.
3. **Low value added**: raw materials are processed abroad, where the jobs and profits are created.
4. **Dependence on a few exports and a few buyers**.
5. **Trade barriers** in rich countries against processed goods.
6. **Competition from synthetic substitutes** (for example synthetic rubber).

**Remedies:** diversify exports; process raw materials at home; join regional markets such as ECOWAS; commodity agreements; improve quality and marketing; develop agriculture to cut food imports.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A tariff is a **tax** on imports; a quota is a **limit on quantity**. A tariff raises government revenue; a quota does not (unless licences are sold).
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Tariff** (customs duty): a tax on imports that raises their price.
- **Quota**: a limit on the quantity of a good that may be imported.
- **Embargo**: a complete ban on imports of a good or from a country.
- **Infant industry**: a new local industry that needs temporary protection until it is big and efficient enough to compete.
- **Dumping**: selling goods abroad below their cost or home price.
:::

## Key points

- Trade brings **variety, specialisation, larger markets and foreign exchange**, but also **dependence and competition** for local firms.
- **Protection** is used for infant industries, jobs, revenue, the balance of payments and against dumping; it raises prices and shelters inefficiency.
- Methods: **tariffs, quotas, embargoes, exchange control, subsidies, licences**.
- Sierra Leone exports **minerals and cash crops** and imports **food, fuel and manufactures**.
