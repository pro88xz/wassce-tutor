The **Organisation of African Unity (OAU)** was founded at **Addis Ababa, Ethiopia**, on **25 May 1963** by **32 independent African states** to promote **unity and solidarity**, defend their **sovereignty and independence**, and **free the rest of Africa from colonial rule and apartheid**. It helped liberate southern Africa but was criticised as a "**club of heads of state**" that could not stop **civil wars, coups and dictatorships** because of its principle of **non-interference**. In **2002** it was replaced by the **African Union (AU)**, launched at **Durban, South Africa**, which has stronger organs and the right to **intervene in cases of genocide and war crimes**. This lesson explains the **aims, organs, achievements and problems of the OAU** and how the **African Union** differs.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain how and why the OAU was formed
- state the aims and principles of the OAU
- describe the main organs of the OAU
- explain the achievements and failures of the OAU
- explain why the OAU was replaced by the African Union
- describe the aims and organs of the AU and compare it with the OAU

## Formation of the OAU

1. Newly independent states wanted to **work together** and speak with one voice.
2. **Colonial rule** continued in Portuguese colonies, **Rhodesia**, **South West Africa (Namibia)** and **apartheid South Africa**.
3. The **Casablanca** and **Monrovia** groups needed to be reconciled.
4. Emperor **Haile Selassie** of Ethiopia hosted the **Addis Ababa summit**, where the **Charter** was signed on **25 May 1963**, now celebrated as **Africa Day**.

## Aims and principles of the OAU

**Aims (from the Charter):**

1. To promote the **unity and solidarity** of African states.
2. To **coordinate cooperation** to improve the lives of Africans.
3. To **defend the sovereignty, territorial integrity and independence** of member states.
4. To **eradicate all forms of colonialism** from Africa.
5. To promote **international cooperation**, respecting the UN Charter and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

**Principles:**

1. **Sovereign equality** of all member states.
2. **Non-interference** in the internal affairs of states.
3. Respect for the **territorial integrity** of each state.
4. **Peaceful settlement of disputes**.
5. Condemnation of **political assassination** and subversion.
6. Dedication to the **total liberation** of dependent African territories.
7. **Non-alignment** with the Cold War blocs.

| OAU organ | Function |
|---|---|
| **Assembly of Heads of State and Government** | supreme organ; met yearly; made policy |
| **Council of Ministers** | foreign ministers; prepared the Assembly's work and carried out its decisions |
| **General Secretariat** | headed by a **Secretary-General** at Addis Ababa; ran the OAU's daily work |
| **Commission of Mediation, Conciliation and Arbitration** | to settle disputes between members peacefully |
| **Liberation Committee** | based in **Dar es Salaam**; aided **liberation movements** with money, arms and training |

## Achievements of the OAU

1. **Liberation of Africa:** it supported liberation movements in **Angola, Mozambique, Guinea-Bissau, Zimbabwe (1980), Namibia (1990)** and the end of **apartheid in South Africa (1994)**.
2. **Opposition to apartheid:** it led campaigns for sanctions and the isolation of South Africa.
3. **Settling borders:** in **1964** it agreed that colonial borders should be **respected**, preventing many border wars.
4. **Mediation** in some disputes, such as the **Algeria–Morocco** border war of 1963.
5. **A common African voice** at the **United Nations** and in world affairs.
6. **Refugees:** the **1969 Refugee Convention** protected African refugees.
7. **Economic cooperation:** the **Lagos Plan of Action (1980)** and the **Abuja Treaty (1991)** planned an African Economic Community.
8. **Human rights:** the **African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights (1981)**.

## Failures and problems of the OAU

1. **Non-interference** stopped it acting against **dictators, coups and civil wars**, such as in Nigeria (1967–70), Uganda under **Idi Amin**, Liberia and **Sierra Leone (1991–2002)**, and the **Rwandan genocide (1994)**.
2. **Lack of funds**: many members did not pay their dues.
3. **No army** to enforce its decisions or keep the peace.
4. **Divisions** among members: Cold War alliances, French- and English-speaking blocs, and disputes such as **Western Sahara**, which caused **Morocco to leave in 1984**.
5. **Neo-colonialism**: members depended on former colonial powers.
6. It became known as a "**club of heads of state**" more concerned with protecting rulers than people.

## The African Union

The **Sirte Declaration (1999)** called for a new body. The **Constitutive Act** was adopted at **Lomé (2000)**, and the **African Union** was launched at **Durban on 9 July 2002**, with its headquarters at **Addis Ababa**. It now has **55 member states**. **Morocco rejoined in 2017**.

**Aims of the AU include:** greater **unity**; defence of sovereignty; **economic integration**; **peace, security and stability**; **democracy, good governance and human rights**; and sustainable development. Its long-term plan is **Agenda 2063**.

| Point | OAU | AU |
|---|---|---|
| **Main focus** | **liberation** from colonialism and apartheid | **development, integration, peace and good governance** |
| **Intervention** | strict **non-interference** | **right to intervene** in grave circumstances: **genocide, war crimes and crimes against humanity** (Article 4(h)) |
| **Unconstitutional change** | coups often tolerated | members whose governments take power **unconstitutionally** are **suspended** |
| **Organs** | few, weak | many: **Pan-African Parliament**, **Peace and Security Council**, **African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights**, AU Commission |
| **Peacekeeping** | none | AU missions, such as in **Burundi, Darfur and Somalia** |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone was a **founding member of the OAU** in 1963. During the civil war (1991–2002) the OAU itself could do little, but **ECOWAS** and its monitoring group **ECOMOG**, together with the UN, intervened. Sierra Leone has since taken part in AU affairs: President **Ernest Bai Koroma** coordinated the AU's **Committee of Ten** on UN Security Council reform, and Sierra Leonean troops served in the AU mission in **Somalia (AMISOM)**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The OAU was **not** founded in Accra. It was founded at **Addis Ababa** in **1963**. Accra hosted the **All-African Peoples' Conference** in 1958, and the AU was launched in **Durban** in 2002.
:::

## Key figures and terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figures and terms"}
- **Organisation of African Unity (OAU)**: the organisation of independent African states founded on 25 May 1963 in Addis Ababa by 32 states.
- **African Union (AU)**: the body that replaced the OAU, launched on 9 July 2002 in Durban, which can intervene in cases of genocide and war crimes.
- **Agenda 2063**: the African Union's long-term plan for the development and unity of Africa.
:::

## Key points

- **OAU**: founded **25 May 1963**, **Addis Ababa**, **32 states**; aims: **unity, sovereignty, end of colonialism**.
- Organs: **Assembly, Council of Ministers, Secretariat, Commission of Mediation, Liberation Committee**.
- Achievements: **liberation of southern Africa, opposition to apartheid, acceptance of borders**, a common voice.
- Failures: **non-interference, lack of money and an army, divisions**, failure to stop wars and dictators.
- **AU**: launched **9 July 2002**, **Durban**; can **intervene in genocide and war crimes**; suspends coup governments; **Agenda 2063**.

2. Give three aims of the OAU.

6. Give two differences between the OAU and the AU.

**Answers:**

1. On 25 May 1963, at Addis Ababa, Ethiopia.
2. Any three: to promote unity and solidarity; to coordinate cooperation for better lives; to defend sovereignty and independence; to eradicate colonialism; to promote international cooperation.
3. It aided liberation movements fighting colonial and white-minority rule with money, arms and training.
4. Any two: liberation of southern Africa; opposition to apartheid; acceptance of colonial borders; mediation in some disputes; a common voice at the UN; the 1969 Refugee Convention; the African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights.
5. Because of its principle of non-interference in members' internal affairs, its lack of money and its lack of an army.
