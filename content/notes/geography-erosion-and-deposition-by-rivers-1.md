# River processes: erosion, transportation and deposition

## Learning objectives
You should be able to explain how rivers erode, transport and deposit material, identify the main processes involved and relate them to changes along a river course.

## The river system
A river is a natural channel through which water flows from a source towards a mouth, usually entering a lake, sea or another river. The area drained by a river and its tributaries is a drainage basin. A watershed is the higher land that separates neighbouring drainage basins.

Rivers change their channels and valleys through three connected processes: erosion, transportation and deposition. Their relative importance depends on discharge, velocity, gradient, channel shape, sediment load, geology and vegetation.

## River erosion
- **Hydraulic action:** the force of moving water compresses air in cracks and loosens material from the bed and banks.
- **Abrasion or corrasion:** sediment carried by the river scrapes and grinds the bed and banks.
- **Attrition:** transported rock fragments collide and become smaller and rounder.
- **Solution or corrosion:** soluble minerals dissolve in river water and are carried in solution.

Vertical erosion tends to deepen a valley, while lateral erosion widens the channel and valley. The balance changes downstream and with local conditions.

## River transportation
A river transports its load in several ways:
- **Traction:** large stones roll or slide along the bed.
- **Saltation:** smaller pebbles bounce along the bed.
- **Suspension:** fine particles are carried within the water.
- **Solution:** dissolved minerals are carried as ions.

The river's total load depends on the amount and size of material supplied and the river's capacity to transport it. Competence refers to the largest particle size a river can move; capacity refers to the total quantity of sediment it can carry.

## River deposition
Deposition occurs when a river loses energy or competence and can no longer carry some of its load. This may happen when velocity falls, discharge decreases, the channel becomes shallower or the river enters still water. Coarser material is commonly deposited before finer sediment, although local flow conditions affect the pattern.

## Changes along the river
In the upper course, steep gradients and energetic flows often produce vertical erosion, narrow V-shaped valleys, interlocking spurs, waterfalls and rapids. In the middle course, lateral erosion and transportation become more prominent; meanders and wider valleys may develop. In the lower course, deposition becomes important and floodplains, levees and deltas may form where conditions allow.

These are broad tendencies, not rigid rules. A river may erode, transport and deposit at different points along the same reach during a flood.

## WASSCE examination focus
Distinguish the four transportation methods and the four erosion processes. When explaining deposition, identify the loss of energy or competence. Use labelled diagrams to show the direction of flow and the location of landforms.

## Quick check
1. Define a drainage basin.
2. Distinguish abrasion from attrition.
3. Name the four methods of river transportation.
4. Why does deposition often occur when a river enters a lake or sea?

**Answers:** 1. The area drained by a river and its tributaries. 2. Abrasion is scraping of the bed and banks by load; attrition is collision between transported particles. 3. Traction, saltation, suspension and solution. 4. Flow commonly loses velocity and energy as it enters still water, so sediment is deposited.
