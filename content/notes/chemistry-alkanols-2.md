A roadside breath test, a bottle of hand sanitiser, the fuel in a spirit lamp and the smell of nail-varnish remover all depend on reactions of ethanol. Most of its reactions involve the **–OH group**: the O–H bond can break (with sodium), the C–OH carbon can be oxidised, or the whole –OH can be removed (dehydration).

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the combustion of ethanol
- describe the reactions of ethanol with sodium, oxidising agents, concentrated sulphuric acid and alkanoic acids
- give tests for alkanols and distinguish primary, secondary and tertiary alkanols
- state the uses of ethanol and methanol
- explain the effects of alcohol on health

## Reactions of ethanol

![Ethanol in the centre: with sodium it gives sodium ethoxide and hydrogen; with acidified dichromate it is oxidised to ethanoic acid; with concentrated sulphuric acid at 170 °C it gives ethene; with ethanoic acid it gives ethyl ethanoate](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/ethanol-reactions.svg)

*Figure 1. Four key reactions of ethanol. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

### 1. Combustion

Ethanol burns with a **clean, pale blue, non-smoky flame**, releasing much heat:

$$\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 3\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CO}_2 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O} \qquad \Delta H = -1371 \text{ kJ/mol}$$

### 2. Sodium

Sodium **fizzes** steadily in ethanol (less violently than in water), giving **hydrogen** and **sodium ethoxide**:

$$2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 2\text{Na} \rightarrow 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{ONa} + \text{H}_2$$

This reaction is a test for an **–OH group**.

### 3. Oxidation

Warm ethanol with **acidified potassium dichromate(VI)**. The solution changes from **orange to green** (Cr₂O₇²⁻ → Cr³⁺). Ethanol is oxidised first to **ethanal** (an aldehyde), then to **ethanoic acid**:

$$\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + [\text{O}] \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CHO} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

$$\text{CH}_3\text{CHO} + [\text{O}] \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$$

- **Distil off** the product as it forms to collect **ethanal** (bp 21 °C).
- **Heat under reflux** with excess oxidant to obtain **ethanoic acid**.
- **Acidified KMnO₄** also oxidises ethanol: **purple → colourless**.
- **Secondary** alkanols give **alkanones** (propan-2-ol → propanone); **tertiary** alkanols are not oxidised, so the dichromate **stays orange**.

The old police **breathalyser** used this colour change: ethanol in the breath turned orange dichromate crystals green.

### 4. Dehydration

| Conditions | Product |
|---|---|
| **Excess conc. H₂SO₄, 170 °C** (or hot Al₂O₃) | **Ethene**, C₂H₄ |
| **Excess ethanol, conc. H₂SO₄, 140 °C** | **Ethoxyethane** (ether), C₂H₅OC₂H₅ |

$$2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OC}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

### 5. Esterification

Ethanol reacts with **ethanoic acid**, with a little **concentrated sulphuric acid** as catalyst, to form a sweet-smelling **ester**, **ethyl ethanoate**:

$$\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{CH}_3\text{COOH} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

## Tests for alkanols

| Test | Observation |
|---|---|
| **Sodium metal** | Effervescence; hydrogen gas "pops" with a lighted splint |
| **Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, warm** | Orange → green (primary and secondary only) |
| **Ethanoic acid + conc. H₂SO₄, warm** | Sweet, fruity smell of an ester |
| **Iodoform test** (iodine + NaOH, warm) | **Yellow precipitate** of triiodomethane (CHI₃) with an antiseptic smell: given by **ethanol** but **not methanol** |

## Uses of ethanol and methanol

**Ethanol:**

- **solvent** for perfumes, medicines, varnishes, inks and dyes
- **fuel**, alone or blended with petrol (gasohol), and in spirit lamps
- **alcoholic drinks** (beer, wine, spirits)
- **antiseptic** (70% ethanol kills bacteria; hand sanitisers)
- **methylated spirit**: ethanol made undrinkable with methanol and a purple dye, used as fuel and cleaner
- making **ethanoic acid**, esters and ethoxyethane
- liquid in thermometers for low temperatures (freezes at −114 °C)

**Methanol:** solvent, fuel, and raw material for **methanal** (formaldehyde) plastics. Methanol is **very poisonous**: as little as 10 cm³ can cause **blindness**, and more can kill.

## Ethanol and health

Ethanol is a **depressant**: it slows the brain and nervous system. Small amounts reduce judgement and slow reactions, which is why drinking and driving is dangerous. Long-term heavy drinking damages the **liver** (cirrhosis), brain and heart, and leads to addiction. Drinks contaminated with methanol cause blindness and death.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Identifying an alkanol.** An alkanol X, C₃H₈O, is oxidised by acidified dichromate to Y, which does **not** react with sodium carbonate. Identify X and Y.

- Y does not react with Na₂CO₃, so Y is **not an acid**: it is a ketone.
- Only a **secondary** alkanol gives a ketone, so X is **propan-2-ol**, CH₃CH(OH)CH₃.
- Y is **propanone**, CH₃COCH₃.

(If X were propan-1-ol, it would give propanoic acid, which fizzes with Na₂CO₃.)
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
During the Ebola and COVID-19 outbreaks, hand-washing stations and **alcohol-based hand sanitisers** became common in Sierra Leonean schools, markets and clinics. Sanitisers work best with about **60–80% ethanol** (or propan-2-ol). Pure ethanol evaporates too fast, and too little alcohol does not kill germs.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not write **NaOH** as the product of sodium and ethanol. The product is **sodium ethoxide**, C₂H₅ONa, and the gas is **hydrogen**.
:::

## Key points

- Ethanol **burns** with a clean blue flame: C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O.
- **Sodium** → sodium ethoxide + **hydrogen** (test for –OH).
- **Oxidation** (acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, orange → green): ethanol → **ethanal** → **ethanoic acid**; secondary → ketone; tertiary not oxidised.
- **Conc. H₂SO₄:** 170 °C → **ethene**; 140 °C → **ethoxyethane**.
- With **alkanoic acids** → **esters** (conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst).
- Uses: **solvent, fuel, drinks, antiseptic**; methanol is **poisonous**.

## Quick check

1. What gas is evolved when sodium reacts with ethanol, and how is it tested?
2. State the colour change when ethanol is warmed with acidified potassium dichromate(VI).
3. Name the product of heating ethanol with excess concentrated sulphuric acid at 170 °C.
4. Why is methylated spirit coloured purple?

**Answers:**

1. Hydrogen; it burns with a "pop" when a lighted splint is applied.
2. Orange to green.
3. Ethene.
4. To warn people that it is poisonous and must not be drunk (it contains methanol).
