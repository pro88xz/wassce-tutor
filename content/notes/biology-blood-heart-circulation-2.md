# Blood, heart and circulation: the mammalian heart and double circulation

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- describe the structure of the mammalian heart
- trace the flow of blood through the heart, lungs and body
- explain the functions of arteries, veins and capillaries
- distinguish pulmonary and systemic circulation
- explain how exercise affects circulation

## Structure of the heart

The mammalian heart is a muscular organ in the thoracic cavity, between the lungs. A septum separates its right and left sides, preventing oxygenated blood from mixing directly with deoxygenated blood. Each side has an atrium (upper receiving chamber) and a ventricle (lower pumping chamber).

The right atrium receives deoxygenated blood from the body through the venae cavae. It passes through the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle. The right ventricle pumps blood through the pulmonary artery to the lungs. In the lungs, carbon dioxide leaves the blood and oxygen enters it. Oxygenated blood returns through the pulmonary veins to the left atrium, passes through the bicuspid (mitral) valve into the left ventricle, and is pumped through the aorta to the rest of the body.

The left ventricular wall is thicker than the right ventricular wall because it must generate enough pressure to send blood around the entire body. The right ventricle pumps blood only to the nearby lungs and therefore works at lower pressure. Valves prevent backward flow and help maintain one-way movement.

## The cardiac cycle

The cardiac cycle is the sequence of contraction and relaxation during one heartbeat. During atrial systole, the atria contract and push blood into the ventricles. During ventricular systole, the ventricles contract; the atrioventricular valves close and blood is forced into the pulmonary artery and aorta. During diastole, the heart muscle relaxes and the chambers fill with blood.

The familiar heartbeat sounds are mainly associated with valve closure. The first sound is associated with closure of the atrioventricular valves; the second with closure of the semilunar valves. The heart's rhythm is coordinated by specialised cardiac tissue.

## Double circulation

Mammals have a double circulatory system because blood passes through the heart twice during one complete circuit.

- **Pulmonary circulation:** right ventricle → lungs → left atrium. It carries blood between the heart and lungs for gas exchange.
- **Systemic circulation:** left ventricle → body tissues → right atrium. It supplies tissues with oxygen and nutrients and collects carbon dioxide and other wastes.

Double circulation allows blood to be pumped to the lungs at a lower pressure, protecting delicate lung capillaries, and to the body at a higher pressure for efficient delivery. It also keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood separate.

## Blood vessels

Arteries carry blood away from the heart. Their walls are thick, muscular and elastic to withstand high pressure. Most arteries carry oxygenated blood, but the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs.

Veins carry blood towards the heart. They have thinner walls and a wider lumen than comparable arteries because blood pressure is lower. Many veins, especially in the limbs, contain valves that prevent backflow. Most veins carry deoxygenated blood, but pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart.

Capillaries are microscopic vessels linking arterioles and venules. Their walls are one cell thick, allowing exchange of gases, nutrients and wastes between blood and tissues. Their extensive networks provide a large surface area and bring blood close to cells.

## Pulse, blood pressure and exercise

A pulse is the pressure wave produced when the heart pumps blood into elastic arteries. Pulse rate is commonly used as an estimate of heart rate. Blood pressure is the force exerted by circulating blood on vessel walls. It is usually recorded as systolic pressure over diastolic pressure.

During exercise, working muscles use more oxygen and glucose and produce more carbon dioxide and heat. Heart rate and the force of contraction increase, raising cardiac output. Blood flow is redistributed so active muscles receive more oxygen and nutrients. Breathing rate also rises to support gas exchange.

## WASSCE examination focus

Learn the direction of blood flow and label the chambers, valves and major vessels accurately. Do not reverse the pulmonary artery and pulmonary vein. Arteries are defined by carrying blood away from the heart, not simply by carrying oxygenated blood. Explain how vessel structure relates to pressure and function.
