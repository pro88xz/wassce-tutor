:::callout{type=definition}
A **drainage pattern** is the shape formed by a river and its tributaries on a map.
:::

The six main **types of drainage patterns** are **dendritic** (branching like a tree), **trellis** (a rectangular grid), **radial** (flowing outwards from a central high point), **centripetal** (flowing inwards to a basin), **rectangular** (following right-angled joints) and **parallel**. Each reflects the rocks and slope beneath. This lesson explains drainage basins, the six patterns, how lakes are formed, and the rivers and lakes of Sierra Leone and West Africa.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a drainage basin and label its features
- describe and explain the six main drainage patterns
- classify lakes by their mode of formation, with examples
- state the uses of rivers and lakes
- describe the main rivers of Sierra Leone and West Africa

## The drainage basin

:::callout{type=definition}
A **drainage basin** (river basin or catchment) is the area of land drained by a river and its tributaries. It is separated from neighbouring basins by high ground called the **watershed** (divide).
:::

**Features:** the **source** (where the river begins), **tributaries** (smaller rivers joining the main river), **confluences** (where rivers meet), the **main channel**, the **mouth** (where the river enters the sea or a lake) and the **watershed**.

## Drainage patterns

![Six drainage patterns: dendritic (branching), trellis (rectangular grid), radial (outwards from a peak), centripetal (inwards to a lake), rectangular (right-angled bends) and parallel](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/drainage-patterns.svg)

*Figure 1. The six main drainage patterns. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Pattern | Description | Where it develops |
|---|---|---|
| **Dendritic** | tributaries join the main river at acute angles, branching like a tree | rocks of even resistance (granite, clay); the most common pattern |
| **Trellis** | main rivers run parallel, joined at right angles by short tributaries | alternating bands of hard and soft rock, often in folded areas |
| **Radial** | rivers flow outwards in all directions from a central high point | volcanic cones and dome-shaped uplands, such as Mount Cameroon |
| **Centripetal** | rivers flow inwards from all directions into a central basin or lake | basins and craters, such as the rivers draining into Lake Chad |
| **Rectangular** | rivers make sharp right-angled bends | rocks with a grid of joints or faults |
| **Parallel** | rivers run side by side | a uniform, steady slope |

## Lakes

:::callout{type=definition}
A **lake** is a large body of water occupying a hollow (basin) in the land surface. Lakes are classified by how their basins were formed.
:::

| Type | Formation | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Tectonic (rift valley) lakes** | water fills a basin formed by faulting | Lakes Tanganyika and Malawi (long and deep) |
| **Volcanic (crater) lakes** | water fills the crater of an extinct volcano | Lake Nyos (Cameroon), Lake Bosomtwe (Ghana, a meteorite crater) |
| **Down-warped lakes** | a shallow basin formed by sagging of the crust | Lake Victoria |
| **Glacial lakes** | basins scoured or dammed by ice | the Great Lakes of North America, tarns in corries |
| **River lakes** | formed by rivers | ox-bow lakes on floodplains |
| **Coastal lakes (lagoons)** | water cut off from the sea by a bar or spit | Lagos Lagoon, Keta Lagoon |
| **Artificial lakes (reservoirs)** | water held behind a dam | **Volta Lake** (Akosombo, Ghana), **Kainji** (Nigeria), the **Bumbuna** reservoir (Sierra Leone) |
| **Inland drainage lakes** | rivers drain into a basin with no outlet to the sea | Lake Chad |

## Uses of rivers and lakes

1. **Water supply** for homes, farms and industry.
2. **Hydroelectric power** from dams.
3. **Fishing**, a major source of protein and jobs.
4. **Transport** by boat and canoe.
5. **Irrigation** for crops.
6. **Tourism and recreation.**
7. **Moderating local climate.**

## Rivers of Sierra Leone and West Africa

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone is drained by about a dozen major rivers that flow generally **south-westwards** from the plateaus of the north and east to the Atlantic:

- the **Great Scarcies (Kolente)**, forming part of the boundary with Guinea, and the **Little Scarcies (Kaba)**
- the **Rokel (Seli)**, on which the **Bumbuna** hydroelectric dam stands, entering the sea through the **Sierra Leone River estuary**
- the **Jong (Taia)** and the **Sewa**, flowing south-west to the Sherbro coast
- the **Moa**, flowing through the east past the Gola forests
- the **Mano**, forming part of the boundary with Liberia

Their basins contain fertile **inland valley swamps** for rice, and alluvial diamond and gold deposits.
:::

West Africa's greatest river is the **Niger**, about 4,180 km long. It rises in the **Fouta Djallon** highlands of Guinea, close to the Sierra Leone border, flows north-east into Mali, turns south-east through Niger and Nigeria, and builds the **Niger Delta**. Other major rivers include the **Volta**, **Senegal** and **Gambia**.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Radial** drainage flows **outwards** from a high point; **centripetal** drainage flows **inwards** to a low basin. They are opposites.
:::

## Key points

- A **drainage basin** is the area drained by a river and its tributaries; the **watershed** separates basins.
- Patterns: **dendritic** (even rock), **trellis** (hard and soft bands), **radial** (from a peak), **centripetal** (into a basin), **rectangular** (joints), **parallel** (uniform slope).
- Lakes: **tectonic, volcanic, down-warped, glacial, river (ox-bow), coastal (lagoon), artificial, inland drainage**.
- Sierra Leone's rivers flow **south-west** to the Atlantic; the **Rokel** powers **Bumbuna**.
- The **Niger** rises in the **Fouta Djallon** and ends in the **Niger Delta**.

## Quick check

1. What is a watershed?
2. Which drainage pattern develops on alternating bands of hard and soft rock?
3. Where would you expect radial drainage?
4. How is a tectonic lake formed? Give an example.
5. On which river is the Bumbuna hydroelectric dam?

**Answers:**

1. High ground separating one drainage basin from another.
2. Trellis.
3. Around a volcanic cone or dome-shaped upland, such as Mount Cameroon.
4. Water fills a basin formed by faulting, such as Lake Tanganyika or Lake Malawi.
5. The Rokel (Seli) River.
