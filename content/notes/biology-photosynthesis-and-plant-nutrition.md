# Photosynthesis and plant nutrition: food production in green plants

## Learning objectives
You should be able to define photosynthesis; write its word and balanced symbol equations; explain the roles of light, chlorophyll, carbon dioxide and water; describe factors affecting the rate; and distinguish mineral nutrition from photosynthesis.

## What photosynthesis does
Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants and some other organisms use light energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into carbohydrates, releasing oxygen as a by-product. Light energy is transformed into chemical energy stored in organic molecules. The process occurs in chloroplasts, where chlorophyll absorbs light.

Word equation:

Carbon dioxide + water —(light energy and chlorophyll)→ glucose + oxygen

Balanced symbol equation:

6CO₂ + 6H₂O —(light energy)→ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

The equation summarises the overall process. In a leaf, carbon dioxide usually enters through stomata and diffuses through air spaces to photosynthetic cells. Water is absorbed by roots and transported through xylem. Light is captured by chlorophyll in chloroplast membranes.

## Uses of products
Glucose may be used in respiration, converted to sucrose for transport, stored as starch, or used to make cellulose and other organic compounds. Plants also require mineral ions from the soil to build proteins, nucleic acids and chlorophyll. Nitrate ions are needed to make amino acids and proteins. Magnesium ions are needed to form chlorophyll. Mineral ions are not a substitute for the carbon compounds made by photosynthesis; both sources of materials are required for growth.

## Factors affecting the rate
Light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature can limit photosynthesis. Increasing light intensity can increase the rate when light is limiting, but eventually another factor becomes limiting and the rate levels off. Increasing carbon dioxide concentration may increase the rate until another factor limits it. Photosynthesis is enzyme-controlled, so a rise in temperature can increase the rate up to an optimum; temperatures above the optimum can reduce the rate as enzymes lose their functional shape.

This is the principle of limiting factors: the factor in shortest effective supply relative to demand restricts the overall rate. A plant in bright light may still photosynthesise slowly if it lacks water or carbon dioxide.

## Demonstrating starch in a leaf
A common practical test first kills the leaf by heating it in water, then removes chlorophyll using ethanol in a hot water bath, rinses the leaf to soften it, and adds iodine solution. A blue-black colour indicates starch; iodine remains yellow-brown where starch is absent. Ethanol is flammable and must be heated using a water bath away from a naked flame. Decolourising the leaf makes the iodine colour easier to see.

To test the need for light, part of a destarched leaf can be covered with opaque material and the plant exposed to light. After testing for starch, the exposed region should turn blue-black while the covered region should not, provided the experimental controls are sound.

## WASSCE examination focus
Learn the equation, conditions and role of chlorophyll. For an experiment question, state the control, procedure, observation and conclusion. Distinguish gas exchange through stomata from water transport in xylem, and explain why starch is tested rather than simply saying “the leaf contains food”.

## Check your understanding
1. Write the word equation for photosynthesis.
2. Name the organelle and pigment involved.
3. Explain what is meant by a limiting factor.
4. Why is ethanol used in the starch test?
5. State the functions of nitrate and magnesium ions.

## Answers for self-assessment
1. Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, using light energy and chlorophyll.
2. Chloroplast; chlorophyll.
3. A factor in insufficient supply that restricts the rate when other conditions are suitable.
4. It removes chlorophyll so the iodine colour change can be observed clearly.
5. Nitrate is needed for amino acids and proteins; magnesium is needed for chlorophyll.
