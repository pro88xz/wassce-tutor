A river does three kinds of work: **erosion** (wearing away its bed and banks), **transportation** (carrying its load of rock fragments and dissolved material downstream) and **deposition** (dropping that load when it slows down). How much of each it does depends on its speed and volume, which change from the steep, fast **upper course** near the source to the slow, wide **lower course** near the mouth. This lesson explains the processes; the next shows the landforms they create.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a river and the main parts of a river system
- describe the four processes of river erosion
- describe the four ways a river transports its load
- explain when and why a river deposits its load
- describe the characteristics of the upper, middle and lower courses of a river

## The river system

:::callout{type=definition}
A **river** is a natural stream of fresh water flowing in a channel towards a sea, lake or another river. Its starting point is the **source**; the point where it enters the sea or a lake is the **mouth**; a smaller river that joins it is a **tributary**, and the place where two rivers join is a **confluence**.
:::

The area drained by a river and its tributaries is its **drainage basin**, and the high ground separating one basin from another is the **watershed** (divide).

## River erosion

| Process | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Hydraulic action** | the sheer force of moving water pushes into cracks in the banks, compresses air and loosens rock |
| **Abrasion (corrasion)** | stones carried by the river scrape and grind the bed and banks, like sandpaper |
| **Attrition** | stones knock against each other, becoming smaller and rounder |
| **Solution (corrosion)** | slightly acidic water dissolves soluble rocks such as limestone |

Erosion acts in two directions:

- **Vertical erosion** deepens the channel (strongest in the upper course).
- **Lateral erosion** widens it (strongest in the middle course, on the outside of bends).

**Headward erosion** cuts back towards the source, lengthening the valley.

## River transportation

The material a river carries is its **load**.

| Method | Load | How it moves |
|---|---|---|
| **Traction** | large boulders and stones | rolled along the bed |
| **Saltation** | pebbles and coarse sand | bounced along the bed in short hops |
| **Suspension** | fine sand, silt and clay | carried within the water, making it muddy |
| **Solution** | dissolved minerals | carried invisibly in the water |

The **capacity** of a river is the total load it can carry; its **competence** is the size of the largest particle it can move. Both rise sharply when the river is in **flood**.

## River deposition

A river **deposits** its load when it loses energy, that is, when its speed or volume falls. This happens:

1. on the **inside of bends**, where water flows more slowly
2. where the **gradient becomes gentle**, in the lower course
3. when the river **enters a lake or the sea** and slows suddenly
4. when the **volume falls** in the dry season or after a flood
5. when the load suddenly **increases**, for example after a landslide

The heaviest material is dropped first and the finest last, so deposits are **sorted** by size.

## The three courses of a river

![The long profile of a river from a steep source to a gently sloping mouth, divided into the upper course (vertical erosion, V-shaped valley, waterfalls), middle course (lateral erosion, meanders) and lower course (deposition, floodplain, levees, ox-bow lakes, delta)](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/river-long-profile.svg)

*Figure 1. The long profile of a river. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| | Upper course | Middle course | Lower course |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gradient** | steep | moderate | gentle |
| **Speed and volume** | fast, low volume | moderate, more volume | slow, large volume |
| **Main work** | vertical erosion | lateral erosion and some deposition | deposition |
| **Load** | large boulders | smaller stones and sand | fine silt and clay |
| **Valley shape** | narrow, steep **V-shape** | wider, open valley | broad, flat **floodplain** |
| **Landforms** | waterfalls, rapids, gorges, potholes, interlocking spurs | meanders, river cliffs, slip-off slopes | floodplains, levees, ox-bow lakes, deltas, estuaries |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's main rivers, including the **Rokel (Seli)**, **Moa**, **Sewa**, **Jong (Taia)**, **Mano**, and the **Great and Little Scarcies**, rise in the higher land of the north and east (some in Guinea) and flow generally **south-westwards** to the Atlantic. In the rainy season their volume and load rise dramatically, the water turns brown with **suspended silt**, and they carry the sand and gravel that wash diamonds into river beds in Kono and Kenema, where they are dug from **alluvial deposits**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Abrasion** and **attrition** are different. In abrasion the load wears away the **bed and banks**; in attrition the pieces of load wear **each other** down.
:::

## Key points

- Rivers **erode**, **transport** and **deposit**.
- Erosion: **hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, solution**; vertical in the upper course, lateral in the middle course.
- Transport: **traction, saltation, suspension, solution**; capacity and competence rise in flood.
- Deposition happens when a river **loses speed or volume**.
- Upper course: steep, V-shaped; middle: meanders; lower: floodplain and deposition.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **name and explain the processes of river erosion and transportation**, to **explain why rivers deposit their load**, and to **compare the upper, middle and lower courses**. A long-profile diagram or a table comparing the three courses scores well.
:::

## Quick check

1. Define the term "drainage basin".
2. What is the difference between abrasion and attrition?
3. How is fine silt carried by a river?
4. Give two reasons why a river deposits its load.
5. Which kind of erosion is strongest in the upper course?

**Answers:**

1. The area of land drained by a river and its tributaries.
2. Abrasion is the load wearing away the bed and banks; attrition is pieces of load wearing each other down.
3. In suspension.
4. Any two: it slows on the inside of a bend; the gradient becomes gentle; it enters a lake or the sea; its volume falls; its load suddenly increases.
5. Vertical erosion.
