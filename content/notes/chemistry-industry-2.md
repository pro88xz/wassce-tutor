You do not need a giant refinery to see chemical industry at work in Sierra Leone. Soap is boiled in yards, palm wine is fermented and distilled, salt is evaporated from the sea, beer is brewed in Freetown, and cement blocks are moulded on building sites. This lesson describes the chemistry of these local industries, and the problems and opportunities they face.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the chemistry of soap making, including traditional methods
- describe fermentation and the brewing of beer, and the making of palm wine and local spirits
- describe the production of salt
- outline the chemistry of paint making and cement block making
- state the problems facing local chemical industries and suggest solutions

## Soap making

Soap is made by boiling **fats or oils** with an **alkali**. This reaction is **saponification**:

$$\text{fat (or oil)} + \text{sodium hydroxide} \rightarrow \text{soap} + \text{glycerol}$$

**Factory or small-scale method:**

1. **Palm kernel oil, palm oil or coconut oil** is heated with **caustic soda** (sodium hydroxide) solution.
2. Common **salt** is added to make the soap separate out and float (**salting out**).
3. The soap is skimmed off, washed, and **perfume, colour and fillers** are added. It is moulded into bars and allowed to cure.
4. **Glycerol** (glycerine) is a valuable by-product, used in cosmetics and medicines.

**Traditional "country soap" (black soap):** the alkali comes from **wood ash, cocoa pods or plantain peel ash**. Water is filtered through the ash to make a lye of **potassium carbonate and hydroxide**, which is boiled with palm kernel oil. Potassium soaps are **softer** than sodium soaps.

The chemistry of soap and detergents is covered fully in the Soaps, Detergents and Oils topic.

## Fermentation and brewing

**Fermentation** is the breakdown of sugars by the **enzymes of yeast**, without oxygen, to give **ethanol and carbon(IV) oxide**:

$$\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6\text{(aq)} \rightarrow 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH(aq)} + 2\text{CO}_2\text{(g)}$$

It works best at about **30–35 °C**. It stops at about 14% ethanol, when the alcohol kills the yeast.

![Brewing beer: malting, mashing, boiling with hops, fermenting with yeast, maturing and bottling](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/brewing-flow.svg)

*Figure 1. The main stages of brewing beer. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Malting** | Barley (or sorghum) is soaked and allowed to **germinate**; enzymes (amylase) form. It is then dried and roasted |
| **Mashing** | Malt is mixed with hot water; enzymes turn **starch into sugar** (maltose). The sweet liquid is the **wort** |
| **Boiling** | The wort is boiled with **hops**, for bitterness and preservation; boiling also sterilises it |
| **Fermenting** | Cooled wort is fermented with **yeast**: sugar → ethanol + CO₂ |
| **Maturing** | Beer is stored cool for flavours to develop; it is clarified |
| **Packaging** | Filtered, carbonated, bottled or canned and **pasteurised** |

**Palm wine:** sap tapped from oil palm or raffia palm ferments naturally with wild yeasts. Fresh palm wine is sweet. Within hours it becomes more alcoholic, and then **sour**, as bacteria oxidise the ethanol to **ethanoic acid**.

**Local spirits:** fermented palm wine or sugar-cane juice is **distilled** to concentrate the ethanol. Poorly made spirits can contain dangerous **methanol**, which causes blindness and death, so only properly distilled and tested spirits are safe.

## Salt production

1. **Solar evaporation:** sea water is let into shallow **salt pans**; the sun and wind evaporate the water and salt crystallises out.
2. **Boiling brine:** in coastal communities, brine (often concentrated by filtering sea water through salty soil) is boiled in large pans over wood fires.
3. The crude salt is **washed** and dried, then **iodised** by spraying with potassium iodate solution.

## Paint making

Paint has four main parts:

| Part | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Pigment** | Colour and covering power | **Titanium(IV) oxide** (white), iron oxides (red, yellow, brown) |
| **Binder** | Holds the pigment and sticks it to the surface; hardens into a film | Drying oils, acrylic or alkyd resins |
| **Solvent (thinner)** | Makes the paint runny enough to apply; evaporates | Water (emulsion paints), white spirit (gloss paints) |
| **Additives** | Driers, fungicides, thickeners | |

Gloss paints dry partly by **oxidation**: oxygen from the air links the oil molecules.

## Problems facing local chemical industries

| Problem | Possible solutions |
|---|---|
| **Unreliable and costly electricity** | Hydro and solar power; energy-efficient equipment |
| **Lack of capital** | Credit schemes, cooperatives, investment incentives |
| **Imported raw materials and spare parts** | Using local raw materials (palm oil, rutile, salt, cassava starch) |
| **Shortage of skilled workers** | Technical and vocational training; links with universities |
| **Poor quality control** | Standards and testing (Sierra Leone Standards Bureau); training |
| **Competition from cheap imports** | Better quality, branding and supportive trade policies |
| **Pollution and safety** | Waste treatment, safe handling of caustic soda and fuels, environmental licensing |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many of these industries are part of everyday life in Sierra Leone:

- **Beer:** the Sierra Leone Brewery in Wellington, Freetown, brews beer.
- **Soap:** small soap makers turn palm kernel oil and caustic soda into bar soap.
- **Salt:** coastal families evaporate sea salt.
- **Palm wine:** tappers sell palm wine every morning.

Each is a chemical industry, and each improves with an understanding of the reactions involved.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Fermentation needs **no oxygen** (it is anaerobic). If air gets in, bacteria oxidise the ethanol to **ethanoic acid**, which is why palm wine left open turns sour.
:::

## Key points

- **Soap:** fat or oil + alkali (NaOH, or wood-ash potash in country soap) → soap + **glycerol** (saponification); salting out separates the soap.
- **Fermentation:** C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ (yeast enzymes, about 30 °C, no air).
- **Brewing:** malting → mashing → boiling with hops → fermenting → maturing → packaging. Palm wine sours as ethanol is oxidised to ethanoic acid.
- **Salt:** solar evaporation or boiling brine, then **iodisation**.
- **Paint:** pigment (e.g. **TiO₂**), binder, solvent and additives.
- Local industries face **power, capital, skills and competition** problems.

## Quick check

1. Name the process by which soap is made and its by-product.
2. Write the equation for the fermentation of glucose.
3. Why does palm wine turn sour after some hours?
4. Name the white pigment made from rutile.

**Answers:**

1. Saponification; glycerol.
2. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂.
3. Bacteria oxidise the ethanol in it to ethanoic acid.
4. Titanium(IV) oxide, TiO₂.
