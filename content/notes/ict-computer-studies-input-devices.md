:::callout{type=definition}
An **input device** is any hardware device used to enter data or instructions into a computer, such as a keyboard, a mouse, a scanner, a microphone or a touch screen.
:::

When you shade an objective answer sheet in a WASSCE examination, type a text message, tap a phone screen or have a barcode scanned at a supermarket till, you are using an **input device**. This lesson describes the **types of input devices and their functions**: the keyboard and its keys, pointing devices, scanning and recognition devices such as OMR, OCR and MICR, audio and video devices, biometric devices and sensors, and how to choose the right one for a job.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define an input device and give examples
- describe the groups of keys on a keyboard and the use of special keys
- describe pointing devices and their uses
- explain OMR, OCR, MICR, barcode readers and other recognition devices, with examples of their use
- name audio, video and biometric input devices and sensors
- choose a suitable input device for a given task

## The keyboard

:::callout{type=definition}
**Keyboard:** the most common input device for text and numbers, with keys for letters, digits, symbols and commands; most use the **QWERTY** layout, named after the first six letters on the top row of letters.

**Function keys:** the keys F1 to F12 along the top of a keyboard, which carry out special tasks set by each program, such as F1 for help.
:::

*Figure 1. The groups of keys on a computer keyboard. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Group of keys | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Alphanumeric keys** | letters A–Z, digits 0–9, punctuation | typing text and numbers |
| **Function keys** | F1 to F12 | special tasks set by each program; F1 opens help |
| **Numeric keypad** | 0–9, +, −, *, / | fast entry of numbers |
| **Cursor (arrow) keys** | ↑ ↓ ← → | moving the cursor |
| **Editing keys** | Insert, Delete, Home, End, Page Up, Page Down | moving through and changing text |
| **Modifier keys** | Shift, Ctrl, Alt | used with other keys for shortcuts, such as Ctrl + C to copy |

### Special keys and what they do

- **Enter:** carries out a command, or starts a new paragraph in a document.
- **Backspace:** deletes the character to the **left** of the cursor.
- **Delete:** deletes the character to the **right** of the cursor, or a selected item.
- **Shift:** types capital letters and the upper symbols on keys, when held down.
- **Caps Lock:** switches capital letters on or off for all letters typed.
- **Tab:** moves the cursor to the next tab stop or the next box on a form.
- **Esc (Escape):** cancels the current action or closes a menu.
- **Space bar:** types a space between words.

## Pointing devices

:::callout{type=definition}
**Pointing device:** an input device used to move a pointer on the screen and to select items, such as a mouse, a touchpad, a trackball or a joystick.

**Touch screen:** a display that also detects where it is touched by a finger or stylus, so it works as both an input device and an output device.
:::

- **Mouse:** moved on a flat surface to control the pointer. A **left click** selects, a **double-click** opens, a **right click** shows a menu of options, **dragging** moves items and the **scroll wheel** moves up and down a page. Most mice today are **optical** (they use a light), and many are **wireless**.
- **Touchpad (trackpad):** a flat pad on a laptop; you move a finger on it to move the pointer.
- **Trackball:** a ball turned by the thumb or fingers while the device stays still, useful where space is tight.
- **Joystick:** a lever that moves in all directions, used for games and for controlling machines and flight simulators.
- **Light pen:** a pen-shaped device used to draw or select directly on a screen.
- **Stylus and graphics tablet:** a pen and a flat drawing board used by artists and designers.
- **Touch screen:** a screen that is also an input device; you select by touching it. Used on smartphones, tablets, ATMs and ticket machines.

## Scanning and recognition devices

These devices read data **directly** from a document or a product, so there is no typing and fewer mistakes.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Scanner:** an input device that copies printed text, pictures or documents into the computer as a digital image.

**Optical mark reader (OMR):** a device that detects pencil or pen marks made in set places on a form, such as the shaded answers on a multiple-choice answer sheet.

**Optical character recognition (OCR):** software, used with a scanner or camera, that turns an image of printed or written text into text that can be edited.

**Magnetic ink character recognition (MICR):** the reading of characters printed in magnetic ink, used for the cheque number, bank code and account number on cheques.

**Barcode reader:** a device that reads the pattern of black and white bars printed on a product to identify it.
:::

| Device | What it reads | Where it is used |
|---|---|---|
| **Scanner** (flatbed, handheld, sheet-fed) | whole pages and pictures | offices, schools, photocopying shops |
| **OMR** | shaded marks in fixed places | **objective answer sheets** in examinations, lottery and survey forms |
| **OCR** | printed or handwritten characters | turning a scanned page into editable text, reading passports |
| **MICR** | characters in magnetic ink | **cheques** in banks |
| **Barcode reader** | barcodes | supermarket tills, libraries, warehouses |
| **QR code reader** (often a phone camera) | square QR codes | opening a web link, paying by scanning a code |
| **Magnetic stripe and chip (smart card) readers** | the stripe or chip on a card | ATMs and point-of-sale machines |

:::callout{type=local-example}
**OMR in your WASSCE examination.** The objective answer sheet you shade with an HB pencil is marked by an optical mark reader. It detects which box in each row is shaded, so marks that are too light, or two boxes shaded for one question, can be misread. Shade one box firmly for each question and rub out changes cleanly.
:::

## Audio, video and biometric devices

- **Microphone:** inputs sound and voice, for voice calls, recordings and voice notes.
- **Webcam:** a camera that inputs video, used for video calls and online lessons.
- **Digital camera and video camera:** input photographs and video.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Speech (voice) recognition:** the use of software to turn words spoken into a microphone into text or commands, so a user can dictate or give orders without typing.

**Biometric device:** an input device that identifies a person by a body feature, such as a fingerprint, the face, the iris of the eye or the voice.

**Sensor:** an input device that measures a physical quantity, such as temperature, light, motion or pressure, and sends the readings to a computer automatically.
:::

Biometric devices are used to unlock phones, record staff attendance and register people for identity cards and passports. A fingerprint cannot be forgotten like a password, which makes them secure.

Sensors are found in burglar alarms (motion), automatic doors (infrared), weather stations (temperature and rainfall) and phones (light, tilt and fingerprint).

## Choosing an input device

Ask what **kind of data** is being entered, **how much** of it there is, how **fast** and how **accurately** it must be entered, the **cost**, and **who** will use it.

- A shop with many products uses a **barcode reader**: fast and accurate.
- An examination with thousands of multiple-choice scripts uses **OMR**.
- A bank processing cheques uses **MICR**.
- A person who cannot use their hands may use **speech recognition** with a microphone.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Mixing up OMR and OCR.** OMR detects **marks** in fixed places (shaded boxes); it does not read letters. OCR recognises **characters** (letters and digits) and turns them into editable text.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Define an input device and give three examples."}
A hardware device used to **enter data or instructions** into a computer. Examples: keyboard, mouse, scanner, microphone, touch screen, barcode reader (any three).
:::

:::reveal{title="2. State the difference between the Backspace key and the Delete key."}
**Backspace** deletes the character to the **left** of the cursor; **Delete** deletes the character to the **right** of the cursor (or the selected item).
:::

:::reveal{title="3. Which input device is used to mark objective answer sheets, and how does it work?"}
An **optical mark reader (OMR)**. It shines light on the sheet and detects where marks have been made in fixed positions, so it knows which option was shaded for each question.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Where is MICR used, and why?"}
In **banks**, to read the cheque number, bank code and account number printed in **magnetic ink** on cheques. It is fast and accurate, and the magnetic characters are hard to forge.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. Name two biometric input devices and one use of each."}
Any two: a **fingerprint scanner** (unlocking a phone, staff attendance); a **face-recognition camera** (unlocking a phone, border control); an **iris scanner** (high-security entry).
:::

## Key points

- An **input device** enters data and instructions into a computer.
- **Keyboard** groups: alphanumeric, function, numeric keypad, cursor, editing and modifier keys.
- **Pointing devices**: mouse, touchpad, trackball, joystick, light pen, stylus, touch screen.
- Recognition devices: **scanner**, **OMR** (marks), **OCR** (characters), **MICR** (cheques), **barcode** and **QR code** readers, card readers.
- Others: **microphone, webcam, digital camera, biometric devices, sensors**.
- Choose by the **type and amount** of data, **speed, accuracy, cost** and **user**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
On input devices, the paper typically asks you to name input devices and state their uses, to explain the function of particular keys, to say where OMR, OCR, MICR or barcode readers are used, and to choose a suitable input device for a situation and give a reason. Name the device in full the first time (optical mark reader, not just OMR) and link it to a clear use.
:::
