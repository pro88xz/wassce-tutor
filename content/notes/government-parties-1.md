At election time in Sierra Leone the streets turn green for the SLPP and red for the APC, and smaller parties campaign in their own colours. Each party hopes to win power and put its programme into action. **Political parties** are the main vehicles through which citizens compete for power in a democracy. This lesson explains what parties are, the types of party, what parties do and the different party systems.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a political party and state its features
- classify political parties into types
- explain the functions of political parties
- distinguish one-party, two-party and multi-party systems, with merits and demerits
- identify the problems facing political parties in West Africa

## What is a political party?

:::callout{type=definition}
A **political party** is an organised group of people who share broadly similar political ideas and aims, and who seek to **win control of government** through elections in order to carry out their programme.
:::

**Features of a political party:**

1. An **organised structure**: national executive, regional and constituency branches.
2. **Leadership**: a party leader, chairman and other officers.
3. A **manifesto or programme**: the policies it promises to carry out.
4. **Membership**, with members' cards and dues.
5. The **aim of winning power** through elections.
6. A **name, symbol and colours** that identify it.
7. A **constitution** that governs its internal affairs.
8. **Registration** with the state: in Sierra Leone, with the **Political Parties Regulation Commission (PPRC)**.

## Types of political party

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Mass party** | Seeks the widest possible membership among ordinary people | Many nationalist parties before independence |
| **Elite (cadre) party** | Led and controlled by a small group of notable or educated people | Early colonial-era parties of the educated elite |
| **Ideological party** | Built around a clear ideology | Communist and socialist parties |
| **Broker (catch-all) party** | Tries to attract voters from all groups by balancing interests | Most modern major parties |
| **Charismatic party** | Built around a popular leader | Parties formed around one founder |
| **Ethnic or regional party** | Draws support mainly from one ethnic group or region | Parties with strong regional strongholds |

## Functions of political parties

1. **Contesting elections** and forming the government when they win.
2. **Recruiting and training leaders** for public office.
3. **Formulating policies** and presenting them in manifestos.
4. **Political education**: informing voters about issues.
5. **Interest aggregation**: combining the demands of many groups into one programme.
6. **Providing an opposition** that criticises and checks the government.
7. **Linking the government and the people.**
8. **Promoting national unity** when parties draw support from all regions.
9. **Political socialisation**: shaping citizens' political attitudes.
10. **Giving voters a choice** between alternative programmes and leaders.

## Party systems

![One-party, two-party and multi-party systems, with circles showing parties of different sizes and examples: Sierra Leone 1978 to 1991, the USA, and Sierra Leone since 1991](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/party-systems.svg)

*Figure 1. One, two or many parties. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| System | Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|---|
| **One-party** (only one party allowed, *de jure*, or effectively, *de facto*) | Can promote unity; avoids divisive party conflict; quick decisions | No real choice; no opposition; leads to dictatorship and abuse of rights; elections become a formality |
| **Two-party** (two major parties alternate in power) | Stable government; clear choice; strong opposition ready to take over | Limited choice; minorities and small parties are ignored |
| **Multi-party** (several parties compete) | Wide choice; minorities represented; encourages debate | Can produce weak coalition governments; instability; parties may form along ethnic lines |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone has experienced every type of system. The **1978 Constitution** made the **All People's Congress (APC)** the only legal party, a *de jure* one-party state. The **1991 Constitution** restored **multi-party** politics. Today about **17** parties are registered with the PPRC, but national politics is dominated by two: the **Sierra Leone People's Party (SLPP)**, founded in **1951**, and the **APC**, founded in **1960**. Power has alternated between them since 2007, so some describe the system as multi-party with **two dominant parties**.
:::

## Problems of political parties in West Africa

- **Ethnic and regional voting**, which makes parties look like ethnic associations
- **Poor internal democracy**: leaders imposed rather than elected
- **Lack of funds**, and dependence on rich sponsors
- **Weak ideology**: parties differ little in policy
- **Violence** by party supporters during campaigns
- **Defections** ("carpet-crossing") and splits
- **Personality cults** around leaders

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A party with the most members is not necessarily a **mass party**, and a two-party system does not mean only two parties exist. In the USA many small parties exist, but only the Democrats and Republicans have a real chance of winning power. What matters is how many parties **compete effectively for power**.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Mass party**: a party that seeks the widest possible membership among ordinary people.
- **Elite party** (cadre party): a party led and controlled by a small group of notable or educated people.
- **Ideological party**: a party built around a clear ideology.
- **Broker party** (catch-all party): a party that tries to attract voters from all groups by balancing their interests.
- **Political Parties Regulation Commission (PPRC)**: the body with which political parties in Sierra Leone must register.
:::

## Key points

- A **political party** is an organised group that seeks to **win power through elections** to carry out its programme.
- Types: **mass, elite, ideological, broker, charismatic, ethnic or regional**.
- Functions: **contesting elections, recruiting leaders, policy-making, political education, interest aggregation, opposition**.
- Systems: **one-party** (Sierra Leone 1978–1991), **two-party** (USA), **multi-party** (Sierra Leone since 1991).
- Sierra Leone: **SLPP (1951)** and **APC (1960)** dominate; parties register with the **PPRC**.

## Quick check

1. What is a political party?
2. State three functions of political parties.
3. What is a *de jure* one-party state? Give an example.
4. Which body registers and regulates political parties in Sierra Leone?

**Answers:**

1. An organised group of people with similar political ideas who seek to win control of government through elections to carry out their programme.
2. Any three: contesting elections; recruiting and training leaders; formulating policies; political education; interest aggregation; providing an opposition; linking government and people; promoting unity; giving voters a choice.
3. A state in which the law or constitution allows only one party, for example Sierra Leone under the 1978 Constitution, when the APC was the only legal party.
4. The Political Parties Regulation Commission (PPRC).
