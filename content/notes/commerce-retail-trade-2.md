# Retail pricing, stock control and customer service

## Learning objectives
Explain retail pricing methods, calculate simple mark-up and gross profit, and describe effective stock control.

## Retail pricing
A retailer must cover the purchase cost and operating expenses while setting a price customers are willing to pay. Common approaches include:
- **Cost-plus pricing:** add a planned mark-up to unit cost.
- **Competitive pricing:** consider the prices charged by comparable sellers.
- **Promotional pricing:** temporarily reduce price to attract customers or clear stock.
- **Value-based pricing:** consider the value customers place on the product.

A mark-up is calculated on cost, while a gross-profit margin is calculated on selling price. They are not the same.

### Worked example
A shop buys a kettle for NLe 400 and adds a 25% mark-up on cost.

Mark-up = 25% × 400 = NLe 100.
Selling price = 400 + 100 = **NLe 500**.
Gross profit = NLe 100, before rent, wages, transport and other expenses.

Gross-profit margin = 100 ÷ 500 × 100 = **20%**.

## Stock control
Stock control ensures that the right quantity is available without tying up excessive funds. Important records include stock cards, goods-received notes, sales records and stocktaking reports.

The **re-order level** is the stock level at which a fresh order should be placed, taking account of expected demand during lead time and any safety stock.

A simple approach is:

**Re-order level = Demand during lead time + Safety stock**

If daily demand is 12 units, supplier lead time is 5 days and safety stock is 20 units, re-order level = (12 × 5) + 20 = **80 units**.

## Customer service and returns
Accurate product descriptions, clear prices, receipts, safe handling, respectful communication and fair complaint procedures build trust. Retailers should explain warranties and return conditions accurately and comply with applicable consumer law.

## WASSCE examination focus
Show whether a percentage is based on cost or selling price. In stock questions, identify lead time and safety stock before calculating.

## Quick check
1. Distinguish mark-up from gross-profit margin.
2. A product costs NLe 240. Calculate its selling price with a 15% mark-up on cost.
3. Calculate re-order level for daily demand of 8 units, 4-day lead time and safety stock of 10 units.
4. State three elements of good customer service.

**Answers:** 1. Mark-up is profit as a percentage of cost; margin is profit as a percentage of selling price. 2. NLe 276. 3. 8 × 4 + 10 = 42 units. 4. Accurate information, clear pricing, receipts, respectful handling and fair complaint resolution.
