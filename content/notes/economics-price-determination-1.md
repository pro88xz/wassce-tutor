## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain how the forces of demand and supply determine price
- find the equilibrium price and quantity from a table and a graph
- explain excess demand and excess supply and how the market removes them

## The price mechanism

:::callout{type=definition}
The **price mechanism** (the market system) is the way the forces of **demand and supply** interact to determine the **price** of a good and the **quantity** bought and sold.
:::

In a free market, nobody sets the price of plantain at Kenema market. It is settled by buyers and sellers bargaining, until the amount buyers want equals the amount sellers offer.

## Equilibrium

:::callout{type=definition}
The **equilibrium price** is the price at which the **quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied**. The amount bought and sold at that price is the **equilibrium quantity**. At equilibrium the market is "cleared": there is no shortage and no surplus, and no pressure for the price to change.
:::

Putting the demand and supply schedules for plantain side by side:

| Price (NLe) | Demanded | Supplied | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 0 | 120 | Surplus of 120 |
| 25 | 20 | 100 | Surplus of 80 |
| 20 | 40 | 80 | Surplus of 40 |
| **15** | **60** | **60** | **Equilibrium** |
| 10 | 80 | 40 | Shortage of 40 |
| 5 | 100 | 20 | Shortage of 80 |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
Reading the table:

- **Equilibrium price:** NLe 15 a bunch, where 60 are demanded and 60 supplied.
- **Equilibrium quantity:** 60 bunches a week.
- Above NLe 15 there is a **surplus** (excess supply); below it there is a **shortage** (excess demand).
:::

On a graph, the equilibrium is where the **demand curve crosses the supply curve**. Mark the price on the vertical axis ($P_e$) and the quantity on the horizontal axis ($Q_e$), with dotted lines from the crossing point.

## Excess supply and excess demand

:::callout{type=definition}
**Excess supply (a surplus)** exists when, at a given price, the quantity supplied is **greater** than the quantity demanded.

**Excess demand (a shortage)** exists when, at a given price, the quantity demanded is **greater** than the quantity supplied.
:::

### How the market returns to equilibrium

**If the price is too high (NLe 20):** traders have 80 bunches but buyers want only 40. Unsold plantain starts to ripen, so traders **cut their prices**. As the price falls, buyers take more (an extension of demand) and traders offer less (a contraction of supply), until the price reaches NLe 15 and the surplus disappears.

**If the price is too low (NLe 10):** buyers want 80 bunches but only 40 are offered. Buyers compete for the scarce plantain and traders **raise their prices**. As the price rises, buyers take less and farmers offer more, until the price reaches NLe 15 and the shortage disappears.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Late in the afternoon at a fish market, traders with fish left over lower their prices to clear the stock before it spoils: excess supply pushing the price down. In the early morning, when few boats have landed and many buyers are waiting, prices are high: excess demand pushing the price up.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
At the equilibrium price, people still want more of the good than they get, and sellers would like a higher price. "Equilibrium" does not mean everyone is satisfied: it means the **quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied**, so there is no pressure for the price to change.
:::

## Functions of the price mechanism

The price mechanism helps answer the basic economic problems:

1. **Rationing**: a high price limits the use of scarce goods to those willing to pay most.
2. **Signalling**: rising prices tell producers what consumers want more of.
3. **Incentive**: higher prices and profits encourage producers to supply more.
4. **Allocating resources**: factors move to the industries where they earn the most.

## Key points

- Price is set where **demand meets supply**: the **equilibrium price**.
- Above equilibrium: **excess supply**, so price falls. Below equilibrium: **excess demand**, so price rises.
- The price mechanism **rations**, **signals**, gives **incentives** and **allocates resources**.
