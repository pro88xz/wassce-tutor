**Decolonisation** brought independence to most of Africa between **1951 and 1975**, with **seventeen countries** becoming independent in **1960** alone, the "**Year of Africa**". But independent African states soon faced serious problems: **military coups** (beginning with **Togo in 1963**, then **Nigeria and Ghana in 1966** and **Sierra Leone in 1967**), **civil wars** such as the **Nigerian Civil War (1967–1970)**, **neo-colonialism** and economic dependence, and the struggle against **apartheid** in South Africa, which ended with the election of **Nelson Mandela** in **1994**. To cooperate, West African states founded the **Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS)** by the **Treaty of Lagos (28 May 1975)**. This lesson explains **decolonisation and the challenges of independent Africa**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the main waves of decolonisation in Africa
- explain the causes and effects of military coups in Africa
- explain the causes of civil wars in independent Africa, with examples
- explain the meaning of neo-colonialism
- describe apartheid in South Africa and how it ended
- describe the aims and achievements of ECOWAS

Decolonisation was **peaceful and constitutional** in most of West Africa, but needed **armed struggle** where many white settlers lived or where the colonial power refused to leave: **Algeria (1954–62)**, **Kenya** (the Mau Mau rising, 1952–60), the **Portuguese colonies** and **Zimbabwe**.

## Military coups

:::callout{type=definition}
A **military coup d'état** is the sudden, illegal seizure of government by members of the armed forces, usually by force or the threat of force.
:::

**Early coups in West Africa:** **Togo (January 1963)**, when President **Sylvanus Olympio** was killed; **Nigeria (January and July 1966)**; **Ghana (February 1966)**, which overthrew **Nkrumah**; and **Sierra Leone (March 1967)**.

**Causes of military coups:**

1. **Corruption** and mismanagement by civilian governments.
2. **Economic problems**: falling export prices, debt, unemployment and inflation.
3. **Rigged elections** and **one-party states**, which left no peaceful way to change government.
4. **Ethnic and regional rivalry** within politics and the army.
5. **Army grievances** over pay, promotion or political interference.
6. **Personal ambition** of officers.
7. **Foreign influence** during the Cold War.
8. **The example** of successful coups elsewhere.

**Effects:** suspension of **constitutions**, ban on **political parties**, abuse of **human rights**, **counter-coups** and instability; sometimes short-term order, but usually **more corruption** and **delayed development**.

## Civil wars

| War | Dates | Main causes |
|---|---|---|
| **Congo crisis** | 1960–1965 | sudden Belgian withdrawal; Katanga's secession; Cold War rivalry; murder of **Patrice Lumumba (1961)** |
| **Nigerian Civil War (Biafra)** | **1967–1970** | ethnic and regional tension; the 1966 coups and killings of Igbo in the North; the East's secession as **Biafra** under **Ojukwu**; oil |
| **Liberian Civil War** | 1989–1997, 1999–2003 | dictatorship of **Samuel Doe**; ethnic conflict; **Charles Taylor's** NPFL invasion |
| **Sierra Leone Civil War** | **1991–2002** | bad governance, corruption, youth unemployment; **RUF** invasion from Liberia; **diamonds** |
| **Rwandan genocide** | 1994 | Hutu extremists killed about **800,000** Tutsi and moderate Hutu in about 100 days |

## Neo-colonialism

:::callout{type=definition}
**Neo-colonialism** is the continued **control of an independent country by a foreign power** through **economic, financial, cultural or military** means instead of direct rule. The term was popularised by **Kwame Nkrumah** in his book *Neo-Colonialism: The Last Stage of Imperialism* (1965).
:::

Signs of neo-colonialism include dependence on **one or two export crops or minerals**, **foreign debt**, control of the economy by **multinational companies**, foreign **military bases** and **aid with conditions**.

## Apartheid in South Africa

:::callout{type=definition}
**Apartheid** ("separateness" in Afrikaans) was the system of **legal racial segregation and white-minority rule** introduced by the **National Party** after it won power in **1948**.
:::

| Apartheid laws and features | Struggle and end |
|---|---|
| **Population Registration Act (1950)**: everyone classified by race | **ANC** (1912) **Defiance Campaign (1952)**; **Freedom Charter (1955)** |
| **Group Areas Act (1950)**: separate residential areas | **Sharpeville massacre (21 March 1960)**: police killed 69 protesters; ANC and PAC banned |
| **pass laws** controlling Black movement | **Nelson Mandela** jailed for life after the **Rivonia Trial (1964)** |
| **Bantu Education Act (1953)**: inferior schooling | **Soweto uprising (1976)** by students |
| **Bantustans** ("homelands") | international **sanctions**, boycotts; OAU and UN pressure |
| no vote for Black South Africans | Mandela **released 11 February 1990**; first **non-racial election, April 1994**; **Mandela President** |

## ECOWAS

The **Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS)** was founded by the **Treaty of Lagos** on **28 May 1975**, led by **General Yakubu Gowon** of Nigeria and **General Gnassingbé Eyadéma** of Togo. It had **15** founding members; **Cabo Verde** joined in **1977**, **Mauritania** left in **2000**, and **Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger** left in **January 2025**. Its headquarters is in **Abuja**.

**Aims:** to promote **economic cooperation and integration**; remove **trade barriers** and customs duties; allow **free movement of people** (protocol of **1979**); develop **transport and communications**; and, since the **revised treaty of 1993**, promote **peace, security and democracy**.

**Achievements:**

1. **Free movement**: ECOWAS citizens can travel without visas, with an **ECOWAS passport**.
2. **Peacekeeping**: **ECOMOG** intervened in **Liberia (from 1990)** and **Sierra Leone (1997–2000)**, and ECOWAS has acted against coups.
3. **Infrastructure**: projects such as the **West African Gas Pipeline** and trans-West African highways.
4. **Institutions**: the ECOWAS Court of Justice and Parliament.

**Problems:** **political instability and coups**, **language divisions** (English, French, Portuguese), **poor roads and links**, **lack of funds**, slow progress on a **common currency**, and the **2025 withdrawal** of three Sahel states.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone is a **founding member of ECOWAS**. During the civil war, **ECOMOG**, led mainly by **Nigerian** troops, restored the elected government of **President Ahmad Tejan Kabbah** in **1998** after the AFRC junta, and defended Freetown in **January 1999**. The **UN mission (UNAMSIL)** later took over, and the war was declared over on **18 January 2002**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not confuse **ECOWAS** with the **OAU/AU**. ECOWAS is a **regional** economic community of **West African** states, founded in **1975**; the OAU (1963) and AU (2002) are **continental** organisations of all African states.
:::

## Key figures and terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figures and terms"}
- **Decolonisation**: the process by which colonies won their independence, which in Africa ran from the 1950s to the 1990s.
- **Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS)**: the organisation founded by the Treaty of Lagos on 28 May 1975 to integrate the economies of West Africa.
- **ECOMOG**: the West African peacekeeping force of ECOWAS, which served in Liberia and Sierra Leone.
:::

## Key points

- Decolonisation: **1950s** North Africa and Ghana; **1960** "Year of Africa"; **1970s** Portuguese colonies; **Zimbabwe 1980, Namibia 1990, South Africa 1994**.
- **Military coups** from **1963** (Togo), caused by **corruption, economic crisis, rigged elections, ethnic rivalry, army grievances**.
- **Civil wars**: Congo, **Nigeria (1967–70)**, Liberia, **Sierra Leone (1991–2002)**, Rwanda.
- **Neo-colonialism**: economic and other control after independence (Nkrumah, **1965**).
- **Apartheid** (1948–1994): racial laws; **Sharpeville 1960**, **Soweto 1976**; Mandela freed **1990**, elected **1994**.
- **ECOWAS**: **Treaty of Lagos, 28 May 1975**; free movement, **ECOMOG**, integration.
