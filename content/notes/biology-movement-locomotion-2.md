# Movement and locomotion: locomotion in selected animals

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- describe how fish swim
- explain the adaptations of birds for flight
- describe how mammals walk and run
- explain the roles of muscles, fins, wings and body shape in locomotion
- relate locomotory structures to the environments in which animals live

## Locomotion and adaptation

Locomotion is movement from one place to another. The structures used depend on the animal's habitat, body plan and way of life. Water provides buoyancy but creates resistance; air is less dense but flight requires sufficient lift and thrust; movement on land requires support against gravity and traction against the ground.

### Swimming in fish

A fish has a streamlined body that reduces resistance as it moves through water. Its muscular body produces side-to-side waves that pass towards the tail. The tail fin (caudal fin) provides much of the thrust that drives the fish forward. Other fins help with steering, balance, braking and changes in direction.

The paired pectoral and pelvic fins help control movement and stability. The dorsal and anal fins reduce rolling. The caudal fin provides propulsion. Overlapping scales and a mucus-covered body surface help reduce friction and protect the skin. The swim bladder in many bony fish helps regulate buoyancy, allowing the fish to maintain depth without constant swimming; it is not a propulsive organ.

### Flight in birds

Birds have several adaptations for flight:

- **Wings and flight feathers** provide surfaces that generate lift and help control movement.
- **Streamlined body shape** reduces air resistance.
- **Powerful flight muscles** move the wings. The pectoralis major powers the downstroke; the supracoracoideus helps raise the wing during the upstroke.
- **Light but strong skeleton** reduces mass. Many bones are fused for rigidity, and some are pneumatic (contain air spaces).
- **Efficient respiration and circulation** supply oxygen to active flight muscles. Birds have lungs with air sacs that maintain one-way airflow through the lungs.
- **Tail feathers** assist steering, braking and balance.

During flight, the wings interact with air to generate lift and thrust. The exact forces depend on wing shape, angle and movement. A bird does not simply remain airborne because its bones are hollow; flight depends on coordinated wing action, muscle power and an efficient respiratory and circulatory system.

### Walking and running in mammals

Mammalian limbs are positioned beneath the body in many species, helping support body weight and produce efficient movement on land. The bones of the limbs act as levers, joints provide pivots and muscles contract in coordinated sequences. Tendons transmit muscular force to bones, while ligaments stabilise joints.

Different mammals show adaptations to their mode of movement. Horses have long limbs and reduced digits that support fast running. Primates have flexible limbs and grasping hands or feet suited to climbing. Kangaroos have powerful hind limbs and use hopping as an efficient form of movement. Aquatic mammals such as dolphins have streamlined bodies and flippers, while their tails provide propulsion.

## Comparing locomotion in fish, birds and mammals

| Feature | Fish | Birds | Typical land mammals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main propulsive structure | Body muscles and tail fin | Wings and flight muscles | Limb muscles acting on bones |
| Environment | Water | Air | Land |
| Important adaptation | Streamlined body and fins | Wings, feathers and efficient oxygen supply | Strong limbs, joints and traction |
| Main resistance | Water drag | Air resistance and gravity | Gravity and ground friction |

## Energy requirements

Locomotion requires energy because muscles use ATP during contraction. Active animals need a continuous supply of oxygen and respiratory substrate to support aerobic respiration. During intense activity, energy demand may temporarily exceed the rate at which oxygen can be supplied, so anaerobic energy release may contribute. Recovery requires the body to restore normal conditions.

## WASSCE examination focus

When asked for adaptations, link each feature to its function. For example, a streamlined body reduces drag; a fish's tail fin provides thrust; bird flight muscles power wing movement; and strong mammalian limbs support body weight. Avoid listing structures without explaining how they assist locomotion.

## Quick check

1. Which fin provides most forward thrust in a typical fish?
2. State two functions of the paired fins of a fish.
3. Name the two main flight muscles in birds and their actions.
4. Why is a streamlined body useful in water?
5. How do bones and muscles work together during walking?

**Answers:** 1. The caudal (tail) fin. 2. Steering, balance, braking and manoeuvring (any two). 3. Pectoralis major powers the downstroke; supracoracoideus assists the upstroke. 4. It reduces resistance (drag). 5. Muscles contract and pull on bones through tendons; bones act as levers around joints.
