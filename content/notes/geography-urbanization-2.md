The **problems of urbanization** in Africa include **slums and informal settlements**, **housing shortages**, **traffic congestion**, **unemployment**, **crime**, **pollution and waste**, **flooding**, and **pressure on water, electricity, schools and hospitals**. They arise because cities grow faster than jobs and services can be provided. The **solutions** include urban planning, low-cost housing, better public transport, waste management and developing rural areas so that fewer people need to migrate. This lesson examines each problem, with Freetown as the main case study.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the problems caused by rapid urbanization
- describe land-use zones in a city
- describe the problems of Freetown as a case study
- suggest solutions to urban problems
- explain the effects of urbanization on rural areas

## Land use in a city

![A model city: the central business district at the centre, a transition zone of mixed use, inner residential areas with crowded and informal housing, outer residential areas, an industrial zone along transport routes and a rural–urban fringe](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/urban-land-use.svg)

*Figure 1. Land-use zones in a city (simplified model). WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

The **central business district (CBD)** has the highest land values, the tallest buildings and the most traffic; it holds shops, banks, offices and markets. Around it are zones of mixed use, housing of different qualities, industry along roads and ports, and the **rural–urban fringe**, where the city is spreading into farmland.

## Problems of rapid urbanization

| Problem | Cause and description |
|---|---|
| **Slums and informal settlements** | migrants cannot afford housing and build shacks on unplanned land, often on steep slopes, swamps or the shore |
| **Housing shortage and high rents** | demand far exceeds supply |
| **Unemployment** | more job seekers than jobs; many are underemployed or work in the informal sector (petty trading, okada riding) |
| **Traffic congestion** | narrow roads, too many vehicles, poor public transport, street trading |
| **Overstretched services** | water, electricity, schools and hospitals cannot keep up |
| **Waste and pollution** | uncollected rubbish, blocked drains, polluted water and air |
| **Flooding** | building in waterways, blocked drains and paved surfaces increase runoff |
| **Crime and social problems** | poverty and unemployment encourage theft, drug abuse and gangs |
| **Disease** | overcrowding and poor sanitation spread cholera, malaria and typhoid |
| **Loss of farmland** | the city spreads over surrounding farms and forests |

## Case study: Freetown

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Freetown** shows almost every urban problem:

- **Informal settlements** such as **Kroo Bay** and **Susan's Bay** crowd the shoreline, while new houses climb the forested hillsides above the city.
- **Flooding and landslides** follow heavy rain, as in the **2017 Regent mudslide**, made worse by building on steep slopes and in drainage channels.
- **Fires** spread quickly through closely packed shacks; a major fire destroyed hundreds of homes at **Susan's Bay** in **March 2021**.
- **Traffic congestion** is severe because the city is squeezed into a narrow strip between the sea and the mountains, with few through roads.
- **Water shortages** occur in the dry season, as the Guma Valley supply cannot meet demand.
- **Waste:** large dumpsites at **Kingtom** and **Granville Brook** receive much of the city's rubbish, and plastic blocks drains.

The city council and government have responded with tree-planting campaigns, improved waste collection, road projects and efforts to relocate people from the most dangerous slopes.
:::

## Solutions to urban problems

1. **Urban planning** and enforcing building regulations, including no building on steep slopes or in waterways.
2. **Low-cost housing schemes** and upgrading slums with water, toilets, drains and paths.
3. **Better public transport**, wider roads, bypasses and traffic management.
4. **Job creation** through industry, small businesses and skills training.
5. **Waste management:** regular collection, recycling, safe landfills.
6. **Drainage** improvements and keeping waterways clear.
7. **Expanding water and electricity supply.**
8. **Rural development:** providing jobs, roads and amenities in rural areas to reduce migration.
9. **Decentralisation:** developing regional cities such as Bo, Kenema and Makeni to share the growth.
10. **Satellite towns** to take pressure off the main city.

## Effects of urbanization on rural areas

- **Loss of young workers**, leaving the old to farm; food production may fall.
- **Remittances** from town workers improve rural incomes and housing.
- **New ideas and goods** spread from towns.
- **Better markets** for rural produce near growing towns.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
"Slum" is not the same as "rural area". Slums are **urban**: overcrowded, poorly built areas inside or on the edge of cities, often lacking water, sanitation and secure land rights.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Slum**: an overcrowded area of poor housing built on unplanned land, often on steep slopes, swamps or the shore.
- **Informal sector**: small-scale work outside official employment, such as petty trading and okada riding.
- **Traffic congestion**: roads blocked by too many vehicles, narrow streets and street trading.
- **Loss of farmland**: the spread of a city over the surrounding farms and forests.
:::

## Key points

- Cities have zones: **CBD, transition, residential, industrial, rural–urban fringe**.
- Problems: **slums, housing shortage, unemployment, congestion, overstretched services, waste, flooding, crime, disease, loss of farmland**.
- **Freetown:** Kroo Bay and Susan's Bay, floods and landslides, fires, congestion, water shortage, Kingtom and Granville Brook dumpsites.
- Solutions: **planning, low-cost housing, transport, jobs, waste management, drainage, rural development, decentralisation**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **explain the problems of rapid urbanization in West Africa** and to **suggest solutions**, usually as a paired essay question. Matching each solution to a problem, and using a named city such as Freetown or Lagos, earns the highest marks.
:::

## Quick check

1. What is the CBD?
2. Give three problems of rapid urbanization.
3. Name two informal settlements in Freetown.
4. Why is traffic congestion severe in Freetown?
5. How can rural development reduce urban problems?

**Answers:**

1. The central business district: the commercial heart of a city, with shops, banks, offices and the highest land values.
2. Any three: slums; housing shortage; unemployment; congestion; overstretched services; waste and pollution; flooding; crime; disease; loss of farmland.
3. Kroo Bay and Susan's Bay.
4. The city is squeezed between the sea and the mountains, with narrow roads, few through routes and many vehicles.
5. By providing jobs and amenities in rural areas, so fewer people migrate to the cities.
