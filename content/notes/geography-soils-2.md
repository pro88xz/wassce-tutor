The main **types of soil** by texture are **sandy soil** (large particles, drains quickly, low in nutrients), **clay soil** (tiny particles, holds water, sticky when wet) and **loam** (a balanced mixture of sand, silt and clay, the best soil for most crops). Geographers also classify soils as **zonal** (mature soils shaped by climate), **intrazonal** (shaped by local conditions such as waterlogging) and **azonal** (young soils without clear horizons, such as alluvium). This lesson explains each type, the soils of Sierra Leone, and how soil erosion can be prevented.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- compare sandy, clay and loamy soils
- classify soils as zonal, intrazonal and azonal, with examples
- describe the main soils of Sierra Leone and their uses
- explain the types and causes of soil erosion
- describe methods of soil conservation

## Soil types by texture

| Property | Sandy soil | Clay soil | Loam |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Particle size** | large, gritty | very fine, smooth and sticky | mixture of sand, silt and clay |
| **Drainage** | drains very quickly; dries out | drains poorly; may waterlog | drains well but holds enough water |
| **Air** | well aerated | poorly aerated when wet | well aerated |
| **Nutrients** | low; easily leached | high but may be locked up | good |
| **Working** | light and easy to dig | heavy; hard when dry, sticky when wet | easy to work |
| **Uses** | groundnuts, cassava, coconuts with manure | rice, sugar cane; brick and pottery making | most crops: vegetables, maize, cocoa |

## Classifying soils by their formation

| Group | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Zonal soils** | mature, well-developed soils whose character is set mainly by **climate** and vegetation; they cover large zones | tropical red and yellow **ferralitic (lateritic)** soils; desert soils; podzols of cool forests; chernozems (black earths) of temperate grasslands |
| **Intrazonal soils** | soils shaped mainly by **local** factors such as poor drainage, salt or parent rock | **hydromorphic** (waterlogged, gleyed) swamp soils; saline soils; soils on limestone |
| **Azonal soils** | **young, immature** soils without clear horizons, because material is still being added or removed | **alluvial** soils on floodplains and deltas; volcanic ash soils; sand dunes; thin mountain soils |

## The soils of Sierra Leone

:::callout{type=local-example}
| Soil | Where | Character | Main uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Upland lateritic soils** | most of the country | red or yellow, deep, leached, acidic, low fertility; ironstone gravel | upland rice, cassava, groundnuts, oil palm, tree crops after fallow |
| **Inland valley swamp soils** | valley bottoms throughout the country | waterlogged, grey, richer in humus and nutrients | **swamp rice**, vegetables in the dry season |
| **Riverine grassland and bolilands** | floodplains of the north, such as along the Rokel and Scarcies | seasonally flooded clay soils | rice, grazing |
| **Mangrove swamp soils** | coast and estuaries (Scarcies, Sierra Leone River, Sherbro) | soft, salty, often very acid when drained | mangrove rice after careful management; fishing and salt making |
| **Coastal sandy soils** | beach ridges | sandy, well drained, low fertility | coconuts, cassava, vegetables with manure |
:::

## Soil erosion

:::callout{type=definition}
**Soil erosion** is the removal of the topsoil by water or wind faster than new soil can form. Because the topsoil holds most of the humus and nutrients, erosion seriously reduces fertility.
:::

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Splash erosion** | raindrops hit bare soil and throw particles into the air |
| **Sheet erosion** | a thin layer of topsoil is washed evenly off a slope |
| **Rill erosion** | water cuts small channels (rills) a few centimetres deep |
| **Gully erosion** | rills grow into deep, wide channels (gullies) that cannot be ploughed over |
| **Wind erosion** | dry, loose soil is blown away, especially in the Sahel |

**Causes:** heavy rainfall on bare soil; **deforestation**; **bush burning**; **overgrazing**; **steep slopes**; **farming up and down slopes**; **mining** and road building; continuous cropping without fallow.

**Effects:** loss of fertile topsoil and lower crop yields; gullies that destroy farmland, roads and buildings; **siltation** of rivers, dams and swamps; floods; desertification in dry areas.

## Soil conservation

1. **Contour ploughing and ridging** across the slope, so ridges hold back water.
2. **Terracing** steep slopes into steps.
3. **Cover crops and mulching** to protect the soil from raindrops and sun.
4. **Afforestation** and **windbreaks** (shelter belts) of trees.
5. **Crop rotation and bush fallowing** to restore fertility and keep plant cover.
6. **Strip cropping:** alternating strips of crops and grass across slopes.
7. **Controlled grazing** and **no bush burning**.
8. **Adding manure, compost and fertiliser** to keep the soil structure and nutrients.
9. **Gully plugging** with stones, logs and vegetation.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Loam** is not a separate kind of particle: it is a **mixture** of sand, silt and clay in balanced proportions, with humus. That balance is what makes it the best general farming soil.
:::

## Key points

- Texture: **sandy** (drains fast, poor), **clay** (holds water, heavy), **loam** (balanced, best for most crops).
- Classification: **zonal** (climate: lateritic, desert, podzol), **intrazonal** (local: swamp, saline), **azonal** (young: alluvial, volcanic, dunes).
- Sierra Leone: **upland lateritic** soils, **inland valley swamps**, **bolilands**, **mangrove** and **coastal sandy** soils.
- Erosion: **splash, sheet, rill, gully, wind**; caused by bare soil, deforestation, burning, overgrazing, slopes.
- Conservation: **contours, terraces, cover crops, mulch, trees, rotation, fallow, controlled grazing**.

## Quick check

1. Why is loam the best soil for most crops?
2. What is an azonal soil? Give an example.
3. Which Sierra Leone soils are best for growing swamp rice?
4. Distinguish rill erosion from gully erosion.
5. State three methods of soil conservation.

**Answers:**

1. It is a balanced mixture that drains well but holds enough water, is well aerated, easy to work and fertile.
2. A young, immature soil without clear horizons, such as alluvial soil, volcanic ash soil or sand dunes.
3. Inland valley swamp soils (and riverine grassland or boliland soils).
4. Rills are small, shallow channels; gullies are deep, wide channels that grow from rills.
5. Any three: contour ploughing; terracing; cover crops and mulching; afforestation and windbreaks; crop rotation and fallow; strip cropping; controlled grazing; manure and compost; gully plugging.
