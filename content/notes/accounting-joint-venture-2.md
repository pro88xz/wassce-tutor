A **consignment** is an arrangement in which one trader, the **consignor**, sends goods to an agent, the **consignee**, who sells them on the consignor's behalf in return for a **commission**. The goods remain the **consignor's property** until they are sold. The consignee sends the consignor an **account sales**, a statement showing the sales made, the expenses paid and the commission deducted, together with the **balance due**. This lesson explains consignment terms and prepares the consignor's **consignment account** to find the profit.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain consignment, consignor, consignee and account sales
- distinguish a consignment from a sale
- describe ordinary, del credere and overriding commission
- prepare the consignment account and the consignee's account in the consignor's books
- value unsold goods on consignment

## How a consignment works

![The consignor sends goods to the consignee, who sells them, pays selling expenses and deducts commission, then sends an account sales with the balance due](https://wasscetutor.com/img/accounting/consignment-flow.svg)

*Figure 1. How a consignment works. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Consignor** | the owner who sends the goods (the principal) |
| **Consignee** | the agent who receives and sells them |
| **Pro-forma invoice** | a document sent with the goods showing their details and price; **not** a demand for payment |
| **Account sales** | the consignee's statement of sales, expenses, commission and balance due |
| **Ordinary commission** | a percentage of sales for selling the goods |
| **Del credere commission** | extra commission for **bearing the risk of bad debts** on credit sales |

| Consignment | Sale |
|---|---|
| ownership stays with the **consignor** until sold | ownership passes to the **buyer** |
| the consignee is an **agent** | the buyer is a **principal** |
| unsold goods are the **consignor's** inventory | goods belong to the buyer |
| consignee earns **commission** | buyer earns profit on resale |

## Worked example

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Kamara of Freetown sent 100 bags of rice costing NLe 600 each to Bah, his agent in Kenema, and paid freight of NLe 3,000. Bah paid storage of NLe 1,000 and sold 80 bags at NLe 900 each. Bah is entitled to 5% commission on sales. He sent Kamara an account sales and the balance due. Prepare the consignment account and Bah's account in Kamara's books.**
:::

**Workings:**

- Goods sent $= 100 \times 600 = 60\,000$
- Sales $= 80 \times 900 = 72\,000$
- Commission $= 5\% \times 72\,000 = 3\,600$
- **Unsold 20 bags** valued at cost plus their share of the consignor's freight: $20 \times 600 + \frac{20}{100} \times 3\,000 = 12\,000 + 600 = 12\,600$
- Balance remitted by Bah $= 72\,000 - 1\,000 - 3\,600 = 67\,400$

**Consignment to Kenema account (Kamara's books)**

| Debit | NLe |
|---|---|
| Goods sent on consignment | 60,000 |
| Bank: freight | 3,000 |
| Bah: storage | 1,000 |
| Bah: commission | 3,600 |
| **Profit on consignment** | **17,000** |
| **Total** | **84,600** |

| Credit | NLe |
|---|---|
| Bah: sales | 72,000 |
| Inventory on consignment c/d | 12,600 |
| **Total** | **84,600** |

**Bah (consignee) account**

| Debit | NLe |
|---|---|
| Consignment: sales | 72,000 |

| Credit | NLe |
|---|---|
| Consignment: storage | 1,000 |
| Consignment: commission | 3,600 |
| Bank: balance remitted | 67,400 |

**Check:** cost of 80 bags sold $= 48\,000$ plus $\frac{80}{100}$ of freight $= 2\,400$; add storage $1\,000$ and commission $3\,600$: total $55\,000$. Profit $= 72\,000 - 55\,000 = 17\,000$ ✓.

## Valuing unsold goods

Unsold goods on consignment are valued at **cost plus a proportion of the expenses incurred in getting them to the consignee** (freight, insurance in transit, unloading). Expenses incurred **after** arrival to sell the goods (selling expenses, commission) are **not** included.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not include the **commission** or **selling expenses** in the value of unsold goods, and do not treat goods sent on consignment as a **sale**. The consignor's sales are only the goods the consignee has actually sold.
:::

## Key points

- In a **consignment**, the consignee sells the consignor's goods as an **agent** for **commission**.
- Ownership stays with the **consignor**; unsold goods are the consignor's inventory.
- The **account sales** reports sales, expenses, commission and the balance due.
- **Consignment account:** debit goods sent and all expenses; credit sales and unsold goods; the balance is **profit**.
- Unsold goods: **cost + share of expenses to bring them to the consignee**.

## Quick check

1. What is an account sales?
2. What is del credere commission?
3. Why is goods sent on consignment not a sale?
4. 50 units costing NLe 200 each are sent; freight NLe 1,000; 10 remain unsold. Value the unsold goods.
5. Sales NLe 40,000; commission 5%; consignee's expenses NLe 500. How much does the consignee remit?

**Answers:**

1. A statement from the consignee to the consignor showing sales made, expenses paid, commission deducted and the balance due.
2. Extra commission paid to the consignee for bearing the risk of bad debts on credit sales.
3. Because ownership stays with the consignor; the consignee is only an agent.
4. $10 \times 200 + \frac{10}{50} \times 1\,000 = 2\,000 + 200 = 2\,200$: NLe 2,200.
5. $40\,000 - 2\,000 - 500 = 37\,500$: NLe 37,500.
