A **manufacturing account** is prepared by a business that **makes** the goods it sells, to find the **cost of producing** them. It sits before the trading account and builds up costs in stages: **direct materials consumed**, plus **direct labour** and **direct expenses**, give the **prime cost**; adding **factory overheads** and adjusting for **work in progress** gives the **production cost of goods completed**, which is transferred to the **trading account** in place of purchases. This lesson explains the classification of costs and the layout of the manufacturing account.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the purpose of a manufacturing account
- classify costs as direct or indirect, and as factory or office costs
- calculate raw materials consumed, prime cost and production cost
- explain work in progress and how it is adjusted
- apportion shared expenses between the factory and the office

## Classification of costs

| Cost | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct materials** | materials that become part of the product | wood for furniture, cloth for garments |
| **Direct labour** | wages of workers who make the product | machine operators, carpenters on the production line |
| **Direct expenses** | other costs traced to each product | royalties per unit, hire of a special machine |
| **Factory overheads (indirect manufacturing costs)** | factory costs that cannot be traced to a single product | factory rent, power, supervisors' wages, depreciation of machinery, factory insurance |
| **Office (administration) expenses** | costs of running the office | office salaries, office rent, stationery |
| **Selling and distribution expenses** | costs of selling and delivering | advertising, sales staff wages, delivery van costs |

Direct costs and factory overheads go to the **manufacturing account**; office, selling and distribution expenses go to the **profit and loss section** of the income statement.

## Layout of the manufacturing account

![Manufacturing account structure: direct materials consumed plus direct labour and direct expenses give prime cost; adding factory overheads and adjusting for work in progress gives the production cost of goods completed, transferred to the trading account](https://wasscetutor.com/img/accounting/manufacturing-cost-flow.svg)

*Figure 1. The cost structure of a manufacturing account. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Stage | Calculation |
|---|---|
| **Raw materials consumed** | opening raw materials + purchases of raw materials + carriage inwards − returns − closing raw materials |
| **Prime cost** | raw materials consumed + direct labour + direct expenses |
| **Factory cost** | prime cost + factory overheads |
| **Production cost of goods completed** | factory cost + opening work in progress − closing work in progress |

## Work in progress

**Work in progress (WIP)** is goods that are **partly finished** at the end of the period, such as chairs that have been cut and joined but not yet polished. At the year end:

:::callout{type=definition}
- **opening WIP** is **added** (its cost was incurred last year but it is finished this year);
- **closing WIP** is **deducted** (part of this year's cost belongs to goods not yet finished).
:::

## Apportioning shared expenses

Some expenses are shared between the factory and the office and must be **apportioned** on a fair basis:

| Expense | Usual basis |
|---|---|
| rent and rates | floor area |
| electricity | metered units or floor area |
| insurance of buildings | floor area or value |
| depreciation | value of assets used in each part |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Rent of NLe 20,000 is to be shared between the factory and the office in the ratio of floor area, 60% : 40%.**

- Factory rent (manufacturing account) $= 60\% \times 20\,000 = 12\,000$
- Office rent (profit and loss section) $= 40\% \times 20\,000 = 8\,000$
:::

## Three kinds of inventory

A manufacturer holds three kinds of inventory, each shown in a different place:

| Inventory | Where adjusted |
|---|---|
| **raw materials** | manufacturing account (materials consumed) |
| **work in progress** | manufacturing account (after factory overheads) |
| **finished goods** | trading account (cost of sales) |

All three closing inventories are **current assets** in the statement of financial position.

:::callout{type=local-example}
A furniture workshop in Kissy, Freetown, buys timber (direct material), pays carpenters by the piece (direct labour), rents its workshop and uses electricity for machines (factory overheads), and sells chairs and beds from a showroom whose rent and sales staff are office and selling expenses. A manufacturing account shows the owner what it really costs to make each bed.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Office salaries** and **office rent** do not go in the manufacturing account. Only **factory** costs belong there. Put administration, selling and distribution expenses in the profit and loss section.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Direct materials**: materials that become part of the product.
- **Direct labour**: wages of the workers who make the product.
- **Prime cost**: raw materials consumed plus direct labour plus direct expenses.
- **Factory overheads** (indirect manufacturing costs): factory costs that cannot be traced to a single product.
- **Production cost of goods completed**: factory cost plus opening work in progress minus closing work in progress.
- **Raw materials consumed**: opening raw materials plus purchases and carriage inwards, less returns and closing raw materials.
:::

## Key points

- The **manufacturing account** finds the **production cost** of goods made.
- **Prime cost** = direct materials consumed + direct labour + direct expenses.
- **Production cost** = prime cost + factory overheads + opening WIP − closing WIP.
- Shared costs are **apportioned** between factory and office.
- Three inventories: **raw materials, WIP, finished goods**.

## Quick check

1. What is prime cost?
2. Give two examples of factory overheads.
3. Opening raw materials NLe 5,000, purchases NLe 40,000, closing raw materials NLe 7,000. Find raw materials consumed.
4. How is closing work in progress treated?
5. Electricity of NLe 9,000 is shared 2 : 1 between factory and office. How much goes to the manufacturing account?

**Answers:**

1. Direct materials consumed plus direct labour plus direct expenses.
2. Any two: factory rent; power; supervisors' wages; depreciation of machinery; factory insurance.
3. $5\,000 + 40\,000 - 7\,000 = 38\,000$: NLe 38,000.
4. It is deducted in the manufacturing account and shown as a current asset.
5. $\frac{2}{3} \times 9\,000 = 6\,000$: NLe 6,000.
