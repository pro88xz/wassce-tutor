Sodium itself has few everyday uses, but its compounds are everywhere: salt on food, caustic soda in soap factories, washing soda for clothes and baking soda in bread. This lesson covers the manufacture, properties and uses of the most important sodium compounds, and the reactions of sodium hydroxide that are used to identify metal ions.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the manufacture, properties and uses of sodium hydroxide
- describe the reactions of sodium hydroxide with metal ions and use them for identification
- state the sources and uses of sodium chloride
- describe the Solvay process for making sodium carbonate
- state the properties and uses of sodium carbonate and sodium hydrogencarbonate

## Sodium hydroxide, NaOH (caustic soda)

**Manufacture:** by the **electrolysis of brine** (concentrated NaCl solution) in a **membrane or diaphragm cell**:

- **Anode:** 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻
- **Cathode:** 2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻
- Na⁺ and OH⁻ ions remain: sodium hydroxide solution.

The membrane lets Na⁺ ions through but keeps the chlorine away from the sodium hydroxide, with which it would react.

**Properties:**

- white, waxy solid; **deliquescent** (absorbs water from air and dissolves)
- dissolves in water with **much heat** given out
- **strong alkali**: soapy feel, very **corrosive** to skin
- absorbs **carbon(IV) oxide** from air: 2NaOH + CO₂ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O (so its solution is stored in closed bottles, and it is not used as a primary standard)
- reacts with acids, ammonium salts (gives NH₃ on warming) and **amphoteric oxides**

### Identifying metal ions with sodium hydroxide

Add sodium hydroxide solution **dropwise**, then in **excess**:

| Metal ion | Precipitate with a few drops | In excess NaOH |
|---|---|---|
| **Cu²⁺** | **Blue** (Cu(OH)₂) | Insoluble |
| **Fe²⁺** | **Dirty green** (Fe(OH)₂) | Insoluble (turns brown in air) |
| **Fe³⁺** | **Red-brown** (Fe(OH)₃) | Insoluble |
| **Zn²⁺** | **White** | **Dissolves** (colourless zincate) |
| **Al³⁺** | **White** | **Dissolves** (colourless aluminate) |
| **Pb²⁺** | **White** | **Dissolves** |
| **Ca²⁺** | **White** | Insoluble |
| **NH₄⁺** | No precipitate; **ammonia** on warming | Turns damp red litmus blue |

Zinc, aluminium and lead hydroxides dissolve in excess because they are **amphoteric**. Aqueous ammonia then separates them: **zinc** hydroxide dissolves in excess ammonia, but aluminium and lead hydroxides do not.

$$\text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2\text{OH}^-\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Cu(OH)}_2\text{(s)}$$

**Uses of sodium hydroxide:**

- **soap making** (saponification of oils)
- **paper** making
- textiles and rayon
- **purifying bauxite** for aluminium extraction
- oven and drain cleaners
- making other sodium compounds
- refining petroleum

## Sodium chloride, NaCl (common salt)

**Sources:** **sea water** (evaporated in salt pans), **rock salt** deposits (mined or dissolved out as brine) and salt lakes.

**Uses:**

| Use | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Food** | Seasoning; essential for the body's nerves and fluids |
| **Preserving** | Salting fish and meat: salt draws water out of microbes |
| **Raw material** | Making **chlorine, sodium hydroxide, hydrogen** (electrolysis of brine), sodium metal and sodium carbonate |
| **Medicine** | Oral rehydration salts; saline drips |
| **Roads** | Melting ice (in cold countries) |
| **Soap making** | "Salting out" soap from the mixture |

## Sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃: the Solvay process

![Solvay process: brine saturated with ammonia is carbonated with CO₂ from a limestone kiln; sodium hydrogencarbonate precipitates, is filtered and heated to give sodium carbonate; ammonia and CO₂ are recycled](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/solvay-process.svg)

*Figure 1. Making sodium carbonate by the Solvay (ammonia–soda) process. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

1. **Brine** is saturated with **ammonia**.
2. **Carbon(IV) oxide** (from heating limestone, CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂) is bubbled through it in a **carbonating tower**. **Sodium hydrogencarbonate**, which is only slightly soluble, precipitates:

$$\text{NaCl} + \text{NH}_3 + \text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{NaHCO}_3 + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$$

3. The NaHCO₃ is **filtered** off and **heated** to give sodium carbonate:

$$2\text{NaHCO}_3 \rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2$$

4. The CO₂ is reused. **Ammonia is recovered** by heating the ammonium chloride with calcium hydroxide (from the CaO). The only waste product is **calcium chloride**.

**Forms:** **anhydrous** Na₂CO₃ (soda ash) and **washing soda**, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O, which is **efflorescent** (loses water to the air and becomes powdery).

**Properties:** soluble in water, giving an **alkaline** solution (hydrolysis); does **not** decompose on heating; reacts with acids, giving CO₂.

**Uses:**

- **making glass** (largest use)
- **softening water** (removes Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺)
- making soap, detergents and paper
- cleaning (washing soda)

## Sodium hydrogencarbonate, NaHCO₃ (baking soda)

- Made in the Solvay process (the intermediate).
- Slightly soluble; its solution is **weakly alkaline**.
- **Decomposes on heating:** 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂.

**Uses:**

- **baking powder**: mixed with a solid acid (such as cream of tartar), it releases CO₂ when wet or heated, making dough rise
- **antacid**: neutralises excess stomach acid
- **fire extinguishers**
- treating **acid** spills and **bee and ant stings**

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Soap makers** in Sierra Leone boil palm kernel oil or palm oil with **caustic soda** (sodium hydroxide) solution to make bar soap. Caustic soda burns skin and eyes, so makers should wear gloves and goggles and always **add the solid slowly to water**, not water to the solid, because a lot of heat is given out.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
To identify **Al³⁺ and Zn²⁺**, both give white precipitates that dissolve in excess NaOH. Use **aqueous ammonia** to tell them apart: zinc hydroxide dissolves in excess ammonia; aluminium hydroxide does not.
:::

## Key points

- **NaOH** is made by **electrolysis of brine**; it is deliquescent, corrosive and absorbs CO₂; used for **soap, paper and bauxite purification**.
- NaOH **identifies metal ions:** Cu²⁺ blue, Fe²⁺ green, Fe³⁺ red-brown; Zn²⁺, Al³⁺ and Pb²⁺ white, dissolving in excess; NH₄⁺ gives ammonia.
- **NaCl:** from sea water and rock salt; for food, preservation, chlorine and NaOH.
- **Solvay process:** brine + NH₃ + CO₂ → NaHCO₃ → (heat) Na₂CO₃; ammonia recycled. Na₂CO₃ is used for **glass, water softening and soap**.
- **NaHCO₃:** baking powder, antacid, fire extinguishers.

## Quick check

1. Why is solid sodium hydroxide not used to make a standard solution?
2. A solution gives a red-brown precipitate with NaOH. Which ion is present?
3. Write the equation for heating sodium hydrogencarbonate in the Solvay process.
4. Give two uses of sodium carbonate.

**Answers:**

1. It is deliquescent and absorbs CO₂ from the air, so its mass cannot be weighed accurately.
2. Fe³⁺ (iron(III)).
3. 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂.
4. Any two: glass making, water softening, soap and detergents, paper, cleaning.
