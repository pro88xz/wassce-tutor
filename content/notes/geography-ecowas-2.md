:::callout{type=definition}
**Regional cooperation** is when neighbouring countries work together to achieve goals they could not reach as easily alone, such as larger markets, shared infrastructure, management of shared rivers, and peace.
:::

Besides ECOWAS, West Africa's main regional organisations are the **Mano River Union (MRU)**, founded in **1973** by **Sierra Leone and Liberia** and later joined by **Guinea** and **Côte d'Ivoire**; the **West African Economic and Monetary Union (WAEMU/UEMOA)**; and river-basin bodies such as the **Senegal River Basin Development Organisation (OMVS)**, the **Niger Basin Authority** and the **Lake Chad Basin Commission**. This lesson describes these organisations and the benefits and problems of regional cooperation.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the meaning and reasons for regional cooperation
- describe the Mano River Union: members, aims and achievements
- describe other regional organisations in West Africa and Africa
- explain the benefits of regional cooperation
- explain the problems of regional cooperation and suggest solutions

## Why countries cooperate

1. **Larger markets** for goods than one small country can offer.
2. **Shared resources** such as rivers, lakes and power.
3. **Shared infrastructure**: roads, railways, power lines, pipelines.
4. **Peace and security** across borders.
5. **Stronger voice** in world trade and politics.
6. **Common problems**: disease, drought, desertification, refugees.

## The Mano River Union

![Map of West Africa with the Mano River Union countries, Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia and Côte d'Ivoire, highlighted, and the headquarters at Freetown](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/mano-river-union-map.svg)

*Figure 1. Member states of the Mano River Union. Outlines from Natural Earth; WASSCE Tutor map.*

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Founded** | **1973**, by the **Mano River Declaration** between **Liberia and Sierra Leone** |
| **Named after** | the **Mano River**, which forms part of the Sierra Leone–Liberia border |
| **Later members** | **Guinea** (1980) and **Côte d'Ivoire** (2008) |
| **Headquarters** | **Freetown**, Sierra Leone |

**Aims:**

1. Expand **trade** between members by removing barriers.
2. Promote **economic cooperation** in agriculture, industry, transport and energy.
3. Build **joint projects** such as roads and bridges.
4. Promote **peace and security** in the sub-region.
5. Raise **living standards** of the people.

**Achievements:**

1. The **Mano River bridge** linking Sierra Leone and Liberia, and road links between member states.
2. Joint **training institutions** and cooperation on customs and immigration.
3. Joint action on **cross-border security** after the civil wars of the 1990s.
4. Cooperation in fighting the **Ebola outbreak (2014–2016)**, which hit Guinea, Liberia and Sierra Leone hardest.
5. Support for the regional **CLSG power line** linking Côte d'Ivoire, Liberia, Sierra Leone and Guinea.

**Problems:** the **civil wars** in Liberia and Sierra Leone (1989–2003) halted its work for years; **lack of funds**; **similar products**, so little trade; **poor roads**; **border disputes** (such as over **Yenga**, on the Sierra Leone–Guinea border); and language differences between English- and French-speaking members.

## Other regional organisations

| Organisation | Founded | Members and purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **West African Economic and Monetary Union (WAEMU / UEMOA)** | **1994** | **eight** mostly French-speaking states (Benin, Burkina Faso, Côte d'Ivoire, Guinea-Bissau, Mali, Niger, Senegal, Togo) sharing the **CFA franc**; central bank (BCEAO) in **Dakar** |
| **Senegal River Basin Development Organisation (OMVS)** | **1972** | **Mali, Mauritania, Senegal**, and **Guinea** since 2006; built the **Manantali** and **Diama** dams for power, irrigation and navigation |
| **Niger Basin Authority** | **1980** | nine countries along the Niger and its tributaries; manages the river's water |
| **Lake Chad Basin Commission** | **1964** | countries around Lake Chad; manages the shrinking lake's water |
| **Alliance of Sahel States (AES)** | **2023–2024** | **Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger**, formed after they broke with ECOWAS |
| **African Union (AU)** | **2002** | **55** member states; replaced the **Organisation of African Unity (OAU, 1963)**; headquarters in **Addis Ababa** |
| **African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA)** | trading from **2021** | aims at a single African market; secretariat in **Accra** |

## Benefits of regional cooperation

1. **Larger markets** and more trade.
2. **Economies of scale** in industry.
3. **Shared infrastructure** and power, such as the **West African Power Pool**.
4. **Joint management** of rivers and lakes.
5. **Free movement** of people for work, study and trade.
6. **Peace and security** through joint action.
7. **Stronger bargaining** with other blocs.

## Problems and solutions

| Problem | Possible solution |
|---|---|
| political instability and coups | promote democracy and good governance |
| language and currency differences | language teaching; harmonised currencies |
| members produce similar goods | diversify and industrialise |
| poor transport links | build regional roads, railways, power lines |
| lack of funds; unpaid dues | automatic levies on imports |
| overlapping memberships | coordinate and merge organisations |
| fear of domination | fair sharing of costs and benefits |
| border disputes | settle through courts and dialogue |

:::callout{type=local-example}
For **Sierra Leone**, a small country with a small market, regional cooperation is especially valuable. Through ECOWAS and the Mano River Union it gains access to larger markets, regional electricity, cross-border roads, free movement for its traders and students, and support for peace.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The **Mano River Union** headquarters is in **Freetown**, not Monrovia. And the MRU began with **two** members (Sierra Leone and Liberia), not four.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Mano River Union**: the organisation founded in 1973 by Liberia and Sierra Leone, later joined by Guinea (1980) and Côte d'Ivoire (2008), with its headquarters in Freetown.
- **African Continental Free Trade Area** (AfCFTA): the Africa-wide free trade area, trading since 2021.
- **Lake Chad Basin Commission**: the body set up in 1964 by the countries around Lake Chad to manage its water and resources.
:::

## Key points

- Regional cooperation brings **larger markets, shared resources and infrastructure, peace and a stronger voice**.
- **Mano River Union:** **1973**, **Sierra Leone and Liberia**, then **Guinea (1980)** and **Côte d'Ivoire (2008)**; headquarters **Freetown**.
- Others: **WAEMU (1994, CFA franc)**, **OMVS (1972)**, **Niger Basin Authority (1980)**, **Lake Chad Basin Commission (1964)**, **AU (2002)**, **AfCFTA (2021)**.
- Problems: **instability, language and currency differences, similar products, poor transport, funding, border disputes**.

## Quick check

1. Which two countries founded the Mano River Union, and when?
2. Where is the headquarters of the Mano River Union?
3. Which organisation uses the CFA franc in West Africa?
4. Give two benefits of regional cooperation.
5. State two problems of the Mano River Union.

**Answers:**

1. Sierra Leone and Liberia, in 1973.
2. Freetown.
3. The West African Economic and Monetary Union (WAEMU/UEMOA).
4. Any two: larger markets; economies of scale; shared infrastructure and power; joint river management; free movement; peace and security; stronger bargaining.
5. Any two: civil wars halted its work; lack of funds; similar products; poor roads; border disputes; language differences.
