The sharp taste of vinegar, the sting of an ant bite and the smell of rancid butter all come from **alkanoic acids** (carboxylic acids). They contain the **carboxyl group, –COOH**, and are weak acids. Their best-known member is **ethanoic acid**, the acid in vinegar.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the general formula and functional group of the alkanoic acids
- name and draw the first members of the series
- explain the physical properties of alkanoic acids
- explain why alkanoic acids are weak acids
- describe the laboratory and industrial preparation of ethanoic acid and the making of vinegar

## Structure and names

:::callout{type=definition}
**Alkanoic acids** are organic acids containing the **carboxyl group, –COOH**. General formula: **CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH** (n = 0, 1, 2 …), or RCOOH.
:::

![Displayed formulas of methanoic acid, ethanoic acid and propanoic acid, each with the carboxyl group outlined](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/alkanoic-acids.svg)

*Figure 1. The first three alkanoic acids. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

The carboxyl group combines a **carbonyl group (C=O)** and a **hydroxyl group (–OH)** on the same carbon. The carbon of –COOH is counted in the name.

| Name | Formula | Boiling point | Found in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Methanoic acid** | HCOOH | 101 °C | Ant stings, nettles |
| **Ethanoic acid** | CH₃COOH | 118 °C | **Vinegar** |
| **Propanoic acid** | C₂H₅COOH | 141 °C | Used as a food preservative |
| **Butanoic acid** | C₃H₇COOH | 164 °C | Rancid butter, sweat |

**Long-chain (fatty) acids** such as **hexadecanoic (palmitic) acid**, C₁₅H₃₁COOH, and **octadecanoic (stearic) acid**, C₁₇H₃₅COOH, are found combined in fats and oils. Palmitic acid is named after the **oil palm**.

## Physical properties

- **High boiling points**, higher than alkanols of similar Mᵣ: the molecules form **two hydrogen bonds** with each other, making pairs (**dimers**).
- The first four are **miscible with water** (they hydrogen-bond with water); solubility falls as the chain grows.
- Small acids are colourless liquids with sharp, sour or unpleasant smells; long-chain acids are waxy solids.
- Pure ethanoic acid freezes at about **17 °C** into ice-like crystals, so it is called **glacial ethanoic acid**.

## Alkanoic acids are weak acids

In water, only a **small fraction** of ethanoic acid molecules ionise (about 1% in a 0.1 mol/dm³ solution):

$$\text{CH}_3\text{COOH(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-\text{(aq)} + \text{H}^+\text{(aq)}$$

So, compared with a **strong acid** (such as HCl) of the **same concentration**, ethanoic acid:

| Property | Hydrochloric acid, 0.1 mol/dm³ | Ethanoic acid, 0.1 mol/dm³ |
|---|---|---|
| **Ionisation** | Complete | Partial (about 1%) |
| **pH** | 1 | About 3 |
| **Electrical conductivity** | High | Low |
| **Rate of reaction with magnesium** | Fast fizzing | Slower fizzing |

The **same amount** of each acid still neutralises the **same amount** of alkali: as H⁺ ions are used up, more ethanoic acid molecules ionise.

## Preparing ethanoic acid

**Laboratory: oxidation of ethanol.** Heat ethanol **under reflux** with excess **acidified potassium dichromate(VI)** (orange → green), then distil off the ethanoic acid:

$$\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 2[\text{O}] \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

Heating under **reflux** (with a vertical condenser) stops the volatile ethanol and ethanal escaping before they are fully oxidised.

**Vinegar: bacterial oxidation.** Bacteria (**Acetobacter**) in air oxidise dilute ethanol (wine, palm wine, cider) to ethanoic acid:

$$\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

Vinegar contains about **4–8% ethanoic acid**.

**Industrial:** mainly by reacting **methanol with carbon(II) oxide** over a rhodium or iridium catalyst:

$$\text{CH}_3\text{OH} + \text{CO} \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Finding the formula of an alkanoic acid.** An alkanoic acid has Mᵣ = 88. Find its formula and name. (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16)

For CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH: $M_r = 12n + 2n + 1 + 45 = 14n + 46$.

$$14n + 46 = 88 \quad\Rightarrow\quad n = 3$$

The acid is **C₃H₇COOH, butanoic acid**.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Palm wine** that stands for a day or two turns **sour**: bacteria from the air oxidise its ethanol to **ethanoic acid**, the same change that turns wine into vinegar. Tappers in Sierra Leone sell fresh palm wine early, before it sours. Sour palm wine is sometimes used like vinegar in cooking.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Count **every** carbon, including the one in –COOH. CH₃COOH has **two** carbons, so it is **ethanoic** acid, not methanoic acid.
:::

## Key points

- **Alkanoic acids**, CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH, contain the **carboxyl group –COOH** (C=O plus –OH).
- Methanoic (ants), **ethanoic (vinegar)**, propanoic, butanoic (rancid butter); fatty acids such as palmitic and stearic acid occur in fats.
- **Hydrogen bonding** (dimers) gives high boiling points; small acids are water-soluble.
- They are **weak acids**: only partly ionised, so pH is higher and conductivity lower than a strong acid of the same concentration.
- Ethanoic acid is made by **oxidising ethanol** (acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, reflux) or, as vinegar, by **Acetobacter**; industrially from **methanol + CO**.

## Quick check

1. What is the functional group of the alkanoic acids?
2. Name the acid with formula C₂H₅COOH.
3. Why does 0.1 mol/dm³ ethanoic acid have a higher pH than 0.1 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid?
4. Why is ethanol heated under reflux when it is oxidised to ethanoic acid?

**Answers:**

1. The carboxyl group, –COOH.
2. Propanoic acid.
3. Ethanoic acid is a weak acid: only a small fraction of its molecules ionise, so the H⁺ concentration is lower.
4. So that volatile ethanol and ethanal condense and return to the flask until they are fully oxidised.
