**Political development in colonial Sierra Leone** from 1900 to 1945 saw the first steps towards African participation in government. Freetown had a **City Council** from **1893**, with **Sir Samuel Lewis** as first mayor. Sierra Leoneans joined the **National Congress of British West Africa (1920)**, and the **1924 Constitution** introduced the **first elected African members** of the Legislative Council (for the Colony) and the first **paramount chiefs** to represent the Protectorate. Workers organised the **1919 and 1926 railway strikes**, and in the **1930s** **I. T. A. Wallace-Johnson** and the **West African Youth League** brought radical politics to Freetown, until his **detention in 1939**. This lesson explains **political developments in Sierra Leone from 1900 to 1945**.

## Government in the early twentieth century

| Part | Government |
|---|---|
| **The Colony** | the **Governor**, an **Executive Council** of senior officials, and a **Legislative Council** of officials and **nominated** members (some of them Krio); **Freetown City Council** (1893) with an elected mayor and councillors |
| **The Protectorate** | **District Commissioners** and **paramount chiefs** under **indirect rule**; no representation in the Legislative Council until **1924**; **Native Administration** with chiefdom treasuries from **1937** |

## Riots and strikes

1. **The 1919 riots:** after the First World War, **prices rose** and **rice was scarce**. In **July 1919** railway workers went on **strike** over low pay, and crowds in Freetown **attacked Lebanese shops**, accusing Lebanese traders of hoarding rice and raising prices. Troops restored order.
2. **The 1926 railway strike:** railway workers struck for weeks over **pay and conditions**; the government defeated the strike and punished the workers, but it showed the **growing strength of organised labour**.

## Sierra Leone and the NCBWA

Sierra Leoneans helped to found the **National Congress of British West Africa** in **Accra (1920)**. Delegates included **Dr H. C. Bankole-Bright** and **F. W. Dove**. The Sierra Leone branch supported the Congress demands for **elected members**, **Africans in senior posts** and a **West African university**.

## The 1924 Constitution (Slater Constitution)

Named after **Governor Alexander Ransford Slater**, it was the first constitution to include **elected Africans** and the **Protectorate**.

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Legislative Council** | **official** members (heads of departments) still in the **majority** |
| **Elected members** | **three** members **elected** for the **Colony** (two for Freetown, one for the rural areas), on a **limited franchise** of property owners and taxpayers |
| **Protectorate members** | **three paramount chiefs**, **nominated** by the Governor |
| **Other nominated members** | some Europeans and Africans chosen by the Governor |

**Importance:**

1. It introduced the **elective principle** in Sierra Leone, a result of NCBWA pressure.
2. For the first time the **Protectorate was represented** in the Legislative Council.
3. **Weaknesses:** officials kept control; the vote was limited to a few; Protectorate members were **nominated chiefs**, not elected; the educated elite of the Protectorate had no voice.

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figure"}
**Bankole-Bright** was one of the elected members and an outspoken critic of the government.
:::

## Wallace-Johnson and the West African Youth League

**I. T. A. Wallace-Johnson (1894–1965)**, a Sierra Leonean journalist and trade unionist, had organised workers and written against colonial rule in **Nigeria** and the **Gold Coast**, where he founded the **West African Youth League (1935)**.

In **1938** he returned to Freetown and founded the **Sierra Leone branch of the Youth League**, which:

1. attracted **workers, youth and market women**, not just the educated elite;
2. organised **trade unions** and **strikes** in **1938–1939**;
3. criticised **colonial injustice** in its newspaper, the ***African Standard***;
4. won seats in the **Freetown City Council** elections.

Alarmed, the government passed laws against sedition, and in **1939**, at the start of the **Second World War**, **detained Wallace-Johnson** under emergency powers. The League declined, but it had shown that nationalism could become a **mass movement**.

## The Second World War and Sierra Leone

1. **Freetown** became a major **Allied convoy port** and naval base.
2. Sierra Leonean soldiers served in the **Royal West African Frontier Force**, including in **Burma**.
3. War brought **jobs**, **rising prices** and **shortages**, and strengthened trade unions.
4. In **1943** two Africans were appointed to the **Executive Council**, a small sign of change.
5. After the war, new constitutional changes, the **Protectorate Assembly (1946)** and the **Stevenson Constitution (1951)**, led towards independence.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Wallace-Johnson's career shows how Sierra Leone was part of a **West African** nationalist movement: he worked in Lagos and Accra, attended the **Manchester Pan-African Congress (1945)**, and later helped to found political parties in Sierra Leone. He is remembered in Freetown through **Wallace-Johnson Street** in the city centre.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The **1924 Constitution** did **not** give Africans control of government. Officials kept a **majority** in the Legislative Council, only **three** members were elected, and the Protectorate was represented by **nominated** chiefs. Its importance lies in introducing the **elective principle** and **Protectorate representation**.
:::

## Key points

- **Colony:** Governor, Executive and Legislative Councils, **Freetown City Council (1893)**; **Protectorate:** DCs and paramount chiefs; **Native Administration (1937)**.
- **1919 riots** (rice shortage, anti-Lebanese, railway strike) and the **1926 railway strike**.
- Sierra Leone in the **NCBWA (1920)**: **Bankole-Bright**, **F. W. Dove**.
- **1924 Constitution:** **three elected** Colony members, **three nominated paramount chiefs**, official majority.
- **Wallace-Johnson** and the **West African Youth League (1938)**: mass politics, strikes; detained **1939**.
