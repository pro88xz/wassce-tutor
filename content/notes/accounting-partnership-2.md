:::callout{type=definition}
**Goodwill** is the value of a business **over and above the value of its separate net assets**, arising from its reputation, location, customers and staff.
:::

It matters in partnerships whenever the **profit-sharing ratio changes**: on the **admission** of a new partner, the **retirement** or **death** of a partner, or a change in the agreement. Existing partners are compensated for the goodwill they built up, and assets are **revalued** so that gains or losses up to that date go to the old partners. This lesson explains goodwill, its valuation, and the entries for admitting a new partner.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain goodwill and the factors that create it
- value goodwill by the average profits and super profits methods
- record goodwill on the admission of a new partner without keeping a goodwill account
- prepare a revaluation account
- explain the main entries on the retirement of a partner

## Goodwill

**Factors that create goodwill:** a good reputation and location, loyal customers, skilled staff and management, strong brands or trademarks, little competition, and a long record of profits.

| Method | Calculation |
|---|---|
| **Average profits** | average annual profit × agreed number of years' purchase |
| **Super profits** | (average profit − normal return on capital) × number of years' purchase |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Profits for the last three years were NLe 36,000, NLe 40,000 and NLe 44,000. Goodwill is valued at two years' purchase of average profits.**

Average profit $= \frac{36\,000 + 40\,000 + 44\,000}{3} = 40\,000$

Goodwill $= 40\,000 \times 2 = 80\,000$
:::

## Admission of a new partner

When a new partner joins:

1. **Revalue the assets and liabilities**: gains and losses go to the old partners in the **old ratio**.
2. **Adjust for goodwill**: the old partners are credited for the goodwill they created; if no goodwill account is kept, all partners are then debited in the **new ratio**.
3. **Record the new partner's capital.**

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Kamara and Sesay share profits 3 : 2. They admit Bah, and the new ratio is Kamara 2, Sesay 2, Bah 1. Goodwill is valued at NLe 20,000 but no goodwill account is to be kept. Premises are revalued upward by NLe 10,000, inventory is reduced by NLe 2,000 and a provision for doubtful debts of NLe 1,000 is created. Bah brings in NLe 30,000 capital in cash.**
:::

**Revaluation account**

| Debit | NLe |
|---|---|
| Inventory (decrease) | 2,000 |
| Provision for doubtful debts | 1,000 |
| **Profit on revaluation: Kamara (3/5)** | **4,200** |
| **Profit on revaluation: Sesay (2/5)** | **2,800** |
| **Total** | **10,000** |

| Credit | NLe |
|---|---|
| Premises (increase) | 10,000 |
| **Total** | **10,000** |

Net gain $= 10\,000 - 2\,000 - 1\,000 = 7\,000$, shared 3 : 2.

**Goodwill adjustment through the capital accounts**

| | Kamara | Sesay | Bah |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credit goodwill in **old** ratio 3 : 2 | +12,000 | +8,000 | |
| Debit goodwill in **new** ratio 2 : 2 : 1 | −8,000 | −8,000 | −4,000 |
| **Net effect** | **+4,000** | **0** | **−4,000** |

Bah, in effect, pays NLe 4,000 to Kamara for his share of goodwill. Sesay neither gains nor loses, because his share stays at two-fifths.

**Bah's capital account:** credit cash 30,000; debit goodwill 4,000; balance **26,000**.

## Retirement of a partner

When a partner retires:

1. **Revalue** assets and liabilities; share gains or losses in the **old** ratio.
2. **Credit the retiring partner** with their share of **goodwill**.
3. Transfer their **current account** balance to their capital account.
4. **Pay** the amount due in cash, or transfer it to a **loan account** to be repaid later, usually with interest.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many family businesses in Sierra Leone are run as partnerships between relatives. When a new member joins, valuing goodwill fairly, and revaluing the shop or land at current prices, prevents later disputes about who built up the business's value.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Revaluation gains and losses, and the goodwill credit, are shared in the **old** profit-sharing ratio: they arose before the new partner joined. Only the write-off of goodwill (when no goodwill account is kept) uses the **new** ratio.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Super profits**: the profit above a normal return on capital; goodwill may be valued as super profits times a number of years' purchase.
- **Revaluation account**: the account that records gains and losses when assets and liabilities are revalued on a change of partners, shared in the old ratio.
- **Admission of a partner**: a change in which a new partner joins the firm; assets are revalued and goodwill is adjusted between the partners.
:::

## Key points

- **Goodwill** is the value of a business above its net assets.
- Valuation: **average profits × years' purchase**, or **super profits × years' purchase**.
- On admission: **revaluation** and goodwill credit in the **old** ratio; goodwill write-off in the **new** ratio.
- On retirement: revalue, credit goodwill, then **pay** or create a **loan account**.

## Quick check

1. Define goodwill.
2. Give three factors that create goodwill.
3. Average profits NLe 25,000; goodwill is three years' purchase. Find goodwill.
4. In which ratio is a revaluation profit shared on admission of a partner?
5. What happens to the amount due to a retiring partner who is not paid at once?

**Answers:**

1. The value of a business over and above the value of its separate net assets.
2. Any three: good reputation; good location; loyal customers; skilled staff; strong brands; little competition; record of profits.
3. $25\,000 \times 3 = 75\,000$: NLe 75,000.
4. In the old profit-sharing ratio.
5. It is transferred to a loan account and repaid later, usually with interest.
