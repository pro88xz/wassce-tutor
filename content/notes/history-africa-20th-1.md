The **First World War (1914–1918)** and the **Second World War (1939–1945)** deeply affected Africa. In the **First World War**, Africans fought and carried supplies in campaigns against the **German colonies** of **Togoland, Kamerun, South West Africa and East Africa**, and over **150,000 West Africans** served France in Europe; Germany lost its colonies, which became **League of Nations mandates**. In the **Second World War**, African soldiers fought in **Ethiopia, North Africa and Burma**, African raw materials fed the Allied war effort, and the war's ideas and results, such as the **Atlantic Charter (1941)**, the **United Nations** and a **weakened Europe**, gave a powerful boost to **nationalism and independence**. This lesson explains **the effects of the two World Wars on Africa**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe Africa's part in the First World War
- explain the effects of the First World War on Africa
- describe Africa's part in the Second World War
- explain the political, economic and social effects of the Second World War on Africa
- explain how the Second World War encouraged decolonisation

## Africa in the First World War

| Campaign | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Togoland (August 1914)** | British and French forces from the Gold Coast and Dahomey conquered German Togoland in a few weeks, the first Allied victory of the war |
| **Kamerun (1914–1916)** | British, French and Belgian forces, including the **West African Frontier Force**, conquered German Kamerun |
| **South West Africa (1915)** | conquered by **South African** forces |
| **East Africa (1914–1918)** | the German commander **Lettow-Vorbeck** fought a long guerrilla campaign; huge numbers of African **carriers (porters)** were used, and many thousands died of disease and hunger |

**France** recruited heavily in its colonies: over **150,000 West African soldiers**, the **Tirailleurs Sénégalais**, served in Europe. In **1918** the Senegalese deputy **Blaise Diagne** led a recruitment drive in return for promises of rights.

### Effects of the First World War on Africa

1. **Loss of life:** many African soldiers and carriers died.
2. **German colonies redistributed** as **League of Nations mandates**: **Togoland** and **Kamerun** were divided between **Britain and France**; **Tanganyika** went to Britain, **Ruanda-Urundi** to Belgium and **South West Africa** to South Africa.
3. **Economic hardship:** trade with Germany stopped; prices of imports rose; **forced recruitment** and requisitions took labour and food.
4. **Revolts** against forced recruitment, for example in **western Upper Volta and Dahomey (1915–1917)**.
5. **New ideas:** US President Wilson's idea of **self-determination**, and soldiers' experience abroad, encouraged demands for rights.
6. **Early nationalism:** the war helped inspire the **NCBWA (1920)** and its demands for representation.

## Africa in the Second World War

1. **Ethiopia:** Italy invaded in **1935–36**; British, Ethiopian and African troops, including West Africans, helped liberate it, and **Emperor Haile Selassie** returned to Addis Ababa in **1941**.
2. **North Africa:** fierce fighting in Libya and Egypt, including **El Alamein (1942)**, and Allied landings in Morocco and Algeria.
3. **Burma:** the **81st and 82nd (West Africa) Divisions** of the Royal West African Frontier Force fought the Japanese in Burma.
4. **French Africa:** **French Equatorial Africa**, led by the Black governor **Félix Éboué**, joined **de Gaulle's Free French** in **1940**, and **Brazzaville** became the Free French capital in Africa.
5. **Raw materials:** Africa supplied **rubber, tin, palm oil, cocoa, iron ore** and other goods, especially after Japan seized Malaya's rubber and tin in 1942.
6. **Bases and routes:** West African ports and airfields, such as **Freetown** and **Takoradi**, served Allied shipping and aircraft.

:::callout{type=local-example}
During the Second World War, **Freetown** became one of the Allies' most important **convoy ports**. Hundreds of merchant ships gathered in the deep, sheltered harbour to be escorted across the Atlantic. Sierra Leonean soldiers served in the **Royal West African Frontier Force** in **Burma**, and the war brought jobs, rising prices and new political awareness to Freetown, which helped the growth of trade unions and parties after 1945.
:::

## Effects of the Second World War on Africa

![How the Second World War changed Africa: African soldiers fought in Burma and came home as ex-servicemen; the Atlantic Charter of 1941 spoke of the right of peoples to choose their government; Britain and France were weakened; the USA and USSR opposed old-style colonialism; the United Nations Charter of 1945 supported self-determination; and wartime shortages, price controls and unfair produce buying caused hardship. Together these led to stronger demands for independence](https://wasscetutor.com/img/history/world-war-two-effects.svg)

*Figure 1. How the Second World War changed Africa. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Area | Effects |
|---|---|
| **Political** | the **Atlantic Charter (1941)** declared "the right of all peoples to choose the form of government under which they will live"; the **UN Charter (1945)** supported **self-determination**; Britain and France were **weakened**; the **USA and USSR** opposed colonial empires; **ex-servicemen** became active in politics |
| **Economic** | higher demand for African produce; **marketing boards** controlled crop prices; **shortages and inflation**; growth of some industries, ports and roads; colonial governments began **development plans** (Colonial Development and Welfare Act, **1940**) |
| **Social** | growth of **towns** and wage labour; soldiers saw Europeans as **equals**, learned skills and gained literacy; spread of new ideas |
| **On nationalism** | the war turned nationalism into a **mass movement** and led directly to events such as the **Accra riots (1948)** |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**How the Second World War led to the Accra riots**

West African ex-servicemen had been promised jobs, pensions and better conditions. When they came home, they found **unemployment**, **rising prices** and **European firms** controlling trade. On **28 February 1948**, ex-servicemen marching in Accra to present a petition to the Governor were fired on by police; three were killed. Riots followed, and the British set up the **Watson Commission**, which led to constitutional reform and, eventually, Ghana's independence.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not say that Africans "were not involved" in the World Wars because the wars began in Europe. Africa was a **battlefield** in both wars, and **hundreds of thousands of Africans** served as soldiers and carriers.
:::

## Key figures and terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figures and terms"}
- **Mandate**: a former German colony, such as Togoland or Kamerun, that the League of Nations gave to another power, such as Britain or France, to govern after the First World War.
- **Tirailleurs Sénégalais**: African soldiers recruited by France from its colonies, who served in large numbers in Europe.
- **Atlantic Charter** (1941): the declaration by Britain and the United States of the right of peoples to choose their own government, which African nationalists quoted.
:::

## Key points

- **First World War:** campaigns in **Togoland, Kamerun, South West Africa, East Africa**; **Tirailleurs Sénégalais** in Europe; German colonies became **mandates**; hardship and revolts; ideas of **self-determination**.
- **Second World War:** fighting in **Ethiopia, North Africa and Burma**; **Free French** in Equatorial Africa; Africa supplied raw materials and bases such as **Freetown**.
- Effects of the Second World War: **Atlantic Charter**, **UN**, **weakened Europe**, **ex-servicemen**, economic hardship, mass **nationalism**.

## Quick check

1. Name two German colonies in Africa conquered during the First World War.
2. What happened to German colonies after the First World War?
3. Where did West African divisions fight in the Second World War?
4. What did the Atlantic Charter of 1941 say?
5. Why was Freetown important in the Second World War?
6. Give three effects of the Second World War on Africa.

**Answers:**

1. Any two: Togoland; Kamerun; South West Africa; German East Africa.
2. They became League of Nations mandates governed by Britain, France, Belgium and South Africa.
3. In Burma (and earlier in Ethiopia and East Africa).
4. That all peoples had the right to choose the form of government under which they would live.
5. It was a major Allied convoy port where merchant ships gathered to be escorted across the Atlantic.
6. Any three: the Atlantic Charter and the UN encouraged self-determination; Britain and France were weakened; ex-servicemen became active in politics; shortages and inflation; marketing boards; growth of towns; development plans; mass nationalism.
