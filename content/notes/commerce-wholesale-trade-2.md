# Wholesale operations: breaking bulk, warehousing and distribution

## Learning objectives
Analyse wholesaler services, compare wholesale formats and explain how wholesalers manage inventory and distribution risk.

## The wholesaler's role
A wholesaler buys goods in substantial quantities, often from producers or importers, and resells mainly to retailers or other business users. The wholesaler bridges the difference between large-scale production and the smaller, varied orders required by retailers.

Services include:
- **Breaking bulk:** dividing large consignments into smaller quantities.
- **Storage:** holding goods until retailers need them.
- **Financing:** paying suppliers before retailers have sold all the stock.
- **Risk bearing:** accepting risks of damage, theft, deterioration and price changes.
- **Transport and distribution:** delivering goods to retail outlets.
- **Market information:** reporting demand, complaints and local trends to producers.

## Types of wholesaler
A merchant wholesaler takes ownership of goods and earns from resale. A cash-and-carry wholesaler sells mainly to buyers who collect and pay promptly. A general wholesaler handles many product lines, while a specialist wholesaler concentrates on a narrower range. Agents and brokers arrange transactions but may not take ownership of goods.

## Inventory decisions
Too much stock increases storage, insurance, deterioration and financing costs. Too little stock causes lost sales and weakens retailer confidence. Wholesalers forecast demand, set reorder levels, inspect deliveries and monitor slow-moving stock.

For perishable products, first-expiry-first-out (FEFO) helps ensure items with the earliest expiry dates are dispatched first. For non-perishable stock, first-in-first-out (FIFO) is commonly used to rotate older stock.

## Why manufacturers may bypass wholesalers
Large retailers may buy directly from manufacturers because they have enough scale, storage and distribution capacity. Online platforms can also connect producers and buyers. However, removing a wholesaler transfers storage, credit, transport and sales responsibilities to another party; it does not make those costs disappear.
