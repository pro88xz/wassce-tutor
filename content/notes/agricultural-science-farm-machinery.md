A hand hoe can till about a tenth of a hectare in a hard day's work; a tractor with a disc plough can do several hectares. **Mechanization**, the use of machines in farming, multiplies what each farmer can do, and it is a key to producing more rice and other food in Sierra Leone. But machines are expensive, need fuel, spare parts and trained operators, and suit some farms better than others. This lesson describes the tractor and its implements in the order they are used, other farm machines, and the advantages, problems and maintenance of farm mechanization.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define farm mechanization
- describe the main parts of a tractor and their functions
- name tractor implements for each farm operation and state their uses
- describe other farm machines, including processing machines
- state the advantages and limitations of mechanization in Sierra Leone
- describe the maintenance of farm machinery

## Farm mechanization

:::callout{type=definition}
**Farm mechanization** is the use of machines, powered by engines, electricity or animals, to carry out farm operations such as land clearing, tillage, planting, spraying, harvesting and processing.
:::

## The tractor

The tractor is the main power unit on a mechanized farm. It pulls or powers other implements.

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **engine** | produces power (usually diesel) |
| **power take-off (PTO)** | a rotating shaft at the back that drives implements such as rotavators, sprayers and threshers |
| **hydraulic system** | raises and lowers mounted implements |
| **three-point linkage** | attaches implements to the back of the tractor |
| **drawbar** | a bar for pulling trailed implements and trailers |
| **tyres** | large, deep-treaded rear tyres give grip in soft soil |

A **power tiller** (two-wheel or walking tractor) is a small tractor steered by an operator walking behind it. It is well suited to small farms and **inland valley swamps**.

## Implements for each operation

![The sequence of operations from bush to harvest, with hand tools and machines for each: clearing, ploughing, harrowing, ridging, planting, weeding and harvesting](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/tillage-sequence.svg)

| Operation | Implement | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **land clearing** | bulldozer, tree pusher, rotary slasher | removes trees, stumps and bush |
| **primary tillage** | **disc plough**, mouldboard plough | breaks and turns the soil, buries weeds and trash |
| **secondary tillage** | **disc harrow**, spike-tooth harrow, rotavator | breaks clods into a fine **tilth** and levels the land |
| **ridging** | **ridger** | makes ridges for cassava, sweet potato, groundnut |
| **planting** | **planter**, seed drill, rice transplanter | places seeds at the right depth and spacing |
| **crop care** | **boom sprayer**, fertiliser distributor | applies chemicals and fertilisers evenly |
| **harvesting** | **combine harvester**, reaper, mower | cuts (and threshes) the crop |
| **transport** | trailer | carries inputs and produce |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Mixing up plough and harrow.** The **plough** does the first, deep breaking of the soil (**primary tillage**). The **harrow** comes after, to break clods and make a fine seedbed (**secondary tillage**).
:::

## Processing machines

- **Rice thresher**: separates grains from the straw.
- **Rice mill (huller)**: removes the husk to give milled rice.
- **Maize sheller**: removes grains from the cob.
- **Cassava grater** and **press**: for gari and fufu.
- **Oil palm press** and digester: extract palm oil.
- **Groundnut sheller** and **decorticator**.

These machines save women and children many hours of hard work and reduce losses.

## Advantages of mechanization

- Larger areas can be farmed, so **output increases**.
- Work is done **faster and on time** (planting at the start of the rains matters).
- **Less drudgery**; farming becomes more attractive to young people.
- Operations such as ploughing and spraying are more **uniform**.
- **Less dependence on hired labour**, which is scarce at peak times.
- Processing machines **reduce post-harvest losses** and add value.

## Limitations in Sierra Leone

- **High cost** of tractors, implements, fuel and spare parts.
- **Small, scattered farms** and fragmented land holdings.
- **Stumps, rocks and steep slopes** on upland farms; waterlogged swamps where heavy tractors sink.
- **Lack of skilled operators and mechanics**; spare parts are hard to find.
- **Poor roads** to farms.
- Possible **soil compaction** and erosion from heavy machines.
- Machines can **replace labour**, reducing jobs in rural areas.

## Making mechanization work

- **Hiring services**: farmers hire a tractor and operator by the hectare instead of buying.
- **Agricultural Business Centres** and **cooperatives**: farmers share machines and services.
- **Appropriate machines**: power tillers for swamps, small threshers and mills for villages.
- **Training** operators and mechanics; stocking spare parts locally.
- **Land development**: clearing stumps and consolidating plots.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Power tillers in the bolilands and swamps.** In the bolilands of the north and in inland valley swamps, heavy four-wheel tractors often get stuck. Two-wheel **power tillers** fitted with cage wheels can puddle the wet soil for rice and are cheap enough for a farmer group or cooperative to own together. Paired with a small **thresher** and **rice mill**, they turn a family swamp farm into a much more productive one.
:::

## Maintenance of farm machinery

:::callout{type=definition}
**Daily checks before work:** engine **oil** level, **coolant** (radiator water), **fuel**, **tyre pressure**, **battery**, loose nuts and bolts.

**Regular servicing:** grease moving parts at the grease nipples; change the engine oil and filters at the hours stated in the manual; clean the air filter (very important in dusty dry season work).

**After use:** wash off mud and plant material; store under a shed; disconnect the battery for long storage.

**Always** follow the **operator's manual**, and let only trained people operate machines.
:::

:::

## Key points

- **Mechanization** = using powered machines for farm operations.
- Tractor parts: **engine, PTO, hydraulics, three-point linkage, drawbar, tyres**; the **power tiller** suits swamps.
- Order: **clear → plough → harrow → ridge → plant → spray → harvest**; know the implement for each.
- Processing machines: **threshers, mills, shellers, graters, presses**.
- Advantages: more output, timeliness, less drudgery. Limitations: cost, small farms, terrain, skills, spare parts.
- Maintenance: **daily checks**, greasing, servicing, cleaning and proper storage.
