Is a trader really making money, or just moving stock around? A handful of calculations answer that question: working capital, capital employed, turnover, rate of turnover, gross and net profit, and mark-up and margin. WASSCE Commerce sets them almost every year, and they are easy marks once you know the steps.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- calculate capital owned, working capital and capital employed from a list of assets and liabilities
- calculate the cost of goods sold, gross profit and net profit
- calculate average stock and the rate of turnover
- calculate and convert mark-up and margin

## Assets and liabilities

| Term | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed assets** | Long-lasting assets used in the business | Premises, equipment, vehicles, furniture |
| **Current assets** | Short-term assets that change often | Stock, debtors, cash at bank and in hand |
| **Current liabilities** | Debts due within a year | Creditors, bank overdraft |
| **Long-term liabilities** | Debts due after more than a year | Long-term bank loans, debentures |

$$\text{working capital} = \text{current assets} - \text{current liabilities}$$

$$\text{capital employed} = \text{fixed assets} + \text{working capital}$$

$$\text{capital owned} = \text{total assets} - \text{total liabilities}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Kamara Stores' assets and liabilities (NLe).**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Premises | 30,000 |
| Equipment | 8,000 |
| Stock | 6,000 |
| Debtors | 3,000 |
| Cash | 2,000 |
| Creditors | 4,000 |
| Bank overdraft | 1,000 |
| Long-term bank loan | 10,000 |

- Fixed assets $= 30000 + 8000 = 38000$
- Current assets $= 6000 + 3000 + 2000 = 11000$
- Current liabilities $= 4000 + 1000 = 5000$
- **Working capital** $= 11000 - 5000 = $ **NLe 6,000**
- **Capital employed** $= 38000 + 6000 = $ **NLe 44,000**
- Total assets $= 38000 + 11000 = 49000$; total liabilities $= 5000 + 10000 = 15000$
- **Capital owned** $= 49000 - 15000 = $ **NLe 34,000**
:::

## Turnover, cost of sales and profit

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Turnover** is the total value of **net sales** in a period (sales minus returns).
- **Cost of goods sold (cost of sales)** = opening stock + purchases − closing stock.
- **Gross profit** = turnover − cost of goods sold.
- **Net profit** = gross profit − expenses (rent, wages, transport, electricity, interest).
:::

### Rate of turnover (rate of stock turn)

The **rate of turnover** shows how many times the average stock is sold and replaced in a period. A higher rate usually means goods sell quickly and profit is earned more often.

$$\text{average stock} = \dfrac{\text{opening stock} + \text{closing stock}}{2}$$

$$\text{rate of turnover} = \dfrac{\text{cost of goods sold}}{\text{average stock at cost}}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A trader's figures for the year (NLe):** opening stock 4,000; purchases 20,000; closing stock 6,000; sales 25,000; expenses 2,500.

1. Cost of goods sold $= 4000 + 20000 - 6000 = 18000$
2. Gross profit $= 25000 - 18000 = 7000$
3. Net profit $= 7000 - 2500 = 4500$
4. Average stock $= \dfrac{4000 + 6000}{2} = 5000$
5. Rate of turnover $= \dfrac{18000}{5000} = 3.6$ times a year
:::

**Ways to increase the rate of turnover:** reduce prices, advertise, stock goods customers want, improve display and service, sell on credit to reliable customers, and avoid holding too much stock.

## Mark-up and margin

![Bar showing cost price NLe 100 plus profit NLe 25 making a selling price of NLe 125; mark-up is 25 per cent of cost and margin is 20 per cent of selling price](https://wasscetutor.com/img/commerce/markup-margin.svg)

*Figure 1. The same profit gives a 25% mark-up but a 20% margin. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Mark-up** is gross profit expressed as a percentage of the **cost price**.
- **Margin** is gross profit expressed as a percentage of the **selling price**.
:::

$$\text{mark-up} = \dfrac{\text{gross profit}}{\text{cost price}} \times 100\%$$

$$\text{margin} = \dfrac{\text{gross profit}}{\text{selling price}} \times 100\%$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Using the trader's figures above:** gross profit = 7,000; cost of goods sold = 18,000; sales = 25,000.

- Mark-up $= \dfrac{7000}{18000} \times 100\% \approx 38.9\%$
- Margin $= \dfrac{7000}{25000} \times 100\% = 28\%$
:::

### Converting between mark-up and margin

If the mark-up is $\dfrac{a}{b}$, the margin is $\dfrac{a}{a + b}$. If the margin is $\dfrac{a}{b}$, the mark-up is $\dfrac{a}{b - a}$.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A trader adds a mark-up of 25% ($\tfrac{1}{4}$). What is the margin?** Margin $= \dfrac{1}{1 + 4} = \dfrac{1}{5} = 20\%$.

**A margin of 20% ($\tfrac{1}{5}$). What is the mark-up?** Mark-up $= \dfrac{1}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{1}{4} = 25\%$.

**Selling price from cost.** Goods cost NLe 800 and the mark-up is 25%: selling price $= 800 + \tfrac{1}{4} \times 800 = $ NLe 1,000.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Mark-up is always **larger** than margin for the same profit, because cost price is smaller than selling price. If your margin comes out bigger than your mark-up, you have divided by the wrong figure.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
A provision shop that buys a carton of milk tins for NLe 240 and sells the tins for a total of NLe 300 makes NLe 60 gross profit: a 25% mark-up and a 20% margin. Rent, electricity and transport must then come out of that NLe 60 before any net profit is left.
:::

## Key points

- **Working capital** = current assets − current liabilities; **capital employed** = fixed assets + working capital; **capital owned** = total assets − total liabilities.
- **Cost of goods sold** = opening stock + purchases − closing stock.
- **Gross profit** = sales − cost of goods sold; **net profit** = gross profit − expenses.
- **Rate of turnover** = cost of goods sold ÷ average stock.
- **Mark-up** is profit on cost; **margin** is profit on selling price.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
WASSCE gives a list of figures and asks for **working capital, capital employed, capital owned, cost of sales, gross and net profit, average stock, rate of turnover, mark-up or margin**. Write the formula first, substitute the figures, and give the answer with NLe or "times". Method marks are awarded even if a later figure is wrong.
:::

## Quick check

1. Current assets are NLe 9,000 and current liabilities NLe 3,500. What is the working capital?
2. Opening stock NLe 2,000, purchases NLe 15,000, closing stock NLe 3,000. Find the cost of goods sold.
3. Using question 2, find the rate of turnover.
4. Goods cost NLe 400 and sell for NLe 500. Find the mark-up and the margin.

**Answers:** 1. $9000 - 3500 = $ NLe 5,500. 2. $2000 + 15000 - 3000 = $ NLe 14,000. 3. Average stock $= \dfrac{2000 + 3000}{2} = 2500$; rate $= \dfrac{14000}{2500} = 5.6$ times. 4. Profit = NLe 100; mark-up $= \dfrac{100}{400} \times 100\% = 25\%$; margin $= \dfrac{100}{500} \times 100\% = 20\%$.
