:::callout{type=definition}
**Public sector accounting** is the recording, classifying, summarising and reporting of the **financial transactions of government** and its ministries, departments and agencies (MDAs), in order to show how **public money** has been raised and spent and to hold those in charge **accountable**.
:::

Unlike a business, the government does not aim to make a profit: its accounts focus on **legal authority** to spend, **budget control** and **stewardship** of public funds. This lesson explains the meaning, objectives and features of public sector accounting and the sources of government revenue in Sierra Leone.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define public sector accounting and state its objectives
- distinguish public sector accounting from private sector accounting
- describe the cash and accrual bases in the public sector and IPSAS
- name the main sources of government revenue
- name the institutions responsible for public finance in Sierra Leone

## Objectives of public sector accounting

1. To show that public money has been **raised and spent according to law** (the budget approved by Parliament).
2. To ensure **accountability** and **transparency** in the use of public funds.
3. To provide information for **budgeting and planning**.
4. To **control** spending and prevent waste, fraud and misuse.
5. To report the government's **financial position** and performance.

## Public sector and private sector accounting

| Public sector | Private sector |
|---|---|
| aims to provide **services**, not profit | aims to make a **profit** |
| funds come mainly from **taxes**, fees, grants and loans | funds come from **owners**, lenders and sales |
| spending must be **authorised by law** (the budget) | spending decided by management |
| emphasis on **budget control** and stewardship | emphasis on **profit** and financial position |
| accountable to **Parliament and citizens** | accountable to **owners** |
| governed by **public finance laws** and IPSAS | governed by company law and IFRS |

## Bases of accounting

- **Cash basis:** records receipts and payments when cash moves. It is simple, and many governments have used it.
- **Accrual basis:** records revenue and expenses when they arise, and shows assets and liabilities. It gives a fuller picture.
- The **International Public Sector Accounting Standards (IPSAS)**, issued by the IPSAS Board, set standards for government accounts on both bases, and many African governments, including Sierra Leone, are moving towards **accrual-based IPSAS**.

## Sources of government revenue

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Tax revenue** | income tax on individuals and companies; **goods and services tax (GST)**; customs and excise duties |
| **Non-tax revenue** | fees and licences; **mining royalties** and licence fees; fines; rents on government property; dividends from state enterprises |
| **Grants** | aid from development partners (donor countries, the European Union, the World Bank) |
| **Borrowing** | domestic loans (treasury bills and bonds) and foreign loans |

In Sierra Leone, most taxes and many fees are collected by the **National Revenue Authority (NRA)**.

## Institutions of public finance in Sierra Leone

| Institution | Role |
|---|---|
| **Parliament** | approves the **budget** (Appropriation Act) and examines public accounts through its **Public Accounts Committee** |
| **Ministry of Finance** | prepares the budget and manages public finances |
| **Accountant-General's Department** | keeps government accounts, makes payments and prepares the government's financial statements |
| **National Revenue Authority (NRA)** | assesses and collects taxes and other revenue |
| **Audit Service Sierra Leone** (headed by the **Auditor-General**) | audits public accounts and reports to Parliament |
| **Bank of Sierra Leone** | the government's banker |

Public finance in Sierra Leone is governed by the **1991 Constitution** and the **Public Financial Management Act 2016**.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Government accounts do not report a "profit". A government's income exceeding its spending is a **surplus**, and spending exceeding income is a **deficit**, which must be financed by borrowing or grants.
:::

## Key points

- **Public sector accounting** records and reports how public money is raised and spent, for **accountability**.
- It differs from private accounting in **aim, sources of funds, legal control and reporting**.
- **IPSAS** set standards; governments are moving from **cash** to **accrual** accounting.
- Revenue: **taxes, non-tax revenue, grants, borrowing**.
- Key institutions: **Parliament, Ministry of Finance, Accountant-General, NRA, Auditor-General**.

## Quick check

1. State two objectives of public sector accounting.
2. Give two differences between public and private sector accounting.
3. What is IPSAS?
4. Name two sources of non-tax revenue.
5. Which body audits government accounts in Sierra Leone?

**Answers:**

1. Any two: show money was raised and spent according to law; accountability and transparency; budgeting and planning; control of spending; report financial position.
2. Any two: service versus profit motive; taxes versus owners' capital; spending authorised by law versus management; accountable to Parliament versus owners; IPSAS versus IFRS.
3. The International Public Sector Accounting Standards, which set standards for government financial reporting.
4. Any two: fees and licences; mining royalties; fines; rents; dividends from state enterprises.
5. The Audit Service Sierra Leone, headed by the Auditor-General.
