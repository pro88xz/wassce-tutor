Everything is made of atoms — but what is inside an atom? A century of clever experiments revealed a tiny, dense nucleus surrounded by electrons in fixed energy levels, which explains why every element glows with its own colours.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the models of the atom from Thomson to Bohr
- describe Rutherford's alpha-scattering experiment and its conclusions
- state the structure of the atom: protons, neutrons, electrons, proton number and nucleon number
- define isotopes

## Models of the atom

| Model | Main idea | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Dalton** (1808) | atoms are tiny indivisible spheres | atoms are divisible (electrons were discovered) |
| **Thomson's "plum pudding"** (1904) | a sphere of positive charge with electrons embedded in it | could not explain alpha-particle scattering |
| **Rutherford's nuclear model** (1911) | a tiny, dense, positive **nucleus** with electrons orbiting it | could not explain why orbiting electrons do not spiral into the nucleus, or line spectra |
| **Bohr's model** (1913) | electrons move only in certain **allowed orbits (energy levels)**; they emit or absorb light only when they jump between levels | works well for hydrogen only |
| **Electron cloud (quantum) model** | electrons are described by probability "clouds" (orbitals) | the modern model |

## Rutherford's alpha-scattering experiment

Geiger and Marsden, working with Rutherford, fired a beam of **alpha particles** at a very thin **gold foil** in a vacuum and counted the scattered particles with a zinc sulphide screen.

| Observation | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| **most** alpha particles passed straight through | most of the atom is **empty space** |
| a **few** were deflected through small angles | there is a **positive** charge concentrated in the atom that repels the positive alpha particles |
| a **very few** (about 1 in 8000) bounced back | the positive charge and almost all the mass are concentrated in a **tiny, dense nucleus** |

The nucleus is about $10^{-15}$ m across — about 100 000 times smaller than the atom (about $10^{-10}$ m).

:::callout{type=local-example}
If an atom were the size of the National Stadium in Freetown, its nucleus would be about the size of a pea on the centre spot — yet it would contain almost all the atom's mass.
:::

## The structure of the atom

| Particle | Location | Relative charge | Relative mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Proton** | nucleus | +1 | 1 |
| **Neutron** | nucleus | 0 | 1 |
| **Electron** | orbits (energy levels) | −1 | about $\dfrac{1}{1840}$ |

Protons and neutrons are together called **nucleons**.

:::callout{type=definition}
- The **proton number (atomic number) $Z$** is the number of **protons** in the nucleus. It identifies the element.
- The **nucleon number (mass number) $A$** is the total number of **protons + neutrons**.
- Number of neutrons $N = A - Z$.

A nuclide is written $^{A}_{Z}\text{X}$, for example $^{23}_{11}\text{Na}$: 11 protons, 12 neutrons.
:::

In a neutral atom, the number of electrons equals the number of protons.

## Isotopes

:::callout{type=definition}
**Isotopes** are atoms of the **same element** (same proton number) with **different numbers of neutrons** (different nucleon numbers).
:::

| Isotope | Protons | Neutrons |
|---|---|---|
| $^{1}_{1}\text{H}$ (hydrogen) | 1 | 0 |
| $^{2}_{1}\text{H}$ (deuterium) | 1 | 1 |
| $^{3}_{1}\text{H}$ (tritium) | 1 | 2 |
| $^{12}_{6}\text{C}$ | 6 | 6 |
| $^{14}_{6}\text{C}$ (radioactive) | 6 | 8 |

Isotopes of an element have the **same chemical properties** (same electrons) but different masses, and some are **radioactive**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**How many protons, neutrons and electrons are in a neutral atom of $^{238}_{92}\text{U}$?**

Protons $= 92$; electrons $= 92$; neutrons $= 238 - 92 = 146$.
:::

## Key points

- Models: Dalton → Thomson → Rutherford (nucleus) → Bohr (energy levels) → quantum.
- Alpha scattering: atom mostly empty; tiny dense positive nucleus.
- $Z$ = protons; $A$ = protons + neutrons; neutrons = $A - Z$.
- Isotopes: same $Z$, different $A$; same chemistry.

## Quick check

1. What did the few alpha particles that bounced back from gold foil show?
2. How many neutrons are in $^{27}_{13}\text{Al}$?
3. What are isotopes?

**Answers:** 1. That the atom has a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus. 2. $27 - 13 = 14$. 3. Atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.
