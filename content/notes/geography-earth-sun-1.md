The **rotation of the Earth** is its spin on its own axis, from west to east, once every 24 hours; it causes day and night. The **revolution of the Earth** is its journey around the Sun, once every 365¼ days; with the tilt of the Earth's axis, it causes the seasons and changes in the length of day and night. This lesson explains both movements, the shape and size of the Earth, and the solar system it belongs to.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name the planets of the solar system in order and describe the Earth's place in it
- describe the shape and size of the Earth and give evidence that it is spherical
- explain the rotation of the Earth and its effects
- explain the revolution of the Earth and its effects, including the seasons
- explain equinoxes, solstices and the apparent movement of the overhead Sun

## The solar system

:::callout{type=definition}
The **solar system** is the Sun and all the bodies that travel around it: eight planets, their moons, dwarf planets such as Pluto, asteroids and comets. The Sun is a star that provides the light and heat on which life on Earth depends.
:::

![The eight planets in order from the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune, with the inner rocky planets and outer giant planets marked](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/solar-system.svg)

*Figure 1. The planets in order from the Sun (sizes approximate, distances not to scale). WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Group | Planets | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Inner (terrestrial) planets** | Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars | small, rocky, few or no moons |
| **Outer (giant) planets** | Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune | very large, made mostly of gas or ice, many moons and rings |

The Earth is the **third planet** from the Sun, about **150 million km** away. It is the only planet known to support life, because it has liquid water, a breathable atmosphere and a suitable range of temperatures. Its natural satellite is the **Moon**.

## Shape and size of the Earth

The Earth is a **sphere slightly flattened at the poles**, called an **oblate spheroid**. Its diameter is about **12,756 km** at the Equator and **12,714 km** from pole to pole, and its circumference at the Equator is about **40,075 km**.

**Evidence that the Earth is spherical:**

1. **Photographs and satellite images** taken from space.
2. A **ship sailing away** disappears hull first and mast last.
3. During a **lunar eclipse**, the Earth's shadow on the Moon is always curved.
4. The **horizon** looks circular and widens as you climb higher.
5. People have **sailed and flown round the world** (circumnavigation).
6. **Sunrise and sunset** happen at different times in different places.

## Rotation of the Earth

![The Earth's orbit around the Sun with its axis tilted at 23½°, showing the positions on 21 March, 21 June, 23 September and 22 December](https://wasscetutor.com/img/geography/earth-revolution.svg)

*Figure 2. The Earth's revolution and the seasons. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

:::callout{type=definition}
**Rotation** is the spinning of the Earth on its **axis**, an imaginary line through the North and South Poles. The Earth rotates from **west to east** (anticlockwise when seen from above the North Pole) once in about **24 hours**.
:::

**Effects of rotation:**

1. **Day and night:** the half of the Earth facing the Sun has day; the other half has night.
2. **The Sun appears to rise in the east and set in the west**, because the Earth turns towards the east.
3. **Time differences** between places: places to the east see the Sun first, so their time is ahead (the next lesson shows how to calculate this).
4. **Deflection of winds and ocean currents** (the Coriolis effect): to the right in the northern hemisphere and to the left in the southern hemisphere.
5. **Two high tides a day** at most places, as each place passes through the bulges of water raised by the Moon's pull.
6. **Daily rhythm of temperature**: heating by day and cooling by night.

## Revolution of the Earth

:::callout{type=definition}
**Revolution** is the movement of the Earth around the Sun in an elliptical (oval) path called an **orbit**. One revolution takes about **365¼ days**. Because a calendar year has 365 days, an extra day is added every fourth year, a **leap year** (29 February).
:::

The Earth's axis is **tilted at 23½°** from the vertical and always points in the same direction in space. This tilt, together with revolution, causes:

1. **The seasons** (summer, autumn, winter and spring in temperate lands).
2. **Changes in the length of day and night** through the year.
3. **The apparent movement of the overhead Sun** between the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn.
4. **Changes in the altitude (height) of the midday Sun** through the year.

### Equinoxes and solstices

| Date (approximate) | Name | Overhead (midday) Sun | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **21 March** | Spring (vernal) equinox | Equator | day and night equal everywhere |
| **21 June** | Summer solstice (north) | Tropic of Cancer (23½° N) | longest day in the northern hemisphere |
| **23 September** | Autumn equinox | Equator | day and night equal everywhere |
| **22 December** | Winter solstice (north) | Tropic of Capricorn (23½° S) | longest day in the southern hemisphere |

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone lies between about **7° N and 10° N**, close to the Equator. The midday Sun is high in the sky all year and passes directly overhead twice a year, in **April** and again in **August**. Day length in Freetown changes very little, staying close to 12 hours, which is why Sierra Leone has no summer and winter. Its seasons are instead the **rainy season** (about May to October) and the **dry season** (about November to April), caused by the movement of rain-bearing winds that follow the overhead Sun.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The seasons are **not** caused by the Earth being nearer to or farther from the Sun. The Earth is actually closest to the Sun (**perihelion**) in early January, during the northern winter. The seasons are caused by the **tilt of the axis** as the Earth revolves.
:::

## Rotation and revolution compared

| | Rotation | Revolution |
|---|---|---|
| **Movement** | spinning on its axis | travelling around the Sun |
| **Direction** | west to east | anticlockwise (seen from above the North Pole) |
| **Time taken** | about 24 hours | about 365¼ days |
| **Main effects** | day and night, time differences, deflection of winds | seasons, varying day length, movement of the overhead Sun, leap years |

## Key points

- The solar system has **eight planets**; Earth is the **third** from the Sun.
- The Earth is an **oblate spheroid**: about 40,075 km round the Equator.
- **Rotation:** west to east in **24 hours** → day and night, time differences, deflection of winds.
- **Revolution:** around the Sun in **365¼ days**, with the axis tilted **23½°** → seasons, varying day length, overhead Sun moving between the tropics.
- **Equinoxes** (21 March, 23 September): Sun overhead at the Equator. **Solstices** (21 June, 22 December): Sun overhead at a tropic.

## Quick check

1. Which planet is nearest to the Sun, and which is the largest?
2. Give two pieces of evidence that the Earth is spherical.
3. State two effects of the rotation of the Earth.
4. On what dates are day and night equal all over the world?
5. Why is there no winter in Sierra Leone?

**Answers:**

1. Mercury is nearest; Jupiter is the largest.
2. Any two: photographs from space; a ship's hull disappears before its mast; the curved shadow on the Moon in an eclipse; the circular, widening horizon; circumnavigation; different sunrise times.
3. Any two: day and night; the Sun's apparent rising in the east; time differences; deflection of winds and currents; tides; daily changes in temperature.
4. At the equinoxes, about 21 March and 23 September.
5. It lies close to the Equator, so the midday Sun is always high and day length stays close to 12 hours; temperatures stay high all year.
