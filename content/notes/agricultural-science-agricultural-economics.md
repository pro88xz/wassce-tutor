A farm is a business. Every season the farmer decides **what** to grow or rear, **how much**, and **how**, using limited land, labour, money and time. **Agricultural economics** applies the principles of economics to these decisions: it explains why adding more and more labour to the same plot eventually gives less extra output, how costs and revenue determine profit, and why prices of farm produce rise and fall with the seasons. This lesson covers the basic concepts, the factors of production, the law of diminishing returns, costs, revenue and profit, and demand and supply of farm produce.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define agricultural economics and explain scarcity, choice, scale of preference and opportunity cost
- describe the factors of production and their rewards
- explain the law of diminishing returns using total, average and marginal product
- distinguish fixed and variable costs and calculate total cost, revenue and profit
- explain demand, supply and price determination for farm produce
- explain why prices of farm produce fluctuate

## Basic concepts

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Agricultural economics** is the application of economic principles to the production, distribution and consumption of agricultural goods and services.
- **Scarcity**: resources (land, labour, capital) are **limited** compared with wants.
- **Choice**: because resources are scarce, the farmer must **choose** between alternatives.
- **Scale of preference**: a list of wants arranged in order of importance.
- **Opportunity cost**: the **next best alternative given up** when a choice is made.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A farmer has one hectare of swamp. She can plant rice, expected to earn NLe 6000, or vegetables, expected to earn NLe 9000. She chooses vegetables. What is the opportunity cost?**

The opportunity cost of growing vegetables is the **rice she gave up**, worth NLe 6000 of income (the next best alternative).
:::

## Factors of production

![The four factors of production and their rewards: land earns rent, labour earns wages, capital earns interest, and management earns profit](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/factors-of-production.svg)

| Factor | Meaning | Features in farming |
|---|---|---|
| **land** | all natural resources: soil, water, forests, minerals | fixed in supply; cannot be moved; varies in fertility and location |
| **labour** | human effort, mental and physical | family labour, hired labour, communal work groups; very seasonal (peaks at planting, weeding and harvest) |
| **capital** | man-made goods used to produce other goods | **fixed capital** (land improvements, buildings, machines, tree crops) and **working capital** (seed, fertiliser, feed, fuel, cash) |
| **management** (entrepreneurship) | organising the other factors and **bearing the risk** | decisions on what, how much and how to produce; the farmer often provides it |

## The law of diminishing returns

![Total product rises quickly, then more slowly, reaches a maximum and falls as more labour is added to fixed land; marginal product rises, then falls to zero where total product is greatest, and becomes negative; stages I, II and III are marked](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/production-function.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
**The law of diminishing returns:** when more and more units of a **variable** input (e.g. labour or fertiliser) are added to a **fixed** input (e.g. a hectare of land), the **extra output** from each additional unit eventually **decreases**.

- **Total product (TP)**: the total output.
- **Average product (AP)** $= \dfrac{\text{TP}}{\text{units of variable input}}$.
- **Marginal product (MP)**: the **extra** output from one more unit of input.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A farmer adds workers to 1 ha of maize. Complete the table.**

| Workers | TP (bags) | AP | MP |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| 2 | 22 | 11 | 14 |
| 3 | 36 | 12 | 14 |
| 4 | 46 | 11.5 | 10 |
| 5 | 52 | 10.4 | 6 |
| 6 | 55 | 9.2 | 3 |
| 7 | 55 | 7.9 | 0 |
| 8 | 52 | 6.5 | −3 |

**MP** starts to fall after the 3rd worker: **diminishing returns** set in. **TP** is greatest (55) when MP $= 0$ (7th worker). Adding the 8th worker makes **TP fall** (MP is negative): the workers get in each other's way.
:::

**Stages of production:** **Stage I** (AP rising, up to maximum AP): the farmer should add more input. **Stage II** (from maximum AP to MP $= 0$): the **rational** stage where a sensible farmer operates. **Stage III** (MP negative): never produce here.

## Costs, revenue and profit

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Fixed costs** do not change with the level of output: rent, depreciation of tools and buildings, interest on loans.
- **Variable costs** change with output: seed, fertiliser, feed, hired labour, fuel, pesticides.
- **Total cost (TC)** $=$ fixed costs $+$ variable costs.
- **Total revenue (TR)** $=$ price $\times$ quantity sold.
- **Profit** $= \text{TR} - \text{TC}$.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A farmer grows 1 ha of maize. Fixed costs: land rent NLe 500, depreciation of tools NLe 300. Variable costs: seed NLe 600, fertiliser NLe 1500, hired labour NLe 1800, pesticide NLe 300. She harvests 40 bags and sells at NLe 200 per bag. Find (a) total cost, (b) total revenue, (c) profit, (d) the break-even price per bag.**

**(a)** Fixed $= 800$; variable $= 4200$; **TC $=$ NLe 5000**.

**(b)** **TR** $= 40 \times 200 = $ **NLe 8000**.

**(c)** **Profit** $= 8000 - 5000 = $ **NLe 3000**.

**(d)** Break-even price $= \dfrac{5000}{40} = $ **NLe 125 per bag** (below this, she makes a loss).
:::

## Demand, supply and price

![Demand slopes down and supply slopes up; they cross at the equilibrium price P and quantity Q; at harvest supply shifts right to S1 and the price falls to P1](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/supply-demand.svg)

- **Demand**: the quantity buyers are willing and able to buy at a given price. **Law of demand:** as price rises, quantity demanded falls. Affected by price, income, tastes, population, prices of substitutes (e.g. imported rice).
- **Supply**: the quantity sellers offer at a given price. **Law of supply:** as price rises, quantity supplied rises. Affected by price, weather, inputs, technology, number of farmers, season.
- **Equilibrium price**: where demand equals supply.

### Why prices of farm produce fluctuate

- **Seasonality**: plenty at harvest (prices fall), scarcity in the lean season (prices rise).
- **Weather**: drought or floods cut supply.
- **Perishability**: farmers must sell quickly, often at low prices.
- **Poor storage and transport**.
- **Imports** and world market prices.
- Many small farmers with **little market information**.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Confusing "a change in demand" with "a change in quantity demanded".** A change in the **price** of rice moves buyers **along** the demand curve (quantity demanded changes). A change in **income, tastes or population** **shifts** the whole demand curve.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Rice prices through the year.** In many rice-growing areas, the price of local rice is lowest just after harvest (October to December), when everyone is selling, and highest in the "hungry season" (July and August). Farmers who can **dry and store** rice safely and sell later earn more; those who must sell at harvest to pay debts or school fees get the lowest price. Storage, credit and cooperatives all help farmers capture the better price.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. Define opportunity cost and give a farming example."}
The next best alternative given up when a choice is made. E.g. a farmer who uses NLe 2000 to buy fertiliser instead of a goat: the opportunity cost is the goat.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. Name the four factors of production and the reward for each."}
Land (rent), labour (wages), capital (interest), management/entrepreneurship (profit).
:::

:::reveal{title="3. State the law of diminishing returns."}
When more units of a variable input are added to a fixed input, the extra (marginal) output from each additional unit eventually decreases.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Distinguish fixed costs from variable costs, with two examples of each."}
**Fixed costs** do not change with output (rent, depreciation, loan interest). **Variable costs** change with output (seed, fertiliser, feed, hired labour, fuel).
:::

:::reveal{title="5. A farmer's total cost is NLe 3600 and she harvests 30 bags. What is the break-even price per bag? What is her profit if she sells at NLe 150?"}
Break-even $= 3600 \div 30 = $ NLe 120. Profit $= 30 \times 150 - 3600 = 4500 - 3600 = $ NLe 900.
:::

## Key points

- **Scarcity → choice**; **opportunity cost** is the next best alternative forgone.
- Factors: **land (rent), labour (wages), capital (interest), management (profit)**.
- **Diminishing returns**: MP eventually falls; work in **Stage II**.
- **TC = fixed + variable**; **TR = price × quantity**; **profit = TR − TC**; break-even price $= \text{TC} \div$ quantity.
- Price is set by **demand and supply**; farm prices fluctuate with **season, weather, perishability, storage and imports**.
