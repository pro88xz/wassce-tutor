Forests once covered most of Sierra Leone. Today the large forests that remain, such as Gola in the east, are precious, while much of the country is farm bush, secondary forest and savanna. Forests give timber, firewood and charcoal, bushmeat, medicines, clean water and protection for the soil, and their loss contributes to erosion, flooding and changing rainfall. **Forestry** is the science of managing forests for these benefits; **agroforestry** combines trees with crops and animals on the same farm. This lesson describes the importance of forests, the causes and effects of deforestation, how forests are regenerated and managed, and the main agroforestry systems.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define forestry and describe the types of forest in Sierra Leone
- state the importance and products of forests
- explain the causes and effects of deforestation
- describe natural and artificial regeneration and silvicultural practices
- define agroforestry and describe its systems, benefits and problems
- state the role of forest reserves and forestry laws

## Forests and forestry

:::callout{type=definition}
- A **forest** is a large area of land covered mainly by trees.
- **Forestry** is the science and practice of establishing, managing, using and conserving forests and their resources.
:::

**Forest types in Sierra Leone:** tropical rainforest (east and south-east, e.g. Gola); secondary forest and farm bush (regrowth after farming); savanna woodland (north); mangrove forest (coast and estuaries); and plantations of planted trees.

## Importance of forests

| Benefit | Examples |
|---|---|
| **timber and poles** | building, furniture, boats, bridges |
| **fuel** | firewood and **charcoal**, the main cooking fuels for most households |
| **food** | bushmeat, fruits, nuts, honey, mushrooms, snails |
| **medicines** | bark, roots and leaves used in traditional and modern medicine |
| **other products** | rattan and cane, bamboo, raffia, gums, kola |
| **soil protection** | roots hold soil; leaf litter adds humus; less erosion |
| **water** | forests protect **catchments**, keep streams flowing and dams clean |
| **climate** | store carbon; influence rainfall; shade and cool |
| **wildlife** | habitat for chimpanzees, elephants, pygmy hippos, birds |
| **income and jobs** | logging, sawmilling, carpentry, charcoal, tourism |

## Deforestation

**Causes:** shifting cultivation with short fallows; logging (legal and illegal); **charcoal and firewood** demand; mining; road building and settlements; bush fires.

**Effects:** soil erosion and loss of fertility; flooding and landslides; drying of streams; loss of wildlife and medicinal plants; changes in rainfall; more carbon dioxide in the air; shortage of fuel and timber.

## Regenerating forests

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Natural regeneration**: letting the forest regrow from seeds, stumps and roots already present, by protecting the area from fire and cutting.
- **Artificial regeneration**: **planting** trees, by **afforestation** (planting where there was no forest) or **reforestation** (replanting after cutting).
:::

### Silvicultural practices

Silviculture is the care of trees from seed to harvest:

1. **Nursery**: raise seedlings in polybags or beds; shade and water them.
2. **Site preparation and planting**: at the start of the rains, at the correct spacing.
3. **Weeding** around young trees and **protection** from fire (firebreaks) and animals.
4. **Thinning**: removing weaker trees so the best have room to grow.
5. **Pruning**: removing lower branches for straight, knot-free timber.
6. **Harvesting** at the right age, then **replanting**.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Thinking a forest is "free" once planted.** Young trees need weeding, **firebreaks** and protection for several years; one dry-season bush fire can destroy a plantation. Thinning and pruning are also needed to produce good timber.
:::

## Agroforestry

:::callout{type=definition}
**Agroforestry** is the deliberate growing of **trees together with crops and/or animals** on the same piece of land, so that they benefit each other.
:::

### Agroforestry systems

| System | Description |
|---|---|
| **alley cropping** | food crops grown in alleys between rows of leguminous trees (e.g. *Gliricidia*, *Leucaena*) that are pruned for mulch and green manure |
| **taungya** | farmers grow food crops between young timber trees in a new plantation, weeding the trees as they weed their crops, until the tree canopy closes |
| **home gardens (compound farms)** | fruit trees, plantain, vegetables and small animals mixed around the home |
| **windbreaks and shelterbelts** | rows of trees protecting crops and soil from wind |
| **live fences and boundaries** | trees planted as fences that also give fodder, stakes and firewood |
| **improved fallows** | planting fast-growing legumes on fallow land to restore fertility quickly |
| **silvopasture** | trees with grazing animals; trees give shade and fodder |
| **multistorey cropping** | e.g. cocoa or coffee under shade trees, with plantain |

![Alley cropping: rows of leguminous trees such as Gliricidia run 4–6 m apart, with maize and cassava growing in the alleys; prunings are spread in the alleys as mulch and green manure](https://wasscetutor.com/img/agriculture/alley-cropping.svg)

### Benefits

- **Soil fertility**: leguminous trees fix nitrogen; leaf litter and prunings add organic matter.
- **Erosion control**: roots and canopy protect the soil.
- **Diversity of products**: fuelwood, fodder, fruits, timber, stakes, as well as crops.
- **Shorter fallows** without losing fertility.
- Shade, windbreaks, and a better microclimate.
- Income spread through the year; less risk.

### Problems

- Trees **compete** with crops for light, water and nutrients if not pruned.
- Trees take **years** to give returns.
- Trees may **harbour pests** (birds, rodents).
- Needs labour for pruning and management; **land tenure** problems where farmers do not own the land and cannot be sure of benefiting from trees they plant.

## Forest reserves and laws

The government, through the **Forestry Division** and national park authorities, manages **forest reserves**, **national parks** (such as Gola Rainforest and the Western Area Peninsula) and community forests. Laws require **licences** for logging and charcoal production, ban cutting in protected areas and along river banks, and set rules for exporting timber. Communities often protect **sacred forests**, which are important refuges for plants and animals.

:::callout{type=local-example}
**Charcoal and the forests near towns.** Most households in Freetown and other towns cook with charcoal, and the bags stacked along the main roads come from trees cut in the surrounding areas. Planting **fast-growing fuelwood trees** in woodlots, using **efficient charcoal kilns and cookstoves**, and agroforestry on farms all help meet this demand while protecting the remaining forests.
:::

## Key points

- **Forests** give timber, fuel, food, medicine, water protection, soil protection, climate benefits, wildlife and jobs.
- **Deforestation** comes from farming, logging, charcoal, mining and fire, causing erosion, floods and loss of wildlife.
- Regenerate by **natural regeneration**, **afforestation** and **reforestation**; manage by nursery, weeding, firebreaks, **thinning** and **pruning**.
- **Agroforestry** systems: **alley cropping, taungya, home gardens, windbreaks, improved fallows, silvopasture**.
- Forest reserves, national parks, licences and community forests protect what remains.
