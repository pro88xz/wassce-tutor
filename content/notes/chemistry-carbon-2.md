Washing soda, baking soda and limestone are all **carbonates or hydrogencarbonates**. These compounds connect directly to the carbon cycle that moves carbon between the air, living things, rocks and fuels. This lesson covers their properties and tests, and the carbon cycle with its link to climate change.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the solubility of carbonates and hydrogencarbonates
- describe the effect of heat on carbonates and hydrogencarbonates
- describe the test for carbonate ions and distinguish carbonates from hydrogencarbonates
- state the uses of important carbonates and hydrogencarbonates
- explain the formation of limestone caves and hard water
- describe the carbon cycle and the effect of human activity on it

## Carbonates and hydrogencarbonates

- **Carbonates** contain the CO₃²⁻ ion; **hydrogencarbonates** (bicarbonates) contain HCO₃⁻.
- **Solubility:** carbonates are **insoluble**, except those of **sodium, potassium and ammonium**. Hydrogencarbonates of sodium and potassium exist as solids; those of calcium and magnesium exist **only in solution** (they cause temporary hardness).

### Effect of heat

| Compound | Effect of heat | Equation |
|---|---|---|
| **Na₂CO₃, K₂CO₃** | **No decomposition** (only lose water of crystallisation) | — |
| **Most other carbonates** (Ca, Mg, Zn, Pb, Cu) | Decompose to the **oxide + CO₂** | CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ |
| | Colour changes help identify them | ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂ (oxide yellow hot, white cold) |
| | | CuCO₃ → CuO + CO₂ (green → black) |
| **Sodium hydrogencarbonate** | Decomposes to **carbonate, water and CO₂** | 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ |
| **Calcium hydrogencarbonate** (solution) | Decomposes on boiling | Ca(HCO₃)₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ |
| **Ammonium carbonate** | Decomposes completely to gases | (NH₄)₂CO₃ → 2NH₃ + H₂O + CO₂ |

The more reactive the metal, the more **stable** its carbonate.

### Test for carbonate ions

Add **dilute hydrochloric acid**. A carbonate (or hydrogencarbonate) **effervesces**, giving a gas that turns **lime water milky**:

$$\text{CO}_3^{2-}\text{(aq)} + 2\text{H}^+\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O(l)} + \text{CO}_2\text{(g)}$$

### Telling a carbonate from a hydrogencarbonate

| Test | Carbonate (e.g. Na₂CO₃) | Hydrogencarbonate (e.g. NaHCO₃) |
|---|---|---|
| Add **magnesium sulphate** solution, cold | **White precipitate** at once (MgCO₃) | **No precipitate** in the cold; precipitate only on **boiling** |
| **Heat the solid** (sodium salts) | No CO₂ given off | **CO₂ given off** (turns lime water milky) |
| Phenolphthalein in solution | Deep pink (more alkaline) | Pale pink or colourless |

### Uses

| Compound | Uses |
|---|---|
| **Sodium carbonate** (washing soda, soda ash) | Softening water; making **glass**, soap, detergents and paper |
| **Baking soda**, NaHCO₃ | **Baking powder** (gives CO₂ to raise dough); **antacid** for indigestion; fire extinguishers |
| **Calcium carbonate** (limestone, marble, chalk) | **Cement**, quicklime and slaked lime, **glass**, iron extraction (flux), building stone, antacid tablets, toothpaste |
| **Magnesium carbonate** | Antacids; insulation |

## Limestone caves and hard water

Rainwater containing dissolved CO₂ slowly dissolves **limestone**:

$$\text{CaCO}_3\text{(s)} + \text{H}_2\text{O(l)} + \text{CO}_2\text{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Ca(HCO}_3)_2\text{(aq)}$$

- Going forward, this hollows out **caves** and makes water **temporarily hard**.
- Going backward, where water drips and loses CO₂, calcium carbonate is redeposited as **stalactites** (hanging from cave roofs) and **stalagmites** (rising from cave floors), and as **scale** in kettles.

## The carbon cycle

![The carbon cycle: CO₂ in the air is taken in by plants by photosynthesis; animals eat plants; respiration and combustion return CO₂ to the air; dead matter can form fossil fuels over millions of years](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/carbon-cycle.svg)

*Figure 1. How carbon moves between the air, living things and fuels. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Process | Effect on CO₂ in the air |
|---|---|
| **Photosynthesis**: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (sunlight, chlorophyll) | **Removes** CO₂ |
| **Respiration** by plants, animals and decomposers | **Adds** CO₂ |
| **Combustion** of wood, charcoal and fossil fuels | **Adds** CO₂ |
| **Decay** of dead matter by decomposers | **Adds** CO₂ |
| **Dissolving** in the oceans and forming shells and limestone | **Removes** CO₂ (stored for very long periods) |
| **Volcanoes** and heating limestone (lime kilns, cement) | **Adds** CO₂ |

For thousands of years these processes kept the CO₂ level roughly constant (about 0.03%). Since industry began, **burning fossil fuels** and **cutting down forests** have raised it to about **0.04%**. CO₂ is a **greenhouse gas**, so the extra CO₂ is warming the earth (see Chemistry and the Environment).

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's forests, such as the **Gola rainforest**, are a large carbon store: their trees take CO₂ from the air through photosynthesis. Clearing forest for farms, charcoal and firewood releases that carbon. Protecting forests and replanting trees helps keep CO₂ out of the atmosphere.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Sodium carbonate does not decompose** when heated in the laboratory; sodium **hydrogencarbonate** does. This is one way of telling them apart.
:::

## Key points

- Carbonates are insoluble except **Na, K and NH₄**; Ca and Mg hydrogencarbonates exist only in solution.
- Heat: **Na₂CO₃ and K₂CO₃ are stable**; other carbonates → **oxide + CO₂**; **NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂**.
- **Carbonate test:** dilute acid gives effervescence; the gas turns **lime water milky**.
- **MgSO₄** distinguishes carbonate (precipitate in the cold) from hydrogencarbonate (only on boiling).
- Uses: washing soda (glass, water softening), **baking soda** (baking, antacid), **limestone** (cement, lime, glass, iron).
- **Carbon cycle:** photosynthesis removes CO₂; respiration, combustion and decay add it; fossil-fuel burning is raising CO₂ levels.

## Quick check

1. Write the equation for the effect of heat on sodium hydrogencarbonate.
2. How would you show that a white powder is a carbonate?
3. Name one process that removes CO₂ from the air and two that add it.
4. Why does baking soda make bread rise?

**Answers:**

1. 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂.
2. Add dilute HCl: it fizzes, and the gas turns lime water milky.
3. Removes: photosynthesis (or dissolving in oceans). Adds: any two of respiration, combustion, decay, volcanoes.
4. When heated (or with acid), it gives off carbon(IV) oxide, whose bubbles make the dough rise.
