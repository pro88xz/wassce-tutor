The **1991 Constitution** is the constitution under which Sierra Leone is governed today. It was approved just months after the civil war began, was suspended by the military in 1992 and 1997, and came fully into its own only after peace in 2002. This lesson explains how the 1991 Constitution came about, its main features, and how it compares with the country's earlier constitutions.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the background to the 1991 Constitution
- state its main features
- compare the 1961, 1971, 1978 and 1991 constitutions
- explain the challenges the 1991 Constitution has faced

## Background

1. **Pressure for change**: by 1990, economic collapse, corruption and the wave of democratisation across Africa created demands for multi-party politics.
2. **Constitutional Review Commission**: in **1990**, President Momoh appointed a commission under **Dr Peter Tucker** to recommend a new constitution.
3. **Referendum**: the new constitution was approved in a **referendum in 1991**.
4. **In force**: it came into effect on **1 October 1991** as **Act No. 6 of 1991**.

## Features of the 1991 Constitution

1. **Multi-party democracy** restored; parties are registered by an independent commission (now the **Political Parties Regulation Commission**).
2. **Executive President**: head of state, head of government and commander-in-chief.
3. **Presidential election**: at least **55%** of valid votes needed to win in the first round; otherwise a run-off.
4. **Term limit**: **five-year** terms, **no more than two**.
5. **Vice-President** elected on the same ticket.
6. **Ministers** appointed with **Parliament's approval**; an MP who becomes a minister gives up the seat.
7. **Unicameral Parliament** of elected members and **Paramount Chief members**.
8. **Fundamental human rights** in **Chapter III**, enforceable in the courts.
9. **Fundamental Principles of State Policy** in **Chapter II**.
10. **Independent judiciary**: Supreme Court, Court of Appeal and High Court; judges appointed on the advice of the **Judicial and Legal Service Commission** with Parliament's approval.
11. **Independent institutions**: the **Electoral Commission**, the **Ombudsman**, the **Public Service Commission** and the **Audit Service**.
12. **Rigid amendment procedure** (section 108), with **entrenched provisions** requiring a referendum.
13. **Chieftaincy guaranteed.**

## Challenges since 1991

- **1992**: the **NPRC** military coup suspended the constitution; it was restored after the **1996** elections.
- **1997–1998**: the **AFRC** junta suspended it again until President Kabbah was restored.
- **Civil war (1991–2002)** prevented normal government.
- Since **2002**, regular elections have been held under it, with peaceful changes of ruling party in **2007** and **2018**.
- A **Constitutional Review Commission** (2013–2016) recommended reforms, and debate about amending the constitution continues.

:::callout{type=local-example}
The 1991 Constitution's two-term limit has been tested. **Ahmad Tejan Kabbah** (1996–2007) and **Ernest Bai Koroma** (2007–2018) each served two terms and then stood down, as the constitution requires. Their departures helped build confidence that the rules would be respected.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The 1991 Constitution has **not** been in continuous force since 1991. It was **suspended** by the **NPRC (1992–1996)** and the **AFRC (1997–1998)**. Its uninterrupted operation dates from the restoration of civilian government in **1998** and, fully, from the end of the war in **2002**.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Constitutional Review Commission**: the commission under Dr Peter Tucker, appointed by President Momoh in 1990, to recommend a new constitution.
- **Constitution of 1991**: the constitution, approved by a referendum and in force from 1 October 1991, that restored multi-party democracy.
- **Executive President**: a president who is head of state, head of government and commander-in-chief.
- **Run-off**: a second round of a presidential election, held when no candidate wins at least 55% of valid votes in the first.
- **Political Parties Regulation Commission**: the independent commission that registers political parties in Sierra Leone.
:::

## Key points

- The **Tucker Commission (1990)** and a **1991 referendum** produced the **1991 Constitution**, in force from **1 October 1991**.
- Features: **multi-party democracy, executive President, 55% rule, two five-year terms, Chapter III rights, independent judiciary and commissions, rigid amendment**.
- Compared: **1961 parliamentary monarchy → 1971 republic → 1978 one-party → 1991 multi-party presidential**.
- Suspended in **1992** and **1997**; operating fully since **2002**, with peaceful transfers in **2007** and **2018**.
