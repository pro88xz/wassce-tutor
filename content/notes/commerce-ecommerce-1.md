A tailor in Bo posts photos of new designs on WhatsApp and Facebook, takes orders in the comments, receives payment by mobile money and sends the clothes with a delivery rider. She has no website and no shop on a main road, yet she is doing **e-commerce**. Electronic commerce has changed how goods are bought and sold everywhere, including Sierra Leone.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define e-commerce and its main types
- explain how an online purchase works
- describe the requirements and payment methods for e-commerce
- state the advantages and disadvantages of e-commerce to businesses and consumers
- compare the costs of selling online with selling from a shop

## Meaning of e-commerce

:::callout{type=definition}
**Electronic commerce (e-commerce)** is the **buying and selling of goods and services**, and the transfer of money, **through electronic networks**, mainly the **internet** and **mobile phones**.
:::

E-commerce includes **online shops**, **social media selling**, **mobile money payments**, **online banking**, and electronic ordering between businesses.

## Types of e-commerce

| Type | Who trades with whom | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **B2C** (business to consumer) | A business sells to individual buyers | An online supermarket or phone shop selling to the public |
| **B2B** (business to business) | Businesses trade with each other | A wholesaler taking orders from retailers online |
| **C2C** (consumer to consumer) | Individuals sell to each other through a platform | Selling a used phone on a Facebook group |
| **C2B** (consumer to business) | Individuals sell products or services to firms | A freelance designer selling work to companies online |
| **G2B / G2C** (government to business / citizens) | Government services online | Online tax filing, business registration, exam result checking |
| **B2G** (business to government) | Firms supply government through online tenders | E-procurement |

**Social commerce**, selling through **WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram and TikTok**, is the most common form of e-commerce for small traders in West Africa.

## How an online purchase works

![How an online purchase works: the customer orders on a phone, the online shop receives the order, payment is made by mobile money or card, the seller confirms and packs, and a delivery rider brings the goods](https://wasscetutor.com/img/commerce/ecommerce-flow.svg)

*Figure 1. The steps of an online purchase. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

1. The customer **browses** the products on a website, app or social media page.
2. The customer **orders**: selects the items, quantity and delivery address.
3. The customer **pays** by mobile money, card or bank transfer, or chooses **cash on delivery**.
4. The seller **confirms** the order and **packs** the goods.
5. A **delivery rider or courier** brings the goods; the customer may **track** them.
6. The customer **receives** the goods and leaves **feedback or a review**.

## Requirements for e-commerce

- **Internet access** and **devices**: smartphones, computers.
- A **website, app or social media page** to display goods.
- **Electronic payment systems**: mobile money, cards, online banking.
- **Delivery services**: couriers, delivery riders, postal services.
- **Electricity**.
- **Trust and security**: secure payments, honest sellers, data protection.
- **Laws** on electronic transactions, consumer protection and cybercrime.
- **Digital skills** for sellers and buyers.

## Payment methods

| Method | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Mobile money** | Most widely used in Sierra Leone; works without a bank account |
| **Debit and credit cards** | Common for international purchases; need a bank account |
| **Bank transfer / internet banking** | Used for larger payments |
| **Cash on delivery** | Builds trust where buyers fear fraud; risky for sellers |
| **Payment gateways** | Services that let websites accept mobile money and cards |

## Advantages and disadvantages

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **To businesses** | **Wider market**, local and global; **lower costs** (no rent for a large shop, fewer staff); open **24 hours**; easy **advertising** on social media; customer data helps marketing; faster ordering from suppliers | Needs internet, power and skills; **delivery problems**; **fraud** and fake payments; strong competition; returns and complaints are costly; cyber attacks |
| **To consumers** | **Convenience**: shop from home at any time; **wide choice**; **easy price comparison**; home delivery; saves time and transport costs | Cannot **inspect goods** before buying; risk of **fraud and fake sellers**; delivery delays; **data and privacy** risks; needs data and a phone; returns can be difficult |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Online or shop?** A trader sells 120 dresses a month at NLe 150 each. Each dress costs her NLe 100.

| Monthly item | Shop | Online (social media) |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit $120 \times (150 - 100)$ | NLe 6,000 | NLe 6,000 |
| Shop rent | NLe 2,500 | none |
| Data and advertising | none | NLe 400 |
| Delivery (NLe 10 per order paid by trader) | none | NLe 1,200 |
| **Net profit** | **NLe 3,500** | **NLe 4,400** |

Selling online earns NLe 900 more a month here, and can reach buyers outside her town. But she must build **trust**, deliver reliably and guard against payment fraud. Many traders do both: a small shop plus online selling.
:::

## E-commerce in Sierra Leone

**Growth factors:**

- rapid spread of **mobile phones** and **mobile money**
- cheaper **data**
- popularity of **WhatsApp and Facebook**
- **delivery riders** in Freetown and other towns
- young, tech-aware buyers

**Problems:**

- poor network coverage outside the towns
- unreliable electricity
- **lack of street addresses**, which makes delivery hard
- **online fraud** and fake sellers
- low trust in paying in advance
- low digital skills
- high data costs for some
- weak laws and enforcement against cybercrime

:::callout{type=local-example}
Many Freetown businesses now take orders through **WhatsApp catalogues**, accept **mobile money**, and use **okada and keke delivery riders**. Because there are few formal street addresses, deliveries rely on **landmarks and phone calls**: "near the mosque at Lumley, call me when you reach".
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
E-commerce is not only "buying on websites". Selling through **social media**, paying with **mobile money**, **online banking** and **electronic ordering between businesses** are all e-commerce.
:::

## Key points

- **E-commerce** is buying and selling through **electronic networks** (internet and mobile phones).
- Types: **B2C, B2B, C2C, C2B, G2B/G2C, B2G**; **social commerce** is the most common for small traders.
- It needs **internet, devices, payment systems, delivery, power, trust and laws**.
- It gives **wider markets, lower costs and convenience**, but brings **fraud, delivery and trust problems**.
