Magnesium burns with a dazzling white light and lightens the alloys in aircraft; calcium builds bones, teeth and seashells. They belong to **group 2**, the alkaline earth metals. This lesson covers their properties and trends, the reactions of magnesium and calcium, and how they compare with the alkali metals of group 1.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name the alkaline earth metals and relate their properties to their electron configuration
- compare group 2 metals with group 1 metals
- describe and write equations for the reactions of magnesium and calcium with oxygen, water, steam and acids
- state and explain the trend in reactivity down group 2
- describe the extraction and uses of magnesium and calcium
- state the biological importance of magnesium and calcium

## The alkaline earth metals

Group 2: **beryllium (Be), magnesium (Mg), calcium (Ca)**, strontium (Sr), barium (Ba) and radium (Ra).

| Element | Configuration | Ion |
|---|---|---|
| Beryllium | 2, 2 | Be²⁺ |
| Magnesium | 2, 8, 2 | Mg²⁺ |
| Calcium | 2, 8, 8, 2 | Ca²⁺ |

Each has **two outer electrons**, which it loses to form a **2+ ion**. Their oxides and hydroxides are basic. The name "alkaline earth" comes from the fact that their oxides are alkaline and were once called "earths".

## Group 2 compared with group 1

| Property | Group 1 (e.g. Na) | Group 2 (e.g. Mg) |
|---|---|---|
| Outer electrons | 1 | 2 |
| Ion | M⁺ | M²⁺ |
| Hardness | Soft (cut with a knife) | **Harder** |
| Melting point | Low | **Higher** (stronger metallic bonding: two electrons per atom) |
| Density | Lower | Higher |
| Reactivity | Very reactive | **Less reactive** (two electrons must be removed) |
| Reaction with cold water | Vigorous | Mg very slow; Ca steady |
| Carbonates on heating | Stable | Decompose to oxide + CO₂ |
| Hydroxides | Very soluble, strong alkalis | Less soluble (Ca(OH)₂ slightly soluble; Mg(OH)₂ insoluble) |

## Reactions of magnesium and calcium

### With oxygen

Magnesium burns with a **dazzling white flame**, leaving white magnesium oxide:

$$2\text{Mg(s)} + \text{O}_2\text{(g)} \rightarrow 2\text{MgO(s)}$$

Calcium burns with a **brick-red** flame: 2Ca + O₂ → 2CaO.

### With water and steam

| Metal | Cold water | Steam |
|---|---|---|
| **Magnesium** | **Very slow** (bubbles over days): Mg + 2H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ + H₂ | **Burns brightly**: Mg + H₂O(g) → MgO + H₂ |
| **Calcium** | **Steady fizzing**; sinks; solution turns cloudy (Ca(OH)₂ is slightly soluble): Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂ | Vigorous |

### With dilute acids

Both react **rapidly**, giving a salt and hydrogen:

$$\text{Mg(s)} + 2\text{HCl(aq)} \rightarrow \text{MgCl}_2\text{(aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{(g)}$$

Calcium with dilute **sulphuric acid** soon stops, because insoluble calcium sulphate coats the metal.

### With carbon(IV) oxide and nitrogen

Burning magnesium continues to burn in **carbon(IV) oxide**, leaving black specks of carbon: 2Mg + CO₂ → 2MgO + C. It also combines with **nitrogen** when burned in air, forming some magnesium nitride: 3Mg + N₂ → Mg₃N₂.

## Trend in reactivity

**Reactivity increases down the group** (Be < Mg < Ca < Sr < Ba). The two outer electrons are **further from the nucleus** and more **shielded** in larger atoms, so they are lost more easily. Barium reacts with cold water about as vigorously as sodium.

## Extraction

Both metals are very reactive, so they are extracted by the **electrolysis of their molten chlorides**:

- **Magnesium:** from **sea water**. Magnesium ions are precipitated as Mg(OH)₂ (using lime), converted to MgCl₂ with HCl, dried and electrolysed: Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Mg (cathode); 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ (anode).
- **Calcium:** electrolysis of molten **calcium chloride**.

## Uses

| Magnesium | Calcium |
|---|---|
| **Light alloys** (with aluminium) for aircraft, car wheels and bicycles | Removing oxygen and sulphur from steel |
| **Flares, fireworks** and tracer bullets (bright white light) | Extracting other metals (e.g. uranium) |
| **Sacrificial anodes** to protect ships and pipelines from rusting | Its compounds: lime, cement, plaster (next lesson) |
| Magnesium hydroxide ("milk of magnesia") as an **antacid** and laxative | |

## In living things

- **Calcium:** bones and teeth (calcium phosphate), shells (calcium carbonate); needed for blood clotting and muscle contraction. Good sources: milk, small fish eaten whole, leafy greens.
- **Magnesium:** at the centre of the **chlorophyll** molecule, so plants cannot photosynthesise without it; also needed by enzymes in the body.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Small dried fish such as **bonga** and the tiny fish eaten whole in Sierra Leonean sauces are an excellent source of **calcium**, because their soft bones are eaten too. Palm-tree and other crops showing yellowing between leaf veins may be short of **magnesium**, because magnesium is part of chlorophyll. Agricultural lime containing magnesium (dolomite) can correct it.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Group 2 metals are **less** reactive than group 1 metals in the same period: removing **two** electrons needs more energy than removing one. Magnesium is much less reactive than sodium.
:::

## Key points

- Group 2 metals have **two outer electrons** and form **M²⁺ ions**.
- They are **harder, denser, higher-melting and less reactive** than group 1 metals.
- **Mg** burns with a dazzling white flame, reacts very slowly with cold water but burns in steam; **Ca** reacts steadily with cold water.
- **Reactivity increases down the group**.
- Extracted by **electrolysis of molten chlorides**; Mg comes from **sea water**.
- Uses: **Mg alloys, flares, sacrificial anodes, antacids**. Ca is vital for **bones and teeth**; Mg is in **chlorophyll**.

## Quick check

1. Why are group 2 metals less reactive than group 1 metals?
2. Write the equation for magnesium reacting with steam.
3. Why does the reaction of calcium with dilute sulphuric acid soon stop?
4. Give two uses of magnesium.

**Answers:**

1. They must lose two electrons, which needs more energy than losing one.
2. Mg + H₂O(g) → MgO + H₂.
3. Insoluble calcium sulphate forms a coating on the calcium.
4. Any two: light alloys, flares and fireworks, sacrificial anodes, antacids (as Mg(OH)₂).
