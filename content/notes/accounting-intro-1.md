**Financial accounting** is the process of **recording, classifying, summarising and reporting** the financial transactions of a business so that owners and other users can judge its **profit or loss** and its **financial position**. It turns everyday transactions, such as buying stock, paying wages and selling goods, into two key reports: the **income statement** (trading, profit and loss account), which shows the profit for a period, and the **statement of financial position** (balance sheet), which shows what the business owns and owes on a given date. This lesson explains the meaning, objectives and users of accounting information.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define accounting, bookkeeping and financial accounting
- state the objectives (purposes) of accounting
- identify the users of accounting information and what each needs to know
- describe the qualities that make accounting information useful
- explain the role of the accountant in a business

## Meaning of accounting

:::callout{type=definition}
**Accounting** is the art of **recording, classifying, summarising, analysing and interpreting** financial transactions and communicating the results to the people who need them. **Financial accounting** is the branch that prepares **financial statements** for people outside the day-to-day running of the business, such as owners, lenders and the tax authority.
:::

The work of accounting follows a logical order:

| Stage | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Recording** | each transaction is written down from a source document | a sales invoice is entered in the sales day book |
| **Classifying** | similar items are grouped in ledger accounts | all wages paid go to the wages account |
| **Summarising** | the accounts are totalled and listed | the trial balance; the final accounts |
| **Analysing and interpreting** | the results are explained | profit has fallen because expenses rose |
| **Communicating** | reports are given to users | the owner and the bank receive the accounts |

## Objectives of accounting

1. **To keep a systematic record** of all business transactions, so nothing is forgotten.
2. **To find the profit or loss** made over a period (the income statement).
3. **To show the financial position** on a given date: assets, liabilities and capital (the statement of financial position).
4. **To control assets** and prevent fraud, theft and waste.
5. **To provide information for decisions** such as pricing, borrowing and expansion.
6. **To meet legal requirements**, including tax returns to the **National Revenue Authority (NRA)** and the duties of registered companies.
7. **To show amounts owed** to the business (trade receivables) and by the business (trade payables).

## Users of accounting information

![Financial statements at the centre with eight users around them: owners, managers, lenders and banks, suppliers, the government and NRA, employees, investors and customers, and what each wants to know](https://wasscetutor.com/img/accounting/users-of-accounts.svg)

*Figure 1. Users of accounting information and what each wants to know. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| User | What they want to know |
|---|---|
| **Owners (proprietors, shareholders)** | Is the business profitable? Is my capital safe and growing? |
| **Managers** | Information to plan, control costs and make decisions |
| **Lenders and banks** | Can the business repay a loan and pay interest? |
| **Suppliers (trade payables)** | Will the business pay for goods bought on credit? |
| **Employees and trade unions** | Job security, wages and bonuses |
| **Customers** | Will the business keep supplying goods and honour guarantees? |
| **Government and the NRA** | Correct tax (income tax, goods and services tax); statistics for planning |
| **Investors and potential buyers** | Is it worth investing in or buying the business? |
| **The public** | The effect of the business on jobs and the community |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A trader in Bo wants a loan of NLe 50,000 from a bank to open a second shop. Why will the bank ask for her accounts?**

The bank wants to see:

- the **profit** she has made in past years, to judge whether the new shop is likely to succeed;
- her **assets and liabilities**, to see what she already owes and what security she could offer;
- her **cash position**, to judge whether she can pay the monthly instalments and interest.

Without reliable accounts, the bank cannot measure the risk, so it is likely to refuse the loan or charge a higher rate.
:::

## Qualities of useful accounting information

Accounting information is only valuable if it is:

- **Relevant:** it helps users make decisions.
- **Reliable (faithful):** free from errors and bias, and backed by evidence.
- **Understandable:** clearly presented to people with reasonable knowledge.
- **Comparable:** prepared the same way each year, so years and businesses can be compared.
- **Timely:** available soon enough to be useful.

These are the **qualitative characteristics**, covered in detail in the next topic.

## The accountant's role

An accountant keeps or supervises the records, prepares the final accounts, advises management on costs, budgets and tax, and helps to prevent fraud. In Sierra Leone, professional accountants are regulated by the **Institute of Chartered Accountants of Sierra Leone (ICASL)**.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not say that the only purpose of accounting is "to calculate profit". Examiners expect several objectives: recording, profit measurement, financial position, control of assets, decision-making and legal or tax requirements.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Users of accounting information**: the people who rely on a business's accounts, such as owners, managers, lenders, suppliers, employees, customers, the government and investors.
- **Classifying**: grouping similar items in ledger accounts.
- **Summarising**: totalling and listing the accounts.
:::

## Key points

- **Accounting** records, classifies, summarises, analyses and communicates financial information.
- **Financial accounting** prepares statements for owners and outside users.
- Main reports: the **income statement** (profit or loss) and the **statement of financial position** (assets, liabilities, capital).
- Users: **owners, managers, lenders, suppliers, employees, customers, government (NRA), investors, the public**.
- Useful information is **relevant, reliable, understandable, comparable and timely**.

## WASSCE examination focus

Expect to **define accounting or financial accounting**, to **state the objectives of accounting**, and to **list users of accounting information and explain what each needs**. Give a reason for each user, not just a list of names.
:::

## Quick check

1. Define financial accounting.
2. State three objectives of accounting.
3. Why would a supplier be interested in a customer's accounts?
4. Name the two main financial statements.
5. Give two qualities of useful accounting information.

**Answers:**

1. The recording, classifying, summarising and reporting of a business's financial transactions in financial statements for owners and other users.
2. Any three: keep a systematic record; find the profit or loss; show the financial position; control assets and prevent fraud; provide information for decisions; meet legal and tax requirements.
3. To judge whether the customer can pay for goods supplied on credit.
4. The income statement (trading, profit and loss account) and the statement of financial position (balance sheet).
5. Any two: relevant; reliable; understandable; comparable; timely.
