The titre is only half the job: WASSCE marks depend on turning it into concentrations, masses, formulas and percentages. Every titration calculation uses the same chain: **known solution → moles → mole ratio → moles of the unknown → its concentration**. This lesson works through the standard types of question step by step.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- calculate the concentration of an unknown solution in mol/dm³ and g/dm³
- use the mole ratio from the balanced equation, including 1 : 2 ratios
- use the formula $\dfrac{C_AV_A}{C_BV_B} = \dfrac{n_A}{n_B}$
- find the molar mass of a substance and water of crystallisation from titration data
- find percentage purity and the basicity of an acid

## The method

1. Write the **balanced equation** and note the **mole ratio**.
2. Find the **moles of the known solution**: $n = \dfrac{C \times V}{1000}$ (V in cm³).
3. Use the mole ratio to find the **moles of the unknown**.
4. Find its **concentration**: $C = \dfrac{n \times 1000}{V}$ (mol/dm³). Multiply by M for g/dm³.

Many candidates use the shortcut

$$\dfrac{C_AV_A}{C_BV_B} = \dfrac{n_A}{n_B}$$

where A is the acid, B the base, and $n_A : n_B$ the mole ratio from the equation.

## Type 1: concentration of an alkali (1 : 1)

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution needed an average of 22.50 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid. Find the concentration of the NaOH in (a) mol/dm³, (b) g/dm³.** (NaOH = 40)

$$\text{NaOH} + \text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \qquad (1 : 1)$$

1. Moles of HCl $= \dfrac{0.100 \times 22.50}{1000} = 0.00225$ mol.
2. Moles of NaOH = 0.00225 mol (1 : 1).
3. (a) $C = \dfrac{0.00225 \times 1000}{25.0} = 0.0900$ mol/dm³.
4. (b) $0.0900 \times 40 = 3.60$ g/dm³.
:::

## Type 2: a 1 : 2 mole ratio

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide was neutralised by 20.0 cm³ of sulphuric acid. Find the concentration of the acid.**

$$\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \qquad (\text{acid : base} = 1 : 2)$$

Using the formula:

$$\dfrac{C_A \times 20.0}{0.200 \times 25.0} = \dfrac{1}{2} \quad\Rightarrow\quad C_A = \dfrac{0.200 \times 25.0}{2 \times 20.0} = 0.125 \text{ mol/dm}^3$$

By moles: NaOH $= 0.200 \times 25.0 \div 1000 = 0.00500$ mol; H₂SO₄ $= 0.00500 \div 2 = 0.00250$ mol; $C = 0.00250 \times 1000 \div 20.0 = 0.125$ mol/dm³.
:::

## Type 3: molar mass and water of crystallisation

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A solution contains 14.30 g/dm³ of washing soda, Na₂CO₃·xH₂O. 25.0 cm³ of it needed 25.00 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ HCl, using methyl orange. Find x.** (Na = 23, C = 12, O = 16, H = 1)

$$\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow 2\text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2$$

1. Moles of HCl $= 0.100 \times 25.00 \div 1000 = 0.00250$ mol.
2. Moles of Na₂CO₃ $= 0.00250 \div 2 = 0.00125$ mol in 25.0 cm³.
3. Concentration $= 0.00125 \times 1000 \div 25.0 = 0.0500$ mol/dm³.
4. Molar mass $= \dfrac{\text{mass concentration}}{\text{molar concentration}} = \dfrac{14.30}{0.0500} = 286$ g/mol.
5. $106 + 18x = 286$, so $18x = 180$ and **x = 10**.
:::

## Type 4: percentage purity

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**2.00 g of impure sodium hydroxide was dissolved and made up to 250 cm³. 25.0 cm³ of this needed 20.00 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ HCl. Find the percentage purity of the NaOH.**

1. Moles of HCl $= 0.200 \times 20.00 \div 1000 = 0.00400$ mol, so NaOH in 25.0 cm³ = 0.00400 mol.
2. NaOH in 250 cm³ $= 0.00400 \times 10 = 0.0400$ mol.
3. Mass of pure NaOH $= 0.0400 \times 40 = 1.60$ g.
4. Percentage purity $= \dfrac{1.60}{2.00} \times 100 = 80.0\%$.
:::

## Type 5: basicity of an acid

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ of an acid H_nX was neutralised by 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ NaOH. Find the basicity of the acid.**

- Moles of acid $= 0.100 \times 25.0 \div 1000 = 0.00250$ mol.
- Moles of NaOH $= 0.200 \times 25.0 \div 1000 = 0.00500$ mol.
- Ratio acid : NaOH $= 0.00250 : 0.00500 = 1 : 2$.

Each mole of acid neutralises 2 moles of OH⁻, so it supplies 2 H⁺: the acid is **dibasic**.
:::

## Reporting answers

- Give concentrations to **three significant figures** (0.0900, not 0.09) unless told otherwise.
- Show **units** at every stage: mol, mol/dm³, g/dm³, g/mol.
- State the **mole ratio** from the equation in words, e.g. "1 mole of H₂SO₄ reacts with 2 moles of NaOH".

:::callout{type=local-example}
Water-treatment laboratories, such as those serving Freetown's supply, use titrations every day. Examples include measuring the alkalinity of raw water before adding alum, and the amount of chlorine left in treated water. The same calculation steps you practise here are used to set chemical doses safely.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
When the mole ratio is not 1 : 1, use it in the **right direction**. In H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH, moles of acid = moles of NaOH **÷ 2**, not × 2. Check by asking: "which needs more moles?" (Here, the base.)
:::

## Key points

- Chain: **moles of known (C × V ÷ 1000) → mole ratio → moles of unknown → concentration**.
- Shortcut: $\dfrac{C_AV_A}{C_BV_B} = \dfrac{n_A}{n_B}$.
- **Molar mass** = mass concentration (g/dm³) ÷ molar concentration (mol/dm³); then find **x** in hydrated salts.
- **Percentage purity** = pure mass ÷ sample mass × 100 (scale up from the aliquot to the whole solution).
- **Basicity** = moles of NaOH ÷ moles of acid.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
The volumetric analysis question in the practical paper always asks you to **calculate concentrations** in mol/dm³ and g/dm³, often a **molar mass, water of crystallisation or percentage purity**, from your own titre. Write the balanced equation, state the mole ratio and show every step with units. Marks are given for method even if your titre is slightly off.
:::

## Quick check

1. 20.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol/dm³ HCl neutralises 25.0 cm³ of KOH. Find the concentration of the KOH.
2. 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ Na₂CO₃ needs 20.0 cm³ of HCl. Find the concentration of the HCl.
3. A solution of an acid has a mass concentration of 6.3 g/dm³ and a molar concentration of 0.050 mol/dm³. Find its molar mass.
4. Express 0.125 mol/dm³ H₂SO₄ in g/dm³ (H₂SO₄ = 98).

**Answers:**

1. Moles HCl = 0.00300; KOH = 0.00300 mol; $C = 0.00300 \times 1000 \div 25.0 = 0.120$ mol/dm³.
2. Na₂CO₃ = 0.00250 mol; HCl = 0.00500 mol; $C = 0.00500 \times 1000 \div 20.0 = 0.250$ mol/dm³.
3. $6.3 \div 0.050 = 126$ g/mol.
4. $0.125 \times 98 = 12.25$ g/dm³.
