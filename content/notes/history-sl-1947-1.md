**Political developments in Sierra Leone from 1947 to 1951** turned on a bitter conflict between the **Colony** and the **Protectorate** over the **Stevenson Constitution**, proposed by **Governor Sir Hubert Stevenson** in **1947** and introduced in **1951**. It gave Sierra Leone its first **African majority** in the Legislative Council, with the **Protectorate**, which held most of the people, in the majority. Colony (mainly Krio) leaders in the **National Council of the Colony of Sierra Leone (NCSL)**, led by **Dr H. C. Bankole-Bright**, fiercely opposed it; Protectorate leaders formed the **Sierra Leone People's Party (SLPP)** in **1951** under **Dr Milton Margai**, which won the **1951 elections**. This lesson explains **the Stevenson Constitution, the Colony–Protectorate conflict and the rise of the SLPP**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the background to political change after the Second World War
- describe the Protectorate Assembly and the Stevenson proposals
- explain why Colony leaders opposed the 1951 constitution
- explain why Protectorate leaders supported it

- explain the importance of the 1951 constitution

## Background after 1945

1. **The Second World War** had weakened Britain and spread ideas of **self-determination** (see the topic on nationalism).
2. Britain's **Labour government** accepted gradual **self-government** for its colonies.
3. In the Protectorate, a new **educated elite**, many trained at **Bo School** and **Fourah Bay College**, wanted a voice in national government.
4. The **1924 Constitution** gave the Protectorate only **three nominated chiefs**, while the much smaller Colony had **elected** members.

## The Protectorate Assembly and the Stevenson proposals

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **District Councils (1946)** | set up in each Protectorate district, made up mainly of paramount chiefs |
| **Protectorate Assembly (1946)** | a body of chiefs and some educated members, chosen through the District Councils, to advise the government on the Protectorate |
| **Stevenson proposals (1947)** | Governor Stevenson proposed a **Legislative Council** with an **unofficial African majority**, in which the **Protectorate** would have **more members than the Colony** |
| **Stevenson Constitution (1951)** | after four years of argument, the constitution came into force; elections were held in **November 1951** |

## The Colony–Protectorate conflict

**Why Colony leaders opposed the constitution:**

1. Colony people were **British subjects** with older political rights; Protectorate people were only **British-protected persons**.
2. They argued that the Protectorate had **fewer educated people** and was not ready to govern.
3. They feared that **chiefs**, controlled by the colonial government, would dominate.
4. They wanted the Colony and Protectorate to **advance separately**, or the Colony to keep its lead.
5. Memories of the **Hut Tax War**, when many Krio were killed, added to their fears.

In **1950** Colony leaders formed the **National Council of the Colony of Sierra Leone (NCSL)** under **Dr H. C. Bankole-Bright** to oppose the proposals.

**Why Protectorate leaders supported it:**

1. The Protectorate had **most of the population** (over nine-tenths) and produced most of the country's **wealth**.
2. A Protectorate majority was **fair and democratic**.
3. It would end the **Colony's domination** of politics and jobs.
4. Sierra Leone had to advance as **one nation** towards self-government.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Bankole-Bright and Milton Margai: two views of the nation**

**Bankole-Bright**, a Krio doctor and veteran politician of the NCBWA, saw the Krio as the natural leaders of Sierra Leone because of their education and long political experience. **Milton Margai**, a Mende doctor and the first Protectorate man to qualify in medicine, argued that the country's future lay with the **majority in the Protectorate**, working with the chiefs and the educated elite. The 1951 elections showed that Margai's view had won, and it shaped the path to independence.
:::

## The formation of the SLPP

The **Sierra Leone People's Party (SLPP)** was formed in **1951** by the merger of:

1. the **Sierra Leone Organisation Society (SOS)**, founded in **1946** by Protectorate educated men, including **Dr John Karefa-Smart** and **Siaka Stevens**;
2. the **People's Party**, a Colony group led by **Rev. Etheldred Jones (Lamina Sankoh)**;
3. members of the **Protectorate Educational Progressive Union (PEPU)**.

Its leader was **Dr Milton Margai**. Its motto "**One Country, One People**" stressed national unity, and it had the support of most **paramount chiefs**.

## The 1951 elections and their results

1. In **November 1951** the **SLPP** won the support of most members in the new Legislative Council.
2. **Milton Margai** became **Leader of Government Business**; in **1953** Africans were made **ministers**, and in **1954** Margai became **Chief Minister**.
3. The **NCSL** became the opposition, but steadily lost influence.

## Importance of the 1951 constitution

1. First **African majority** in the Legislative Council.
2. First time the **Protectorate** had a **majority** of representatives.
3. Brought the **Colony and Protectorate together** in one legislature.
4. Led to the **SLPP government** and the path to **independence**.
5. But it **deepened the Colony–Protectorate divide** for a time.

:::callout{type=local-example}
The **SLPP's palm tree symbol** and its base in the **south and east** date from this period, when the party grew out of Protectorate organisations and the support of paramount chiefs. The rivalry between Colony and Protectorate later gave way to rivalry between the **SLPP** and the **APC**, founded in 1960.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The SLPP was **not** formed only by Protectorate people. The **People's Party** of Lamina Sankoh was a **Colony** group, and the SLPP always presented itself as a **national** party. But its main strength in 1951 came from the **Protectorate** and the chiefs.
:::

## Key points

- **Protectorate Assembly (1946)**; **Stevenson proposals (1947)**; **constitution and elections (1951)**.
- First **African majority**, with a **Protectorate majority**, in the Legislative Council.
- **NCSL (1950)**, led by **Bankole-Bright**, opposed it; feared Protectorate and chiefly domination.
- **SLPP (1951)** formed from the **SOS, People's Party and PEPU**, led by **Milton Margai**; won in **1951**.
- Margai: **Leader of Government Business (1951)**, **Chief Minister (1954)**.
