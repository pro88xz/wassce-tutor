Circle geometry is one of the most reliable sources of marks in WASSCE Mathematics, and one of the most feared. The fear comes from the number of theorems; the reliability comes from the fact that there are only **seven**, and every question is solved by spotting which ones apply. This lesson names the parts of a circle, teaches the seven theorems with a diagram for each, and shows how to find angles step by step with reasons.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name the parts of a circle
- state and use the seven circle theorems
- recognise which theorem applies in a diagram
- find unknown angles, giving a reason for each step

## Parts of a circle

![A circle with centre O showing a radius, a diameter, a chord, an arc, a sector, a segment and a tangent](https://wasscetutor.com/img/mathematics/circle-parts.svg)

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **radius** | a line from the centre to the circle |
| **diameter** | a chord through the centre; twice the radius |
| **chord** | a straight line joining two points on the circle |
| **arc** | part of the circumference |
| **sector** | the region between two radii and an arc (a "slice") |
| **segment** | the region between a chord and an arc |
| **tangent** | a straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point |

## The seven circle theorems

![Six panels: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference; angles in the same segment are equal; the angle in a semicircle is 90°; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°; a tangent is perpendicular to the radius; tangents from an external point are equal](https://wasscetutor.com/img/mathematics/circle-theorems.svg)

:::callout{type=definition}
1. **The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference** standing on the same arc.
2. **Angles in the same segment are equal** (angles standing on the same arc, at the circumference, on the same side).
3. **The angle in a semicircle is a right angle** (an angle subtended by a diameter is $90°$). This is theorem 1 with the centre angle $180°$.
4. **Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180°$.** (A cyclic quadrilateral has all four vertices on the circle.) Also: an exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the interior opposite angle.
5. **A tangent is perpendicular to the radius** at the point of contact.
6. **The two tangents from an external point are equal in length.**
7. **The alternate segment theorem:** the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.
:::

![A tangent at T and a chord TA; the angle z between them equals the angle TBA at a point B on the circle in the other segment](https://wasscetutor.com/img/mathematics/circle-alternate-segment.svg)

## Spotting which theorem to use

| If the diagram has… | look for… |
|---|---|
| the **centre** and two radii to the ends of an arc | theorem 1 (centre = $2 \times$ circumference) |
| two angles at the circumference on the **same arc** | theorem 2 (equal) |
| a **diameter** | theorem 3 ($90°$) |
| **four points** on the circle joined in a quadrilateral | theorem 4 (opposite angles $180°$) |
| a **tangent** and a **radius** | theorem 5 ($90°$) |
| **two tangents** from one point | theorem 6 (equal lengths, so an isosceles triangle) |
| a **tangent** and a **chord** from the point of contact | theorem 7 (alternate segment) |

:::callout{type=tip}
Radii are all equal, so **any triangle with two radii as sides is isosceles**. This hidden fact unlocks many circle questions.
:::

## Worked examples

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**O is the centre of the circle. A, B and C are on the circle, and $\angle AOB = 130°$. Find $\angle ACB$ (C on the major arc).**

$$\angle ACB = \tfrac12 \times 130° = 65°$$

(The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.)
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**PQRS is a cyclic quadrilateral with $\angle P = 3x + 10°$ and $\angle R = 2x - 5°$. Find $x$ and the angles.**

$$3x + 10 + 2x - 5 = 180$$

(Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180°$.)

$$5x = 175 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 35$$

**$\angle P = 115°$, $\angle R = 65°$.**
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**AB is a diameter of a circle and C is a point on the circle with $\angle CAB = 34°$. Find $\angle ABC$.**

$\angle ACB = 90°$ (angle in a semicircle).

$\angle ABC = 180° - 90° - 34° = 56°$ (angle sum of triangle ABC).
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**PA and PB are tangents from P to a circle with centre O. $\angle APB = 50°$. Find $\angle AOB$ and $\angle PAB$.**

$\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90°$ (tangent ⟂ radius).

In quadrilateral PAOB: $\angle AOB = 360° - 90° - 90° - 50° = 130°$ (angle sum of a quadrilateral).

$PA = PB$ (tangents from an external point), so triangle PAB is isosceles:

$$\angle PAB = \frac{180° - 50°}{2} = 65°$$
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**TS is a tangent at T. Chord TA makes an angle of $58°$ with the tangent. B is on the circle in the alternate segment. Find $\angle TBA$.**

$\angle TBA = 58°$ (alternate segment theorem).
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Using a theorem without its condition.** Theorem 1 needs the angles to stand on the **same arc**; theorem 4 needs **all four** vertices on the circle; theorem 5 needs a **radius** to the point of contact. Check the condition before you write the reason.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Round huts and water tanks in rural Sierra Leone often have circular bases. A builder who wants a perfect circle stretches a rope from a central peg (a radius) and walks round. And to check that the corner of a wall is truly square, some builders use theorem 3: any point on a circle with a diameter as its base makes a right angle.
:::

## Practice

:::reveal{title="1. O is the centre and ∠ABC = 47° at the circumference, standing on arc AC. Find ∠AOC and the reflex angle AOC."}
$\angle AOC = 2 \times 47° = 94°$ (centre twice circumference). Reflex $\angle AOC = 360° - 94° = 266°$.
:::

:::reveal{title="2. In a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, ∠B = 78°. Find ∠D."}
$\angle D = 180° - 78° = 102°$ (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral).
:::

:::reveal{title="3. A tangent from P touches a circle of radius 5 cm, centre O, at T. OP = 13 cm. Find PT."}
$\angle OTP = 90°$ (tangent ⟂ radius). By Pythagoras: $PT = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{144} = 12$ cm.
:::

:::reveal{title="4. Angles ACB and ADB stand on the same arc AB, on the same side. ∠ACB = 3y and ∠ADB = y + 40°. Find y."}
$3y = y + 40$ (angles in the same segment) $\Rightarrow y = 20°$.
:::

:::reveal{title="5. O is the centre, OA and OB are radii, and ∠OAB = 28°. Find ∠AOB."}
$OA = OB$ (radii), so $\angle OBA = 28°$. $\angle AOB = 180 - 56 = 124°$.
:::

## Key points

- Know the parts: **radius, diameter, chord, arc, sector, segment, tangent**.
- **Seven theorems**: centre $= 2 \times$ circumference; same segment equal; semicircle $90°$; cyclic quadrilateral opposite angles $180°$; tangent ⟂ radius; tangents from a point equal; alternate segment.
- **Radii are equal**: triangles with two radii are **isosceles**.
- Give the **theorem as the reason** for every step.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Circle theorems appear in both papers: objective questions asking for one angle, and theory questions that need several theorems in sequence, often with Pythagoras (tangent and radius) or algebra. Marks are given for each correct angle **with its reason**.
:::
