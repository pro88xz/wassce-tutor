Chew a piece of plain bread for a few minutes and it starts to taste sweet: an enzyme in saliva is **hydrolysing starch** into sugar. This lesson covers the laboratory tests for carbohydrates, how starch and sucrose are hydrolysed, and what happens when sugars ferment or are dehydrated.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe tests for starch, reducing sugars and carbohydrates in general
- explain why sucrose gives a positive test for reducing sugars only after hydrolysis
- describe the hydrolysis of starch and sucrose by acids and enzymes
- describe the fermentation of sugars and the dehydration of sucrose
- describe an experiment to show the action of saliva on starch

## Tests for carbohydrates

![Starch with iodine turns blue-black; glucose with Benedict's solution gives a brick-red precipitate; sucrose stays blue until it has been hydrolysed by acid](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/sugar-tests.svg)

*Figure 1. Tests for starch and reducing sugars. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Test | Method | Positive result | Shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Iodine test** | Add a few drops of iodine solution | **Blue-black** colour | **Starch** |
| **Benedict's test** | Add Benedict's solution and **heat** in a boiling water bath | Blue → green → yellow → **brick-red precipitate** | **Reducing sugars** (glucose, fructose, maltose, lactose) |
| **Fehling's test** | Add Fehling's solutions A and B and **heat** | **Brick-red precipitate** | Reducing sugars |
| **Tollens' test** | Warm with ammoniacal silver nitrate | **Silver mirror** on the tube | Reducing sugars |
| **Molisch's test** | Add Molisch's reagent, then pour conc. H₂SO₄ carefully down the side | **Violet ring** where the layers meet | **Any carbohydrate** |

**Reducing sugars** reduce the blue **copper(II)** ions in Benedict's or Fehling's solution to brick-red **copper(I) oxide**, Cu₂O. The colour reached (green, yellow, orange, red) gives a rough idea of how much sugar is present.

**Sucrose** gives **no** precipitate with Benedict's solution. If it is first **boiled with dilute hydrochloric acid** and then **neutralised** with sodium hydrogencarbonate, it gives a brick-red precipitate, because the acid has hydrolysed it to glucose and fructose.

## Hydrolysis of carbohydrates

**Sucrose** (dilute acid, or the enzyme **invertase/sucrase**):

$$\text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{22}\text{O}_{11} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$$

The products are **glucose + fructose**.

**Maltose** (dilute acid, or **maltase**) gives two glucose molecules:

$$\text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{22}\text{O}_{11} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$$

**Starch:**

- **Boiling with dilute acid** (HCl or H₂SO₄) hydrolyses starch completely to **glucose**:

$$(\text{C}_6\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_5)_n + n\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow n\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$$

- **Enzymes**: **amylase** (ptyalin in saliva, also from the pancreas) and **diastase** (in malt) hydrolyse starch to **maltose**. Maltase then splits maltose to glucose.

**Cellulose** is hydrolysed to glucose only by **concentrated** acids or the enzyme **cellulase**.

## The action of saliva on starch

1. Put equal volumes of **starch solution** into two test tubes, A and B.
2. Add saliva to A; add the same volume of **boiled** saliva (enzyme destroyed) to B as a **control**. Keep both at about **37 °C**.
3. Every minute, test a drop from each with **iodine** on a white tile.
4. **Results:** A stops giving a blue-black colour after a few minutes, and then gives a **brick-red** precipitate with Benedict's solution. B stays **blue-black** with iodine.
5. **Conclusion:** the enzyme in saliva (amylase) hydrolyses starch to a **reducing sugar** (maltose); boiling destroys the enzyme.

## Fermentation

Yeast ferments glucose to ethanol and carbon(IV) oxide, without air, at 25–35 °C (see the Alkanols topic):

$$\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \rightarrow 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 2\text{CO}_2$$

Starchy foods are first hydrolysed (by malt or by moulds), then fermented, to make beer, local brews and industrial ethanol.

## Dehydration of sucrose

**Concentrated sulphuric acid** removes the elements of water from sugar. White sugar turns yellow, then brown, then a **black, spongy mass of carbon** rises out of the beaker, with steam and much heat:

$$\text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{22}\text{O}_{11} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4} 12\text{C} + 11\text{H}_2\text{O}$$

This shows that sucrose contains carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, and that concentrated sulphuric acid is a **dehydrating agent**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Identifying a carbohydrate.** Substance X is a white, sweet, crystalline solid. Its solution gives no colour with iodine and no change with Benedict's solution. After boiling with dilute HCl and neutralising, it gives a brick-red precipitate with Benedict's solution. Identify X.

- Sweet, crystalline and soluble: a **sugar**, not starch (and iodine is negative).
- Not a reducing sugar, but becomes one after acid hydrolysis.

X is **sucrose**.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Local brews such as **"omole"** start from sugary juices (palm sap, sugar cane) that ferment directly. To brew from **rice, maize or cassava**, the starch must first be broken down: germinated (malted) grain supplies **diastase**, which hydrolyses the starch to maltose before yeast can ferment it. This is the same chemistry as the saliva experiment.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Benedict's and Fehling's tests need **heating**. Without heat, even glucose gives no precipitate, and you may wrongly conclude that no reducing sugar is present.
:::

## Key points

- **Iodine** → blue-black with **starch**; **Benedict's or Fehling's** (heat) → **brick-red Cu₂O** with **reducing sugars**; **Molisch's** → violet ring with any carbohydrate.
- **Sucrose** is non-reducing until **hydrolysed** (dilute acid) to glucose + fructose.
- **Starch** → glucose (boiling dilute acid) or → maltose (**amylase**, diastase).
- **Saliva** digests starch at 37 °C; boiled saliva does not (enzyme denatured).
- **Fermentation:** glucose → ethanol + CO₂; **conc. H₂SO₄** dehydrates sugar to carbon.

## Quick check

1. What colour does iodine solution give with starch?
2. What is the brick-red precipitate in Benedict's test?
3. Name the products of the acid hydrolysis of sucrose.
4. Why is boiled saliva used in the control tube?

**Answers:**

1. Blue-black.
2. Copper(I) oxide, Cu₂O.
3. Glucose and fructose.
4. Boiling destroys (denatures) the enzyme, so the control shows that the change in the test tube is caused by the active enzyme.
