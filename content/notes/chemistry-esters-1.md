The smell of a ripe banana, a pineapple or nail-varnish remover comes from **esters** (alkanoates). Esters form when an **alkanoic acid** reacts with an **alkanol**. This lesson covers how esters are made, how to name them, their properties and their uses.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe esterification, with the conditions and the role of concentrated sulphuric acid
- write equations for the formation of esters
- name esters and draw their structures from the parent acid and alkanol
- describe the physical properties and uses of esters
- explain how the yield of an ester can be increased
- identify functional group isomers of esters and acids

## Esterification

:::callout{type=definition}
**Esterification** is the reaction between an **alkanoic acid** and an **alkanol** to form an **ester** and **water**. It is slow and **reversible**, and is catalysed by **concentrated sulphuric acid**.
:::

$$\text{acid} + \text{alkanol} \rightleftharpoons \text{ester} + \text{water}$$

![Ethanoic acid and ethanol: the OH of the acid and the H of the alcohol's OH leave as water, forming ethyl ethanoate](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/esterification.svg)

*Figure 1. In esterification, water is lost between the acid and the alkanol. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

$$\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

The **–OH** comes from the **acid** and the **H** from the **alkanol's –OH group**. The ester link **–COO–** joins the two parts.

**Laboratory preparation of ethyl ethanoate:**

1. Mix ethanol and glacial ethanoic acid in a boiling tube or flask; add a few drops of **concentrated sulphuric acid**.
2. **Warm** in a water bath (or heat under reflux) for several minutes.
3. Pour into **sodium carbonate solution** to neutralise unreacted acid.
4. An oily layer of **ester** floats on top, with a **sweet, fruity smell**.

**Role of concentrated sulphuric acid:**

- it is a **catalyst** (speeds up the reaction)
- it is a **dehydrating agent**: it absorbs the water formed, shifting the equilibrium to the right and increasing the yield

## Naming esters

An ester name has two parts:

1. the **alkyl group from the alkanol**, first (methyl, ethyl, propyl …)
2. the **acid part** with **-oic acid** changed to **-oate** (methanoate, ethanoate, propanoate …)

So ethanol + ethanoic acid → **ethyl ethanoate**. When written as a formula, the acid part usually comes first: CH₃COO–C₂H₅.

| Ester | Formula | Made from | Smell or use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Methyl ethanoate** | CH₃COOCH₃ | Methanol + ethanoic acid | Solvent |
| **Ethyl ethanoate** | CH₃COOC₂H₅ | Ethanol + ethanoic acid | Nail-varnish remover, glue solvent; pear drops |
| **Ethyl methanoate** | HCOOC₂H₅ | Ethanol + methanoic acid | Rum, raspberry flavour |
| **Pentyl ethanoate** | CH₃COOC₅H₁₁ | Pentan-1-ol + ethanoic acid | **Banana** |
| **Octyl ethanoate** | CH₃COOC₈H₁₇ | Octan-1-ol + ethanoic acid | **Orange** |
| **Ethyl butanoate** | C₃H₇COOC₂H₅ | Ethanol + butanoic acid | **Pineapple** |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Naming and drawing.**

1. Name CH₃CH₂COOCH₃. The acid part, CH₃CH₂COO–, has **3 carbons** (propanoate); the alkyl part, –CH₃, is **methyl**. Name: **methyl propanoate**.
2. Write the ester formed from propan-1-ol and ethanoic acid. Acid part: CH₃COO–; alkyl: –CH₂CH₂CH₃ (propyl). Ester: **propyl ethanoate**, CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃.
:::

## Physical properties

- Colourless, **volatile** liquids (small esters) with **sweet, fruity smells**.
- **Insoluble** (or only slightly soluble) in water; they float on water.
- **Lower boiling points** than the acids they come from, because esters **cannot form hydrogen bonds** with each other (no O–H group). Ethyl ethanoate (Mᵣ 88) boils at 77 °C; butanoic acid (Mᵣ 88) boils at 164 °C.
- Good **solvents** for oils, fats, varnishes and plastics.

## Isomerism between acids and esters

Esters and alkanoic acids with the same number of carbons are **functional group isomers**. For C₃H₆O₂:

| Compound | Formula | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Propanoic acid | CH₃CH₂COOH | Alkanoic acid |
| Methyl ethanoate | CH₃COOCH₃ | Ester |
| Ethyl methanoate | HCOOCH₂CH₃ | Ester |

Only the **acid** fizzes with sodium hydrogencarbonate.

## Increasing the yield

Esterification is an **equilibrium**: with equal moles of acid and alkanol, only about **two-thirds** is converted to ester. Following Le Chatelier's principle, the yield is increased by:

- using an **excess** of the cheaper reactant (usually the alkanol)
- **removing water** (concentrated H₂SO₄ absorbs it)
- **distilling off the ester** as it forms

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Percentage yield.** 6.0 g of ethanoic acid is warmed with excess ethanol and gives 5.28 g of ethyl ethanoate. Calculate the percentage yield. (CH₃COOH = 60; CH₃COOC₂H₅ = 88)

- Moles of acid $= 6.0 \div 60 = 0.10$ mol, so the maximum ester is $0.10$ mol
- Theoretical mass $= 0.10 \times 88 = 8.8$ g
- Percentage yield $= 5.28 \div 8.8 \times 100 = 60\%$
:::

## Uses of esters

- **Flavourings** in sweets, soft drinks and ice cream
- **Perfumes** and cosmetics
- **Solvents** for nail varnish, glues, paints and inks (ethyl ethanoate)
- **Plasticisers**, which make plastics flexible
- **Fats and oils** are natural esters; **polyesters** (such as Terylene) are fibres for clothing

:::callout{type=local-example}
The bright fruit flavours of sweets, ice lollies and soft drinks sold in Sierra Leone usually come from **synthetic esters**, not from real fruit. A banana-flavoured sweet may contain **pentyl ethanoate**; a pineapple drink may contain **ethyl butanoate**. Ripe fruits make the same esters naturally as they ripen.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
In the name, the **alkanol part comes first** (ethyl), but in the condensed formula the **acid part is usually written first** (CH₃COO–). CH₃COOC₂H₅ is **ethyl ethanoate**, and HCOOCH₃ is **methyl methanoate**.
:::

## Key points

- **Acid + alkanol ⇌ ester + water**, catalysed by **conc. H₂SO₄** (catalyst and dehydrating agent); warm or reflux.
- The ester link is **–COO–**; the –OH comes from the acid.
- Name = **alkyl (from alkanol) + -oate (from acid)**: ethyl ethanoate, methyl propanoate.
- Esters are **volatile, fruity-smelling, insoluble** in water, with **lower boiling points** than acids (no hydrogen bonding).
- Increase yield with **excess reactant** or by **removing water or ester**.
- Uses: **flavourings, perfumes, solvents, plasticisers**.

## Quick check

1. Name the ester formed from methanol and ethanoic acid.
2. State two roles of concentrated sulphuric acid in esterification.
3. Name the ester CH₃COOC₃H₇ and its parent alkanol.
4. Why do esters have lower boiling points than alkanoic acids of similar Mᵣ?

**Answers:**

1. Methyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₃.
2. It acts as a catalyst and as a dehydrating agent that absorbs water, increasing the yield.
3. Propyl ethanoate; propan-1-ol.
4. Ester molecules have no O–H group, so they cannot form hydrogen bonds with each other.
