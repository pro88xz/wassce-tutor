The **Scramble for Africa** was the rapid race among **European powers** to claim and conquer African territory between about **1880 and 1914**. In 1870 Europeans controlled only about a tenth of Africa, mostly along the coasts; by 1914 they ruled almost the whole continent except **Ethiopia** and **Liberia**. Its causes were **economic** (raw materials, markets and investment after the **Industrial Revolution**), **political** (national pride and rivalry), **strategic** (the **Suez Canal** and the route to India), **humanitarian and religious**, and new **technology** that made conquest possible. This lesson explains the **causes of the European partition of Africa**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain the meaning of the Scramble for and partition of Africa
- explain the economic causes of the partition
- explain the political and strategic causes
- explain the humanitarian, religious and personal factors
- explain the factors that made the conquest of Africa possible
- describe the events that started the Scramble

## Meaning

:::callout{type=definition}
The **Scramble for Africa** is the competition among European countries to claim African territory in the late nineteenth century. The **partition of Africa** is the **division** of the continent among those countries, through **treaties, agreements and conquest**, between about **1880 and 1914**.
:::

Before 1880, Europeans in West Africa had mainly **coastal forts and small colonies** such as **Freetown**, **Lagos** (annexed 1861), **Saint-Louis** and **the Gold Coast forts**. Within thirty years they had conquered the interior.

## Causes of the Scramble for Africa

### 1. Economic causes

| Cause | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Raw materials** | Europe's factories needed **palm oil, rubber, cotton, groundnuts, cocoa, timber** and **minerals** (gold, diamonds, copper) |
| **Markets** | Europe produced more manufactured goods than it could sell at home; colonies became **protected markets** |
| **Investment** | bankers and companies wanted safe places to invest **surplus capital** in mines, railways and plantations |
| **The Great Depression of 1873–1896** | falling prices and trade made governments protect their trade with colonies and **tariffs** |
| **Rivalry over trade** | African middlemen controlled trade; Europeans wanted to **reach producers directly** and stop rivals taking the trade |

### 2. Political causes

1. **Nationalism and prestige:** colonies were a sign of a great power. After **France** lost **Alsace-Lorraine** to Germany in the **Franco-Prussian War (1870–71)**, it looked to Africa to restore its pride.
2. **New powers:** the unification of **Germany (1871)** and **Italy (completed in 1870)** created new states that wanted colonies too.
3. **Balance of power:** each power feared its rivals would grow stronger, so it claimed land to keep others out.
4. **Domestic politics:** some leaders used colonies to win popular support and distract from problems at home.

### 3. Strategic causes

1. The **Suez Canal**, opened in **1869**, became Britain's short route to **India**.
2. To protect it, Britain **occupied Egypt in 1882**, which alarmed France and helped to start the Scramble.
3. Naval powers wanted **coaling stations** and ports along Africa's coasts.

### 4. Humanitarian, religious and cultural causes

1. **Missionaries** wanted to spread **Christianity** and asked their governments for protection.
2. Many Europeans claimed a duty to end the remaining **slave trade** in Africa.
3. Racist ideas of a **"civilising mission"** (France's *mission civilisatrice*, Britain's "white man's burden") were used to justify conquest.
4. **Social Darwinism** taught, wrongly, that some races were superior and destined to rule others.

### 5. What made the conquest possible

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Quinine** (used against malaria from the 1850s) | Europeans could survive inland |
| **Steamships, railways and the telegraph** | faster movement of troops, goods and orders |
| **The Maxim gun** and breech-loading rifles | small European armies could defeat large African forces |
| **Explorers** such as **Mungo Park**, **David Livingstone** and **H. M. Stanley** | mapped rivers and routes and publicised the interior |
| **African disunity** | Europeans could play rival states against each other |

## Events that started the Scramble

1. **King Leopold II of Belgium** used **Stanley (1879–1884)** to make treaties in the **Congo basin** for his private company.
2. The French explorer **de Brazza** made treaties north of the Congo River (1880), which France ratified in 1882.
3. **Britain occupied Egypt (1882)**.
4. **Germany** claimed **Togoland, Kamerun, South West Africa** (1884) and **East Africa** (1885).
5. To prevent war over these rival claims, the German chancellor **Bismarck** called the **Berlin Conference (1884–85)**, studied in the next lesson.

:::callout{type=local-example}
In West Africa, **French** expansion from Senegal towards the **Niger** and the Futa Jallon, and **British** worries about losing Freetown's trade with the interior, pushed both powers inland. The trade of **Freetown** with the Rokel, Scarcies and Sherbro areas was threatened by French claims, which is one reason Britain declared the **Sierra Leone Protectorate** in **1896**.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not say the Scramble happened because Africa was "empty" or "uncivilised". Africa had organised states, trade and governments. The causes lay mainly in **European economic needs and rivalries**, and conquest was made possible by **superior weapons**.
:::

## Key figures and terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key figures and terms"}
- **Maxim gun**: an early machine gun that gave European armies overwhelming firepower in the conquest of Africa.
- **Quinine**: a medicine against malaria that allowed Europeans to survive in tropical Africa.
- **Suez Canal** (opened 1869): the canal in Egypt linking the Mediterranean and the Red Sea, Britain's short route to India.
:::

## Key points

- The **Scramble** (about **1880–1914**) left only **Ethiopia** and **Liberia** independent.
- **Economic:** raw materials, markets, investment, the depression of 1873–96.
- **Political:** nationalism, prestige, **French defeat in 1870–71**, new powers (Germany, Italy), balance of power.
- **Strategic:** **Suez Canal (1869)**, **Egypt (1882)**, route to India.
- **Humanitarian and religious:** missions, anti-slavery, the "civilising mission".
- **Made possible by** quinine, steamships, railways, the **Maxim gun** and explorers.

## Quick check

1. What was the Scramble for Africa?
2. Which two African countries remained independent in 1914?
3. Give two economic causes of the partition.
4. Why did France seek colonies after 1871?
5. Why was the Suez Canal important to Britain?
6. How did quinine and the Maxim gun help European conquest?

**Answers:**

1. The race among European powers to claim and conquer African territory between about 1880 and 1914.
2. Ethiopia and Liberia.
3. Any two: the need for raw materials; the need for markets; places to invest surplus capital; the effects of the depression of 1873–96.
4. To restore national pride and power after losing Alsace-Lorraine in the Franco-Prussian War.
5. It was the short sea route to India, Britain's most valuable colony.
6. Quinine protected Europeans from malaria so they could survive inland; the Maxim gun let small European forces defeat large African armies.
