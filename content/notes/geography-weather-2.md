**Weather instruments** measure the elements of weather: a **thermometer** measures temperature, a **rain gauge** rainfall, a **wind vane** wind direction, an **anemometer** wind speed, a **barometer** air pressure, a **hygrometer** (wet- and dry-bulb thermometers) humidity, and a **sunshine recorder** hours of sunshine. They are kept at a **weather station**, with the thermometers inside a white box called a **Stevenson screen**. This lesson explains each instrument, how to read it and how to calculate temperature and rainfall statistics.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- name the instruments used to measure each element of weather, and their units
- describe the Stevenson screen and explain its features
- explain how to use a rain gauge, wind vane and anemometer
- calculate mean temperature, temperature range and total rainfall
- state the requirements for siting a weather station
- explain the importance of weather records and forecasts

## The instruments

| Instrument | Measures | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| **Maximum and minimum (Six's) thermometer** | highest and lowest temperatures in a day | °C |
| **Wet- and dry-bulb thermometers (hygrometer)** | relative humidity | % |
| **Rain gauge** | amount of rainfall | mm |
| **Wind vane** | wind direction | compass points |
| **Anemometer** | wind speed | km/h or knots |
| **Barometer** (mercury or aneroid) | air pressure | millibars (mb) |
| **Sunshine recorder** (Campbell–Stokes) | hours of sunshine | hours |

## The Stevenson screen

The **Stevenson screen** is a wooden box that houses the thermometers and hygrometer.

| Feature | Reason |
|---|---|
| **painted white** | reflects the Sun's heat, so the instruments record air temperature, not direct sunshine |
| **louvred sides** (slatted, like shutters) | let air circulate freely while keeping out sun and rain |
| **stands on legs** about 1.2 m above the ground | avoids heat radiated from the ground |
| **double roof** | insulates against the Sun's heat |
| **sited on grass** in an open place | avoids heat reflected from concrete or buildings |
| **door opens away from the Sun** | stops sunlight falling on the thermometers when it is opened |

## Using the main instruments

**Rain gauge:** rain falls into a **funnel** and collects in a jar inside a metal cylinder. The gauge is sunk into the ground with its rim about 30 cm above the surface, so that rain does not splash in. Each day at the same time the water is poured into a **measuring cylinder** and the reading in mm is recorded.

**Wind vane:** the arrow **points to the direction from which the wind is blowing**. A wind blowing from the south-west is called a **south-westerly** wind.

**Anemometer:** three or four cups on arms spin in the wind; the faster they spin, the stronger the wind.

**Wet- and dry-bulb thermometers:** the wet bulb is wrapped in muslin dipped in water. Evaporation cools it, so it reads lower than the dry bulb. The **bigger the difference**, the **drier** the air; if both read the same, the air is saturated (100% humidity). Relative humidity is read from tables.

**Barometer:** average sea-level pressure is about **1,013 mb**. Falling pressure often means cloud and rain; rising pressure, fine weather.

## Calculating with weather records

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A weather station in Kenema records a maximum temperature of 31 °C and a minimum of 23 °C. Find the mean daily temperature and the daily (diurnal) range.**

Mean daily temperature $= \dfrac{31 + 23}{2} = 27\,°\text{C}$.

Daily range $= 31 - 23 = 8\,°\text{C}$.
:::

- **Mean monthly temperature** = sum of daily means ÷ number of days.
- **Annual range of temperature** = mean temperature of the hottest month − mean temperature of the coldest month.
- **Total annual rainfall** = the sum of the 12 monthly totals.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**A town's hottest month has a mean temperature of 29 °C and its coldest month 24 °C. Its monthly rainfall totals add up to 2,850 mm. State its annual temperature range and annual rainfall.**

Annual range $= 29 - 24 = 5\,°\text{C}$; annual rainfall $= 2{,}850$ mm.
:::

## Showing weather on maps

Lines joining places with equal values are **isolines**:

- **isotherms:** equal temperature
- **isohyets:** equal rainfall
- **isobars:** equal air pressure

## Siting a weather station

- an **open, level** site, away from tall buildings and trees that block wind and rain
- on **grass**, not concrete
- **secure** from people and animals
- easily reached so that readings can be taken at the same time every day

## Importance of weather records and forecasts

1. **Farming:** deciding when to plant, spray and harvest.
2. **Aviation and shipping:** safe flights and voyages.
3. **Disaster warnings:** floods, storms and landslides.
4. **Planning:** water supply, construction, tourism and events.
5. **Climate studies:** long-term records show climate change.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Weather observations in Sierra Leone are coordinated by the **Sierra Leone Meteorological Agency**, which runs stations including the one at **Lungi** International Airport. During the rainy season, its warnings of very heavy rain help communities on Freetown's steep hillsides prepare for floods and landslides.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A **wind vane points towards where the wind is coming from**, and the wind is named after that direction. A "north-east wind" blows **from** the north-east, like the Harmattan.
:::

## Key points

- Thermometers (°C), **rain gauge** (mm), **wind vane** (direction), **anemometer** (speed), **barometer** (mb), **hygrometer** (humidity %), **sunshine recorder** (hours).
- The **Stevenson screen** is white, louvred, on legs, double-roofed and sited on grass, so thermometers record true shade air temperature.
- Mean daily temperature $= \dfrac{\max + \min}{2}$; daily range $= \max - \min$.
- **Isotherms, isohyets, isobars** join places of equal temperature, rainfall and pressure.

## Quick check

1. Which instrument measures wind speed?
2. Why is the Stevenson screen painted white?
3. What does a large difference between wet- and dry-bulb readings show?
4. The maximum temperature is 33 °C and the minimum 21 °C. Find the mean and the range.
5. What is an isohyet?

**Answers:**

1. The anemometer.
2. To reflect the Sun's heat, so the thermometers record the true air temperature.
3. The air is dry (low relative humidity).
4. Mean $= 27\,°$C; range $= 12\,°$C.
5. A line on a map joining places with equal rainfall.
