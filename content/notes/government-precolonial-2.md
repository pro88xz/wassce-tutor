In 1788 a Temne ruler, **King Naimbana** of Koya, put his mark on a treaty granting land on the Freetown peninsula to settlers from Britain. The **Temne** of the north and centre of Sierra Leone had well-established rulers whose authority rested on ritual, ancestry and powerful societies. This lesson explains how the Temne were governed before colonial rule and compares their system with that of the Mende.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the political organisation of the Temne before colonial rule
- explain how a Temne paramount chief was selected and installed
- explain the roles of societies such as the Ragbenle and Poro
- describe the influence of Islam in Temne country
- compare the Mende and Temne political systems

## Temne political organisation

![A Temne chiefdom: the Ragbenle or Poro select and install the chief, who goes through kantha (seclusion and instruction) before ruling as a sacred paramount chief, advised by title-holders and elders and ruling through sub-chiefs, town chiefs and village heads](https://wasscetutor.com/img/government/temne-structure.svg)

*Figure 1. A Temne chiefdom before colonial rule. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

Like the Mende, the Temne lived in **many separate chiefdoms**, each headed by a paramount ruler. Temne rulers bore titles such as **Bai** or **Obai** (as in **Bai Bureh**), and some northern chiefdoms used titles of Muslim or Mande origin such as **Alikali** and **Alimamy**.

| Office or body | Role |
|---|---|
| **Paramount chief (Bai, Obai)** | Sacred head of the chiefdom: ruled, judged, led rituals for the welfare of the land and people, controlled land, and represented the chiefdom |
| **Title-holders and elders** | Advised the chief; many held hereditary titles with specific duties at court and in ritual |
| **Sub-chiefs** | Ruled parts of the chiefdom on the chief's behalf |
| **Town chiefs and village heads** | Ruled local communities and settled minor disputes |
| **Societies (Ragbenle, Poro, Ramena)** | Selected and installed the chief, instructed him in law and custom, carried out rituals |

### Selection and installation

1. When a chief died, candidates came from the **ruling houses**.
2. The elders and the leaders of the chiefdom's ritual society (the **Ragbenle** in eastern Temne country; the **Poro** or **Ramena** in other areas) chose the new chief.
3. The chief-elect then went through **kantha**: a long period of **seclusion and instruction**, lasting months, during which he was taught the laws, boundaries and history of the chiefdom and the duties of rule.
4. He was then **crowned** in public ceremonies.

Because of these rituals, the Temne chief was regarded as **sacred**. His wellbeing was linked to the prosperity of the land.

## The influence of Islam

From the eighteenth century, **Muslim traders and teachers** of Mande, Susu and Fula origin spread into northern Sierra Leone. Many Temne rulers adopted Islam or employed Muslim scholars as **advisers, scribes and teachers**, and Muslim titles such as **Alimamy** became part of northern politics. Islam brought literacy in Arabic, which helped in diplomacy and record-keeping.

## Comparing the Mende and Temne

| Feature | Mende | Temne |
|---|---|---|
| **Location** | South and east | North and centre |
| **Unit of government** | Many independent chiefdoms; some confederacies | Many independent chiefdoms |
| **Ruler** | Chief (*mahei*) | Paramount chief (Bai, Obai) |
| **Basis of authority** | Ruling families, warfare and alliances | Ruling houses, ritual and sacred kingship |
| **Selection** | By elders and the Poro from ruling families | By elders and societies (Ragbenle, Poro) from ruling houses |
| **Installation** | Ceremonies led by elders and the Poro | **Kantha** seclusion, then crowning |
| **Key societies** | Poro (men), Sande (women) | Ragbenle, Poro, Ramena; Bondo (women) |
| **Women rulers** | Common (e.g. Madam Yoko) | Rare |
| **Islamic influence** | Limited | Strong in many northern chiefdoms |
| **Checks on the ruler** | Elders, Poro, custom | Elders, societies, ritual rules, custom |

**Similarities:** both had chiefs from **ruling families**, **councils of elders**, **sub-chiefs**, **secret societies** with political roles, **customary law** and **traditional courts**, and both were **decentralised** into many chiefdoms.

:::callout{type=local-example}
The Temne ruler **Bai Bureh** of **Kasseh** led resistance against British rule in the **Hut Tax War of 1898**. A skilled war leader, he held off British forces for months before he was captured and exiled to the Gold Coast. He was later allowed to return home. Today he is remembered as one of Sierra Leone's national heroes.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
"Kantha" is not a society. It is the **period of seclusion and instruction** that a Temne chief-elect goes through before installation. The **Ragbenle** (or Poro or Ramena) is the society that conducts it.
:::

## Key points

- The **Temne** of the north and centre lived in **many chiefdoms** ruled by **sacred paramount chiefs** with titles such as **Bai** or **Obai**.
- Chiefs were chosen from **ruling houses** by elders and **societies (Ragbenle, Poro)** and installed after **kantha**.
- **Islam** strongly influenced northern chiefdoms, bringing advisers, literacy and titles such as **Alimamy**.
- Mende and Temne both had **ruling families, councils, sub-chiefs and secret societies**; they differed in **ritual, women's rule and Islamic influence**.

## Quick check

1. What is kantha?
2. Name a society that installs Temne chiefs.
3. Give two differences between the Mende and Temne political systems.
4. Which Temne ruler led resistance in the Hut Tax War of 1898?

**Answers:**

1. The period of seclusion and instruction that a Temne chief-elect goes through before being installed.
2. The Ragbenle (or the Poro or Ramena in some chiefdoms).
3. Any two: the Temne chief was regarded as sacred and installed after kantha, while Mende chiefs relied more on war and alliances; women chiefs were common among the Mende but rare among the Temne; Islam was more influential among the Temne; different societies (Ragbenle among the eastern Temne, Poro and Sande among the Mende).
4. Bai Bureh of Kasseh.
