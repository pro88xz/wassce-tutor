A **settlement hierarchy** ranks settlements by size and importance: **isolated dwellings**, **hamlets**, **villages**, **towns**, **cities** and **conurbations**. The higher a settlement in the hierarchy, the more people it has, the larger the area it serves and the more **functions** it provides: administration, trade, services, industry, transport and more. This lesson explains the hierarchy, the functions of towns, and how settlements in Sierra Leone fit into the pattern.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the settlement hierarchy from isolated dwelling to conurbation
- explain the terms sphere of influence, range and threshold
- classify towns by their main functions, with examples
- describe the urban hierarchy and main towns of Sierra Leone
- explain how settlements grow and change their functions

## The settlement hierarchy

| Level | Approximate size | Typical services |
|---|---|---|
| **Isolated dwelling** | one household | none |
| **Hamlet** | a few houses | perhaps a small shop or water point |
| **Village** | hundreds to a few thousand people | primary school, place of worship, weekly market, health post |
| **Town** | thousands to tens of thousands | secondary schools, hospital, banks, daily market, offices |
| **City** | hundreds of thousands or more | universities, specialist hospitals, government offices, large markets |
| **Conurbation** | towns and cities that have grown together | everything; a huge built-up area (Lagos, Greater Accra) |

As you go up the hierarchy: settlements become **fewer**, **larger** and **further apart**, and they offer **more and higher-order services**.

## Sphere of influence, range and threshold

- **Sphere of influence (hinterland):** the area served by a settlement, from which people come to use its services.
- **Range:** the maximum distance people will travel to use a service or buy a good. People travel further for a specialist hospital than for bread.
- **Threshold:** the minimum number of customers needed for a service to stay in business. A university needs a much larger threshold than a corner shop.
- **Low-order** goods and services (bread, kerosene, a primary school) are bought often and found in small settlements; **high-order** ones (jewellery, a specialist doctor, a university) are bought rarely and found only in large towns.

## Functions of towns

| Function | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Administrative** | seat of government | **Freetown** (national capital); **Bo, Kenema, Makeni, Port Loko** (regional headquarters) |
| **Commercial (market)** | trade and markets | Bo, Kenema; Kano and Onitsha in Nigeria |
| **Mining** | grew around mines | **Koidu** (diamonds); Obuasi (gold, Ghana); Jos (tin, Nigeria) |
| **Port** | handles shipping | **Freetown**; Pepel (iron ore); Tema, Lagos, Abidjan |
| **Industrial** | manufacturing | Tema; Ikeja (Lagos) |
| **Educational** | universities and colleges | **Njala** (Njala University) |
| **Religious** | centres of pilgrimage or worship | Touba (Senegal); Ile-Ife (Nigeria) |
| **Route centre (transport)** | where roads, railways or rivers meet | Masiaka; Kumasi (Ghana) |
| **Residential and tourist** | homes and tourism | beach towns and resorts |

Most large towns have **several** functions; one is usually dominant.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone's urban hierarchy is headed by **Freetown**, the capital and main port, with over a million people in the city and its surroundings. Below it are regional cities: **Bo** (Southern Province) and **Kenema** (Eastern Province), both commercial centres; **Makeni** (Northern Province), a growing administrative and educational centre; and **Koidu** in Kono, a mining town. **Port Loko** became the headquarters of the new **North West Province** in 2017. Below these are district headquarter towns, chiefdom headquarters, villages and hamlets.
:::

## How settlements grow and change

Settlements grow and their functions change because of:

1. **New economic activities**, such as the discovery of minerals (Koidu after diamonds were found in the 1930s).
2. **Transport links:** new roads, bridges, railways and ports.
3. **Government decisions:** making a town a regional or district headquarters.
4. **Migration** from rural areas.
5. **Education and health institutions.**

Settlements can also **decline** when mines close, railways shut (Sierra Leone's passenger railway closed in the 1970s) or trade moves elsewhere.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Range** and **threshold** are different. Range is about **distance** (how far people will travel); threshold is about **numbers** (how many customers a service needs to survive).
:::

## Key points

- Hierarchy: **isolated dwelling → hamlet → village → town → city → conurbation**.
- Higher settlements are **fewer, bigger, further apart** and offer **higher-order services**.
- **Sphere of influence**, **range** (distance) and **threshold** (minimum customers).
- Town functions: **administrative, commercial, mining, port, industrial, educational, religious, route centre**.
- Sierra Leone: **Freetown → Bo, Kenema, Makeni, Koidu, Port Loko → district towns → villages**.

## Quick check

1. Arrange in order of size: town, hamlet, conurbation, village, city.
2. What is the threshold of a service?
3. Give an example of a mining town in Sierra Leone.
4. Name one educational town in Sierra Leone.
5. Why are high-order services found only in large towns?

**Answers:**

1. Hamlet, village, town, city, conurbation.
2. The minimum number of customers needed for the service to stay in business.
3. Koidu.
4. Njala.
5. They need a large threshold population and people will travel far to use them, so only large towns with big spheres of influence can support them.
