A trader in Kailahun buys coffee to sell in Freetown. The coffee must travel 300 kilometres, wait in a store until buyers are ready, be protected against theft and fire, be paid for across the distance, and be made known to buyers. None of this is buying and selling, yet without it the trade could not happen. These helping services are the **aids to trade**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define aids to trade and name the six main aids
- explain the hindrance to trade that each aid removes
- explain the importance of each aid to a business
- give Sierra Leonean examples of each aid

## What are aids to trade?

:::callout{type=definition}
**Aids to trade** are the services that make buying and selling easier by removing the **hindrances** (obstacles) to trade.
:::

:::callout{type=definition}
A **hindrance to trade** is any problem that stands between producers and consumers and makes exchange difficult, such as distance, time, risk, lack of money or lack of information.
:::

## The six aids to trade

| Aid | Hindrance it removes | How it helps |
|---|---|---|
| **Transport** | **Place**: producers and consumers are far apart | Moves goods and people from where they are produced to where they are needed |
| **Warehousing** | **Time**: goods are produced at one time and needed at another | Stores goods safely until they are wanted, and keeps prices steadier |
| **Insurance** | **Risk** of loss by fire, theft or accident | Pays compensation if insured goods are lost or damaged |
| **Banking** | **Finance** and **payment**: lack of money and difficulty paying | Provides loans, keeps money safely and makes payments easy |
| **Communication** | **Distance between traders**: they need to exchange information | Lets traders place orders, ask prices and confirm deliveries quickly |
| **Advertising** | **Ignorance**: consumers do not know what is available | Informs and persuades consumers about goods and services |

## Each aid in more detail

### Transport

Transport may be by **road** (lorries, buses, poda-podas, okadas), **water** (ships and boats along the coast and rivers), **rail** (the iron ore railway) or **air**. It widens the market, allows specialisation in different areas and brings goods to consumers.

### Warehousing

Warehouses store goods produced in bulk or in season, such as rice after harvest, so they can be sold all year. Bonded warehouses store imported goods until customs duty is paid.

### Insurance

:::callout{type=definition}
**Insurance** is a contract in which an insurance company agrees to pay compensation for a specified loss in return for a regular payment called a **premium**.
:::

Insurance lets traders take risks they could not otherwise afford, such as shipping goods by sea or keeping large stocks.

### Banking

Banks accept **deposits**, give **loans and overdrafts**, make payments by cheque, transfer and card, and advise businesses. Mobile money services now let traders pay and receive money by phone.

### Communication

Telephone calls, text messages, email, the internet and the post let traders place orders, check prices and arrange deliveries without travelling.

### Advertising

Advertising on radio, television, billboards, social media and posters tells consumers what is for sale, where and at what price, and persuades them to buy.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Do not list "trade" itself as an aid to trade, and do not mix up the hindrance each aid removes. Transport removes the hindrance of **place**; warehousing removes the hindrance of **time**.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Matching the aid to the problem.** A cassava flour producer in Moyamba faces these problems. Name the aid that helps.

1. Her flour is needed in Freetown, 100 km away. **Transport**
2. She produces most flour in the dry season but sells all year. **Warehousing**
3. She fears her store may catch fire. **Insurance**
4. She needs money to buy a new grater before sales come in. **Banking (a loan)**
5. Shops in Bo do not know her brand exists. **Advertising**
6. She wants to confirm an order with a buyer in Kenema today. **Communication**
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Mobile money has become one of Sierra Leone's most important aids to trade. A trader in a village can now pay a wholesaler in Freetown, receive money from customers and save without visiting a bank, removing the hindrance of payment over distance.
:::

## Key points

- **Aids to trade** are services that remove the **hindrances** to trade.
- **Transport** (place), **warehousing** (time), **insurance** (risk), **banking** (finance and payment), **communication** (exchange of information) and **advertising** (ignorance).
- Each aid widens the market and makes trade cheaper, safer and quicker.
- Insurance is paid for with a **premium**; banks provide loans, savings and payments, increasingly through mobile money.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
WASSCE asks you to **name the aids to trade**, to **explain how each one helps trade**, and to **state the hindrance each removes**. A two-column table (aid and hindrance) followed by a sentence of explanation for each earns marks quickly. Give a Sierra Leonean example where you can.
:::

## Quick check

1. Define aids to trade.
2. Which aid removes the hindrance of time?
3. What is a premium?
4. How does advertising help trade?

**Answers:** 1. Services that make buying and selling easier by removing the hindrances to trade. 2. Warehousing. 3. The regular payment made to an insurance company for cover. 4. It removes the hindrance of ignorance by informing consumers about goods and persuading them to buy.
