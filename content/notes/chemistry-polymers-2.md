After heavy rain in Freetown, gutters overflow and streets flood, partly because drains are choked with plastic bags and sachet-water wrappers. Plastics are useful precisely because they are light, cheap and long-lasting, and that same durability makes them a serious waste problem. This lesson covers the types and properties of plastics, rubber and fibres, and how plastic waste can be managed.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- distinguish thermoplastics from thermosetting plastics and explain the difference
- state the advantages and disadvantages of plastics
- describe natural rubber, vulcanisation and synthetic rubber
- distinguish natural and synthetic fibres
- describe the problems of plastic disposal and ways of reducing them

## Thermoplastics and thermosetting plastics

![Left: separate polymer chains held by weak forces soften on heating; right: chains joined by cross-links do not soften](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/thermoplastic-thermoset.svg)

*Figure 1. Cross-links decide how a plastic behaves when heated. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Feature | Thermoplastics | Thermosetting plastics (thermosets) |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | Long chains, **not cross-linked**; weak forces between chains | Chains joined by strong **covalent cross-links** |
| **On heating** | **Soften and melt**; can be **remoulded** many times | **Do not soften**; char or burn on strong heating |
| **Recycling** | Can be melted and **recycled** | Cannot be remelted |
| **Examples** | Poly(ethene), poly(propene), PVC, polystyrene, nylon, Perspex | **Bakelite**, **melamine**, urea-methanal, epoxy resins |
| **Typical uses** | Bags, bottles, pipes, buckets, fibres | Electrical plugs and switches, saucepan handles, melamine plates, worktops |

## Properties of plastics

**Advantages:**

- **light** but strong
- **cheap** and **easily moulded** into any shape
- **do not rust or corrode**; resist water and many chemicals
- good **electrical and heat insulators**
- can be coloured; often transparent

**Disadvantages:**

- most are **not biodegradable**: they last hundreds of years in the environment
- **burning** releases toxic gases (**hydrogen chloride** from PVC, carbon(II) oxide, dioxins) and black smoke
- made from **non-renewable** petroleum
- many **soften** at fairly low temperatures; they burn easily

## Rubber

**Natural rubber** comes from **latex**, the milky sap of the rubber tree (*Hevea brasiliensis*). Latex is coagulated with **methanoic or ethanoic acid**, then dried. Rubber is an **addition polymer of isoprene** (2-methylbuta-1,3-diene).

Raw rubber is **soft and sticky** when warm and **brittle** when cold.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Vulcanisation** is heating rubber with **sulphur** (about 2–3%) to form **sulphur cross-links (–S–S–)** between the chains. Vulcanised rubber is **harder, stronger, more elastic** and less affected by temperature. More sulphur (about 30%) gives hard **ebonite**.
:::

**Synthetic rubbers** such as **SBR** (styrene–butadiene rubber) and **neoprene** are made from petroleum. They are used for tyres, hoses, shoe soles and seals.

## Fibres

| Natural fibres | Synthetic fibres |
|---|---|
| **Cotton** (cellulose), **linen** (cellulose), **wool** and **silk** (proteins), raffia, jute | **Nylon** (polyamide), **Terylene** (polyester), acrylic |
| From plants and animals; renewable | From petroleum; non-renewable |
| Absorb water; comfortable; may shrink or crease | Strong, light, quick-drying, do not rot; melt if ironed too hot |
| Biodegradable | Mostly not biodegradable |

**Rayon** is a **semi-synthetic** fibre: natural cellulose (from wood pulp) chemically treated and re-formed into fibres.

## Disposing of plastic waste

| Method | Advantages | Problems |
|---|---|---|
| **Landfill** | Cheap, simple | Plastics do not rot; uses land; chemicals leak |
| **Burning (incineration)** | Reduces volume; energy can be recovered | **Toxic gases** (HCl, dioxins, CO) and smoke unless done in special incinerators |
| **Recycling** | Saves petroleum and energy; reduces waste | Plastics must be sorted by type; collection is costly |
| **Biodegradable plastics** | Broken down by bacteria | More expensive; need the right conditions to break down |

The best approach follows the order **reduce, reuse, recycle**: use fewer disposable plastics, reuse containers and bags, and recycle the rest.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Choosing a plastic.** Explain why electrical plugs are made of a thermosetting plastic, but water pipes are made of PVC (a thermoplastic).

- A plug can become **hot**. A thermoset will **not soften** or melt, and it is a good **electrical insulator**, so the plug keeps its shape safely.
- Water pipes must be made cheaply in long lengths and bent or joined. **PVC** can be **melted and moulded (extruded)**, does not corrode and is light, and the water inside stays cool.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sachet-water wrappers, plastic bags and bottles make up a large share of the litter in Sierra Leonean towns. They **block drains**, which worsens flooding in Freetown during the rainy season, and they wash into the sea, where fish and turtles swallow them. Burning them in open heaps releases **toxic smoke**. Some community groups now collect plastic bottles and bags for **recycling** into new products, an example of reduce, reuse, recycle in action.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
"Plastic" does not mean "thermoplastic". **Thermosetting plastics** (Bakelite, melamine) are plastics too, but they **cannot** be melted down and recycled.
:::

## Key points

- **Thermoplastics** (no cross-links) **soften on heating** and can be remoulded and recycled; **thermosets** (cross-linked) **do not soften**.
- Plastics are **light, cheap, durable insulators**, but most are **non-biodegradable** and give **toxic gases** when burned.
- **Natural rubber** (poly-isoprene from latex) is **vulcanised** with **sulphur** to make it harder and more elastic.
- **Natural fibres** (cotton, wool, silk) vs **synthetic fibres** (nylon, polyester).
- Manage plastic waste by **reduce, reuse, recycle**, controlled incineration and biodegradable plastics.

## Quick check

1. Give one example each of a thermoplastic and a thermosetting plastic.
2. What is vulcanisation and why is it carried out?
3. Why is burning PVC especially harmful?
4. State two ways of reducing plastic pollution.

**Answers:**

1. Thermoplastic: poly(ethene), PVC, polystyrene or nylon. Thermoset: Bakelite or melamine.
2. Heating rubber with sulphur to form cross-links between the chains, which makes the rubber harder, stronger, more elastic and less sensitive to temperature.
3. It releases poisonous hydrogen chloride gas (and dioxins).
4. Any two: reduce use of disposable plastics; reuse bags and containers; recycle; use biodegradable plastics; collect waste instead of dumping or burning it in the open.
