Spectacles, cameras, projectors, microscopes and the lens in your own eye all bend light to form images. A lens is two refracting surfaces working together, and three simple rays tell you where its image will be.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- distinguish converging (convex) and diverging (concave) lenses
- define the terms principal axis, optical centre, principal focus and focal length
- draw ray diagrams to locate images formed by both kinds of lens
- describe the image for different object positions

## Types of lens

| Converging (convex) lens | Diverging (concave) lens |
|---|---|
| thicker in the middle than at the edges | thinner in the middle than at the edges |
| brings parallel rays **together** at a real focus | spreads parallel rays **out**, as if from a virtual focus |
| can form real or virtual images | always forms virtual images |
| e.g. magnifying glass, camera lens, the eye lens | e.g. spectacles for short sight, door peephole |

## Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Optical centre (C)** | the centre of the lens; rays through it pass straight on |
| **Principal axis** | the line through the optical centre, perpendicular to the lens |
| **Principal focus (F)** | for a converging lens, the point where rays parallel to the axis **meet** after refraction; for a diverging lens, the point from which they **appear to diverge** |
| **Focal length (f)** | the distance from the optical centre to the principal focus |

A lens has a principal focus on **each** side, at equal distances.

## Rules for ray diagrams

1. A ray **parallel to the principal axis** is refracted **through F** (converging) or **as if from F** on the near side (diverging).
2. A ray through the **optical centre** passes **straight through** undeviated.
3. A ray **through F** (or heading towards F for a diverging lens) emerges **parallel** to the axis.

## Images formed by a converging lens

| Object position | Image position | Nature of image | Application |
|---|---|---|---|
| At infinity | at F | real, inverted, point-sized | burning glass; telescope objective |
| Beyond 2F | between F and 2F | real, inverted, **diminished** | **camera**, the eye |
| At 2F | at 2F | real, inverted, **same size** | photocopier |
| Between 2F and F | beyond 2F | real, inverted, **magnified** | **projector** |
| At F | at infinity | — (parallel beam) | spotlight |
| Between F and the lens | same side as the object | **virtual, upright, magnified** | **magnifying glass** |

![A convex lens used as a magnifying glass: the object is closer than F, the rays leaving the lens diverge and, traced back, meet behind the object to form a virtual, upright, magnified image](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/magnifying-glass.svg)

*Figure 2. A converging lens as a magnifying glass. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

## Images formed by a diverging lens

![Ray diagram for a diverging lens: a ray parallel to the axis leaves as if from F on the object side, and a ray through the centre goes straight on; the image is virtual, upright and diminished](https://wasscetutor.com/img/physics/concave-lens-ray.svg)

*Figure 3. A diverging lens always gives a virtual, upright, diminished image. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

For **any** object position, the image is **virtual, upright, diminished**, on the **same side** as the object, between F and the lens.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Real images** are formed where rays actually meet and can be caught on a screen; they are always **inverted** (for a single lens). **Virtual images** are where rays only appear to come from; they cannot be caught on a screen and are **upright**. In ray diagrams, draw the extensions of rays to a virtual image as **dashed** lines.
:::

### Finding the focal length roughly (practical)

Focus the image of a distant object (such as a window or a tree across the compound) sharply on a screen with a converging lens. The distance from the lens to the screen is approximately the focal length, because rays from a distant object are almost parallel.

:::callout{type=local-example}
On a sunny afternoon, a hand lens held above a dry leaf focuses sunlight to a tiny bright spot at its focal point; the leaf can start to smoulder. That is why broken bottles left in dry bush can start fires.
:::

## Key points

- Converging lenses are thicker in the middle and have a real focus; diverging lenses are thinner in the middle with a virtual focus.
- Ray rules: parallel → through F; through the centre → straight; through F → parallel.
- Converging lens: object beyond F gives a real, inverted image; inside F gives a virtual, upright, magnified image.
- Diverging lens: always virtual, upright and diminished.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
You will be asked to **draw ray diagrams** (sometimes to scale on graph paper) to find the position and size of an image, **state the nature of the image**, and **match object positions to instruments** (camera, projector, magnifying glass). Use a ruler, arrows on rays, and dashed lines for virtual rays.
:::

## Quick check

1. Where must an object be placed for a converging lens to act as a magnifying glass?
2. What kind of image does a diverging lens always form?
3. Which instrument uses an object placed between F and 2F of a converging lens?

**Answers:** 1. Between the lens and its principal focus. 2. Virtual, upright and diminished. 3. A projector.
