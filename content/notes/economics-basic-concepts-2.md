## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state the basic economic problems every society faces
- draw and explain a production possibility curve (PPC)
- use a PPC to show opportunity cost, unemployment and economic growth

## The basic economic problems

Because resources are scarce, every society, whatever its economic system, must answer the same questions:

| Problem | The question | Example in Sierra Leone |
|---|---|---|
| **What to produce?** | Which goods and services, and how much of each? | More rice or more cocoa? More schools or more roads? |
| **How to produce?** | Which methods: more labour or more machines? | Hand hoes or tractors on a large farm |
| **For whom to produce?** | Who gets the goods and services produced? | Who can afford the rice: everyone, or only those with higher incomes? |
| **How efficiently?** | Are resources being used without waste? | Are workers idle while there is work to be done? |

The first three are the ones WAEC asks about most. Different economic systems answer them in different ways, as you will see in *Economic Systems*.

## The production possibility curve

:::callout{type=definition}
A **production possibility curve (PPC)** shows the different combinations of two goods that an economy can produce when it uses **all** its resources fully and efficiently, with a given level of technology. It is also called the production possibility frontier or transformation curve.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
A farming community has a fixed amount of land, labour and tools. It can grow rice, cassava or a mix of the two. Its possible combinations each season are:

| Point | Rice (bags) | Cassava (bags) | Cassava given up for 2 more rice |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0 | 20 | — |
| B | 2 | 18 | 2 |
| C | 4 | 14 | 4 |
| D | 6 | 8 | 6 |
| E | 8 | 0 | 8 |

Plotted on a graph (rice on the horizontal axis, cassava on the vertical axis), these points form a curve that bends outwards from the origin.
:::

### What the PPC shows

**1. Scarcity.** Combinations outside the curve, such as 6 bags of rice and 14 of cassava, cannot be reached with the resources the community has.

**2. Choice.** The community must pick one point on the curve: A, B, C, D or E.

**3. Opportunity cost.** Moving along the curve, more of one good means less of the other. Going from B to C, the 2 extra bags of rice cost 4 bags of cassava.

**4. Increasing opportunity cost.** The cost of each extra 2 bags of rice rises: 2, then 4, then 6, then 8 bags of cassava. This happens because resources are not equally good at producing both crops: the land best suited to rice is used first, and later rice must be grown on land much better for cassava. This is why the curve **bends outwards** (it is concave to the origin).

**5. Unemployment or inefficiency.** A point **inside** the curve, such as 2 bags of rice and 8 of cassava, means some resources are idle or wasted. The community could produce more of both.

**6. Economic growth.** If the community gets better seeds, more land or more skilled workers, it can produce more of both crops. The whole curve **shifts outwards**.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A point inside the curve is **possible** but wasteful. A point outside the curve is **impossible** with present resources. Do not confuse the two. And remember that growth is shown by the *whole curve* shifting out, not by a movement along it.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
If a new irrigation scheme lets farmers grow rice in the dry season as well as the rainy season, the same community can produce more rice **and** more cassava. On the PPC, this is an outward shift.
:::

## Key points

- Every society must decide **what**, **how** and **for whom** to produce.
- A PPC shows the maximum combinations of two goods with full use of resources.
- Points **on** the curve: efficient. **Inside**: unemployment or waste. **Outside**: unattainable.
- Moving along the curve shows **opportunity cost**; an outward shift shows **economic growth**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
You may be given a PPC table and asked to **draw the curve**, **calculate the opportunity cost** of a move, or **explain** points inside, on and outside the curve. Label both axes with the goods and their units, plot points accurately and join them with a smooth curve. When you calculate opportunity cost, always state it in units of the good given up.
:::

## Quick check

1. Using the table above, what is the opportunity cost of moving from C to D?
2. What does a point inside the PPC show?
3. Name one thing that would shift the whole PPC outwards.

**Answers:** 1. 6 bags of cassava (cassava falls from 14 to 8). 2. Unemployed or inefficiently used resources. 3. For example: more land or workers, better technology, better seeds or more training.
