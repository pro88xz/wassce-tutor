## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define total, average and marginal revenue and calculate them
- explain how revenue behaves when price is constant and when it falls
- find the profit-maximising output using the rule MC = MR

## Revenue

:::callout{type=definition}
**Revenue** is the money a firm receives from selling its output.

- **Total revenue (TR)** $= P \times Q$ (price × quantity sold)
- **Average revenue (AR)** $= \dfrac{TR}{Q}$, which is the same as the **price**
- **Marginal revenue (MR)** $= \Delta TR$: the extra revenue from selling one more unit
:::

## Case 1: the price stays the same

When a firm can sell as much as it likes at the market price (as in **perfect competition**), every extra unit brings in the same amount. So **P = AR = MR**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
A firm sells at a fixed price of **NLe 15**:

| $Q$ | Price (AR) | TR | MR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 | 15 | 15 |
| 2 | 15 | 30 | 15 |
| 3 | 15 | 45 | 15 |
| 4 | 15 | 60 | 15 |

TR rises in a straight line; AR and MR are equal and constant.
:::

## Case 2: the price falls as more is sold

Most firms must **lower their price** to sell more. Then MR is **less than** the price, because the lower price applies to all units sold, not just the extra one.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
A sandal maker in Bo finds that to sell more pairs she must lower her price:

| $Q$ | Price (AR) | TR | MR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 | 20 | 20 |
| 2 | 18 | 36 | 16 |
| 3 | 16 | 48 | 12 |
| 4 | 14 | 56 | 8 |
| 5 | 12 | 60 | 4 |
| 6 | 10 | 60 | 0 |

- TR at $Q = 4$: $14 \times 4 = 56$
- MR of the 4th pair: $56 - 48 = 8$, much less than its price of 14.

TR is at its **maximum when MR = 0** (5 or 6 pairs, NLe 60).
:::

## Profit and the profit-maximising rule

:::callout{type=definition}
**Profit** $= TR - TC$. A firm makes the **most profit** at the output where **marginal cost equals marginal revenue (MC = MR)**, with MC rising.
:::

The logic: if the next unit adds more to revenue than to cost ($MR > MC$), producing it raises profit, so produce more. If it adds more to cost than to revenue ($MC > MR$), producing it lowers profit, so produce less. Profit is greatest where they are equal.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
Use the cost table from the previous lesson with a **constant price of NLe 60** (so MR = 60).

| $Q$ | TR | TC | Profit | MC | MR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 60 | 140 | −80 | 40 | 60 |
| 2 | 120 | 170 | −50 | 30 | 60 |
| 3 | 180 | 190 | −10 | 20 | 60 |
| 4 | 240 | 220 | 20 | 30 | 60 |
| 5 | 300 | 270 | **30** | 50 | 60 |
| 6 | 360 | 340 | 20 | 70 | 60 |

- Up to the 5th unit, MC is less than MR, so each unit adds to profit.
- The 6th unit costs 70 but brings in only 60, so it **reduces** profit (from 30 to 20).
- Profit is greatest at **5 units: NLe 30**, the last output where MC does not exceed MR.
:::

### Break-even

A firm **breaks even** when $TR = TC$: it makes neither profit nor loss. In the table, the firm moves from a loss at 3 units to a profit at 4 units, so it breaks even between 3 and 4 units.

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
The profit-maximising output is **not** where TR is highest, nor where average cost is lowest. It is where **MC = MR**. In the sandal example, TR is highest at 5 or 6 pairs, but that tells you nothing about profit until you know the costs.
:::

## Key points

- $TR = P \times Q$; $AR = \dfrac{TR}{Q} = P$; $MR = \Delta TR$.
- If price is constant, $P = AR = MR$. If price falls as output rises, **MR is below AR**.
- **Profit = TR − TC**, and it is maximised where **MC = MR**.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Examiners often combine cost and revenue in one table and ask you to **calculate TR, MR and profit**, **find the profit-maximising output** and **explain why**. State the MC = MR rule in words, show your profit calculation for each output, and point to the exact row where profit is highest.
:::

## Quick check

1. A trader sells 40 bags at NLe 25 each. What is the total revenue?
2. If TR is NLe 90 at 6 units and NLe 96 at 7 units, what is MR of the 7th unit?
3. State the profit-maximising rule.

**Answers:** 1. $40 \times 25 = $ NLe 1,000. 2. $96 - 90 = $ NLe 6. 3. A firm maximises profit where marginal cost equals marginal revenue (MC = MR), with MC rising.
