:::callout{type=definition}
A **bank reconciliation statement** is a statement prepared to **explain the difference** between the **bank balance in the cash book** and the **balance on the bank statement** on the same date.
:::

The two balances rarely agree because of **timing differences** (cheques paid out but not yet presented, money paid in but not yet credited) and items the business **has not yet recorded** (bank charges, standing orders, direct credits, dishonoured cheques), as well as **errors**. This lesson explains the causes of the difference, the purposes of a bank reconciliation, and the standard format.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- define a bank statement and a bank reconciliation statement
- explain why the cash book and the bank statement balances differ
- explain unpresented cheques, uncredited lodgements, standing orders, direct debits and dishonoured cheques
- state the purposes of a bank reconciliation statement
- set out a bank reconciliation statement in the correct format

## The bank statement

:::callout{type=definition}
The **bank statement** is a copy of the customer's account **in the bank's books**.
:::

Because the bank **owes** the money to the customer, a positive balance appears as a **credit** on the bank statement, while the same money is a **debit** (an asset) in the business's cash book. The two records are **mirror images**.

| Item | Cash book (business) | Bank statement (bank) |
|---|---|---|
| Money paid in | debit | credit |
| Cheque paid out | credit | debit |
| Money in the bank | debit balance | credit balance |
| Overdraft | credit balance | debit balance |

## Why the balances differ

![Items in the cash book but not yet on the bank statement (unpresented cheques, uncredited lodgements) go in the reconciliation statement; items on the bank statement but not yet in the cash book (bank charges, standing orders, direct debits, direct credits, interest, dishonoured cheques) are entered in the cash book](https://wasscetutor.com/img/accounting/bank-reconciliation-reasons.svg)

*Figure 1. Two kinds of difference between the cash book and the bank statement. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Cause | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Unpresented cheques** | cheques **paid out** and entered in the cash book but **not yet presented** to the bank for payment |
| **Uncredited lodgements** (deposits in transit) | money **paid in** and entered in the cash book but **not yet credited** by the bank |
| **Bank charges and interest charged** | deducted by the bank; the business learns of them from the statement |
| **Standing orders and direct debits** | regular payments made by the bank on the customer's instructions (rent, insurance) |
| **Direct credits (credit transfers)** | money paid **directly** into the account by customers, or interest and dividends received |
| **Dishonoured cheques** | a customer's cheque that the paying bank **refused to pay**, so the bank takes the amount back |
| **Errors** | mistakes in the cash book (or, rarely, by the bank) |

## Purposes of a bank reconciliation

1. To **explain** the difference between the two balances.
2. To **detect errors** in the cash book or the bank statement.
3. To **update the cash book** with items the business did not know about.
4. To discover **unpresented cheques** that are old and may be stale.
5. To **prevent and detect fraud**, such as unauthorised payments.
6. To confirm the correct **bank balance** for the statement of financial position.

## The format

The statement usually starts from the **adjusted (updated) cash book balance**:

| Bank reconciliation statement at 31 March 2026 | NLe |
|---|---|
| Balance as per adjusted cash book | X |
| **Add:** unpresented cheques | X |
| | X |
| **Less:** uncredited lodgements | (X) |
| **Balance as per bank statement** | **X** |

**Why add unpresented cheques?** The cash book has already deducted them, but the bank has not, so the bank balance is **higher**. **Why deduct uncredited lodgements?** The cash book has added them, but the bank has not, so the bank balance is **lower**.

If the statement starts from the **bank statement balance**, the signs are reversed: **deduct** unpresented cheques and **add** uncredited lodgements to reach the cash book balance.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**The cash book shows a bank balance of NLe 12,000 (debit). Cheques of NLe 2,500 have not been presented, and NLe 1,800 paid in has not been credited. Find the bank statement balance.**

$12\,000 + 2\,500 - 1\,800 = 12\,700$

The bank statement should show a **credit** balance of NLe 12,700.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
WAEC examiners have reported candidates who **start the bank reconciliation statement from the bank statement balance but use the signs for the cash book**, or who reconcile from the **original** cash book balance instead of the **adjusted** one. Decide your starting point first, then apply the matching signs.
:::

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Unpresented cheques**: cheques paid out and entered in the cash book but not yet presented to the bank for payment.
- **Uncredited lodgements**: money paid in and entered in the cash book but not yet credited by the bank.
- **Standing order**: a regular payment made by the bank on the customer's instructions, such as rent or insurance.
- **Direct credit** (credit transfer): money paid directly into the account by customers, or interest and dividends received.
- **Dishonoured cheque**: a customer's cheque that the paying bank refused to pay, so the bank takes the amount back.
:::

## Key points

- The **bank statement** is the customer's account in the bank's books: the mirror image of the cash book.
- Differences: **unpresented cheques**, **uncredited lodgements**, **bank charges**, **standing orders and direct debits**, **direct credits**, **dishonoured cheques**, **errors**.
- Items only on the bank statement are entered in the **cash book** first.
- From the adjusted cash book: **add unpresented cheques, deduct uncredited lodgements** to reach the bank statement balance.

## Quick check

1. What is a bank reconciliation statement?
2. What is an unpresented cheque?
3. Why are bank charges not in the cash book at first?
4. Starting from the adjusted cash book balance, what is done with uncredited lodgements?
5. Give three purposes of a bank reconciliation.

**Answers:**

1. A statement prepared to explain the difference between the bank balance in the cash book and the balance on the bank statement on the same date.
2. A cheque paid out and entered in the cash book but not yet presented to the bank for payment.
3. Because the business only learns of them when it receives the bank statement.
4. They are deducted.
5. Any three: explain the difference; detect errors; update the cash book; find old unpresented cheques; detect fraud; confirm the correct bank balance.
