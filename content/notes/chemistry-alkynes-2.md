With a triple bond, ethyne is even more unsaturated than ethene. It adds **two** molecules of hydrogen or bromine, and its hydrogen atoms are weakly acidic, so it forms metal compounds that alkenes cannot. This lesson covers the reactions of ethyne and then compares the three families of hydrocarbons, which is a favourite WASSCE question.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the addition reactions of ethyne with hydrogen, halogens, hydrogen halides and water
- describe the polymerisation of ethyne to benzene
- describe the tests for terminal alkynes using ammoniacal silver nitrate and copper(I) chloride
- compare the structure, properties and reactions of alkanes, alkenes and alkynes
- distinguish ethane, ethene and ethyne by chemical tests

## Addition reactions of ethyne

Ethyne adds in **two stages**: first to an alkene-type product, then to a saturated product.

**Hydrogen** (nickel catalyst, 150 °C):

$$\text{HC}{\equiv}\text{CH} + \text{H}_2 \rightarrow \text{CH}_2{=}\text{CH}_2$$

$$\text{CH}_2{=}\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_2 \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_3$$

**Bromine** decolourises quickly, giving **1,2-dibromoethene** and then **1,1,2,2-tetrabromoethane**:

$$\text{HC}{\equiv}\text{CH} + \text{Br}_2 \rightarrow \text{CHBr}{=}\text{CHBr}$$

$$\text{CHBr}{=}\text{CHBr} + \text{Br}_2 \rightarrow \text{CHBr}_2\text{CHBr}_2$$

**Hydrogen chloride** (mercury(II) chloride catalyst) gives **chloroethene**, the monomer for **PVC**:

$$\text{HC}{\equiv}\text{CH} + \text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{CH}_2{=}\text{CHCl}$$

**Water:** ethyne bubbled into dilute sulphuric acid containing **mercury(II) sulphate** at about 60 °C gives **ethanal** (an aldehyde), which can be oxidised to ethanoic acid:

$$\text{HC}{\equiv}\text{CH} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$$

**Potassium manganate(VII):** ethyne decolourises acidified KMnO₄, like ethene.

## Polymerisation to benzene

When ethyne is passed through a **red-hot iron tube**, three molecules join into a ring of **benzene**:

$$3\,\text{C}_2\text{H}_2 \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_6$$

## Tests for ethyne (terminal alkynes)

The hydrogen atoms on a C≡C carbon are **slightly acidic**. They can be replaced by metal ions to form **metal dicarbides** (acetylides):

| Reagent | Observation with ethyne | Product |
|---|---|---|
| **Ammoniacal silver nitrate** | **White** precipitate | Silver dicarbide, Ag₂C₂ |
| **Ammoniacal copper(I) chloride** | **Red-brown** precipitate | Copper(I) dicarbide, Cu₂C₂ |

**Ethene and ethane give no precipitate** with either reagent, so these tests distinguish ethyne from ethene. The dry dicarbides are **explosive** and must be destroyed with dilute acid after the test.

Only alkynes with a **≡C–H** group (terminal alkynes, such as ethyne and propyne) give these precipitates. But-2-yne does not.

## Comparing alkanes, alkenes and alkynes

| Feature | Alkanes | Alkenes | Alkynes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **General formula** | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | CₙH₂ₙ | CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ |
| **Carbon–carbon bond** | Single only (saturated) | One C=C (unsaturated) | One C≡C (unsaturated) |
| **Shape around the bond** | Tetrahedral, 109.5° | Planar, 120° | Linear, 180° |
| **Typical reaction** | Substitution (in UV light) | Addition (one molecule of X₂) | Addition (two molecules of X₂) |
| **Bromine water** | No change in the dark | Decolourised quickly | Decolourised quickly |
| **Acidified KMnO₄** | Stays purple | Decolourised | Decolourised |
| **Ammoniacal AgNO₃ or CuCl** | No precipitate | No precipitate | White (Ag) or red (Cu) precipitate |
| **Flame in air** | Blue, clean (small ones) | Luminous, slightly smoky | Very luminous, very sooty |
| **First member** | Methane, CH₄ | Ethene, C₂H₄ | Ethyne, C₂H₂ |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Distinguishing three gases.** You are given three unlabelled gas jars containing ethane, ethene and ethyne. Identify each.

1. Add **bromine water** to a sample of each. The one that **does not** decolourise it is **ethane**.
2. Bubble the other two through **ammoniacal silver nitrate** (or copper(I) chloride). The one that gives a **white** (or red-brown) **precipitate** is **ethyne**.
3. The remaining gas, which decolourised bromine water but gave no precipitate, is **ethene**.
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Hydrogen uptake.** 50 cm³ of ethyne is completely hydrogenated to ethane. What volume of hydrogen is used (same temperature and pressure)?

$$\text{C}_2\text{H}_2 + 2\text{H}_2 \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_6$$

1 volume of ethyne needs 2 volumes of hydrogen, so **100 cm³** of hydrogen is used.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
**PVC pipes**, used for water and drainage in buildings across Freetown, are made from **chloroethene**. One of the classic routes to chloroethene is adding hydrogen chloride to **ethyne**, though most factories now make it from ethene and chlorine. Either way, the plumbing in a new house is a product of hydrocarbon addition chemistry.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Bromine water **cannot** tell ethene from ethyne: both decolourise it. Use **ammoniacal silver nitrate or copper(I) chloride**, which react only with ethyne.
:::

## Key points

- Ethyne adds **two** molecules of H₂, Br₂ or HX, in two stages; with HCl it gives **chloroethene** (PVC); with water (HgSO₄, H₂SO₄) it gives **ethanal**.
- Three ethyne molecules form **benzene** in a red-hot iron tube.
- **Ammoniacal AgNO₃** (white ppt) and **ammoniacal CuCl** (red-brown ppt) identify **ethyne**; alkenes and alkanes give no precipitate.
- Alkanes: **substitution**; alkenes and alkynes: **addition**.
- Bromine water separates alkanes from unsaturated hydrocarbons; the metal-ion tests separate alkynes from alkenes.

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Expect to **write equations for the addition reactions of ethyne**, to **give a test that distinguishes ethene from ethyne** (with observations), to **compare alkanes, alkenes and alkynes** in a table, and to **calculate volumes of hydrogen or masses of bromine** that react with a given amount of ethyne.
:::

## Quick check

1. Name the final product when excess bromine reacts with ethyne.
2. How would you distinguish ethyne from ethene?
3. What is the product when ethyne is passed through a red-hot iron tube?
4. What volume of hydrogen is needed to convert 30 cm³ of ethyne completely to ethane?

**Answers:**

1. 1,1,2,2-Tetrabromoethane, CHBr₂CHBr₂.
2. Bubble each through ammoniacal silver nitrate: ethyne gives a white precipitate; ethene gives none. (Or ammoniacal copper(I) chloride: red-brown precipitate with ethyne.)
3. Benzene, C₆H₆.
4. 60 cm³.
