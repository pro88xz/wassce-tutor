# Production and occupations: stages, sectors and economic activity

## Learning objectives
Define production, explain utility, classify the stages of production and relate occupations to economic activity.

## Meaning of production
Production is the creation or addition of utility to satisfy human wants. Utility means usefulness or want-satisfying power. Production is not limited to manufacturing physical goods. Services also create utility. A farmer produces crops; a transporter moves them to a market; a retailer makes them available to buyers.

Utility may be added in different ways: **form utility** is created by changing a material, such as milling rice; **place utility** is created by moving goods to where they are needed; **time utility** is created by storing goods until required; and **possession utility** is created when ownership is transferred through a sale.

## Stages of production

| Stage | Main activity | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Obtaining resources from nature | Farming, fishing, forestry, mining |
| Secondary | Processing materials or manufacturing | Rice milling, baking, furniture making |
| Tertiary | Providing services and distributing goods | Banking, transport, teaching, retailing |

The stages depend on one another. A food processor needs agricultural raw materials, labour, equipment, finance and transport. A disruption in one stage can affect the entire supply chain.

## Occupations and specialisation
An occupation is a person's regular work or means of earning a livelihood. Primary occupations obtain natural resources; secondary occupations process materials; tertiary occupations provide services. Some classifications identify a quaternary sector for knowledge-intensive activities such as research and information technology. Use the categories specified by the syllabus or examination question.

Specialisation occurs when a person or firm concentrates on a particular task. It can improve skill and efficiency, but it also makes people dependent on exchange. A carpenter may specialise in furniture rather than growing food, then sell furniture to earn income to buy food and other necessities. Commerce coordinates these exchanges.

Employment may be self-employment or paid employment. A self-employed trader operates on their own account and bears business risks. An employee works for an employer under agreed conditions and receives wages or salary.

## WASSCE examination focus
Classify an occupation by the activity performed, not simply by the workplace. A worker in a factory may be engaged in secondary production, while a driver delivering the factory's products performs a tertiary service. When explaining utility, identify how usefulness is added.

## Quick check
1. Define production.
2. Classify fishing, rice milling and retailing.
3. Explain two forms of utility.
4. Why does specialisation increase the need for commerce?

**Answers:** 1. Creation or addition of utility to satisfy wants. 2. Primary, secondary and tertiary respectively. 3. Transport adds place utility; storage adds time utility; processing adds form utility; selling transfers possession. 4. Specialists do not produce everything they need, so they exchange their output or income for other goods and services.
