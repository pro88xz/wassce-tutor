*The cost of rice varies directly as the weight bought.* *The distance travelled at constant speed varies directly as the time.* When one quantity grows in proportion to another — both double together, both triple together — we have **direct variation**. WAEC tests this in every paper.

This lesson formalises direct variation, introduces the **constant of proportionality**, and works through the standard problem types: finding the constant from given values, then using it to find unknowns.

:::callout{type=definition}
**Direct variation:** $y$ varies directly as $x$ (written $y \propto x$) means $y = kx$ for some non-zero constant $k$.

Equivalently: $\dfrac{y}{x} = k$, a constant.

**Properties:**
- When $x = 0$, $y = 0$. The graph passes through the origin.
- Doubling $x$ doubles $y$. Tripling $x$ triples $y$.
- The graph of $y = kx$ is a straight line through the origin with slope $k$.

**Higher powers:** $y$ varies directly as $x^n$ means $y = kx^n$. Examples:
- $y \propto x^2$: $y = kx^2$ (area varies as square of side).
- $y \propto x^3$: $y = kx^3$ (volume varies as cube of side).
- $y \propto \sqrt{x}$: $y = k\sqrt{x}$ (period of pendulum varies as square root of length).
:::


## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- explain direct variation and the symbol $\propto$
- write a direct variation as an equation $y = kx$
- find the constant of variation and use it
- recognise direct variation from a table or graph

## Reading the notation

The symbol $\propto$ means "varies as" or "is proportional to."

- $y \propto x$: $y$ varies directly as $x$. $y = kx$.
- $y \propto x^2$: $y$ varies directly as the square of $x$. $y = kx^2$.
- $C \propto t$: cost varies directly as time. $C = kt$.
- $A \propto r^2$: area varies directly as the square of the radius. $A = kr^2$ (and $k = \pi$ for circle area).

## Step-by-step technique

For any direct variation problem:

1. **Write the relationship** with the constant: $y = kx$ (or $y = kx^n$).
2. **Find $k$** using the given pair of values.
3. **Use the equation** $y = kx$ to find unknowns.

## Basic direct variation

### Example 1

**$y$ varies directly as $x$. When $x = 4$, $y = 12$. Find $y$ when $x = 7$.**

Step 1: $y = kx$. 
Step 2: $12 = k(4)$, so $k = 3$. 
Step 3: When $x = 7$, $y = 3 \times 7 = 21$.

### Example 2

**$P \propto Q$. When $Q = 5$, $P = 20$. Find $P$ when $Q = 12$.**

$P = kQ$. $20 = 5k$, $k = 4$. 
At $Q = 12$: $P = 48$.

### Example 3

**The cost $C$ varies directly as the weight $W$. If $C = 60$ NLe when $W = 4$ kg, find $C$ when $W = 11$ kg.**

$C = kW$. $60 = 4k$, $k = 15$ NLe/kg. 
At $W = 11$: $C = 165$ NLe.

### Example 4

**$d \propto t$. When $t = 3$, $d = 75$. Find $d$ when $t = 8$.**

$d = kt$. $75 = 3k$, $k = 25$. 
At $t = 8$: $d = 200$.

### Example 5 — Finding $x$ given $y$

**$y \propto x$, $y = 5$ when $x = 2$. Find $x$ when $y = 30$.**

$k = 2.5$. $y = 2.5 x$. $30 = 2.5 x$. $x = 12$.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Solve each direct variation problem.**

**(a)** *$y \propto x$. $y = 18$ when $x = 6$. Find $y$ when $x = 10$.* 
$k = 3$. $y = 30$.

**(b)** *$P \propto t$. $P = 200$ when $t = 5$. Find $P$ when $t = 12$.* 
$k = 40$. $P = 480$.

**(c)** *$y \propto x$. $y = 24$ when $x = 8$. Find $x$ when $y = 33$.* 
$k = 3$. $x = 11$.

**(d)** *$C \propto t$. $C = 480$ NLe at $t = 6$ hours. Find $t$ when $C = 800$ NLe.* 
$k = 80$. $t = 10$ hours.

**(e)** *$A \propto h$. $A = 60$ when $h = 4$. Find $A$ when $h = 9$.* 
$k = 15$. $A = 135$.
:::

## Direct variation with higher powers

### Example 6 — $y \propto x^2$

**The area of a circle varies directly as the square of its radius. A circle of radius $4$ has area $50.27$. Find the area when radius is $7$.**

$A = kr^2$. $50.27 = 16 k$. $k \approx 3.14$ (this is $\pi$, of course). 
At $r = 7$: $A = \pi \times 49 \approx 153.94$.

### Example 7

**$y \propto x^2$. $y = 50$ when $x = 5$. Find $y$ when $x = 8$.**

$k = 50/25 = 2$. $y = 2x^2$. At $x = 8$: $y = 128$.

### Example 8 — $y \propto x^3$

**The volume of a sphere varies directly as the cube of its radius. A sphere of radius $3$ has volume $113.1$. Find volume when radius is $6$.**

$V = kr^3$. $113.1 = 27 k$. $k \approx 4.19$ (i.e., $\frac{4}{3}\pi$). 
At $r = 6$: $V = 4.19 \times 216 = 904.78$.

Note: doubling the radius multiplies the volume by $2^3 = 8$. ($113.1 \times 8 = 904.8$. ✓)

### Example 9

**$y \propto x^2$. $y = 100$ when $x = 5$. Find $x$ when $y = 144$.**

$k = 100/25 = 4$. $y = 4x^2$. $144 = 4x^2$, $x^2 = 36$, $x = 6$.

### Example 10 — $y \propto \sqrt{x}$

**The period of a pendulum varies directly as the square root of its length. A $1$ m pendulum has period $2$ s. Find the period of a $4$ m pendulum.**

$T = k\sqrt{L}$. $2 = k \sqrt{1}$, $k = 2$. 
At $L = 4$: $T = 2\sqrt{4} = 4$ s.

Four times the length, double the period.

### Example 11

**$y \propto \sqrt{x}$. $y = 6$ when $x = 4$. Find $y$ when $x = 25$.**

$k = 6/\sqrt{4} = 3$. $y = 3\sqrt{x}$. At $x = 25$: $y = 15$.

## Multiple-value problems

Sometimes you get two pairs of values and use ratios.

### Example 12

**$y \propto x$. If $y_1 = 6$ when $x_1 = 4$, find $y_2$ when $x_2 = 10$.**

For direct variation: $\dfrac{y_2}{y_1} = \dfrac{x_2}{x_1}$.
$\dfrac{y_2}{6} = \dfrac{10}{4}$, so $y_2 = 15$.

### Example 13 — $y \propto x^2$

**$y_1 = 12$ when $x_1 = 3$. Find $y_2$ when $x_2 = 5$.**

$\dfrac{y_2}{y_1} = \dfrac{x_2^2}{x_1^2}$. $\dfrac{y_2}{12} = \dfrac{25}{9}$. $y_2 = \dfrac{300}{9} \approx 33.33$.

Or find $k$ first: $k = 12/9 = 4/3$. $y_2 = 4/3 \times 25 \approx 33.33$.

## Word problems

### Example 14

**The wages of a worker varies directly as the hours worked. If a worker earns $400$ NLe in $5$ hours, how much in $12$ hours?**

$W = kh$. $400 = 5k$, $k = 80$. At $h = 12$: $W = 960$ NLe.

### Example 15

**The cost of fuel varies directly as the kilometres travelled. A car uses $30$ NLe of fuel for $50$ km. Cost for $200$ km?**

$C = kd$. $30 = 50k$, $k = 0.6$. $C = 120$ NLe.

### Example 16

**The mass of metal varies directly as the volume. $20$ litres of metal weighs $80$ kg. Mass of $35$ litres?**

$M = kV$. $80 = 20k$, $k = 4$. $M = 140$ kg.

### Example 17

**The area of a square varies directly as the square of its side. A square of side $3$ has area $9$. Find the side when area is $64$.**

$A = ks^2$. $9 = 9k$, $k = 1$. $s^2 = 64$, $s = 8$.

### Example 18

**The kinetic energy of an object varies directly as the square of its velocity. At $v = 10$ m/s, $KE = 50$ J. Find $KE$ at $v = 25$ m/s.**

$KE = kv^2$. $50 = 100k$, $k = 0.5$. 
At $v = 25$: $KE = 0.5 \times 625 = 312.5$ J.

### Example 19 — Sierra Leonean context

**A taxi fare varies directly as the distance. Aminata paid $150$ NLe for $10$ km. Mohamed paid $375$ NLe. How far did Mohamed travel?**

$F = kd$. $150 = 10k$, $k = 15$ NLe/km. 
Mohamed: $375 = 15 d$, $d = 25$ km.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Word problems.**

**(a)** *Distance $\propto$ time at constant speed. A car covers $120$ km in $2$ hours. Distance in $5$ hours?* 
$k = 60$. $d = 300$ km.

**(b)** *Cost $\propto$ weight. $4$ kg costs NLe 25. Find cost of $15$ kg.* 
$k = 6.25$. $C = 93.75$ NLe.

**(c)** *Area $\propto$ (side)². If side $= 4$ cm, area $= 16$ cm². Find side when area $= 49$ cm².* 
$k = 1$. $s = 7$ cm.

**(d)** *Mass $\propto$ length for a uniform rod. A $2$-m rod weighs $5$ kg. Mass of $7$ m?* 
$k = 2.5$. $M = 17.5$ kg.

**(e)** *$y \propto x^{1/2}$. $y = 3$ when $x = 9$. Find $y$ when $x = 25$.* 
$k = 1$. $y = 5$.

**(f)** *$y \propto x^3$. $y = 8$ when $x = 2$. Find $y$ when $x = 5$.* 
$k = 1$. $y = 125$.

## Distinguishing direct variation from other relationships

### Example 20

**Is the following a case of direct variation? 
*A taxi charges a NLe 50 flag-down fee plus NLe 15 per kilometre.***

No — this is **NOT** direct variation. The relationship is $F = 50 + 15d$, which has a non-zero intercept. (At $d = 0$, fare is $50$, not $0$.) This is **partial variation**, covered in the next lesson.

### Example 21

**Is the following direct variation? 
*The area of a square scales with the square of its side.***

Yes — direct variation with the square. $A = ks^2$. Pure power-law relationship through the origin.

## Ratio interpretation of direct variation

For direct variation $y \propto x$, the ratio $y/x$ is constant.

### Example 22

**Test if these data fit a direct variation: 
$(x, y) = (2, 8), (5, 20), (3, 12), (10, 40)$.**

Ratios $y/x$: $8/2 = 4$, $20/5 = 4$, $12/3 = 4$, $40/10 = 4$.

All equal. Direct variation with $k = 4$.

### Example 23

**Test: $(1, 4), (2, 16), (3, 36), (4, 64)$.**

Ratios $y/x$: $4, 8, 12, 16$. Not constant.

Ratios $y/x^2$: $4/1 = 4$, $16/4 = 4$, $36/9 = 4$, $64/16 = 4$. All equal.

So $y \propto x^2$ with $k = 4$.

:::callout{type=local-example}
A Sierra Leonean cassava farmer's yield varies directly as the area planted. From a plot of $0.5$ acre, she harvested $400$ kg of cassava.

**Step 1.** $Y = kA$. $400 = 0.5 k$, $k = 800$ kg/acre.

**Step 2.** For a $2.5$-acre plot: $Y = 800 \times 2.5 = 2000$ kg.

If the price of cassava varies directly as the weight (since it's sold by the kilogram), and 1 kg sells for $5$ NLe, then her revenue from a $2.5$-acre plot is $2000 \times 5 = 10000$ NLe.

Direct variation is the natural language for proportional scaling — exactly what WAEC tests in agricultural and business contexts.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
**Common errors in direct variation:**

**1. Confusing direct variation with linear function.**
- $y = kx$: direct variation (passes through origin).
- $y = mx + c$: linear function (general, with $c \neq 0$ usually NOT direct variation).

**2. Forgetting to find $k$ first.**
- Always compute $k$ before applying the formula.

**3. Wrong power.**
- $y \propto x^2$ means $y = kx^2$, not $y = (kx)^2 = k^2 x^2$.

**4. Sign errors with the constant.**
- $k > 0$ usually. If $k$ were negative, increasing $x$ would decrease $y$.

**5. Misreading the proportion.**
- *"$y$ varies as $x$"* = $y \propto x$ = $y = kx$.
- *"$y$ is inversely proportional to $x$"* = $y = k/x$ (next lesson).

**6. Substituting wrong values.**
- Be careful which value goes where in $y = kx$.
:::

## WASSCE examination focus

:::callout{type=waec-tests}
Direct variation WAEC questions:

- **Find $k$.** Given a pair of values.
- **Find $y$ given $x$.** After finding $k$.
- **Find $x$ given $y$.** Use the inverse.
- **Higher powers.** $y \propto x^2$, $y \propto x^3$, $y \propto \sqrt{x}$.
- **Word problems.** Costs, distances, areas, volumes.
- **Ratio comparisons.** $y_2/y_1 = $ (power of) $x_2/x_1$.

Write $y = kx^n$, find $k$, apply.
:::
