For over two thousand years people argued about whether matter could be cut up forever. John Dalton's atomic theory (1808) said no: there are indivisible atoms. A century of experiments then showed that atoms themselves contain smaller particles. This lesson traces that story and teaches you to read the numbers that describe every atom.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- state Dalton's atomic theory and the modifications made to it
- describe the discoveries of the electron, nucleus and neutron
- state the relative masses and charges of protons, neutrons and electrons
- define atomic number and mass number and use nuclide notation
- work out the numbers of particles in atoms and ions
- define isotopes and give examples

## Dalton's atomic theory

1. All matter is made of tiny, **indivisible** particles called **atoms**.
2. Atoms can be neither **created nor destroyed**.
3. Atoms of the **same element are identical** in mass and properties; atoms of different elements differ.
4. Atoms combine in **simple whole-number ratios** to form compounds.
5. Chemical reactions **rearrange** atoms.

### Modifications to the theory

| Dalton said | We now know |
|---|---|
| Atoms are indivisible | Atoms contain **protons, neutrons and electrons** |
| Atoms of an element are identical | **Isotopes** of an element have different masses |
| Atoms cannot be created or destroyed | True in chemical reactions, but **nuclear reactions** change atoms into other atoms |
| Atoms combine in simple ratios | Large organic molecules have complex ratios (e.g. C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁) |

The theory still explains the **law of conservation of mass** and the **law of constant composition**: atoms are rearranged, not lost, in reactions.

## Discovering the particles

| Scientist | Experiment | Discovery |
|---|---|---|
| **J. J. Thomson** (1897) | Cathode rays bent by electric and magnetic fields | The **electron**, a negative particle in all atoms; "plum pudding" model |
| **Ernest Rutherford** (1911) | Alpha particles fired at gold foil | A tiny, dense, positive **nucleus** |
| **Niels Bohr** (1913) | Spectra of hydrogen | Electrons move in fixed **energy levels (shells)** |
| **James Chadwick** (1932) | Bombarding beryllium with alpha particles | The **neutron**, a neutral particle in the nucleus |

![Alpha particles fired at gold foil: most pass straight through, a few are deflected and a very few bounce back](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/gold-foil.svg)

*Figure 1. Rutherford's gold foil experiment. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

**What the gold foil results showed:**

- **Most alpha particles passed straight through**, so the atom is mostly **empty space**.
- **A few were deflected**, so there is a **positive** centre that repels the positive alpha particles.
- **A very few bounced back**, so this centre, the **nucleus**, is very small but holds almost all the **mass**.

## The structure of the atom

![A sodium atom with a nucleus of protons and neutrons surrounded by electrons in shells 2, 8, 1, beside a table of the masses and charges of the particles](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/atom-model.svg)

*Figure 2. The particles in an atom. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

| Particle | Location | Relative mass | Relative charge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Proton** | Nucleus | 1 | +1 |
| **Neutron** | Nucleus | 1 | 0 |
| **Electron** | Shells around the nucleus | $\tfrac{1}{1840}$ (almost 0) | −1 |

A neutral atom has **equal numbers of protons and electrons**, so its charges cancel.

## Atomic number and mass number

:::callout{type=definition}
- **Atomic number (Z)**: the number of **protons** in the nucleus of an atom. It identifies the element.
- **Mass number (A)**: the total number of **protons + neutrons** (nucleons) in the nucleus.
- **Number of neutrons** $= A - Z$.
:::

Nuclide notation writes the mass number at the top left and the atomic number at the bottom left of the symbol:

$${}^{A}_{Z}\text{X} \qquad \text{e.g.} \qquad {}^{23}_{11}\text{Na}$$

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Counting particles.**

| Species | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons |
|---|---|---|---|
| ${}^{23}_{11}\text{Na}$ atom | 11 | 23 − 11 = 12 | 11 (neutral atom) |
| ${}^{23}_{11}\text{Na}^+$ ion | 11 | 12 | 11 − 1 = **10** (lost one) |
| ${}^{16}_{8}\text{O}^{2-}$ ion | 8 | 16 − 8 = 8 | 8 + 2 = **10** (gained two) |
| ${}^{27}_{13}\text{Al}^{3+}$ ion | 13 | 27 − 13 = 14 | 13 − 3 = **10** (lost three) |

A **positive** ion has **lost** electrons; a **negative** ion has **gained** electrons. The protons never change in a chemical reaction.
:::

## Isotopes

:::callout{type=definition}
**Isotopes** are atoms of the **same element** with the **same atomic number** (same number of protons) but **different mass numbers** (different numbers of neutrons).
:::

| Element | Isotopes |
|---|---|
| Hydrogen | ¹H (protium, 0 neutrons), ²H (deuterium, 1 neutron), ³H (tritium, 2 neutrons) |
| Carbon | ¹²C (6 neutrons), ¹³C (7), ¹⁴C (8, radioactive) |
| Chlorine | ³⁵Cl (18 neutrons), ³⁷Cl (20 neutrons) |
| Oxygen | ¹⁶O, ¹⁷O, ¹⁸O |

- Isotopes have the **same chemical properties**, because chemistry depends on **electrons**, and isotopes have the same number of electrons.
- They differ in **physical properties** that depend on mass, such as density and rate of diffusion.
- Some isotopes, such as carbon-14, are **radioactive** (next lesson).

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Isotopes differ in the number of **neutrons**, not protons. If the number of protons changes, the atom becomes a **different element**.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
The diamonds mined in Kono are pure carbon, made mostly of carbon-12 atoms with about 1% carbon-13. Every carbon atom, in a diamond, in charcoal or in your body, has exactly **6 protons**.
:::

## Key points

- **Dalton's theory** pictured indivisible atoms; it was modified after the discovery of **subatomic particles, isotopes and nuclear reactions**.
- **Thomson** found the electron, **Rutherford** the nucleus, **Chadwick** the neutron.
- **Protons** (+1, mass 1) and **neutrons** (0, mass 1) are in the nucleus; **electrons** (−1, mass 1/1840) are in shells.
- **Atomic number** = protons; **mass number** = protons + neutrons; **neutrons** = A − Z.
- **Isotopes**: same protons, different neutrons; same chemical properties.

## Quick check

1. State two postulates of Dalton's atomic theory that have been modified.
2. Which observation in the gold foil experiment showed that the atom is mostly empty space?
3. How many protons, neutrons and electrons are in ${}^{39}_{19}\text{K}^+$?
4. Why do isotopes of an element have the same chemical properties?

**Answers:**

1. Any two: atoms are indivisible; atoms of the same element are identical; atoms cannot be created or destroyed; atoms combine in simple ratios.
2. Most alpha particles passed straight through the foil.
3. 19 protons, 39 − 19 = 20 neutrons, 19 − 1 = 18 electrons.
4. They have the same number of electrons, and chemical properties depend on electrons.
