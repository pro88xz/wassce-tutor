How long must a factory pass current to plate a batch of spoons with silver, or to extract a tonne of aluminium? Michael Faraday answered with two simple laws connecting the **quantity of electricity** to the **mass of product**. This lesson covers Faraday's laws, the calculations based on them, and the industrial uses of electrolysis.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- calculate quantity of electricity using Q = It
- state Faraday's first and second laws of electrolysis
- use the Faraday constant (96 500 C per mole of electrons)
- calculate masses and volumes of products, currents and times
- compare products in cells connected in series
- describe electroplating, purification of copper and other uses of electrolysis

## Quantity of electricity

$$Q = I \times t$$

where **Q** is the charge in **coulombs (C)**, **I** is the current in **amperes (A)** and **t** is the time in **seconds (s)**.

## Faraday's laws

:::callout{type=definition}
- **First law:** the **mass** of a substance liberated at an electrode is **directly proportional to the quantity of electricity** passed.
- **Second law:** when the same quantity of electricity passes through different electrolytes, the **numbers of moles** of substances liberated are inversely proportional to the **charges on their ions**.
:::

The **Faraday constant** is the charge carried by **one mole of electrons**:

$$1 \text{ F} = 96\,500 \text{ C mol}^{-1}$$

So the charge needed to deposit one mole of a metal depends on its ion's charge:

| Half-equation | Electrons per mole of product | Charge for 1 mole |
|---|---|---|
| Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag | 1 | 96 500 C (1 F) |
| Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu | 2 | 193 000 C (2 F) |
| Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al | 3 | 289 500 C (3 F) |
| 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂ | 2 | 193 000 C (2 F) |
| 4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻ | 4 | 386 000 C (4 F) |

## Calculations

Use the chain: **Q = It → moles of electrons (Q ÷ 96 500) → mole ratio from the half-equation → moles of product → mass or volume**.

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**1. Mass deposited.** A current of 2.0 A flows through copper(II) sulphate solution for 30 minutes. What mass of copper is deposited? (Cu = 63.5)

- $Q = 2.0 \times 30 \times 60 = 3600$ C
- Moles of electrons $= 3600 \div 96\,500 = 0.0373$ mol
- Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu, so moles of Cu $= 0.0373 \div 2 = 0.0187$ mol
- Mass $= 0.0187 \times 63.5 = 1.18$ g
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**2. Volume of gas.** What volume of hydrogen, at s.t.p., is produced when 0.50 A passes through dilute sulphuric acid for 1930 s?

- $Q = 0.50 \times 1930 = 965$ C → $965 \div 96\,500 = 0.0100$ mol of electrons
- 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂, so moles of H₂ = 0.00500 mol
- Volume $= 0.00500 \times 22.4 = 0.112$ dm³ = **112 cm³**
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**3. Time needed.** How long must a current of 0.50 A flow to deposit 0.54 g of silver? (Ag = 108)

- Moles of Ag $= 0.54 \div 108 = 0.0050$ mol; Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag, so 0.0050 mol of electrons is needed.
- $Q = 0.0050 \times 96\,500 = 482.5$ C
- $t = Q \div I = 482.5 \div 0.50 = 965$ s (about 16 minutes)
:::

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**4. Cells in series.** The same current passes through silver nitrate and copper(II) sulphate cells in series. 1.08 g of silver is deposited. What mass of copper is deposited? (Ag = 108, Cu = 63.5)

- Moles of Ag $= 1.08 \div 108 = 0.0100$ mol, so 0.0100 mol of electrons passed through **each** cell.
- Moles of Cu $= 0.0100 \div 2 = 0.00500$ mol
- Mass of Cu $= 0.00500 \times 63.5 = 0.318$ g

The same charge deposits **half** as many moles of copper as of silver, because Cu²⁺ needs two electrons per atom (Faraday's second law).
:::

## Uses of electrolysis

### Extraction of reactive metals

Metals high in the reactivity series (sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, aluminium) **cannot be extracted by carbon reduction**, so they are extracted by electrolysis of their **molten** compounds. Aluminium is extracted from purified bauxite (aluminium oxide) dissolved in molten **cryolite**.

### Purification (refining) of copper

![Impure copper anode and pure copper cathode in copper(II) sulphate solution; copper dissolves at the anode and deposits at the cathode, impurities fall as anode sludge](https://wasscetutor.com/img/chemistry/copper-refining.svg)

*Figure 1. Electrolytic refining of copper. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

- **Anode:** impure copper; Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻.
- **Cathode:** thin sheet of pure copper; Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu.
- **Electrolyte:** acidified copper(II) sulphate.
- Impurities such as silver and gold fall to the bottom as **anode sludge**, which is collected and is valuable.
- Pure copper (99.99%) is needed for **electrical wiring**.

### Electroplating

Electroplating coats an object with a thin layer of another metal, for appearance or to prevent corrosion.

- The **object** to be plated is the **cathode**.
- The **plating metal** is the **anode**.
- The **electrolyte** is a solution of a **salt of the plating metal**.

For example, silver-plating a spoon uses a silver anode and a silver salt solution; chromium plating protects bicycle and motorbike parts.

### Other uses

- **Chlor-alkali industry:** electrolysis of brine gives **chlorine** (water treatment, PVC, bleach), **sodium hydroxide** (soap, paper) and **hydrogen**.
- **Anodising** aluminium: thickens its protective oxide layer.
- **Manufacture of hydrogen and oxygen**.

:::callout{type=local-example}
Sierra Leone mines **bauxite**, the ore of aluminium, but the bauxite is exported for processing elsewhere. Extracting aluminium needs a very large, cheap supply of **electricity**: Faraday's laws show that every mole of aluminium (27 g) needs 3 moles of electrons. Countries with big hydroelectric schemes smelt it, which is one reason ore is shipped abroad.
:::

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
Always convert time to **seconds** before using Q = It, and divide by the number of electrons in the half-equation. Forgetting that Cu²⁺ needs **two** electrons per atom doubles your answer.
:::

## Key points

- $Q = It$ (coulombs = amperes × seconds).
- **Faraday's first law:** mass ∝ quantity of electricity. **Second law:** moles liberated ∝ 1 ÷ ionic charge.
- **1 F = 96 500 C** = the charge of one mole of electrons.
- Chain: **Q → moles of e⁻ → mole ratio → moles of product → mass or volume**.
- Uses: **extracting reactive metals (Na, Al), refining copper, electroplating, chlor-alkali industry, anodising**.

## Quick check

1. Calculate the charge passed when 3.0 A flows for 10 minutes.
2. How many faradays are needed to deposit 1 mole of aluminium?
3. What mass of silver is deposited by 0.20 F? (Ag = 108)
4. In electroplating a spoon with nickel, which electrode is the spoon?

**Answers:**

1. $Q = 3.0 \times 600 = 1800$ C.
2. Three (Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al).
3. 0.20 mol of Ag $= 0.20 \times 108 = 21.6$ g.
4. The cathode.
