Not every warehouse does the same job. A supermarket's back store, a government food reserve, a cold room full of fish and a customs-controlled store of imported drinks all hold goods, but they are owned, run and used differently. This lesson covers the types of warehouses, how goods in them are controlled, and the documents that prove who owns them.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- describe the types of warehouses and their uses
- explain how a bonded warehouse works and its advantages
- distinguish public, private and bonded warehouses
- explain stock control methods and calculate a reorder level
- explain warehouse documents: warrants, receipts and delivery orders

## Types of warehouses

| Type | Owned by / used by | Main features |
|---|---|---|
| **Private warehouse** | A manufacturer, wholesaler or retailer, for its **own** goods | Designed for the owner's goods; under the owner's control |
| **Public warehouse** | Government or a company; **rented to anyone** for a fee | Useful to small traders who cannot build their own; issues warrants |
| **Bonded warehouse** | Licensed by **customs**; holds imported goods on which **duty has not been paid** | Under customs control; goods released when duty is paid |
| **Cold storage (refrigerated)** | Public or private | Keeps perishable goods: fish, meat, fruit, drugs, vaccines |
| **Produce warehouse / silo** | Farmers' cooperatives, marketing bodies, government | Stores and grades crops: rice, cocoa, coffee, grain |
| **Bulk storage tanks** | Oil companies | Store petrol, diesel, kerosene and gas |
| **Dock (port) warehouse** | Port authority | Holds goods awaiting shipment or collection at the port |
| **Cooperative warehouse** | Members of a cooperative | Shared storage that small members could not afford alone |
| **Government warehouse** | The state | Food security reserves, medicines, relief supplies |

## The bonded warehouse

:::callout{type=definition}
A **bonded warehouse** is a warehouse, licensed and supervised by the customs authority, where **imported goods are stored before import duty has been paid**. The owner gives a **bond** (a guarantee) to customs that duty will be paid before goods are released for home use.
:::

![Imported goods go into a bonded warehouse under customs control; goods for the home market are released in batches when duty is paid, and re-exported goods leave without paying import duty](https://wasscetutor.com/img/commerce/bonded-warehouse.svg)

*Figure 1. Duty is paid only when goods leave the bonded warehouse for the home market. WASSCE Tutor diagram.*

**Goods often kept in bond:** wines and spirits, tobacco and cigarettes, perfume, and other goods with high import duties.

**Advantages to the importer:**

1. **Duty is paid in instalments**, only on the batch taken out, so capital is not tied up.
2. **No duty on re-exported goods**, which helps entrepôt trade.
3. Goods may be **sampled, repacked, blended or branded** in bond.
4. Goods are **safe** under customs supervision.
5. Ownership can be **transferred** by warrant while the goods are still in bond.

**Advantages to the government:**

1. Duty is **collected easily** under customs control.
2. **Smuggling is reduced**.
3. **Entrepôt trade** is encouraged.

### Public, private and bonded warehouses compared

| | Public | Private | Bonded |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who may use it** | Anyone who pays the fee | The owner only | Importers of dutiable goods |
| **Control** | Warehouse keeper | The owner | **Customs** and the keeper |
| **Duty** | Already paid | Already paid | **Not yet paid** |
| **Typical goods** | Any goods | Owner's stock | Imported spirits, tobacco, perfume |

## Stock control

Stock control makes sure there is **enough stock to meet demand** without tying up too much money or letting goods spoil.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Maximum stock level** | The most stock that should be held, limited by space, cost and spoilage |
| **Minimum (buffer) stock** | A safety stock kept for unexpected demand or delays |
| **Reorder level** | The stock level at which a new order is placed |
| **Lead time** | The time between placing an order and receiving the goods |
| **FIFO** (first in, first out) | Older stock is issued first, so goods do not expire or deteriorate |
| **Bin card / stock card** | A record of goods received, issued and the balance for each item |
| **Stocktaking** | Counting the goods physically and checking against the records |

:::callout{type=worked-example}
**Reorder level.** A Kenema wholesaler sells 40 cartons of tomato paste a day. The supplier takes 5 days to deliver, and the wholesaler keeps a buffer stock of 100 cartons.

$$\text{reorder level} = \text{daily use} \times \text{lead time} + \text{buffer stock}$$

$$\text{reorder level} = 40 \times 5 + 100 = 300 \text{ cartons}$$

When stock falls to 300 cartons the wholesaler orders again. The 200 cartons sold during the 5-day wait leave the 100-carton buffer for emergencies.
:::

## Warehouse documents

| Document | Meaning | Document of title? |
|---|---|---|
| **Warehouse warrant** | Issued by a public warehouse keeper to the owner of stored goods; proves ownership | **Yes**: transferable by endorsement; can be used as **security for a loan** |
| **Dock warrant** | Issued by a dock (port) authority for goods stored at the docks | **Yes**: transferable by endorsement |
| **Warehouse keeper's receipt** | An acknowledgement that goods have been received into store | **No**: only a receipt; not transferable |
| **Delivery order** | An instruction from the owner to the warehouse keeper to release goods to a named person | No; it authorises release |
| **Goods received note** | Records goods arriving at the warehouse | No |
| **Weight note** | Shows the weight of goods received at the port or warehouse | No |

:::callout{type=common-mistake}
A **warehouse warrant** is a document of title and can be transferred or pledged to a bank. A **warehouse keeper's receipt** is only a receipt, so it cannot be used to transfer ownership.
:::

:::callout{type=local-example}
Importers of drinks and cigarettes in Freetown use bonded warehouses supervised by the **Customs Services of the NRA**, and pay duty on each consignment they withdraw. Supermarkets and pharmacies keep private stores and use FIFO so that goods do not expire on the shelves.
:::

## Problems of warehousing in West Africa

1. **High cost** of building and running modern warehouses.
2. **Power cuts**, which spoil goods in cold stores.
3. **Pests and humidity** that damage grain and produce.
4. **Theft and fire**.
5. **Few public warehouses** in rural areas, so farmers sell at low harvest prices.
6. **Poor record keeping** and stock control.

## Key terms

:::callout{type=definition title="Key terms"}
- **Private warehouse**: a warehouse owned by a manufacturer, wholesaler or retailer for its own goods.
- **Public warehouse**: a warehouse rented to anyone for a fee.
- **Maximum stock level**: the most stock that should be held, limited by space, cost and spoilage.
- **Buffer stock** (minimum stock): a safety stock kept for unexpected demand or delays.
- **Reorder level**: the stock level at which a new order should be placed.
- **Dock warrant**: a document issued by a port warehouse as a receipt and title to goods stored there.
:::

## Key points

- Types: **private, public, bonded, cold storage, produce, bulk tanks, dock, cooperative and government** warehouses.
- A **bonded warehouse** stores imported goods **before duty is paid**; duty is paid in batches and not at all on re-exports.
- **Stock control**: maximum, buffer, reorder level and **FIFO**; reorder level = daily use × lead time + buffer stock.
- **Warehouse and dock warrants** are documents of title; a **warehouse keeper's receipt** is not.

## Quick check

1. What is a bonded warehouse?
2. Give two advantages of a bonded warehouse to an importer.
3. Daily sales are 25 units, lead time is 4 days and buffer stock is 50 units. Find the reorder level.
4. Which warehouse document can be used as security for a bank loan?

**Answers:** 1. A customs-licensed warehouse where imported goods are stored before import duty is paid. 2. Any two: duty paid in instalments, no duty on re-exports, goods can be repacked or blended, safety, ownership can be transferred by warrant. 3. $25 \times 4 + 50 = 150$ units. 4. The warehouse warrant (or dock warrant).
